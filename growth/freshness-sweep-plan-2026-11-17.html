Freshness sweep: plan and failure modes

Dealectric · 2026-11-17 · for Barry's go-ahead before anything runs

One full check of every price, stock state, link and code on the site, then a rebuild where each page shows the date its prices and stock were actually read. About 1–2 days of session time to build and about 3 hours to run. It occupies the Agent Chrome for about 2 of those hours. The biggest risks are showing a wrong price with a fresh date, and question pages whose text quotes a price the table has since changed.

Steps

  1. Read everything, change nothing. Run the five checks above into one dated report.
  2. Sort the results. A price that moved 25% or less on the same variant is applied. A bigger move, a different variant, a bundle-or-base mismatch or an unreadable page is held for review and keeps its old date. A sold-out product turns on its sold_out_alt. A broken link is held, never quietly re-pointed.
  3. Record read dates apart from content. A new src/price_reads.json stores the price, stock and time per product or row. The build shows a read's date when that read is newer than the record. products.py changes only where a price really changed.
  4. Stamp what was read.
    • Product pages: the price line's date becomes the read date ("$499 at BLUETTI's US store, checked 11-18").
    • Question pages: under "Updated …", add "Prices and stock in the table checked 11-18". That date is the oldest read among the table's priced rows. A row that couldn't be read shows its own older date.
    • Code pages already carry "last passed <date>".
    • JSON-LD dateModified moves only when content changed. Sitemap lastmod follows the page bytes, so a new stamp counts.
  5. Flag answers the new prices contradict. On the 63 question pages whose short answer quotes a dollar figure, and on any "cheapest" or "under $X" page whose ranking flips, I hold the change and list it for review. I don't rewrite the answer automatically.
  6. Build, check, ship. Build in a temp worktree under the repo lock and check the diff with pagediff.py, so only dates, prices and stock change. Then commit, push, deploy the Worker if the link map changed, push changed URLs to IndexNow, and read a sample of live pages back through r.jina.ai.
  7. Report. A results artifact (what changed, what's held, what couldn't be read), plus To-Dos rows for the held items and the old-Mac fix.

Calls I made (tell me to change any)

  • Price moves of 25% or less are applied without review; larger ones are held.
  • The A/B pages get the stamp on both arms rather than being skipped.
  • Answers aren't rewritten in this run, only flagged.
  • This is a one-time run from the work laptop. Making it daily depends on fixing the old Mac's sweep first.
