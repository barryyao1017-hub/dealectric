# How much power does a household refrigerator use, and what size power station can run it in an outage?

**Short answer:** Our product records don't include refrigerator wattage, startup surge or daily energy figures (including Department of Energy numbers), so we won't quote any. Find those on your fridge's EnergyGuide label or nameplate. Then compare two numbers: the station's watt-hours (Wh) against your fridge's daily Wh, and the station's surge-capable output against your fridge's startup draw. Of the records we have, the **BLUETTI Elite 200 V2** (2,073.6 Wh, 2,600 W continuous, 3,900 W Power Lifting) is listed for backing up a fridge, router and lights for a day or two. The **BLUETTI Elite 300** (3,014.4 Wh) is the pick for multi-day outages.

## Which ones don't fit
- **Tenergy T320** (300 Wh, 200 W inverter), **BLUETTI Elite 30 V2** (288 Wh, 600 W) and **ALLPOWERS R600** (299 Wh, 600 W, sold out at verification) are built for phones, laptops, CPAP and lights. They are too small for fridge backup over an outage.
- **ALLPOWERS S2000 Pro** and **R4000** have the capacity and output, but they are for occasional high-draw use or whole-circuit backup. Both cost more than a single fridge needs.

## How to size it
Divide the station's Wh by your fridge's average watts to estimate runtime in hours. The fridge's startup draw must also fit within the station's surge rating. Real runtime will be shorter than the simple division suggests.
