# How many watts of solar panel to recharge a 1000Wh portable power station, allowing for charging losses and peak sun hours

**Short answer:** Plan on roughly 1,200 Wh of solar energy to fill a 1000Wh power station once charging losses are counted. That is a general rule of thumb, not a figure from our records. A single 400 W panel, like the ALLPOWERS SP037 in our records, would need about 3 peak sun hours to supply that (400 W × 3 h = 1,200 Wh), so with 4 to 5 peak sun hours it can fill a 1000Wh station in a day, and on a weaker day it takes longer. Check that your power station accepts the panel's 37.4 V working and 45.3 V open-circuit voltage before buying.

## The sizing math

- Energy needed: 1000 Wh ÷ about 0.8 efficiency (an assumed allowance for charging losses) = about 1,250 Wh. We use about 1,200 Wh as a round figure.
- Panel watts needed: 1,200 Wh ÷ peak sun hours.
  - 6 peak sun hours: about 200 W
  - 4 peak sun hours: about 300 W
  - 3 peak sun hours: about 400 W
- Real output also falls with heat, haze, panel angle and shade, so choose the next size up if you want a one-day recharge.

The sun-hour and loss figures above are general planning assumptions, not tested results.
