Seen but not cited: what the winners had that we didn't

The same 88 chats as the "Seen but Not Cited" page, read from the other side: for each of the 64 losses, who ChatGPT cited instead, the exact sentence that citation supports, and what those pages contain that ours don't. Then the research angles that follow, and what we are doing right and wrong today.

The short version. We lose to primary sources far more than to aggregators. In the 64 losses ChatGPT cited a maker's own page 37 times, a retailer 31 times, an aggregator 38 times and docs or forums 16 times; only 9 losses went to aggregators alone. Every citation backs a specific, checkable claim: a spec the maker confirms, a listing price, a documentation rule, a measured number, or a derived figure. We get cited in exactly the same way, when we hold a fact of that kind that no primary source in the result set states.

The biggest single lever is still ours to pull. Of the pages ranked first, those with the AI-addressed block were cited 5 of 29 times; those without it, 7 of 9. Fifty of the 88 seen pages still carry the block.

The research question for every page is the one Barry stated: what is the one piece of information the answer needs that no result on the page can supply? The six rival pages read below each answer it in a different way, and none of them claims to test products.

4. What we are doing right

  • Holding a fact nobody else has. Every one of our 24 citations backs one: measured charging noise across models, Wh per pound, the price of a kit under a cap, a pre-order date, the charge time that needs an app setting, a torque range, a RAM requirement. The under-$500 page was the only source cited at rank 5 because it was the only result that answered "kit under $500".
  • Answering the question's mechanism first. The microwave page's cooking-watts-vs-input-watts opening is exactly the kind of sentence the model reuses; it reused it, and cited the site that also had the 85% assumption.
  • Dated, specific prices on products we carry. The model trusts "last verified November 6" enough to repeat the number with the date.
  • Summarising independent tests with attribution. The noise page was cited as "independent testing summarized by Dealectric". That is the honest, citable version of "we tested".
  • Retrieval. 38 of the 88 seen pages ranked first. Getting found is not the problem.

5. What we are doing wrong

  1. Addressing the assistant. The block costs us the citation (17% vs 78% at rank 1) and spends the first 500 characters of the text budget on text no shopper reads. It is still on 50 of 88 seen pages.
  2. Letting the buy block crowd out the facts. Six near-identical code sentences per page, repeated again inside the table, is 1,000+ characters of boilerplate that reaches the model before the specs do. Rivals put one method line at the top and the links in the table.
  3. Publishing "Not confirmed". The rivals that beat us read the manual. A cell that says "Not confirmed" or "Jackery's store refused our reads" tells the model our research stopped where theirs continued.
  4. Pricing only what we sell. "Not sold through Dealectric; listed for comparison" leaves half the table without the number the shopper asked for. PowerSized prices every kit; Starry Hope prices every NAS.
  5. Not naming anyone. No reviewer names, no "Notebookcheck measured", no "Jellyfin's docs say". The model cites judgement only when it can see whose judgement it is.
  6. Wrong page shape for the model's own search. When it searches "DELTA 2 AC180 C1000 1000 v2 specifications", a four-model spec-normalisation page wins and a "best under $Y" listicle loses, even at rank 1.
  7. Hedging the wrong things. "Our estimate" is fine when the arithmetic is shown; "Not confirmed" is not. Backup Power shows its division and gets cited for it.

6. The research angles, in general

Each is a way to answer "what is the one thing the answer needs that no result states". A page needs one of these done thoroughly, not all of them done thinly.

A. Normalise what makers state differently

Cycle life to 80% vs 70%. Surge vs continuous. Cooking watts vs input watts. "Hardware transcoding" on stock firmware vs with a script. One table where the footnote is the product.

B. Derive the number the question is really asking

Wh per pound. Reheats per charge. Furnace hours at 50% and 77% duty. Refill hours from the kit's own panel. Show the arithmetic in one line so the model can reuse the assumption.

C. Exact compatibility, per cell, with source and date

Which pack, which cable, which firmware, which generation is excluded. "OEM source · date" on every cell. Leave the cell off rather than write "Not confirmed".

D. The mechanism that decides the outcome

Flame rectification and floating neutral. PCIe x1 lanes capping an M.2 slot. Quick Sync present but driverless on DSM. One paragraph of why, with the maker's or standard's own words.

E. Named measurements, collected

Notebookcheck's idle watts, RTINGS' microphone score, SoundGuys' test, the two sites that publish fan dB. The model cites the page that has them side by side and names each.

F. The whole category, losers included

Every model the model might pick, priced, with the one reason each loses. The model's search already names its candidates; the page that covers all of them is the one it can cite for the comparison.

G. Price of record, dated per figure

Kit price vs parts separately, 30-day median, out-of-stock kept and dated. Our daily sweep already does this for codes; the page should do it for every product in the table.

H. One honest method line

"Figures from each maker's US product page or manual, checked <date>. We don't test units. We earn a commission on links marked Buy." The six winners all have a version of this. It replaces the AI block.

7. What this means for the deal pages

The two page kinds from the buy-link rethink stay. For the question pages, the safety net Barry described is real: a page cited as the source of the normalised table or the derived number brings the shopper to a page where the code and the pre-applied link sit next to the winner. The research brief for a question page becomes: pick one angle from A to H that the current top 12 lacks, do it completely for the model's own candidate set, cite sources per cell, and put the buy links in the table instead of a block above it.

For the deal pages, the same rule applies in miniature: the one missing fact is the checkout price with the code, dated, against the maker's and the catalog's price the model can see.
