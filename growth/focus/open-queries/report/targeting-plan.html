Two things follow. First, in our own test chats ChatGPT wrote our buy link about once in 215; real shoppers clicked such a link 310 times in three weeks. Our single-question chats are not what shoppers do, so the logs are the better instrument. Second, more than half of all sales arrived through a code, with no link involved.

Why question-first could not work

  • Search platforms count short phrases. Those are the questions ChatGPT answers from its catalog and from makers.
  • Long questions open more often, but no platform counts them, and the same question flips between chats.
  • So each test bought one noisy reading on a question nobody could prove was asked. 700 chats gave 5 openings.

The reframe: openness belongs to the offer

ChatGPT passes our link along when we hold something its catalog and the maker's site do not:

  • A kit the catalog has no row for. Station-plus-panel sets were the rows that stayed free in every round.
  • A price below the store's own, through a code that was tested today.
  • A fact that expires, such as this week's deal. ChatGPT has to search for those.

Demand then comes from the category's main question, which platforms can count. The shopper asks the broad question, ChatGPT names products, and in the follow-up (which one, where, how much) ours is the only offer with that bundle or that price.

Because a page is cheap and the logs read retrieval, visits and clicks, the live page is the real test. Gate lightly (the offer is free of the catalog, the category has demand), publish in batches of about 15, read the logs after a week, and drop what gets no fetches.

Beyond pages

  1. Codes as the handover. A code survives where a link is dropped, and it already carries most BLUETTI sales. Ask each partner for a code in our name. This needs your yes per email.
  2. Kits as products. Brand-sold kits work today; cover every one our partners sell. Our own named bundles are the next step. You put that pilot on hold today until 1 Dec; this data argues for bringing it back sooner.
  3. Deal and price-timing pages. The Black Friday page drew visits within days. We already record each cart price daily, so "is this the lowest price" is a question we can answer and the catalog cannot.

First cycle

  1. Build the offer table from the logs: every live offer with its fetches, landings, clicks, sales, code size, price and kit flag.
  2. List the offers we don't cover yet: partner kits and code-carrying products with no page.
  3. Pull platform counts for the categories and buying phrases behind the top offers.
  4. Run one where-to-buy chat on the top 60 or so, build pages for the best 15, and read the logs a week later.

Steps 1 to 3 use no chat quota and send nothing outside.
