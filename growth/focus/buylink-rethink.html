What makes ChatGPT hand over our buy link

Answering the shopper's question well is table stakes. Every page in the result set does it, and ChatGPT fills the buy slot from its own catalog regardless. The link gets written into an answer only when the link itself is the answer: a price the catalog can't show, for a product the catalog can't vouch for, in reply to a question about price. Our pages are built for the wrong question.

Evidence: 188 plain-prompt ChatGPT chats on 11-07 (one per gap page, gpt-6), 152 real ChatGPT-tagged buy clicks in D1 over 21 days, the under-$500 chat read in full, three winning competitor pages, and OpenAI's merchant and promotions documentation.

Recommendation. Split the site's content into two page kinds with different jobs. Deal pages (one per product and brand) exist to be handed over: a verifiable price-after-code record on one line, aimed at "discount code / cheapest / where to buy" questions. Question pages (gap and comparison) exist to be cited and to convert the shopper who lands: reference first, offer phrased as a checkable fact, no copy aimed at the assistant. Under both, build a trust layer the model can check: a public per-code verification log and a richer buy-link text page. Gate every new page on our pick beating the catalog's best on the query's own metric after the code.

First step, before any rewrite: a 40-chat deal-intent harvest to confirm that the clicks we already get come from price questions. It costs one quota window and decides the rest.

2. Why the buy slot is not ours on general questions

In a shopping answer the buy buttons come from OpenAI's catalog: merchant feeds with prices OpenAI verifies. The model picks which catalog records to show and cannot point one at us. Our link appears only when the model writes it as text or a button, copied from our page. So on "best X" questions three things have to happen at once, and today none of them does reliably:

  1. The model must see the offer. At rank 3–6 it gets 1,000–1,500 characters of a page. Our conversion block is there, but it reads as a sales pitch, and the AI-addressed product record above it cut citation to 13% (vs 52% without it) on 11-07.
  2. The offer must matter. BLUETTI's code is 5%. In the under-$500 chat the catalog showed an EcoFlow RIVER 2 Pro at 768 Wh for $429 from Sam's Club; our pick was a 288 Wh Elite 30 V2 at $465.50 after the code. No copy beats a worse product.
  3. The model must believe it. It did relay the code once, and hedged in the same breath:
"Dealectric reports a $489.99 bundle price, reduced to $465.50 with code DEALECTRIC, last verified November 6. This is an affiliate-reported deal rather than an independently verified checkout price."

Then it sent the button to our page ("View bundle details"), not to /go/. The model has a category for us, affiliate-reported, and a category it prefers, independently verified checkout price. The catalog is in the second category by construction. We are in the first by default, and nothing on the page moves us out of it.

The competitors that took the citations instead share a shape: the answer as reference material, numbers from named studies or the maker's manual, a named author or method, sales links below the analysis or absent. The gas-furnace page that beat ours quotes Lawrence Berkeley Lab furnace models and a Canadian blower study, states "this site hasn't run a furnace on any of these batteries", and sells nothing. Powersized's kit guide, cited twice against us, states where every price came from and the date, plus the kit's saving against the parts and its 30-day price median.

3. Where the link is necessary

The model writes our URL when leaving it out would make the answer worse. Three conditions produce that, and the live clicks fit all three:

  • The question is about price. "Is there a discount code for the P1800 kit", "cheapest way to get an AC180 with a panel". The code is the answer, and the catalog card can't carry a code. (The promotions API OpenAI gives merchants has amount-off, percent-off and free-shipping benefits and no code field, checked today.)
  • No trusted source covers the product. ALLPOWERS has no Consumer Reports, Wirecutter or RTINGS coverage and its catalog card is the brand's own. For that product we are the primary source, and our link is the route.
  • The saving is material and checkable. 10% on a $698 kit is $70 and gets relayed. 5% on a $490 kit gets a hedge.

The inverse holds too. On a general question about a mainstream model, the model has a verified card for EcoFlow at Sam's Club. Our page can add a fact and earn a citation, but the link stays with the card. That is not a copy failure. It is the structure of the answer.

4. Options

AOwn the price question recommended

Build deal pages per product and per brand whose title, H1 and opening line are the price question in the shopper's words: "ALLPOWERS P1800 + SP037 discount code (November 2026): $628.20 after ALLPOWERS26, checked in the cart today". The record on one line: store price, code, saving, price after, cart-test timestamp, what the link does, the next-best price elsewhere. Then the alternatives' store prices, so the page is also the honest comparison.

Trade-off. Demand per query is small and only exists for products shoppers already name. It is also exactly the demand that converted every click we have. The brand discount-code pages already exist; product-level ones and the query-transfer check (what ChatGPT searches for a deal question) do not.

BMake the code verifiable, not reported recommended

Turn the daily sweep into a public record the model can read: one page per code with every day's result (date, product, cart subtotal, discount taken, pass or fail), linked from every code mention and from the /go/ text page ChatGPT fetches 500 times a month. Change the wording from "Dealectric reports" to the observation itself: "checked in BLUETTI's cart 2026-11-07 06:37 PT: subtotal $489.99, code accepted, $465.50; 17 of 17 daily checks passed since 10-22". Add a method page that says plainly what we do (cart tests, daily) and don't do (product tests).

Trade-off. Cheap: the data exists in src/coupons.json and the sweep logs. The unknown is whether the "affiliate-reported" hedge is about evidence (fixable) or about the site's category (not fixable by copy). One replay A/B answers that.

CReference-first question pages recommended, with a change

The 11-07 plan's phase 1: remove the AI-addressed block from all 74 pages, open with the direct answer and its numbers, the mainstream models ChatGPT already names plus ours, sourced specs, author and method; offer below. Target citation 80%.

The change. Don't bury the offer entirely. Keep one line inside the top 1,200 characters, written as a checkable fact in the style of option B, not as a pitch. The question page's job is citation plus converting the 24% who land, so the human-facing buy block below the fold must be strong. Accept that direct handover on general questions stays near zero; that is the catalog's slot.

Trade-off. This reverses the "conversion block opens the page" copy rule of 11-01. That rule was measured in the replay on hand-over, which the live test now shows at 0 for this page kind. The rule stays right for deal pages.

DGate pages on product, not on query recommended

Build a page only when our pick, after the code, beats the catalog's best on the query's own metric (Wh per dollar under a cap, weight, charge time), or when no trusted source covers the product. Otherwise write a cite-only page or skip. Prefer in-stock: 62 of 152 clicks went to pre-order kits that cannot have produced a sale since 10-29.

Trade-off. Fewer pages. The 11-07 demand data already says most of the 173 gap pages have no visits, so this costs little.

EJoin the catalog not available

OpenAI's merchant page admits merchants only and names no route for publishers or affiliates. The promotions API carries no codes. Asking BLUETTI to list our price in their feed would put the sale on their card, not our link. Re-check quarterly; nothing to build now.

A trust risk to fix alongside. Twice on 11-07 ChatGPT credited us with "independent charging measurements" and "independent testing summarized by Dealectric". We ran no tests; those figures are makers' specs. The model values measured data and is reading our tables as if we measured them. Every spec table needs its provenance line in the first sentence of the section, both because it is true and because a later correction would cost exactly the trust option B is trying to build.
