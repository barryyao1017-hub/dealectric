What search platforms count

Platforms can size the main phrases and nothing below them. Of 51 kit variants (by size, use, brand and model) 2 had any exact count; of 26 deal phrases, 2; of 267 brand code phrases, 3. The countable ones are the heads: "solar generator with panels included" (about 4,200 searches on Microsoft Ads' scale, from the earlier pull), "solar generators for home use", and the brand kit names below. Our own pages get almost no organic search either: Bing showed the whole site 10 times, and the top Google query had 35 impressions in 28 days. What I would build first

  1. The 13 in-stock ALLPOWERS kits we don't list. Same store, same 10 to 12% code and same shoppers as our three best offers. Each needs a product record, a cart test and a product page, and then joins the kit comparison pages.
  2. BLUETTI's current kits (Apex 300 and 200, Elite 100 V2, Elite 300, AC200L with panels). About 70 pages; price and stock need reading first.
  3. More kit comparison pages. Five pages carry 95 visits at a 42% click rate. Cut new ones by size, budget and use (1,000 Wh, 3,000 Wh and up, under $1,000, under $1,500, home backup, RV, camping), about ten, each checked against the existing five so two pages never chase the same search. Platforms can't rank these, so the logs will after a week.
  4. Kits from a third brand, to test whether the pattern travels: OSCAL (three in stock, 10% code), then Anker SOLIX (113 sets, no code of ours; one where-to-buy chat each first, because a brand this large is likelier to be in ChatGPT's catalog).
  5. Jackery. "Jackery solar generator" is the largest kit phrase we can count, nine times BLUETTI's, and Jackery sells its kits under that very name. We only link Jackery on Amazon. A direct program is the biggest single lever here; the nudge is already drafted under DLC-140 and needs your yes.

Not first: code pages and deal pages as search targets. Their phrases are below what Bing counts. Codes still matter as the way a sale gets credited, so a code in our name at each kit brand stays worth asking for.

Limits of this read

  • The 310 answer clicks come from at least 133 browsers; 85 of them clicked once. A browser here is a device type, country and network, so one busy signature may be several people or one keen shopper.
  • We see which offer was clicked, not what the shopper asked.
  • Amazon offers (93) have no tracked link, so they have no click numbers at all.
  • All of the evidence that kits win is solar. Nothing here shows the pattern carries to another category.
  • Stock and prices for the missing BLUETTI and Anker SOLIX kits were not read.
