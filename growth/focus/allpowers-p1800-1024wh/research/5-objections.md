## 1. "Only a 24-month warranty, from a brand with poor reviews. Will they honor it?" (strongest objection)

**(a) Where raised.**
- Our own record: "Shorter warranty than ALLPOWERS' other stations: 24 months, not 5 years" (source: /Users/jiayiyao/Desktop/dealectric/src/products.py).
- Trustpilot, iallpowers.com: TrustScore 2.2/5 from 108 reviews, 67% 1-star, 22% 5-star; "Hasn't replied to negative reviews". Recent 1-star reviews cite ignored warranty claims on an R1500 (2026-10-09) and an R600 (2026-10-18) (source: https://www.trustpilot.com/review/iallpowers.com, via WebFetch, accessed 2026-10-28).
- BBB: "Allpowers", 611 Reyes Dr, Walnut, CA, rated F, 7 complaints in 3 years, "Failure to respond to 7 complaint(s)" (source: https://www.bbb.org/us/ca/walnut/profile/electronic-power-supplies/allpowers-1216-1529705, accessed 2026-10-28). That it is the same company as ALLPOWERS INC of Diamond Bar is unverified.
- Slickdeals: a commenter's ALLPOWERS power bank "Only contained 5000mah while advertised for 20k mah" (source: https://slickdeals.net/f/19799412-allpowers-p1800-portable-power-station-1024wh-lifepo4-battery-backup-w-4-1800w-3600w-peak-ac-outlets-140w-usb-c-outpu-solar-generator-359-40, comment 2026-10-01).

**(b) Facts.**

The default is "24 months from the date of purchase" (source: WARRANTY). RETURNS instead says "The warranty period is 18 months" (source: RETURNS). The two ALLPOWERS pages conflict.
- The US PAGE shows "Shipping" and "Return" badges but no warranty line, while the R4000 page carries a "5-Year Full Warranty" badge (source: PAGE; https://iallpowers.com/products/allpowers-r4000-power-station). The site header says "Up to 5-Year Warranty".

**(d) When the objection wins.** If you want a 1 kWh station to last 5+ years with a warranty to match, buy a rival with a 5-year term (see 3-rivals.md), or ask ALLPOWERS in writing for the EU's 5-year term before ordering.

## 2. "3,000 cycles to 70% when rivals claim 4,000 to 80%?"

**(a) Where raised.** Our record: "Cycle rating is to 70% capacity, not the usual 80%" (source: /Users/jiayiyao/Desktop/dealectric/src/products.py).

**(b) Facts. ALLPOWERS publishes five cycle figures for the P1800.**
- PAGE spec table: "3000+ cycles, ≥70% remaining capacity" (source: PAGE).
- IMAGES: "4500+ Cycles" and "10+ Years Lifespan" (source: IMAGES, battery image).
- MAN: "≥4000 cycles with≥70% State of Health (SoH)" (source: MAN spec table).
- EU-PAGE description: "3500 cycle life"; EU comparison table: "LiFePO4 4000+ Cycles"; EU body: "Even after thousands of intensive cycles, it still retains over 80% of its original capacity" (source: EU-PAGE).
- Every figure that names an end point says 70%, except the EU body text, which gives no count.
- **Does it matter?** At one full cycle a week, 3,000 cycles is about 57 years (arithmetic). For outage and camping use, the cycle count is unlikely to be the limit; the 24-month warranty and calendar aging are (inference).

**(c) Page line.** "ALLPOWERS rates the P1800 at 3,000 cycles to 70% on its US page (its manual says 4,000 to 70%); either is more than weekly use will reach, so the warranty length matters more than the cycle count."

**(d) When the objection wins.** Daily cycling (off-grid cabin, daily solar shifting) for many years. Then a unit rated to 80% with a 5-year warranty is the safer buy.

## 3. "Its own page contradicts itself. Which specs are true?"

**(a) Where raised.** Our record: "The US page contradicts itself (3,000 vs 4,500 cycles, 10 vs 15 ms UPS)" (source: products.py). A Slickdeals commenter cited "20ms UPS" (source: Slickdeals URL above, comment 2026-07-28).

**(b) Facts.**

| Cycles | 3,000+ to ≥70% | 4,500+ | ≥4,000 to ≥70% | 3,500; 4,000+ |

| Solar | "11–60V Input Voltage, 95V Open-Circuit Voltage, 13A Max", 650 W | "11-60V 13A MAX" on the panel label | "MPPT (11-60)V 13A 650W Max" | 650 W |

Sources: PAGE, IMAGES, MAN, EU-PAGE (all accessed 2026-10-28).
- The manual is the most conservative on UPS (20 ms) and agrees with the page on 70% end of life. Use MAN figures where they differ (my judgment).
- "95V Open-Circuit Voltage" next to "11–60V" is unexplained. MAN says 60 V. Treat 60 V as the limit.

**(c) Page line.** "ALLPOWERS's own P1800 materials disagree (UPS 10, 15 or 20 ms; 3,000 to 4,500 cycles); we use the manual's figures: 20 ms UPS, 4,000 cycles to 70%, 60 V solar limit, 13 kg."

**(d) When the objection wins.** If a spec is a hard requirement (UPS time for a PC, for example), the conflict alone is a reason to buy a unit whose documents agree.

## 6. "Sale items can't be returned?"

**(a) Where raised.** Our record. One Trustpilot reviewer says ALLPOWERS refused a return authorization within 15 days (search snippet from https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/uk.iallpowers.com, UK store, date not shown, unverified).

**(b) Facts.**
- REFUND-POLICY: "Unfortunately, we cannot accept returns on sale items or gift cards." It also lists "hazardous materials" as non-returnable (source: REFUND-POLICY).
- RETURNS: "30-day money back guarantee ... for any reason", for direct purchases, with no sale-item exclusion. But: "not applicable for items with open box or lack of accessories"; used items returned for non-quality reasons lose "15%-50% depreciation" (later "10% to 50%"); the buyer pays return shipping; and "the shipping cost when we send the product to the customer also needs to be paid by the customer" (source: RETURNS).
- PAGE: "Return 30-Day Money Back" (source: PAGE). The listing shows "Sale price" vs "Regular price", so read literally every P1800 order is a sale item.
- The REFUND-POLICY wording (food, flowers, beauty products, "with tags") reads like generic store boilerplate (inference).
- The P1800 is a UN38.3 lithium battery (MAN certificate row), so a return goes on ALLPOWERS's label (inference).
- BLUETTI and EcoFlow have no sale-item exclusion in their refund policies (sources: https://www.bluettipower.com/policies/refund-policy via WebFetch; https://us.ecoflow.com/policies/refund-policy; both accessed 2026-10-28).

**(c) Page line.** "ALLPOWERS's returns page offers 30-day money-back on unused units, but its store-wide refund policy says sale items can't be returned and every P1800 listing is a sale; confirm in writing with service@allpowers.com and keep the box."

**(d) When the objection wins.** If you will open and test it before deciding, a used return can cost 10–50% plus shipping. At $395 that is $40 to $200 (arithmetic). Buy where opened returns are free if that matters.

## 7. "Can it be a UPS for my PC, router or CPAP?"

**(a) Where raised.**
- IMAGES: "15ms SEAMLESS UPS BACKUP ... Desktop PC, CPAP, Router, Refrigerator" (source: IMAGES, UPS image).
- Slickdeals: "just not good enough (<10ms preferable)", citing the advertised 20 ms (source: Slickdeals URL above, comment 2026-07-28).
- EU 1/5 review of a self-restart in UPS use (section 4).

**(b) Facts.**
- MAN: switches to battery "within 20ms", recommends using "only one device", and says the household circuit must carry 15 A at 100–120 V (source: MAN §3.6, read from the rendered page).
- MAN excludes "medical equipment, and other equipment requiring high reliability" (source: MAN §7).
- ATX 3.1 requires 12 ms hold-up at full load, 17 ms recommended at 80% load (source: https://knowledge.seasonic.com/article/79-comparison-atx-3-0-vs-atx-3-1-standards, accessed 2026-10-28). A 20 ms gap exceeds both (inference: some desktops will reboot).

**(c) Page line.** "The manual gives a switchover of up to 20 ms (the page says 10): fine for a router, lights or a fridge, too slow to guarantee a desktop PC stays on, and ALLPOWERS's manual excludes medical equipment such as a CPAP."

**(d) When the objection wins.** PCs, NAS boxes and medical devices. Use a real UPS, or one between the P1800 and the device.

**(d) When the objection wins.** If you need a UL-listed unit, this one shows no UL mark.

## 9. "Cold garage or car? Self-heating? Storage?"

**(a) Where raised.** No P1800 thread found. Ukrainian EU buyers mention outage use for a fridge and a boiler (source: EU-PAGE reviews dated 2026-10-03 and 2026-10-22), where unheated storage is common (inference).

**(b) Facts.**
- MAN: charge 0 to 40 °C (32 to 104 °F); discharge -10 to 40 °C (14 to 104 °F); storage -20 to 45 °C (-4 to 113 °F), "(Recommended 20°C~30°C Best)" (source: MAN, rendered image).
- No self-heating is mentioned in MAN, PAGE or EU-PAGE. MAN shows a "Low Temperature Warning" icon. That it lacks a heater is inferred from the absence.
- Storage: "discharge the battery to 30% and recharge it to 60% every three months" (source: MAN FAQ, OCR at 4x).

**(c) Page line.** "It won't charge below 32 °F, runs loads down to 14 °F, lists no self-heating, and in storage wants topping up to 60% every three months."

**(d) When the objection wins.** Charging outdoors or in a car below freezing. Buy a self-heating model (see 3-rivals.md).
