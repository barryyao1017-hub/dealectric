# Research: BLUETTI Apex 200 vs Apex 300 for home backup, 2026-11-05 (Fable agent)

Reddit was blocked from this machine. Evidence comes from the BLUETTI community forum, Trustpilot, generator forums and
hands-on reviews.

## Pain points
1. Can it run my well pump, central AC or dryer (240 V hardwired loads)? About 6 threads. A 1 HP submersible pump
   measured 2,392 W running and 7,176 W at start; 4 of 33 stations could start it (generatorchecker). A ½ HP pump is
   about 1,000 W running and 2,100 W at start.
2. Transfer-switch install needs an electrician; inlet mismatches (30 A inlet vs the Apex 300's 50 A); support
   friction (about 5).
3. Fan noise in UPS/passthrough on the Apex 300 (about 5; contested by the Techspective review).
4. Total cost: the Apex 300 has no USB/DC without the $299 Hub D1; the trolley is $249 (about 4).
5. UPS: off by default on the Apex 200 (enable it in the app); the manual excludes servers and medical gear (about 3).

## Honest winner (both ours, BLUETTI on CJ)
- Any 240 V load (well pump, central AC, dryer, a 240 V transfer switch): **Apex 300**, the only one with a NEMA
  14-50R (120/240 V 50 A, both voltages at once). Its 7,680 W "Lifting Power" is a resistive-load mode, not a
  motor-start surge, so a 1 HP pump is marginal.
- 120 V essentials only (fridge, furnace fan, lights, router, CPAP): **Apex 200**. It is $300 less, 59.3 lb vs
  83.78 lb, has USB/12 V built in and 3,000 W solar input vs 2,400 W, and takes the same six-battery expansion.
- Field for big motor starts: Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus (6,000 W 120/240 V, about $2,400–2,600) and EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3
  (8,000 W surge, $2,799). Neither is a partner.
