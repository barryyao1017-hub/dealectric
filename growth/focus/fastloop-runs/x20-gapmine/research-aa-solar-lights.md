# Research: rechargeable AA batteries for outdoor solar lights, 2026-11-05 (Fable agent)

Reddit was blocked by every route. Evidence comes from BudgetLightForum, AnandTech, Hackaday, All About Circuits and
Amazon listing text.

## Pain points
1. New batteries still don't last the night (most common). The panel limits the cell: one measured path-light
   charger gave about 20 mA, about 150 mAh on a sunny day, and 600 mAh replacements didn't fill (All About Circuits,
   one hobbyist's light). AnandTech: usable capacity depends on sunlight. BudgetLightForum is split: a bigger cell
   buffers several sunny days. A Hackaday commenter got until 6 a.m. instead of 2 a.m. with 2500 mAh cells.
2. Dead or weak cells in the pack (EBL, Tenergy and Lightalent reviews all mention it).
3. Chemistry: NiMH 1.2 V replaces NiCd 1.2 V; NiCd tolerates trickle charge better; 3.2 V LiFePO4 (14500) lights
   can't take 1.2 V AA (Tenergy's own Q&A).
4. Stock cells die in a season (no overcharge protection; leaks). Replace NiMH every 1–2 years.
5. Winter: lights are near useless from October in Seattle; eneloop works to -4 °F with shorter runtime (Panasonic).

## Honest winner
Segment (a), replacing stock cells in ordinary path/string lights: **1.2 V NiMH, 1000–1300 mAh**. A near tie:
- **Tenergy Solla 1000 mAh**: 12 for $16.99 on tenergy.com ($13.99 on Amazon, B07T8XLW2W), 4.4★ on 13,277 ratings,
  **2,000 cycles**, -4 to 122 °F, ships pre-charged. The narrow pick (cycle rating; a pre-charged cell helps a weak
  panel).
- EBL Solar AA 1300 mAh: 12 for $12.99 on Amazon (B0BKSC8P5X), 4.4★ on 9,736 ratings, 1,200 cycles, -4 to 140 °F,
  low-self-discharge claim, ships not fully charged.
Segment (b), full sun or wall top-ups: Tenergy Solla Premium 1300 mAh (2,000 cycles). Panasonic eneloop 2000 mAh is
the best cell made but costs 3× per cell and is the wrong buy for a weak panel.
Not recommended: Tenergy Centura 2000 for solar lights. Tenergy NiCd 1100 only where the light's manual says NiCd.
