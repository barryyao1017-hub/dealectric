Query family (~2,600 Bing searches / 3 mo): "cd player with bluetooth", "cd player that connects to bluetooth
speaker", "cd player with speakers and bluetooth", "best cd player with bluetooth", "cd player with bluetooth
transmitter", "cd player vs bluetooth speaker".

## 1. Top block (ready to use)

**(a) Answer sentence (≤220 chars):**
For a CD player with Bluetooth that sends to headphones or a speaker, the FiiO DM13 BT wins: aptX HD / aptX
Low Latency transmit, a 60-second anti-skip buffer and a 10-hour battery. $40 "Bluetooth" portables mostly fail at pairing.

**(b) The one concern (≤300 chars):**
Which way does the Bluetooth go? Most "CD players with Bluetooth" (Sony ZS-RS60BT, Panasonic SC-PM270/PM700, every
TEAC and Tascam) only receive from a phone and cannot feed Bluetooth headphones or a speaker. Only the FiiO DM13 BT,
Shanling EC Play and some $40 portables transmit. Raised in 4 of 5 forum threads read.

Sources for (b): Tom's Guide forum threads "I'm looking for a CD player that has Bluetooth output, so I can connect
to Bluetooth speakers", "Bluetooth headset to CD player", "CD to wireless speakers", "Recommendation for CD player with
Bluetooth speakers"; What Hi-Fi forum "realistic portable CD player options" (Bluetooth-to-headphones called "looking
for unicorns"); B&H Q&A on the Panasonic SC-PM270 ("cannot output to a Bluetooth speaker"). URLs in §6.

## 3. Winner per segment

**Segment 1: portable (Discman-style) that TRANSMITS to Bluetooth headphones or a speaker.**
Winner: **FiiO DM13 BT** ($154.99–$164.99 at US retailers; see table). Deciding fact: it is the only portable with
aptX HD + aptX Low Latency transmit; the Shanling EC Play ($199) transmits SBC only, and the $40 players are the ones
generating the pairing/skip/dead-in-weeks complaints above. Buffer 60 s (CD) / 120 s (MP3), 3,750 mAh, 10 h rated,
~8 h measured. Caveats to print: no LDAC; some headsets negotiate only SBC; the non-BT DM13 cannot transmit at all;
long discs (70+ min) and firmware updates have bitten some Head-Fi owners.
Runner-up: **Shanling EC Play** if the buyer wants the longer battery (12 h rated) or LDAC *receive* (phone → EC Play →
wired headphones) and does not care that transmit is SBC.

**Segment 2: home CD player with speakers that RECEIVES from a phone (the "CD player with speakers and Bluetooth" query).**
Winner for a room: **Panasonic SC-PM700** (80 W, 2 × 4" woofers, CD/FM/USB/Bluetooth; $229.99 at Turntable Lab,
$309.99 at Staples, undated). Winner for a desk or teen's room: **Panasonic SC-PM270** (20 W, 10 + 10 W, SBC, 4.3/5
from 228 Amazon ratings; one review: "too weak for an adult space"). Deciding fact: both are current, in-production
Panasonic systems; the Sony ZS-RS60BT boombox (2 + 2 W) was discontinued after 2022 and is on clearance where stock
remains. Neither Panasonic can send to a Bluetooth speaker (B&H Q&A).

**Segment 3: hi-fi component CD player feeding Bluetooth headphones / a Bluetooth amp or speaker.**
Winner: **keep the CD player, add a transmitter.** No mainstream component CD player with a built-in Bluetooth
transmitter turned up: TEAC CD-P750, Tascam CD-200BT, Shanling EC3 and CD80II are all receive-only. Best pick:
**1Mii B06TX** (transmitter only; optical, coaxial, AUX and RCA inputs; aptX LL / aptX HD / SBC; two headphones but
drops to plain aptX when two aptX-LL sets are connected; 197 ft rated). Alternative: **Avantree Oasis Plus** (transmit
or receive, optical/AUX/RCA, aptX HD + LL; Avantree's site now lists the original as discontinued and points to the
Oasis Plus 2 with aptX Adaptive). Rule to print: low latency only happens when the headphones also support aptX LL;
otherwise expect 70–220 ms, which is fine for music. If the buyer has no CD player yet, the DM13 BT in desktop mode
(mains-powered, coax/optical out) covers this segment too.

## 4. TEAC (our approved CJ brand): does not win, and has no fitting product in the US

- US lineup per TEAC's own USA support page (teac.jp/int/support/usa): **PD-301-X** (slot-in CD, FM tuner, USB
  playback, Burr-Brown PCM5142; $549.99 at Safe and Sound HQ, US dealer) and **AD-850-SE** (CD + cassette + USB
  record, mic/karaoke). Neither has Bluetooth. (Some eBay titles append "Bluetooth" to the PD-301-X; dealer spec
  pages do not list it: treat as seller keyword stuffing.)
- **CD-P750** (Japan, ¥76,780 direct) / **CD-P750DAB** (EU, €509–599): Bluetooth 5.1 **receiver** (SBC/AAC/aptX/aptX
  HD). No US listing found. Receive-only, so it would not answer the "connects to Bluetooth speaker/headphones"
  intent even if imported.
- **Tascam CD-200BT** (TEAC's pro brand): rackmount CD player with Bluetooth **receiver** (SBC/AAC/aptX); Adorama shows
  it as a closeout.
- **TEAC SL-D930** CD/radio with Bluetooth (receive): "no longer available" at Adorama and B&H.
- **TEAC TN-400BT X/TB**: a Bluetooth *turntable* ($629.99, spring 2026), not a CD player.
- Sold direct in the US? **UNVERIFIED.** teac-usa.com would not resolve from this machine and no search result showed
  a TEAC USA storefront; only dealers (Safe and Sound HQ, Adorama, B&H) and the Buena Park service center.
- Bottom line: every TEAC/Tascam Bluetooth product is receive-only, none is a current US CD player with Bluetooth, and
  the one US CD player (PD-301-X, $549.99) has no Bluetooth. Honest page: TEAC is not mentioned, or mentioned only as
  "TEAC's PD-301-X has no Bluetooth; pair it with a transmitter" (segment 3). Do not build the page around TEAC.

## 6. Not recommended, and UNVERIFIED items

Not recommended:
- **$25–$90 "Bluetooth" portables** (HOTT CD903TF, Memorex, ION Audio Air, Studebaker SB3706, GPX PCB319B, Gueray,
  Greadio, Jazmm, Monodeal MD-702): the pairing-failure, drop-out, skip and dead-in-weeks complaints in §2 are almost
  all theirs. If budget is the constraint, a used non-Bluetooth Discman plus a $50 transmitter is the safer build, but
  that is an opinion, not a tested claim; say "reviews of the cheap players cluster on pairing and skipping".
- **Any TEAC or Tascam** for this query (receive-only or no Bluetooth; PD-301-X is $549.99 with none).
- **Sony ZS-RS60BT** as a primary pick: discontinued, 2 + 2 W, clearance stock only. Fine to list as "if you find one".
- **Toshiba TY-CWU700**: sold out at Sharper Image; FM static and confusing controls in reviews.
- **Jensen CD-660 boombox**: "flimsy" in the only hands-on found.
- **Shanling EC Play as the headphone-transmit pick**: SBC-only transmit loses to the DM13's aptX HD/LL.
