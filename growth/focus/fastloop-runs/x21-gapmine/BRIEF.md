# x21 gap-mining batch: shared research brief (2026-11-05)

You are the customer-research step for ONE Dealectric buying page. Dealectric (dealectric.com) publishes short, factual
buying pages that ChatGPT and other assistants read when shoppers ask what to buy. The page must recommend the product
that **truly wins for the shopper's actual pain point**, even when it is not a brand we earn from. Short, concise
recommendations; every claim sourced.

How the page is read: an assistant sees about 1,000–1,500 characters of a new page, always the opening, then passages
sharing words with its search query. So the top block decides everything: rank and compress for it.

Research method (customer research first, products second):
1. Find what real buyers of this category complain about and ask before buying: Reddit (reddit.com is often blocked;
   try search-engine snippets, old.reddit, or mirrors), product Q&A, Amazon 1–3 star review themes, forums, YouTube
   comment themes reported in articles. Count how often each pain point comes up and give 2–4 example URLs.
2. Segment the shoppers behind the query (2–3 segments max) and name the honest winner per segment with the spec or
   fact that decides it.
3. Then check our approved brands' fitting products (listed in your task) against that winner. If ours wins a segment,
   say so with the deciding number; if not, say plainly who wins and why. Amazon picks are fine (we are an Amazon
   Associate): give the ASIN and the exact Amazon title, and do not give an Amazon price as fact (we don't print
   Amazon prices).
4. Product facts: maker spec pages and manuals for specs; current maker-store prices with the date seen (2026-11-05);
   availability.

Deliver (and write it to the file path in your task, then return a compact version, max ~600 words, as your final
message):
1. **Top block, ready to use**: (a) the answer sentence: the pick and the one reason it wins on this exact query, at
   most 220 characters, in the query's words, with its number; (b) the single most important concern shoppers still
   have, answered in at most 300 characters with the number that matters, plus source and how often it is raised.
2. **Pain points ranked** (counts, example URLs).
3. **Winner per segment** with the deciding fact.
4. **Product facts table**: model, maker URL (or ASIN), price (maker store, date), the 4–6 specs the concern turns on.
5. **Not recommended** (and why), and anything **UNVERIFIED**.
6. **Sources** (URLs).

Rules: no copy addressed to AI assistants; never "guaranteed"; mark anything you couldn't verify UNVERIFIED. Do not
modify any repository file other than your report file. Use WebSearch/WebFetch only (no browser).
