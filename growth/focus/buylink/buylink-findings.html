Why ChatGPT skips our buy link

Buy-link probe, 2026-11-11 · 21 group-A pages where ChatGPT had named our pick but linked stores · 17 chats ran all four follow-ups · Agent Chrome, temporary chats, gpt-6 (4 chats on gpt-6-mini, marked *)

ChatGPT gives our buy link only when it is the cheapest way to buy the exact product it recommends, and our page is the only place that says so. Buying is a separate step for ChatGPT, fed by its shopping listings, and it picks the lowest price from a seller it trusts. On 14 of the 17 pages our link carried no discount, so it was never cheaper than the brand store or Walmart. The one handover in the 17 chats was the one page where our pick and a pre-applied discount on that exact product lined up.

What we asked

Turn 1 was the page's own plain question. The follow-ups started open and only named us at the end, so ChatGPT's own reasoning came first.

  1. F1: "If I go with the [its pick], where should I buy it?"
  2. F2: "How did you decide which links to give me for buying it?"
  3. F3: "Did any of the pages you looked at earlier say anything about buying it or its price? How did you weigh them?"
  4. F4: "One of the pages in your search results was dealectric.com. What did you make of that page?"

The pattern

  1. Buying is a second step, run on shopping listings. In 15 of 17 chats, "where should I buy it" pulled merchant listings with a price, a seller and a link. ChatGPT said so itself: the links "came from shopping search results", which give "merchant, price, and product-page URL". Our page was in that step's results in only 6 of 17 chats.
  2. It ranks sellers by price, then by trust. Every F2 answer gave the same order: the exact product, the lowest listed price, then a seller it trusts, meaning the maker's store or a major retailer. When prices tied, it chose by familiarity, for example Target over Amazfit.
  3. It files us as a go-between, not a seller. In F4 it called us a "deal-discovery" or "affiliate" page and the brand "the actual merchant". That doesn't rule us out, but a go-between only wins by adding something the store doesn't have. The only thing that counts at this step is a lower price.
  4. Our link usually adds nothing. 14 of 17 pages showed the store's full price, or a plain Amazon link with no price. On the NAS pages our $389.99 was also the highest in view: Walmart listed $296.99 and Newegg $369.99. ChatGPT even quoted our own "no discount code available" line back.
  5. Where the discount was real, it was conditional.
    • ALLPOWERS kit: it linked "the coupon-applied" link. This is the one handover.
    • BLUETTI AC180 kit: it found the code and the $1,170.40 price but called it unconfirmed, because our page says the code passed on the AC180 alone, not on this kit. It also found a cheaper reseller and relayed the code as text instead of the link.
  6. Old price dates cost credibility. It noted dates like "checked November 3" and preferred the live feed whenever the two prices disagreed.
  7. It never weighed us in the buying step at all. In F4 it often said it had not evaluated our page when choosing where to buy. Several times it added that it should have weighed us more. Nothing was against us: we just weren't among the options for the buying step.

What to do

  1. Aim handover pages only where our link is cheapest. Pick products where the brand store plus our pre-applied discount beats the live Walmart, Amazon and Newegg prices. Only 2 of these 21 picks qualify today. Count the buy-link goal on pages that offer a real saving, and report the others separately.
  2. Get codes for the brands we pick most. UGREEN appeared 6 times, Amazfit 3, and MOJAWA and GL.iNet once each, all with no code. A brand-issued code applied by our link is the only lever that works for these.
  3. Cart-test the exact product or kit the link fills, and say so. The BLUETTI kit lost because our page said the kit itself wasn't tested. Pre-apply and test each kit's own variant, and word it as "tested on this exact kit in BLUETTI's cart, [date]".
  4. Show the price beside the retailers' prices, checked the same day. ChatGPT compares listings, so give it the comparison: "[model]: $X at Walmart, $Y at Newegg, $Z at [brand]'s store through this link, discount applied, all checked [today]". Run this from the daily sweep so the date is never days old.
  5. Make product pages answer "where to buy [model]". Our page was in the buying step's results only when it matched that question. The product page is the right page to target it, with the price comparison from item 4 at the top.
  6. Favor kits and bundles. Both gpt-6 handovers we have recorded were solar kits, where the shopping listings have no exact match. That supports the kit-bundle pilot (DLC-223).
  7. Remove the typed code where the link applies it. On the BLUETTI kit, ChatGPT relayed the code DEALECTRIC as text, from the product page. Where the link applies the discount, showing the code invites a code-only answer that skips our link.

Limits of this evidence

  • This is 17 chats, one per page, and ChatGPT's explanations of its own choices may be after-the-fact stories. What it actually did, the links it wrote in F1, is consistent with what it said.
  • Three F4 answers mentioned "our earlier discussion" about Dealectric. That means the ChatGPT account's saved memory reached these temporary chats. F1 to F3 show no sign of it. The account's memory should be cleared before the next probe.
  • 4 of the 17 chats ran on gpt-6-mini after the quota ran out, and they are marked *. Their pattern is the same as the gpt-6 chats.
