## 3. Win check at today's prices

- **N1 → Trio 3 Pro, conditional win.** Xebec Tri-Screen 3 is $699 and needs a $59 DisplayLink adapter on plain M-chip Macs ($758). The Trio 3 Pro does the same for $433 with a first-party driver and a sharper panel (QHD vs Xebec's smaller FHD wings). Cheap 1080p Amazon triples (Kefeya, Vixtan, InnoView, Llimink; $180–300) mostly need two Alt Mode ports. Several listings say "not compatible with MacBook Air M1/M2/M3". A few single-cable "driver needed" triples exist, and I could not verify one that works on Mac. **Catch: the store lists the Trio 3 Pro for 15–16" laptops**, so the honest audience is the 15" MacBook Air (M2/M3) and 15–16" Windows laptops, not the 13" Air, the most common Air. Top-2, not a clear #1, once a $250 single-cable triple turns out to work on Mac.
- **N3 → Duex Float 2 Pro, likely #1.** Few products stack a single 16" screen above a laptop. NexPad (2022, 736 g, smaller, $129–249, availability unclear) and a mention of an Espresso stand are the only rivals. Stacked dual extenders (InnoView, HotYeah, SideTrak SoloBook) are a different product. Macworld gave the Float 2 Pro Editors' Choice at $299.99. It is $263.49 today with QHD and 120 Hz.
- **N2 → Duex Max DS / Plus DS, top-2 only under a constraint.** On a bare "lightweight attachable second screen" ask, cheaper Kefeya or SideTrak Swivel models and $100 standalone monitors compete, and $195–229 for 1080p and 300 nits is not a clear win. It wins when the asker needs no driver plus a lid mount: SideTrak Slide needs DisplayLink software, and the Duex DS does not. It also suits a 13" MacBook Air that natively allows one external display. Kept, as a narrower cluster.
- **N4 → folded into N1** (same pick, same rivals). Without a base-Mac constraint, a $250 1080p triple beats the Trio on price, and the Trio wins only on "QHD/sharper" or "one cable".
- **N5 dropped:** Mobile Pixels has no 144 Hz standalone, and ASUS ZenScreen, Arzopa and JSAUX FlipGo are better Deck answers. **Glance and Fold dropped:** $150–180 1080p standalones lose to $70–120 Arzopa and KYY. **Plane tray** dropped: thin demand, and no evidence that a lid-mounted screen is the best tray answer.
