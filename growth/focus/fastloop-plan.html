ChatGPT Fast Loop

Input: one product or brand. Start from questions shoppers with a loose purchase intent would really ask, learn which words ChatGPT turns them into, and aim pages at the searches where the current results are weak. We don't try to simulate OpenAI's ranking: we measure the real results, judge the gap, and confirm retrieval live at volume.

Three loops, three speeds

The draft loop uses only steps that passed calibration. The live check confirms retrieval at volume, and every reading goes back into calibration and the gap map.

The gap map

The query space is bounded: the brands and product types we have approved links and codes for, times the ways a shopper asks. Each harvested query gets one row:

  • Open or saturated. Read the real results for that query and judge whether they already answer it well. Earlier gap work found gaps are about quality, not count: AI listicles on expired domains and spam, not empty result sets.
  • Fit. We sell a product that answers it, with a tested code.
  • Does ChatGPT search at all? Vague questions sometimes get a clarifying reply or an answer from memory. No search means no page can win.
  • Demand, roughly: how often the harvest produces the query, plus Bing keyword volume where it exists. There's no ChatGPT demand data.

Why gaps, not ranking

Gap-vs-focus reading, 2026-10-30, 11 blind questions, n=2: the solar-kit gap page reached the answer (T4+) in 4 of 6 chats and was retrieved in 5 of 6, ranked first or second each time. The focus product pages scored 0 of 8; "best X" queries return listicles and stores. One of those chats repeated the first sale's path through a /go/ button.

The same reading shows the limit. A camping phrasing searched "portable power station solar panel kit for camping" and missed our page, which says "solar generator with panels included". Retrieval follows the query's words, so the draft is written in them.

The page formula

Aim for a page that, to the model, sums up the top results and then answers the one concern none of them address. Research goes into that concern; the rest is distilled, not collected.

1 · Shared core

Pull the claims, picks and criteria from each of the top 12 and count how many pages make each one. What about half or more of them say is what kept them there: state it briefly. Points only one page makes are noise, and we drop them.

2 · The open concern

The one thing that still leaves a shopper hesitant and that the top 12 don't answer, found in forum threads, review complaints, product Q&A and return reasons. It gets the deep research. The answer must be true and sourced or worked out from specs: we don't run product tests, so the page never implies we did. Draft two or three candidate concerns and let the answer replay choose.

3 · Length

Pages of 2,000–3,500 characters reached the model whole, even in a 12-result search, when ranked near the top. Rivals got 110–170 words each, and long hubs were cut around 16–23k characters. Until the middle range is measured, aim for about 4,000 characters. Put the answer to the concern near the top, under a heading in the query's words.

What this loop can't tell us

  • Whether a new page will rank. Judgment until it's live. Volume across many gap queries is how we learn, not a model of OpenAI's ranking.
  • The real hand-over rate. In the app, /go/ reaches a shopper only through a button the model writes; the API writes plain text.
  • Catalog cards. On brand questions the brand's own card can take the buy slot whatever our page says.
  • Query drift and quota. ChatGPT's phrasing changes, and app harvests are capped by the Free quota until the transfer test says the API can stand in.

Cost and time, estimated

  • Build: about half a day for fastloop.py and the gap judge, mostly glue around existing tools.
  • Gap map: about 1 cent per query for a one-search replay plus judge tokens; 200 queries is roughly $2–4.
  • Calibration: about 30 no-search replays, about $1.
  • Each draft: tokens only. I expect well under $1 per draft but haven't checked the model's token price.
  • Live retrieval checks: about 1 cent per target query per day.
