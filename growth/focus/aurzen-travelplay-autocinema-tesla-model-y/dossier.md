## Facts that matter

### What it is and how each mode works (`1-maker.md` §1, manual)
- Two detachable panels: front 1,098 × 590 mm, rear 1,031 × 685 mm, sold as "47" + 50"". The largest 16:9 picture
  is about 47.4" on the front panel and 46.6" on the rear (our arithmetic). Spring-steel frame and polyester-spandex
  fabric: white side is the screen, silver side faces the glass. 1 kg, 12 clips, carry bag.
- **Our record's summary has the geometry wrong.** Nothing goes on the rear window. Per the manual:
  - **Sky-roof:** both panels clip into the rim of the glass roof, silver side up. You lie in the back with the seats
    folded; the projector sits on the floor aimed up. Remove any existing roof shade first.
  - **Rear-seat:** the rear panel clips to the *front* center armrest, facing back, with both front seats slid fully
    forward. The projector hangs from the rear roof glass on Aurzen's MagPlay suction mount (or sits on the rear
    armrest).
  - **Tailgate:** the rear panel goes in the open liftgate, watched from outside; the projector stands behind the car.
- Parked only: Aurzen says so four times. It does not say whether the panels may stay up as a sunshade while driving.
- No gain, opacity, heat or UV figures, and no setup time. The only shade claim is that the silver side "helps block
  sunlight".

### Fit (`1-maker.md` §2, `5-objections.md` §4)
- Designed for the 2025-2026 Model Y ("Juniper"). On 2020-2024 cars, roof mode only, and the rear panel leaves a gap.
  The Spanish manual says 2022-2024 instead, so 2020-2021 cars are uncertain.
- Not listed: the Model Y Standard (its glass roof is hidden behind a headliner, so roof and rear-seat modes likely
  can't work), the 6-seat Model Y L, the Model 3. Treat all three as not compatible until Aurzen says otherwise.

### Which projectors fit (`4-usecase.md`; ESTIMATES from published throw ratios and assumed cabin distances)

- So the roof picture with Aurzen's own projectors is roughly a third to half the panel, not 47". The full 47" inside
  the car needs the roof mount: the MagPlay ($39.99) is unavailable in the US store (checked 2026-10-25 and 10-28),
  and the ZIP Pro's MagPlay Pro is "Coming Soon" on aurzen.com ($32.99 on Amazon, fit for this setup unverified).
- Brightness: night and late dusk work for every projector here. Daytime inside the car is washed out for every
  battery projector. Aurzen's own FAQ says to watch after sunset or in low light.
- Power: the ZIP runs about 1.5 h on battery, so plug it into the car's USB-C for a feature film. The ZIP Pro runs
  about 2.5 h in ECO. A 2-hour movie with Camp Mode on uses about 2-3% of a 75 kWh Long Range pack in mild weather,
  about 5% near freezing, about 10% at -22 °C (estimates). The projector itself is under 0.2%.
- Setup (ESTIMATES from the manual's steps; no owner timings exist): rear-seat 2-4 min once practiced; sky-roof
  12-24 min the first time, 4-7 after; tailgate 3-5 min.
- Supercharger stops: a 20-minute stop leaves 13-17 minutes of watching, a 30-minute stop one 22-minute episode.
  Overnight camping is the stronger case.
- Kids: rear-seat mode puts the picture straight ahead of forward-facing car seats in the outboard positions. A car
  seat in the middle blocks the rear armrest; rear-facing infant seats see nothing.

### Owners of similar setups (`2-owners.md`; not TravelPlay evidence)
- Top worries: clips that won't hold, sag and rattles (17 mentions; one Juniper owner had a clip-in roof shade blow
  loose at about 110 km/h with the windows open); Bluetooth audio lag to the car's speakers, which comes and goes with
  Tesla updates (13); daylight washout (7); keeping the projector powered (Camp Mode) (7).
- Parents: the built-in rear screen sits too low for kids in car seats, and its audio is quiet then suddenly loud.
  Aftermarket Android screens draw software complaints, which a fabric screen avoids.

- Warranty: 1 year. Aurzen's table lists all accessories at 12 months; whether the "+1 year with registration" applies

- Brand: Aurzen Inc. (Irvine, CA press dateline; around 2022 per CB Insights, unverified); its projectors are made by
  Shenzhen Neutop Optoelectronics. Trustpilot 4.1 on 102 reviews, 10% one-star, with warranty complaints.

## Pick it if / skip it if

**Pick it if** you have a 2025-2026 Model Y with the see-through glass roof (Premium, Performance); you watch parked,
after dark, at campsites, long charging stops or tailgates; you'd buy a roof shade anyway; and you own or will buy a
ZIP-class projector.

**Skip it if:**
- The kids watch while you drive. **Use the Model Y's built-in 8-inch rear screen** on Premium, Performance and Y L
  (Bluetooth headphones, Premium Connectivity or Wi-Fi), or **a tablet on a headrest mount** (about $95 for a Fire HD 8
  Kids plus a $19.99 mount).
- You drive a Model Y Standard, a Model Y L, a Model 3 or a 2020-2021 Model Y. Aurzen doesn't list them.
- You want daytime viewing. No battery projector beats a sunny cabin.
- You only want shade. **Buy a Juniper roof sunshade** (Basenor $49.99, Tesla $105).
- You only want outdoor tailgate movies. **Buy Aurzen's 120-inch portable screen** ($24.99): a far bigger picture.
- You own a 2020-2024 Model Y and want a permanent back-seat screen. **An aftermarket rear display** ($330-410) fits
  those years.

## The comparison that matters most

**TravelPlay vs the Model Y's own 8-inch rear touchscreen.** It's the screen the shopper already has, and it decides
whether they need anything at all. The rear screen is free on 2025+ Premium, Performance and Y L cars, streams while
driving (Netflix, YouTube, Disney+, Hulu, with up to two Bluetooth headphones) and works in daylight. The TravelPlay
can't do any of that. What it does that the rear screen can't: a picture about 34 times larger, shared by the whole
car or a campsite, lying down under the roof, or behind the liftgate, while parked and after dark. A page that leads
with this split is the one an assistant can quote without misleading anyone.
