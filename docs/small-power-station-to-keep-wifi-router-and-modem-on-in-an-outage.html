What the differences mean

A small station powers its own electronics for as long as an output bank is on. At 10–20 W, that draw is close to what the router and modem use. Each figure below divides the station's energy by the router load plus the station's own draw.

  • Elite 30 V2, own draw on AC: 288 Wh ÷ 11.38 h = 25.3 W total at a 13 W load, so about 12 W goes to the station itself (Reefs.com). The 32 W test gives 288 Wh ÷ 6.65 h = 43.3 W total, so about 11 W.
  • Elite 30 V2 at 20 W on AC: 288 Wh ÷ (20 W + 11.8 W) ≈ 9.1 h.
  • Elite 30 V2 at 20 W on its 12 V barrel ports: 288 Wh ÷ (20 W + 7 to 11 W DC-on draw) ≈ 9.3–10.7 h. On this unit, switching to DC adds about 1.6 h at most.
  • Jackery Explorer 240 v2 on AC: 4% an hour × 256 Wh ≈ 10.2 W idle, and 256 Wh ÷ 30.2 W ≈ 8.5 h.
  • ALLPOWERS R600 on 12 V: 271 Wh ÷ 20 W ≈ 13.6 h. Treat this as a ceiling: the 271 Wh was measured at 36 W, and the station's own draw at 20 W was not measured.
  • Tenergy T320 on 12 V: 300–320 Wh ÷ 20 W = 15–16 h. Also a ceiling, because Tenergy publishes no own-draw figure.
  • EcoFlow RIVER 3: 211 Wh ÷ 20 W ≈ 10.5 h at most on AC. For a router that runs from USB-C, 211 Wh ÷ 15 W ≈ 14 h.

Three settings change the hours:

  • Turn off AC-ECO on the Elite 30 V2. Reefs.com found the unit does not reliably detect loads below about 15 W (it showed 0 W with the 13 W pump running). The tester turned ECO off so the unit would not shut down at low load.
  • Switch off the bank you don't use. Zelpio measured about 11 W with AC on and about 7 W with DC on. If both your router and modem take 12 V barrel plugs, run both from the DC5521 ports and leave AC off.
  • Turn off Energy Saving Mode on a Jackery Explorer 240 v2. The manual says the mode is on by default and turns off every output after 12 hours when the load is at or below a set value. Jackery's help center says you can turn it off.

Before you use a barrel port, read the label on your router's power adapter. The DC5521 sockets above supply 12 V through a 5.5 × 2.1 mm plug, so they suit 12 V adapters only.

Not recommended

The Jackery Explorer 240 v2 is a poor fit for router backup today. Jackery's store lists it at $249 but out of stock (2026-11-14). It switches in under 20 ms, and Jackery says it is not a 0 ms UPS. The Technology Man measured about 4% of capacity lost each hour with the inverter idle (about 10 W), which works out to about 8.5 h at 20 W. Its default Energy Saving Mode can also cut every output at 12 hours.
