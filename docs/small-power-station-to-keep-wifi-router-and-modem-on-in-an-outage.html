Short answer. A small power station keeps a Wi-Fi router and modem running through a power outage for about half a day: the BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 ($289.99 on bluettipower.com, $275.50 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-30-v2) gives roughly 12 hours at a 20 W router-plus-modem load (288 Wh), switches over in 10 ms or less so the router shouldn't reboot, and has 12 V barrel outputs that skip the inverter's losses.

What to know: a modern router and modem draw about 8–20 W together (eero 6 4.4 W, Orbi 6–8 W per unit, a cable modem about 11.6 W, a fiber ONT 3–6 W), so hours are capacity × 85 % ÷ load, our arithmetic. The EcoFlow RIVER 3 ($249 on Amazon (checked 2026-11-12); buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DB1S36YP?tag=dealectric-20) gives about 10 hours, but its manual says the 20 ms variants are not for desktops, NAS or servers, and no one has tested whether a router rides through 20 ms.

What a router and modem draw

eero publishes standardised figures: eero 6 4.4 W, eero Pro 6E 7.5 W, eero Max 7 21.9 W. Netgear lists 6–8 W idle per Orbi unit. A Netgear CM2000 cable modem measured 11.6 W idle and 14.2 W under load (third-party review); an Arris SB4200 is rated 6 W. Users measured fiber ONTs at 2.75–6 W, though older ONTs with their own battery ran 15–25 W. A modem plus one router is typically 10–20 W; a mesh with three nodes in different rooms needs each node powered, which a single station can't do. Anker's guide puts router plus modem at 15–30 W, a vendor estimate.

Why switchover matters, and the DC route

Left on the wall as a UPS, the station must switch to battery fast enough that the router doesn't reboot; a reboot costs a few minutes offline and drops calls. The Elite 30 V2 and R600 are rated 10 ms or less; the RIVER 3's manual lists a 10 ms UPS variant and other variants with 20 ms "emergency power" that EcoFlow says not to use for desktops, NAS or servers, and the Jackery 240 v2 is 20 ms. Running the modem and router from the station's 12 V barrel outputs (most use 12 V barrel adapters; eero uses USB-C) avoids the inverter's own draw and keeps the outputs live without a switch; check each power brick's voltage and plug before buying a cable.

Leaving it on the wall all year

Set the AC output's auto-off timer to "never" (the RIVER 3 turns idle outputs off by default) and set a charge limit in the app where it has one; neither maker gives guidance on permanent duty at 100 %. MacRumors measured about 10 % lost in four hours from the Elite 30 V2 with DC on and nothing plugged in, so a router run all night from DC gets a little less than the table shows. A small 12 V mini UPS for routers is the cheaper single-purpose answer; we found no current US listing price to compare.
