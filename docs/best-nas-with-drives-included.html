Labelled TB vs usable TB on a NAS with storage

The TB number on a drives-included NAS is the raw size of the drives. Two things shrink it. First, formatting and the system take a slice: Synology states about 6.98 TB usable for the 8 TB BeeStation Plus and about 3.45 TB for the 4 TB BeeStation. Second, a mirror stores every file twice. Buffalo's spec sheet for the LS720D0802 lists the drives as 4 TB × 2 and says the unit ships set to RAID 1. A mirror keeps one drive's worth, so an "8 TB" box holds 4 TB of files.

The mirror is the price of a second copy, and the BeeStation has none. Synology's own FAQ says the BeeStation has 'only one built-in hard drive (no RAID)' and that 'Data safety comes from backups, not RAID.' Its USB-A and USB-C ports take an external drive that the BeeStation can back up to automatically. Luka Manestar (Blackvoid) reports that the internal drive is fixed and the whole unit is replaced if it fails. So budget for a USB drive alongside the BeeStation.

If you'd rather have the mirror inside the box, a diskless two-bay plus two matching drives does it, and you choose the drive size. The $239.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nasync-dh2300 is the lowest-priced route at $239.99 before drives. NAS Compares measured it at about 9.5 W idle and 31–32 dBA with two 4 TB drives. On the Synology side, Synology's DSM 7.3 release says 2025-model-year DiskStation Plus units such as the DS225+ accept hard drives that Synology has not validated. M.2 storage pools still need drives on Synology's compatibility list.
