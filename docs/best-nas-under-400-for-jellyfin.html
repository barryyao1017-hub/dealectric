Jellyfin's own rules

  • Free: Jellyfin has no paid pass or tier; hardware acceleration costs nothing.
  • Intel Quick Sync: HEVC 10-bit decode needs Kaby Lake, Apollo Lake, Gemini Lake or newer; AV1 decode needs 11th-gen (Tiger Lake) or newer, which Alder Lake-N chips like the N100 belong to; AV1 encode needs Arc or Meteor Lake, so no N100.
  • Tone mapping: supported on every Intel GPU with HEVC 10-bit decode, through OpenCL (handles Dolby Vision profile 5, needs the OpenCL runtime; Jellyfin notes N95/N100 support from intel-opencl-icd 23.x) or QSV VPP (lower power, Linux only).
  • Docker: the official image includes Intel's media drivers and OpenCL; map /dev/dri/renderD128, add the render group, and check with vainfo inside the container. UGREEN's guide uses a compose file with /dev/dri passed through and host port 9096.
  • Memory: Jellyfin recommends dual-channel RAM for integrated GPUs; the DXP2800 ships with one 8 GB stick, so a second stick (to 16 GB) helps tone mapping as well as containers.

How many streams

No lab has published a concurrent Jellyfin count for the N100. The one measured single-stream test (Linux, March 2024) ran a 4K HDR tone-mapped transcode at about 50–57 fps and 4K-to-1080p with burned-in subtitles at 82 fps; Windows was far slower. Owner reports put the N100 at about two simultaneous 4K HDR tone-mapped streams, with one guide claiming three or four; plan on two 4K HDR streams or several 1080p ones, and use direct play for the TV in the living room.

Jellyfin on UGOS

UGREEN's blog guide installs Jellyfin either from the App Center or by Docker Compose; reviewers describe the App Center as thin and Docker as the route UGREEN support points to, so count on Docker. Put the config and transcode cache on an M.2 SSD and the library on the hard drives.
