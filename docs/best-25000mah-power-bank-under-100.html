Why 25,000 mAh and $100 are the lines: 25,000 mAh at the 3.6 V cell voltage is 90 Wh (25 Ah × 3.6 V), the largest common size under the FAA's 100 Wh carry-on line (FAA PackSafe), and the TSA says power banks "must be packed in carry-on bags". What the differences mean

  • The number in the name is usually a total. "145W", "165W", "170W" and "200W" are all ports added together. One laptop alone gets 140 W from the two UGREEN banks and the Baseus, and 100 W from the AOHi, Sharge, Anker and INIU.
  • 140 W against 100 W. Apple lists a 140 W adapter to fast-charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro: 140 − 100 = 40 W short on a 100 W bank. A 14-inch lists 96 W: 100 − 96 = 4 W to spare, so a 140 W port adds nothing there.
  • Cost per rated watt-hour, 2026-11-26. UGREEN 145W: $59.98 ÷ 90 Wh = $0.67. Baseus FC41: $79.99 ÷ 91.25 Wh = $0.51. INIU P63: $66.99 ÷ 92.5 Wh = $0.72. Sharge: $75.90 ÷ 90 Wh = $0.84. Anker: $91.19 ÷ 90 Wh = $1.01.
  • Refilling the bank. The floor is capacity ÷ input: 90 Wh ÷ 65 W = 1.38 hours for the UGREEN banks; 90.72 Wh ÷ 100 W = 0.91 hours for the AOHi. Real times are longer: smartzone measured 1 h 45 min on the 65 W INIU, and Anker states about 2 hours at 100 W.
  • The label is not what comes out. smartzone (Jan) measured 77.63 Wh from the INIU P63 at 65 W: 77.63 ÷ 92.5 = 84% of the stated figure. Anker prints about 63 to 70 Wh usable for its own 90 Wh bank: 70 ÷ 90 = 78% at best.
  • Same label, different laptop charge. Macworld (Simon Jary) refills a spent 14-inch MacBook Pro twice and adds the two percentages: Sharge HyperTower 170 scored 128%, Anker 25K 92%. 128 ÷ 92 = 1.39, so the Sharge put 39% more into the laptop. Macworld printed no score for the UGREEN 145W; it wrote that it "gets very close" to one full charge of its 14-inch M2 MacBook Pro.

Which laptop gets the full watts, and through which cable

Apple's fast-charge list (support page dated November 5, 2026) is the device side of the question:

Apple's list describes its own adapters; matching a power bank's port to it is our reading of the wattages, not Apple's statement.

Why 140 W is fussier than 100 W. USB-IF: PD "was limited to 100W using a solution based on 20V" with 5 A cables, and the "new 28V, 36V, and 48V fixed voltages enable up to 140W, 180W and 240W". So 100 W = 20 V × 5 A and 140 W = 28 V × 5 A: the extra 40 W exists only at 28 V, and USB-IF defined a 240 W cable rating for those higher levels. UGREEN's spec table lists exactly that step on USB-C1 ("20V-5A 28V-5A 140W Max"), which is why the cable to bring is one rated 240 W, or Apple's MagSafe 3.

Port or fixed cable. On the Baseus FC41, Notebookcheck's launch report puts 140 W on built-in cable 1 and 100 W on the USB-C port. A MagSafe 3 cable has to plug into a port, so a 2021 to early-2023 16-inch MacBook Pro, which Apple fast-charges only through MagSafe 3, is the one laptop the Baseus leaves at 100 W. The UGREEN's 140 W is a port.

The UGREEN 145W changed. The unit Android Central tested in 2023 gave 100 W and 45 W from its USB-C ports, and Android Central (Harish Jonnalagadda) found no Samsung 25 W or 45 W PPS charging. UGREEN's table today lists 140 W and 65 W and names PPS on both USB-C outputs, without a voltage range. The same table prints PD3.1 in its heading and on the input line but only PD3.0 on the output line; Macworld lists the port as 140 W PD 3.1. Read older reviews of this bank as reviews of a different unit.

One laptop or two devices. The 140 W is a laptop-alone figure on the pick: 145 W is the cap for all three ports. The INIU drops furthest: smartzone measured 65 W + 35 W with two USB-C devices, and 65 W + 7.5 W + 7.5 W with three.

Not recommended at this price

Anker Laptop Power Bank 25K 165W at $91.19 (anker.com, 2026-11-26, list $119.99). Each cable or port stops at 100 W, Anker itself prints about 63 to 70 Wh usable, and it scored 92% on Macworld's test, the lowest of the 25,000 mAh banks scored there. Per full 14-inch refill on that test: $91.19 ÷ 0.92 = $99.12, against $75.90 ÷ 1.28 = $59.30 for the Sharge. Its draw is two built-in cables, which the Sharge and the Baseus FC41 also have for less.
