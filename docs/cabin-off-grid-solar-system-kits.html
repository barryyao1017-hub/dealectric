Match the panels to the solar input

A power station's charge controller accepts solar only within a voltage and current window. Panels in series add their voltages. Panels in parallel add their currents. The Elite 200 V2 accepts 12–60 V at up to 20 A, and 1,000 W at most.

  • Two SP200L panels in series: 24.6 V Voc × 2 = 49.2 V at 10.8 A. That is inside both limits, with 10.8 V to spare. Wired in parallel, the same pair would reach 10.8 A × 2 = 21.6 A, over the 20 A rating, so series is the wiring that stays inside both limits. To wire in series, plug one panel's positive MC4 lead into the other panel's negative lead, then connect the two leads left over to the station's solar cable.
  • A third SP200L in series reaches 24.6 × 3 = 73.8 V, which is over 60 V. With these panels, the station tops out at two.
  • Open-circuit voltage rises as panels get colder. BLUETTI doesn't publish a temperature coefficient for the SP200L, so the 10.8 V margin is the buffer you have for cold mornings. Don't add a different panel to the string; BLUETTI's own FAQ advises against mixing panels.
  • In the box: BLUETTI's US page lists the AC cable, a grounding screw and documents. The bundle page has no description. APH Networks' February 2025 review unit came with a solar charging cable. Check the box before the first sunny weekend.

The 48 V kits work the other way: their inverters take high-voltage strings. Sun Gold Power's 5000W 48V inverter listing gives a 500 V, 5,500 W PV limit. The SGR-8K10E listing gives dual MPPT inputs and 11,000 W of PV input. Before you wire a string, multiply the panel's Voc by the number of panels and check the total against the inverter manual's limit.

Not recommended

BLUETTI's Elite 200 V2 + 2×350W Solar bundle costs $2,148, which is $649 more than the 2×200 W bundle. Each 350 W panel is rated 33 V open-circuit, so two in series make 66 V, over the station's 60 V maximum. In parallel, the 20 A input caps the pair at 20 × 33 = 660 W, and the real figure is lower because a panel's operating voltage sits below its Voc (BLUETTI doesn't list the 350 W panel's Vmp). That means $649 for at most 260 W more input than the 400 W set.
