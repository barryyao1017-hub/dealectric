What the differences mean

  • Built-in vs bring-your-own battery: BougeRV 42 qt + BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 = $229.99 + $289.99 = $519.98 for the same 40 L and 288 Wh as the EverFrost 2. That is $799.99 − $519.98 = $280.01 less. The Anker's extra money buys a battery that sits inside the cooler, plus 6-inch wheels and a retractable tow handle (Anker).
  • Premium tier with a battery: Dometic CFX5 45 + PLB40 = $1,049.99 + $539.99 = $1,589.98. The PLB40 holds 512 / 288 = 1.8× the Anker's battery.
  • EverFrost 2 runtime: independent testers reported by ESS News got just over 23 h on one battery as a 4 °C fridge in Eco mode (288 Wh ÷ 23 h ≈ 12.5 W average). Anker claims 52 h, measured at 77 °F with an empty cooler. Plan on one night per battery. A second battery costs $359.99.
  • Dometic on its PLB40: Dometic rates the US CFX5 45 at 1.05 Ah/h at 90 °F (1.05 × 12 V = 12.6 W), so 512 ÷ 12.6 ≈ 41 h in heat. At OutdoorGearLab's measured 6.8 W in a 75 °F lab, 512 ÷ 6.8 ≈ 75 h. Both are before conversion losses.
  • BougeRV 23 qt on an Elite 30 V2: OutdoorGearLab measured 8.3 W, so 288 ÷ 8.3 ≈ 35 h at 75 °F, before conversion losses.

Which battery fits which cooler

Anker: the 288 Wh LFP battery comes with the EverFrost 2 40L. The $359.99 spare fits the EverFrost 2 40L and 58L. Anker says it does not fit the first-generation EverFrost. The battery doubles as a power bank, with a 60 W USB-C port and a 12 W USB-A port.

BougeRV: its 240 Wh LiFePO4 detachable battery ($189.99) goes inside the fridge, and BougeRV lists it only for the CRH20, CRX3, CRD2 V2.0 and Rocky V3.0. The plain 23 qt, the 42 qt and the CRPRO30 are not on that list. For the CRPRO30, BougeRV sells an external 266 Wh power station as a kit instead, at $389.99 − $234.99 = $155 over the fridge alone.

ICECO: the VL45 takes the 250 Wh Magnetic Power Bank ($139 alone, $663.10 bundled with the VL45) as an external pack on a DC cigarette cable. ICECO lists only the VL45PLUS ($799) as 'Magnetic Battery Ready'.

Dometic: the PLB40 (40 Ah, 512 Wh, $539.99) is 'specifically designed for Dometic CFX and other powered coolers' and comes with a 10 A AC/DC charger. Dometic's US CFX5 45 page lists the battery as not applicable: the cooler ships with its two connection cables and no battery.

Running off the car instead: the CFX5 and VL45 both have 3-stage battery protection. For the CFX5, Dometic recommends the High or Medium setting when it runs from the starter battery, and Low when it runs from an accessory or dual battery.

Not recommended

Thermoelectric 'electric coolers' for camping. In OutdoorGearLab's lab, the Cooluli, Wagan and Igloo Versatemp drew 45.7–61.7 W, against 6.8 W for the Dometic CFX5 45. The coldest of them reached only 37.4 °F. On a 288 Wh battery that is 288 ÷ 45.7 ≈ 6 hours, not a night.
