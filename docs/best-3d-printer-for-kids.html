What the differences mean

  • Room for bigger projects: the Centauri Carbon 2 builds 256 × 256 × 256 mm = 16.8 L, the 5M Pro 220 × 220 × 220 mm = 10.6 L and the A1 mini 180 × 180 × 180 mm = 5.83 L. So the CC2 has 16.8 ÷ 5.83 = 2.9 times the A1 mini's space.
  • Rated against measured: the 5M Pro is rated 50–55 dB depending on mode. 3DPrint.com measured a 68 dB average, so 68 − 55 = 13 dB to 68 − 50 = 18 dB above the rating. Elegoo claims 45 dB for the CC2, and Notebookcheck's 44–47 dB average sits right around it.
  • Enclosing the open Adventurer 5M: $249 + $39.99 for Flashforge's enclosure kit = $288.99, and Igor's Lab still measured it at 62.3 dB(A) while printing.
  • Toybox bundles: the Alpha Four Starter is $249 and the Pro Saver is $199. The Pro Saver costs $249 − $199 = $50 less. It includes a Printer Food pack, 5,000 Bolts and a Toybox Pro membership that keeps billing until you cancel it.
  • Toybox build size: 75 × 80 × 90 mm = 0.54 L, against 5.83 L on the A1 mini (5.83 ÷ 0.54 = 10.8 times).

Measured noise against the number on the box

Every maker here quotes a best-case figure. Bambu says the A1 mini gets below 48 dB by sending high-frequency movements to its motors to cancel noise (Notebookcheck's launch report). Flashforge rates the 5M Pro at 50–55 dB depending on the mode you pick (3Dnatives). Elegoo claims 45 dB for the Centauri Carbon 2.

Named reviewers measured something else. Igor's Lab (Tim Kutzner) measured the A1 mini at 55.3 dB(A) from about 40 cm while printing. 3DPrint.com (Orville Wright IV) measured the enclosed 5M Pro at a 68 dB average, with a 79 dB maximum and a 46 dB minimum. He added that it went above 70 dB only for fractions of a second. Notebookcheck (Vineet Washington) measured the CC2 at 44–47 dB on average with peaks up to 54 dB. Igor's Lab put the open Adventurer 5M at 62.3 dB(A) printing and 41.1 dB(A) idle, mostly from its small hotend fan.

These are four tests with different setups. Only Igor's Lab gave a distance for the A1 mini, and 3DPrint.com and Notebookcheck gave none. Read the figures as a ranking, not exact gaps. An enclosure alone doesn't make a printer quiet: the 5M Pro is enclosed and still measured the loudest average. For a printer in a bedroom, the CC2 is the safer bet. If you buy the buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CFD49V4S?tag=dealectric-20, set it to low-noise mode and schedule long prints for when the room is empty.

No named reviewer has published a decibel reading for the Toybox Alpha Four, so it isn't in the table. What Toybox does publish: the bed is unheated, the printer joins 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi only, and setup links it to a Toybox account online. Toybox's help center says any third-party 1.75 mm PLA works and 'will not void your warranty', though it warns against glitter-filled filament. Toybox's own Printer Food costs $65 for four 500 g rolls, which is $32.50/kg.

Not recommended

Flashforge Adventurer 5M as the enclosed option. Its store listing mentions a 'fully enclosed print chamber', yet Flashforge sells a $39.99 enclosure kit for it, and the printer itself is open. Igor's Lab measured 62.3 dB(A) while printing, louder than the open A1 mini's 55.3 dB(A). If you want an enclosed printer, the 5M Pro or the Centauri Carbon 2 comes enclosed.
