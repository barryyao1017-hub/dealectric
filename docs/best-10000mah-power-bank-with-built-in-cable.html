What the differences mean

10,000 mAh is the line because it is the size that refills a phone more than once and still rides in a pocket: Anker's own formula (capacity × 0.65 ÷ phone battery) gives about 1.67 charges of an iPhone 15 from a 10,000 mAh bank, and the five banks here with a published weight run from 191 g to 249 g. Every row is 10,000 mAh by its maker's rating. Left out: INIU's Pawsible, whose cord detaches (INIU); INIU's Pawsible Slim, a different model from the Carry that Techtest measured, so the readings below do not belong to it; and 20,000 mAh banks, which are a different size class.

  • Share of the label you get. 10,000 mAh × 3.7 V = 37 Wh nominal. INIU Carry: 34.9 ÷ 37 = 94%. Anker Nano: 31.5 ÷ 37 = 85% at 5 V 1 A and 27.4 ÷ 37 = 74% at 5 V 3 A (Techtest, Michael Barton, reviews of 2025-05-07 and 2025-06-27). Anker's own FAQ for the Zolo assumes 55–70%.
  • Same load, both banks. At 5 V 1 A: 34.9 ÷ 31.5 = 1.11, so the INIU delivered 11% more. At 9 V 3 A: 34.7 ÷ 29.3 = 1.18, 18% more (Techtest).
  • Energy per 100 g. INIU Carry, 191 g: 34.9 ÷ 1.91 = 18.3 Wh. Anker Nano, 231 g: 31.5 ÷ 2.31 = 13.6 Wh (weights by Techtest).
  • Cost per delivered Wh, INIU. $26.99 ÷ 34.9 Wh = $0.77, at INIU's store price on 2026-11-26 (regular price $36.99; the store posted no end date). The ranking rests on the measurements, not on the sale. The Anker Nano delivered less and costs more: see the Price column.
  • A 45 W Galaxy on a 3 A cable. Samsung's 45 W adapter (EP-T4510) offers 11 V × 4.05 A = 44.6 W on PPS. A cable whose PPS stops at 3 A gives 11 V × 3 A = 33 W, and 33 ÷ 44.6 = 74% of that.
  • Two devices on the Anker Nano. Cable 15 W + port 7.5 W = 22.5 W, half of 45 W (Anker). The phone on the cable gets a third of the headline figure.
  • How long 45 W lasts. Techtest saw the Anker Nano fall from 45 W to about 27 W after 13–16 minutes, and the INIU Carry to about 29 W after about 6 minutes on a MacBook Pro 13 M1. Both are phone banks, not laptop banks.

What each built-in cable carries, as measured

A phone asks the bank for a voltage and current profile, and the cable is part of that offer. An iPhone takes a fixed step: ChargerLAB measured an iPhone 17 Pro Max drawing 15.00 V × 2.54 A = 38.17 W from the UGREEN PB603's built-in cable, a cable whose PPS stops at 3 A. Anker states 40 W max for the iPhone 17 series on the Nano, and Techtest lists a fixed 15 V 3 A step on the INIU Carry. For an iPhone, all the 45 W banks here are fast enough, so delivered energy and price decide.

Samsung's 45 W mode (Super Fast Charging 2.0) runs on PPS instead. Samsung's EP-T4510 adapter lists PPS 3.3–11.0 V at 4.05 A and ships with a 5 A cable; its newer EP-T4511 lists PPS 5.0–20.0 V at 2.25 A. A cable limited to 11 V and 3 A reaches neither.

The INIU's 4.5 A is on its port, so a Galaxy owner would need a separate 5 A cable, which defeats the point of a built-in one. For the UGREEN Nexode Pro and the Baseus FC11, Notebookcheck and Best Batteries list PPS without an amp figure. If you own a 45 W Galaxy, a PPS current above 3 A on the cable is the number to ask for before buying either; for an iPhone they sit between the INIU and the Anker on price, at 249 g (Notebookcheck) and 203 g (Best Batteries).

Not recommended

UGREEN Nexode 45W with Smart Display (PB603) for a 45 W Galaxy. It was $54.98 on amazon.com on 2026-11-26, the highest price in the table, yet ChargerLAB read its cable's PPS at 11 V 3 A, 33 W at most, and its label gives the multi-port simultaneous output as 5 V 3 A. It does feed an iPhone 17 Pro Max 38.17 W, but the INIU Carry has the same 3 A cable limit for about half the price ($26.99 ÷ $54.98 = 0.49).

Any of these for a laptop. Techtest's runs show 45 W lasting 6 to 16 minutes before the banks settle near 27–29 W.
