A per-port display reads the voltage and current leaving each USB-C port and shows them as watts. That lets you see whether a laptop or phone is really drawing what the charger promises without plugging in a USB meter. It matters most if you charge a laptop and a phone from one plug, because the laptop's port gives up watts as soon as a second device goes in. To tell whether a charger really shows each port, look for the maker saying so or a reviewer's photo of the screen. For example, Anker's own page for its 140W 4-Port says only that the screen shows "power, port status, and temperature", and it took Smartzone's review to confirm the per-port overview page. The device sets the number, not the charger: Anker's page for the 2025 Nano 45W says "Maximum power is determined by your device" and "Lower wattage is normal." Dealectric does not test units. Every figure below comes from the maker or the reviewer named next to it.

What the differences mean

  • Screen accuracy. Notebookcheck saw 51 W on the UGREEN's screen against about 49.5 W on a Fnirsi FNB58 meter: 51 − 49.5 = 1.5 W, about 3% of 49.5 W. None of the other reviews cited here checked a screen against a meter.
  • Laptop port beside a second device. UGREEN: 140 − 98 = 42 W given up while C2 fed 55 W (ChargerLAB). Anker 140W 4-Port: 140 − 70 = 70 W given up with two laptops, and 140 − 100 = 40 W beside a phone (Anker's table). Baseus AH22: 100 − 45 = 55 W given up (techtest.org), or 100 − 67 = 33 W in Smart mode (Smartzone).
  • All five ports busy. Notebookcheck measured 56–58 W on the UGREEN's C1 for an hour while four other devices charged: 160 − 58 = 102 W at most for the other four.
  • Full load over time. techtest.org saw the UGREEN hold 140 W for 4 hours and the Anker Prime 160W hold 140 W steadily. Smartzone saw the Anker 140W 4-Port fall to 120 W after about 30 minutes (140 − 120 = 20 W lost). techtest.org saw the AH22 fall from 100 W to 80 W after about 40 minutes.
  • Price. The UGREEN is $56.99 through our link. The Anker 140W 4-Port is $59.99 at Anker US, a sale price with no end date on Anker's page (both read 2026-11-27).

Which screens can show an iPhone 18 Pro charging at full speed

Apple's spec page says the iPhone 18 Pro reaches 50% in about 15 minutes only on a 60 W-capable adapter with Adjustable Voltage Supply (AVS) and a 60 W-capable USB-C cable. Without AVS the phone still charges, just not at that tier. AVS is set port by port, so the phone has to be on a port that has it.

  • UGREEN Nexode Pro 160W: ChargerLAB's protocol readout lists AVS on C1 (9–20 V) and C2. C3 (100 W) has PD 3.0 and PPS but no AVS. Put the iPhone on C1 or C2 and the laptop on the other.
  • Baseus PicoGo Pro AH22: techtest.org lists SPR AVS 9–20 V at 5 A and saw it fast-charge an iPhone 18 Pro Max. The same reviewer notes that about half of all AVS power banks and chargers don't work reliably with that phone. With a second device in, each of the two ports drops to 45 W.
  • Anker 140W 4-Port: Anker's spec sheet lists BC1.2, Apple, QC3.0, PD3.0, FCP, AFC and PPS. AVS is not on it, and the product page's compatibility list stops at iPhone 16.
  • Anker Prime 160W: Anker's page describes PD 3.1 up to 28 V and firmware updates through its app, but it does not list AVS.
  • Anker Nano 45W Smart Display: Anker says AVS arrives through an over-the-air update in the Anker app. Anker's page for the 2025 charger sold under the same name lists 3 A PPS and has no AVS wording, so run the update before expecting the iPhone 18 tier. Anker's "Dynamic 45W - 65W" line sits beside a footnote that gives a maximum PD output of 45W.
  • Satechi ChargeView 240W: Macworld says its two 140 W ports use AVS (PD 3.2).

Not recommended: the Anker 140W 4-Port for an iPhone 18 Pro or a long 140 W load

It has a real per-port screen page, and Anker's table keeps 100 W on the laptop port beside a phone, which suits one laptop and an older phone. But AVS is missing from Anker's protocol list. Smartzone also measured it dropping from 140 W to 120 W after about 30 minutes at 22 °C, with 76 °C at the open ports. On the UGREEN, AVS sits on both 140 W ports, and techtest.org saw it hold 140 W for 4 hours.
