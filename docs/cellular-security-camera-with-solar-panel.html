What the differences mean

  • Cold margin: eufy rates the S330 to -20 °C and Reolink rates the Go PT Ultra to -10 °C. -10 − (-20) = 10 °C, so the eufy has 18 °F more room before it leaves its rated range.
  • Running on stored energy only: the Go PT Ultra is rated down to -10 °C, and Reolink says its battery doesn't charge below 0 °C. From 14 °F to 32 °F (a 10 °C band), it runs only on what is already in the battery.
  • Measured reserve: Outdoor Tech Lab logged the Go PT Ultra using 3–5% of its 21.6 Wh a day. 21.6 × 0.03 to 0.05 = 0.65 to 1.08 Wh a day, so a full battery lasts 100 ÷ 5 = 20 to 100 ÷ 3 ≈ 33 days with no charging.
  • Claimed reserve: Reolink's 2 months implies 21.6 Wh ÷ 60 days ≈ 0.36 Wh a day, one third of the measured upper figure. Plan a freeze around the measured 20 to 33 days.
  • Shade: in woodland, Fstoppers' Darren J. Spoonley saw the Go PT Ultra fall from 100% on Friday to 30% by Monday. 70 points ÷ 3 days ≈ 23 points a day, so a full battery lasts about 100 ÷ 23 ≈ 4 days at that rate.
  • Price with a panel: eufy S330 $249.99 with its 4.5 W panel, against $245 for the Eagle 4G Pro with its panel. They are $4.99 apart.
  • Data: Outdoor Tech Lab's Go PT Ultra used 2.7 GB in 90 days, or 30 MB a day ≈ 0.9 GB per 30 days, at 847 ÷ 90 ≈ 9.4 motion events a day.

Why the solar panel stops helping below 32 °F

Reolink's support page for all of its battery cameras says: "The camera's built-in lithium battery cannot charge properly when the temperature is below 0°C." Its solar panel article gives the charging range as 0 °C to 45 °C. Below that, charging pauses to protect the battery and resumes on its own when the temperature rises. eufy gives the same range for its earlier 4G Starlight Camera: between 0 °C (32 °F) and 45 °C (113 °F), and "otherwise, the camera cannot be charged." The S330 page gives an operating range of -20 °C to 50 °C but no charging range, so plan on the same 32 °F floor.

A bright, sub-freezing week therefore adds nothing to the battery. What decides whether the camera lasts the cold spell is the reserve it starts with. That is why a big battery charged before the freeze matters more than panel wattage. It is also why the Eagle's -22 °F rating counts only for keeping the camera running, not for charging it.

The one named winter log: JC Courtland of Outdoor Tech Lab ran the Go PT Ultra for 90 days in northern Michigan with the 6 W Solar Panel 2, down to 5 °F. The battery entered December at 94%, finished at 91% and never dropped below 85%. Sunny days refilled it in 3–4 hours and overcast days added 1–2%. 5 °F is below Reolink's own 14 °F rating, and this is a single test.

For a Reolink in deep cold, Reolink's Solar Panel 2S carries a 42.48 Wh battery of its own. Between 0 °C and -20 °C, it powers the camera directly instead of charging the camera's battery. Reolink lists it for all battery cameras except Argus, Keen and Doorbell (Battery), but its 2S pages don't name the Go PT Ultra or any 4G model. Confirm with Reolink before buying it for this camera.
