How many watts an iPhone needs from a USB-C block and cable

Apple's fast-charge support document (published 2026-10-22) sets a threshold for each model. iPhone 17, 17 Pro and 17 Pro Max reach about 50% in 20 minutes with a 40 W or higher adapter. iPhone Air and 17e reach about 50% in 30 minutes with 20 W or more. iPhone 15 and 16 need 18 W or more. iPhone 18 Pro and Pro Max reach about 50% in 15 minutes only with a 60 W-capable adapter that uses Adjustable Voltage Supply (AVS) on USB PD 3.2, paired with a 60 W-capable USB-C cable.

A maker listing a phone as "compatible" means the charger will charge it, not that it charges at Apple's fast speed. Belkin lists iPhone 17 and iPhone 18 Pro for its 30 W kit, but 30 W is below both of Apple's bars. Belkin's 45 W kit clears the iPhone 17 bar, although Belkin's own compatibility list stops at iPhone 16.

ESR is the only maker in this table that lists PD 3.2 AVS, and it claims iPhone 17 Pro Max to 50% in 20 minutes. ESR rates the Xtend at 45 W with "dynamic 45–60W" adjustment, which is short of Apple's 60 W-or-higher wording for the 18 Pro. The UGREEN 65W's built-in cable meets Apple's 60 W cable condition, but ChargerLAB's protocol read-out for it shows PD 3.0 and PPS, without AVS. Anker's page for the Nano 45W says it is not compatible with the iPhone 17 Air.

Samsung has a separate rule. Samsung US says 45 W Super Fast Charging 2.0 needs a 45 W or 60 W adapter and a 5 A cable, and that "a 3A cable won't work well". The UGREEN 65W's built-in cable is rated 3 A, so on a Galaxy S22–S24 Plus or Ultra use its USB-C port (PPS 5–11 V at 4.5 A, per ChargerLAB) with a 5 A cable.
