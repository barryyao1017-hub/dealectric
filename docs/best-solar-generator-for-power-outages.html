For a power outage, size a solar generator by what must keep running and for how long. A refrigerator uses about 1–1.5 kWh a day, and a portable power station delivers roughly 85% of its rated capacity as AC power, so a 2 kWh station keeps a fridge going about a day and a half and a 3–4 kWh station two to three days. Solar charging is what carries you past that: 400 W of panel puts back roughly 1.2–1.5 kWh on a sunny day. For 240 V loads such as a well pump, or to feed your home's circuits, you need a station with a 120/240 V L14-30 output and a transfer switch; a TT-30 outlet gives 120 V only.

How to pick for outages

  • Hours first, then capacity. Add up what must run (fridge, Wi-Fi, lights, a CPAP, phones) and multiply by the hours you expect to be without power. Most one-night outages fit 1–2 kWh; storms that take out power for days need 3 kWh or more and a solar panel.
  • How it connects to the house. A TT-30 outlet feeds 120 V circuits through an inlet box and a transfer switch fitted by an electrician. Only stations with an L14-30 120/240 V output (here the DELTA Pro 3 and F3800 Plus) can run 240 V loads such as a well pump.
  • Switchover. A UPS-style switchover of 10–20 ms keeps a router or computer running when the grid drops; the R4000 holds up to 1,500 W of load that way.
  • Room to grow. If you may add capacity later, buy a station that takes extra batteries (DELTA Pro 3, F3800 Plus, HomePower 3600 Plus, DELTA 2 Max, C2000 Gen 2); the Elite 300, Elite 200 V2 and R4000 are fixed.
  • Weight. The R4000 weighs 45 kg and the Elite 300 26.3 kg; plan where it sits before the outage.
  • Prices move. EcoFlow, Anker and Jackery prices here are each store's sale price on 2026-10-30; Anker's include a code the store applies itself, which expires in early November. Our rows carry the price and the code checked in the brand's cart.
