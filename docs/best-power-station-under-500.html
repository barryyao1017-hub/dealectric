The under-$500 figures are Micro-Tools' for the DJI, BLUETTI's store for the Elite 100 mini, EcoFlow's store for the DELTA 3 Classic and the Amazon listing for the Anker. The EcoFlow is under $500 only at its sale price (EcoFlow lists $1,299.00 as the regular price), and EcoFlow's Black Friday pricing runs to November 30, 2026.

The four stations hold almost the same rated energy, so the ranking uses what a named reviewer measured coming out of the AC outlets, then the continuous watts.

  • Cost per delivered Wh, DJI Power 1000 V2: $379.99 ÷ 880 Wh (GearLab) = $0.43. On The Solar Lab's 82% efficiency: 1,024 × 0.82 = 840 Wh, and $379.99 ÷ 840 = $0.45.
  • BLUETTI Elite 100 mini: 1,004.8 × 0.97 = 975 Wh (The Solar Lab's efficiency); $360.05 ÷ 975 Wh = $0.37. The 97% is well above the 82–86% the same reviewer measured on the other three. At 86% it would deliver 864 Wh and cost $0.42 per delivered Wh, about level with the DJI.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Classic: 1,024 × 0.83 = 850 Wh; $459.00 ÷ 850 = $0.54.
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: at GearLab's 890 Wh it matches the DJI's $0.43 only at a price of $384 or less (0.432 × 890).
  • Lost to idle draw in 24 hours with the outlets on and nothing plugged in: DJI 18 W × 24 h = 432 Wh (42% of the battery); EcoFlow 17 × 24 = 408 Wh (40%); Anker 13.8 × 24 = 331 Wh (32%); Elite 100 mini 13.5 × 24 = 324 Wh (32%).
  • Rated Wh per pound: Elite 100 mini 1,004.8 ÷ 23.6 = 42.6; Anker 1,024 ÷ 24.9 = 41.1; EcoFlow 1,024 ÷ 26.7 = 38.4; DJI 1,024 ÷ 31.3 = 32.7.
  • The two "2,600 W" figures: DJI's spec sheet limits the 100–120 V model to 20 A, and 120 V × 20 A = 2,400 W. The 220–240 V model's 10.9 A is where 2,600 W comes from (240 × 10.9 = 2,616 W). X-Boost is a boost mode, not the inverter's continuous rating, so size anything with a motor against 1,800 W.
  • What the watts run: Notebookcheck measured an electric kettle drawing 1,943 W and a coffee machine drawing 727 W on a different 1 kWh station (a BLUETTI Elite 100 V2, priced in euros in that review). On rated watts the DJI (2,400 W) and the Anker (2,000 W) cover that kettle. The EcoFlow's 1,800 W does not without X-Boost, and the Elite 100 mini's 700 W covers neither appliance.

Rated watt-hours against what reaches the outlet

A station's watt-hour figure is what its cells store. What an appliance receives is lower for two reasons. The inverter loses part of the energy converting battery DC to 120 V AC, and it draws its own power whenever the AC outlets are switched on, even with nothing plugged in. Both differ by model, so a single rule-of-thumb factor hides the gap between stations.

Dealectric does not test units. The measurements here are GearLab's (Kenji Mowrey, reviews dated July 23, 2026) and The Solar Lab's. The Solar Lab's reviews do not state the test load, so its figures are quoted with its name.

  • Usable energy barely separates the 1 kWh class. GearLab measured 880 Wh from the DJI Power 1000 V2, 890 Wh from the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2, 870 Wh from the EcoFlow DELTA 3 (the non-Classic model) and 910 Wh from the 1,070 Wh Jackery Explorer 1000 v2. Among the three 1,024 Wh units that is a 20 Wh spread.
  • Price and idle draw do separate them. At an equal 880–890 Wh, the difference in cost per delivered Wh comes from the price paid. Left on for a day with nothing plugged in, the DJI uses about 100 Wh more than the Anker or the Elite 100 mini (432 Wh against 331 and 324).
  • Efficiency, The Solar Lab: BLUETTI Elite 100 mini 97%, Anker about 86%, EcoFlow DELTA 3 Classic about 83%, DJI 82%.
  • Full wall recharge as tested: Anker about 47 minutes in Ultra-Fast mode (The Solar Lab), EcoFlow just under 55 minutes (The Solar Lab), DJI 59 minutes (GearLab), Elite 100 mini about two hours (The Solar Lab). Anker's own page ties its 49-minute claim to UltraFast Charging switched on in the app and a battery above 68 °F.
  • Cycle life as each maker states it: DJI, over 80% capacity after 4,000 cycles; Anker, at least 80% after 4,000 cycles; BLUETTI, 6,000+ cycles for the Elite 100 mini with no capacity percentage; EcoFlow's US pages give a 10-year lifespan estimate in place of a cycle count.
  • Backup switchover: BLUETTI and EcoFlow each state 10 ms, Anker states under 10 ms, and the Micro-Tools listing states 10 ms for the DJI, so this does not separate them.

Not recommended

  • The DJI Power 1000 V2 for solar or car charging. DJI's manual lists AC, SDC, SDC Lite and USB ports only. Solar goes through DJI's separately sold Solar Panel Adapter Module (labelled 400 W, panels in parallel only) and car charging through a separate SDC adapter. The Solar Lab also notes the adapter requirement. If panels are part of the plan, choose a station with the solar input built in.
  • The Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 from Anker's own store on a deadline. Anker's US page read on November 27, 2026 says all C1000 Gen 2 orders ship within 4–9 weeks. Check the delivery date on the Amazon listing before ordering. Anker's page also lists it as not expandable.
  • The BLUETTI Elite 100 mini for kitchen appliances. BLUETTI caps total output at 700 W. The Solar Lab ran it above 1,000 W for a while, but the rating is the figure to plan on.
  • One note on the DJI's seller. DJI's US product pages did not load on November 27, 2026, so the $379.99 is Micro-Tools' listing. DJI's support page for the UK region gives a 5-year default warranty; ask the seller what cover applies to a US order.
