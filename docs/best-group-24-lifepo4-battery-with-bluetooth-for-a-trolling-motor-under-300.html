What the differences mean

Every battery here passes Minn Kota's test, which is that continuous discharge must cover the motor's max amp draw. What separates them is price, energy and water rating.

  • Headroom = continuous rating ÷ 50 A (Minn Kota's max draw for a 12V 50–55 lb motor): 100 ÷ 50 = 2.0×; 120 ÷ 50 = 2.4×; 150 ÷ 50 = 3.0×.
  • Hours at a steady 50 A = Ah ÷ 50 A: 100 ÷ 50 = 2.0 h; 120 ÷ 50 = 2.4 h. Minn Kota says its max figures occur only intermittently, so a real day on the water runs longer.

Why a 150 A BMS buys a 12V trolling motor nothing: Minn Kota's numbers

The motor's own ceiling. Minn Kota's fuse and power-draw table lists 12V motors at 30 A max for 30 lb (30 A breaker), 42 A for 40–45 lb (50 A breaker) and 50 A for 50–55 lb (60 A breaker). Minn Kota specifies a 60 A breaker on the 55 lb motor's circuit, so a battery rated 150 A continuous gives that motor no current a 100 A battery doesn't. The higher-thrust 70 lb (42 A) and 80 lb (56 A) motors run at 24V on two 12V batteries in series. Each battery carries the same current as the motor, so it stays under 100 A as well.

Brushed motors on lithium. Minn Kota tells owners of brushed motors running on lithium to "limit the motor speed to 85%, or Speed 8.5 or lower". Its reason is that lithium holds a higher voltage for longer, which can damage motors built for flooded lead-acid batteries. Below full speed the draw falls further, which leaves even more headroom.

Measured, not just rated. Panbo's Ben Stein tested the WattCycle 100Ah Bluetooth. He measured about 101.75 Ah, and in his high-current test the BMS shut off only at almost 200 A. He reports the BMS handles surges of 220 A for up to 6 seconds and 400 A for 0.3 seconds. Techtest.org measured 100.92 Ah at 40 A from LiTime's Group 24 Bluetooth battery.

Charging on a Minn Kota on-board charger. Minn Kota's PCL chargers charge 12V LiFePO4 at a maximum of 14.5 V, and Minn Kota tells owners to check that against the battery maker's specification. They are not compatible with single 24V or 36V lithium batteries, which is why a 24V or 36V setup needs 12V batteries in series. Panbo saw the WattCycle's BMS cut charging at 14.2 V, but only when 25 A or more was going in. Stein avoided this by setting absorption to 13.9–14.0 V. Minn Kota's MK 330PCL charges at 10 A per bank.

Where each one falls short. The WattCycle page contradicts itself on thrust, with 30–100 lb in the title and 30–70 lb in the text. It also says only "Designed to Group 24 size", so measure your tray before buying. Redodo calls its 100Ah Group 24 "NOT suitable for ... high-power applications". LiTime's new Group 24 Bluetooth battery costs $311.09, which is over this budget. The $256.99 Like New unit is an open-box return that LiTime says carries the same 5-year warranty, though a badge on the same page says 2 years.
