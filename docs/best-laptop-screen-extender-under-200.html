What the differences mean

Pixels added is the working room a screen gives you. Pixel density (PPI) is how small text can get and stay crisp. Here is the arithmetic for each row:

  • FOPO S7: 1920 × 1200 = 2.30 million pixels per screen, × 2 = 4.61 MP. √(1920² + 1200²) = 2,264 pixels on the diagonal ÷ 14 in = 162 PPI.
  • Mobile Pixels Fold 16" QHD: 2560 × 1600 = 4.10 MP. √(2560² + 1600²) = 3,019 ÷ 16 in = 189 PPI, the sharpest screen in the table.
  • Arzopa E1: 1920 × 1080 = 2.07 MP per screen, × 2 = 4.15 MP. 2,203 ÷ 15.6 in = 141 PPI.
  • A single 15.6-inch 1080p screen such as the Arzopa A1: 2.07 MP at 141 PPI. The S7 adds 4.61 ÷ 2.07 = 2.2 times as many pixels. A 14-inch 1080p clip-on comes to 2,203 ÷ 14 = 157 PPI and still adds only 2.07 MP.
  • On an M1, M2 or M3 MacBook Air with the lid open, only one S7 screen lights: 2.30 MP. On that Mac the Fold's one screen gives 4.10 ÷ 2.30 = 1.8 times as many pixels.
  • Weight: the S7 pair weighs 1.61 kg × 2.2046 = 3.55 lb and hangs on the lid (FOPO). The Fold 16" QHD weighs 1.89 lb and stands on the desk (Scan's listing).

Pixels, sharpness and brightness on one scale

Listings give resolution, size, nits and colour gamut in different units, so this page puts them on one scale. Two 14-inch 1920×1200 screens (the S7) beat two 15.6-inch 1080p screens (the E1) on both counts: 4.61 MP against 4.15 MP, and 162 PPI against 141 PPI. One 16-inch 2560×1600 screen (the Fold 16" QHD) has fewer pixels than either pair, but it has the highest density, 189 PPI, and needs only one display output.

Brightness, as the maker or seller states it: FOPO rates the S7 at 250 cd/m², and Scan lists the Fold 16" QHD at 220 cd/m². Arzopa's E1 and A1 listings give no brightness figure. One cd/m² equals one nit. No named lab has measured any screen in the table. The nearest measurement is Notebookcheck's 2022 review of FOPO's older 12-inch dual extender: 193.1 cd/m² peak and 247:1 contrast. Dealectric does not test units, so read every brightness figure here as the maker's or seller's claim.

Colour gamut on listings: BenQ, a monitor maker, puts the area ratio of sRGB to NTSC at 0.72 to 1. A "72% NTSC" listing and a "100% sRGB" listing therefore make the same area claim. BenQ also notes that area is not coverage, so neither figure shows that a screen covers all of sRGB.

Compatibility: whether the pixels show up

Each S7 screen needs its own video connection, with no driver: one video-capable USB-C or Thunderbolt port per screen, or HDMI plus USB power (FOPO). The laptop must also be able to drive two external displays:

  • Apple lists one external display for the M1 and M2 MacBook Air.
  • The M3 MacBook Air and MacBook Pro drive a second external display only with the lid closed, and an extender on an open lid cannot use that mode.
  • M4 and M5 MacBook Airs drive two external displays with the lid open.
  • On Windows, Microsoft's hardware certification, introduced alongside Windows 11 version 24H2, requires DisplayPort Alt Mode on every USB-C port of a certified laptop. Laptops certified before that still need their ports checked.

The Fold 16" QHD takes video and power over one USB-C cable from a port with DisplayPort Alt Mode, or video over HDMI, with no driver (Scan's listing). It therefore lights on any Mac or PC that drives one external display. Arzopa calls the E1 single-cable but does not say how one cable drives both screens. When a design uses MST to do that, macOS mirrors the screens instead of extending them (Plugable). Mac owners should ask Arzopa about this before buying the E1.
