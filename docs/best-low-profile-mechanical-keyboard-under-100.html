Battery ratings are not measured the same way

The hours in the table are best cases. Keychron's and Lofree's listings say "up to" and give no backlight or connection condition in the text we read. Logitech's spec page gives 36 hours with no condition. Only the Flow Lite84 has a reviewer's split by mode. Julian van der Merwe at Notebookcheck estimated it from the drain per hour:

  • Bluetooth, backlight off: 1% per hour, about 100 h, or 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5 days.
  • Bluetooth, full backlight: 6% per hour, about 16.6 h, or 16.6 ÷ 8 = 2.1 days.
  • 2.4 GHz dongle, full backlight: 10% per hour, about 10 h, or 10 ÷ 8 = 1.25 days.

So the backlight and the dongle can cut a rating to a tenth. Dividing capacity by rated hours shows which ratings assume the lightest use:

  • K3 Ultra 8K: 2,600 ÷ 550 = 4.7 mA.
  • K3 Version 3: 1,550 ÷ 124 = 12.5 mA.
  • K3 Max SE: 1,550 ÷ 120 = 12.9 mA.
  • Flow Lite84: 2,000 ÷ 100 = 20 mA.
  • K3 Max: 1,550 ÷ 73 = 21.2 mA.
  • Logitech G515: 1,500 ÷ 36 = 41.7 mA.
  • Keychron K3 HE ($95.99 at Keychron, a Hall effect board): 2,600 ÷ 55 = 47.3 mA, which is 55 ÷ 8 = 6.9 days.
  • Flow84: 2,000 ÷ 40 = 50 mA.

The NuPhy Node75 low-profile cannot be placed in that list. Notebookcheck reports a claim of up to 1,000 hours for the Node75 with no conditions given and without saying which version it covers. The low-profile version has a 3,000 mAh battery against 4,000 mAh in the full-height one, and Bluetooth runs at 125 Hz.

Remapping also splits these boards. Keychron lists its Launcher web app for macOS, Windows and Linux on every K3 model here. Notebookcheck found the Flow Lite84 remaps only through Lofree Key Mapper, a Windows-only download, and that only Lofree x Kailh low-profile switches fit its sockets.
