What the differences mean

About 50 lb is where a 2,000 Wh power station stops being a one-person lift: the NIOSH lifting equation starts from a load constant of 23 kg (about 51 lb) under ideal conditions (CCOHS), and Powerbencher lists the EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max at 50.7 lb and the BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 at 53.35 lb. Every row above is rated 2,000 Wh or more by its maker and the rows are ranked by weight. Left out: the ALLPOWERS R2500 V2 (1,920 Wh), just short of the line, and the Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2 (41.7 lb, 2,048 Wh, 2,400 W on Anker's page), which is not the lightest and which Anker ships in 6-12 weeks.

  • Weight saved: 39.5 − 35.7 = 3.8 lb between the two lightest, on the makers' figures. OutdoorGearLab weighed the Jackery at 38.9 lb, which makes it 3.2 lb.
  • Rated energy per pound: 2,010 ÷ 35.7 = 56.3 Wh/lb for the S2000; 2,042 ÷ 39.5 = 51.7 for the Jackery.
  • Energy per pound at the outlet: 1,900 ÷ 35.7 = 53.2 Wh/lb (Notebookcheck's draw from the S2000); 1,710 ÷ 39.5 = 43.3 Wh/lb (OutdoorGearLab's figure for the Jackery).
  • Weight per kW of output: 35.7 ÷ 1.5 = 23.8 lb for the S2000; 39.5 ÷ 2.2 = 18.0 lb for the Jackery. The S2000 is the lightest per watt-hour and the heaviest per watt in the table.
  • The wait: Nov. 27 + 7 weeks = Jan. 15, 2027; + 13 weeks = Feb. 26, 2027. Four weeks from Nov. 27 is Dec. 25, which the Jackery (by Dec. 11) and the Dabbsson (from Dec. 10 on its own store) both beat.
  • Cycle life on one line: Anker rates the S2000 at 6,000 cycles to 80% capacity and 10,000 to 60%; powerstations.reviews gives the Jackery 4,000 cycles to 70%; Dabbsson states 4,000 cycles to over 80%. A straight line between Anker's two points, 6,000 + (10,000 − 6,000) × 10 ÷ 20, gives about 8,000 cycles at 70%; that is our interpolation, not an Anker figure.

On Nov. 27 Anker's store listed the S2000 at $649.99 (list $999.99, under a Black Friday banner dated Nov. 27-30), below the Jackery's $999.00, so the lighter unit is also the cheaper one. What it costs is the wait and the 1,500 W ceiling.

The S2000 and Jackery figures come from different labs at different loads, so read them as two named measurements, not one test. The cleanest pairing is Notebookcheck's own: at a light load the S2000 gave about 300 Wh more than the C2000 Gen 2 (1,900 against 1,600 Wh) from a label 38 Wh smaller and a body 6 lb lighter. No unit in the table delivered its full 2,000 Wh at the outlet. Notebookcheck also metered just over 2,400 Wh from the wall for a full S2000 charge (about 78% round trip) and saw it use just over 10% of its charge per 24 hours with AC left on. It calls the S2000 “the lightest 2-kWh power station we have come across so far”; Anker credits 314Ah LFP cells. Jackery's store text calls the Explorer 2000 v2 the “Lightest and Most Efficient Compact 2kWh design”; Anker's 35.7 lb is 3.8 lb under it.

Not recommended

The Anker SOLIX S2000, in two cases. First, if you need the station before mid-January: Anker's US store says orders placed on or after Nov. 26 ship within 7-13 weeks. Second, if anything you run draws more than 1,500 W: GearJunkie (Nick Belcaster, 2026-07-30) ran a toaster of about 850 W and a refrigerator for 34 h 15 min, but a planer rated about 1,800 W tripped the overload, and Notebookcheck lists “low output power” and “few ports” among its cons. Anker's own comparison table marks it “Expandable Battery: No” and “12V DC Port: No”. What it keeps: a 10 ms UPS switchover, a 5-year warranty and a full recharge in 2.3 hours at the default 1,150 W input (Anker's table lists AC input up to 1,600 W; UltraFast Mode has to be switched on in the app). For either case the Jackery Explorer 2000 v2 is the lightest alternative: 2,200 W continuous, 0 to 100% in about 1.7 hours and switchover under 20 ms by Jackery's figures.
