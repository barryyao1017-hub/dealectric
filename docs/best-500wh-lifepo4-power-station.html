What the differences mean

  • Jackery to 80%: a 30% loss over 6,000 cycles is 0.005% per cycle, and 20% ÷ 0.005% = about 4,000 cycles.
  • DJI to 80%: a 30% loss over 4,000 cycles is 0.0075% per cycle, and 20% ÷ 0.0075% ≈ 2,670 cycles. On the same basis DJI's 4,000 is below EcoFlow's 3,000.
  • Years at one full cycle a day: EcoFlow 3,000 ÷ 365 = 8.2 years; Jackery ≈4,000 ÷ 365 ≈ 11 years; Goal Zero 4,000 ÷ 365 ≈ 11 years.
  • Price per rated Wh: EcoFlow $309 ÷ 499 Wh = $0.62; Goal Zero $499.95 ÷ 499.2 Wh = $1.00; DJI $299 ÷ 512 Wh = $0.58 (sale price recorded 2026-11-03).

Why 6,000 cycles isn't twice 3,000

A cycle rating counts full charge-and-drain cycles until the battery holds a stated share of its original capacity, so the end-point matters as much as the count. Jackery rates the Explorer 500 v2 at 6,000 cycles to 70%, and DJI rates the Power 500 at 4,000 cycles to 70%. powerstations.reviews lists the RIVER 2 Max 500 at 3,000 cycles to 80%, and Goal Zero rates the Yeti 500 at 4,000+ cycles to 80% at a 1C rate. At 70%, a 512 Wh pack holds about 358 Wh (512 × 0.7). At 80%, a 499 Wh pack holds about 399 Wh (499 × 0.8). So a 70% rating counts cycles well past the point where an 80% rating stops.

The 80% figures for Jackery and DJI assume the battery fades at a steady rate. Real LiFePO4 fade isn't steady, and neither maker publishes a cycle count to 80%, so treat about 4,000 and about 2,670 as estimates. Only DJI and Goal Zero state test conditions: DJI at 25 °C with a 500 W output, Goal Zero at 1C. On the same comparison page, Jackery rates the old Explorer 500 at 800 cycles to 80% and the v2 at 6,000 to 70%, so even the maker switches end-points between generations.

Not recommended

The original Jackery Explorer 500. It shares the name but uses NMC cells, not LiFePO4. Jackery rates it at 800 cycles to 80%, against about 4,000 to 80% for the v2. Check for "v2" in the listing. The BLUETTI AC50P (504 Wh, 700 W) is listed at $339 on BLUETTI's US store, but on 2026-11-14 the store showed it sold out, along with its $549 kit with a 100 W panel.
