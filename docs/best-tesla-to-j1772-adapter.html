What the differences mean

Tesla's spec for the Gen 3 Wall Connector gives an output of 12–48 A. On that charger every adapter here delivers the same power: 48 A × 240 V = 11.52 kW. A higher rating doesn't make the car charge faster on that unit. What changes is how hard each adapter is worked and what it costs.

  • Share of rating in use: 48 ÷ 80 = 60% (Lectron 80A, A2Z Stellar, TeslaTap Mini 80A); 48 ÷ 60 = 80% (TeslaTap Mini 60A); 48 ÷ 48 = 100% (Lectron 48A, for the whole session).
  • Cost per kW delivered: Lectron 80A $129.99 ÷ 11.52 kW = $11.28; TeslaTap Mini 60A $194.96 ÷ 11.52 = $16.92; Lectron 48A $99.99 ÷ 11.52 = $8.68.
  • Price gaps: TeslaTap Mini 60A $194.96 − $129.99 = $64.97 more; TeslaTap Mini 80A $224.96 − $129.99 = $94.97 more; the older Lectron 48A saves $129.99 − $99.99 = $30.00.

Why an adapter may not charge at a Tesla destination charger

Each maker lists Tesla Destination Chargers and Wall Connectors as compatible. The deciding setting is on the charger. Tesla's Wall Connector installation guide has an Access Control menu with four options:

  • All Vehicles, the default: 'any vehicle can charge from this Wall Connector.' Any adapter here works.
  • Only Teslas: 'any Tesla can charge.' A J1772 car on an adapter will not charge.
  • Authorized Teslas Only: only Teslas added in device setup or the Tesla app (by VIN) can charge. A J1772 car on an adapter will not charge.
  • Compatibility Mode: 'allows Wall Connector to be compatible with older generation vehicles … Use it only if you experience faults with your Wall Connector and your electric vehicle.' Tesla notes some software functions are lost in this mode.

Tesla North (Nehal Malik, 2021-01-31) reported that this restriction reached Gen 3 Wall Connectors in firmware 1.4.4, pushed over the air and set from the unit's admin page. Only the person who runs the charger can change it. None of the adapter makers' pages claims an adapter can get around it, and the Lectron 80A, A2Z Stellar and both TeslaTap pages say their adapters don't work at Tesla Superchargers.

Before you drive there: ask the hotel or host whether their Tesla chargers are set to All Vehicles. If the charger is set to All Vehicles but your car starts and then faults, ask them to try Compatibility Mode.

Not recommended

Lectron 48A (older model). It saves $30.00 over the 80A, but on a Gen 3 Wall Connector it runs at 100% of its 48 A rating for the whole session. Lectron lists it only as 'SGS certified under the UL 2251 standard', while its 80A sibling is UL 2252 certified. The 48A page also names no temperature sensor.
