What owners complain about

  • Heat at 48 A on a Tesla Wall Connector (about 7 reports in Rivian, Lucid, F-150 Lightning and InsideEVs forums). Most name Lectron's earlier adapters. One Rivian owner reported 148 °F and a damaged charge port in 2023. Others saw charging throttled to 6-7 kW in a hot garage. Owners' fixes: set the car's AC charge limit to 40 A, or add a short J1772 extension cable so the adapter isn't against the car's port.
  • Too tight or stuck (about 6 reports, mostly the TeslaTap Mini). Owners describe 20 minutes to pull one free, and some use silicone grease. One Lectron buyer said the NACS end is a little tight.
  • Car won't start charging (about 4 reports, on both brands). A Tesla Wall Connector can be set to charge Tesla vehicles only, and only its owner can change that. Some chargers also need a few seconds to finish connecting after you plug in.
  • Rain (2 reports, older Lectron models). The certified 80 A Lectron is rated IP67.

Which one by need

  • Road trips and hotel destination chargers: Lectron NACS to J1772 80A (UL 2252) ($149.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/lectron-nacs-to-j1772-80a-ul2252). It's certified, has a temperature sensor and a 2-year warranty when bought direct, and costs $75 less than the TeslaTap Mini 80A.
  • Every night on a Tesla Wall Connector: owners favor the TeslaTap Mini 80A for its longer track record, US build and padlock points. Test the fit on your car within the return window.
  • Buying a home charger: the Tesla Universal Wall Connector skips the adapter entirely.
  • Tesla or NACS-port car at J1772 stations: Lectron J1772 to NACS 80A (UL 2252) ($45.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/lectron-j1772-to-nacs-80a-ul2252).
  • CCS1 car at Tesla Superchargers: Lectron Vortex Plus ($184.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/lectron-vortex-plus-nacs-to-ccs-500a), but only if your automaker has Supercharger access.

Not recommended

Skip Lectron's older 48 A Tesla-to-J1772 adapter. It is tested to UL 2251 (the standard for the charging plug), not UL 2252, and it is the model behind most of the heat reports. It saves only $37 over the certified 80 A version. Don't buy a NACS-to-CCS (DC) adapter for Level 2 charging either: one Lucid owner who did got an error at a Level 2 charger. Lectron accepts returns only on unused adapters, so check which way the adapter converts and that it works with your car before you plug it in.
