What the differences mean

Days per charge with a daily one-hour GPS workout is worked out as 1 ÷ (1/rated days + 1/GPS hours). It assumes the battery drains at a steady rate, so treat it as an estimate.

  • Amazfit Active 2: 1 ÷ (1/10 + 1/21) = 1 ÷ 0.148 = about 6.8 days, or about 54 charges a year (365 ÷ 6.8).
  • Fitbit Charge 6: 1 ÷ (1/7 + 1/5) = 1 ÷ 0.343 = about 2.9 days, or about 126 charges a year. OutdoorGearLab measured about 8 h of GPS, which gives 1 ÷ (1/7 + 1/8) = 3.7 days, still about half the Active 2.
  • GPS hours: 21 h ÷ 5 h = 4.2 times as much GPS time on the Active 2 as on the Charge 6.
  • Always-on display: Charge 6 drops from 7 to 3 days (57% less); Inspire 3 drops from 10 to 3 days (70% less).
  • Apple Watch: Series 11 and 12 are rated 24 h, a figure that already includes a 60-minute workout and 6 h of sleep tracking, so you charge once a day. SE 3's 18 h covers 18 ÷ 24 = 75% of a day, so sleep tracking needs a daytime top-up or Low Power Mode (32 h).

Why '7 days' and '24 hours' come from different tests

Fitbit's 'up to 7 days' for the Charge 6 comes with a caveat. Google's help center adds that 'using features such as always-on display and GPS require more frequent charging', and gives up to 3 days with the always-on display and up to 5 hours with continuous GPS. Apple's 24 hours is a scripted day: 300 time checks, 90 notifications, 15 minutes of app use, a 60-minute workout with Bluetooth music and 6 hours of sleep tracking. The band's figure leaves the workout out and the watch's figure puts it in, so the two numbers can't be compared directly.

Measured runtimes back up the narrower gap. OutdoorGearLab measured the Charge 6 at just under 7 days of smartwatch use and about 8 h of GPS. Geek Culture's Apple Watch Series 11 lasted about 32 h with the always-on display, sleep tracking and a 2-hour workout. Put on the same basis, the band's lead is about 5 times, not 7. For a GPS-first watch, Garmin's vívoactive 6 manual lists up to 11 days in smartwatch mode and 21 h of GPS-only use (8 h with all satellite systems and music). OutdoorGearLab measured 20 h of GPS and about 5 days with the always-on display.

The Charge 6's heart rate is reliable for steady workouts: Notebookcheck measured about 1% deviation from a Polar H10 chest strap at rest and in exercise. The short GPS rating is the weak point, not the heart-rate sensor.

Your phone also matters. Fitbit bands reply to messages only when paired with an Android phone; on an iPhone they can respond only to Google Health app notifications. Amazfit watches can reply on an iPhone through Zepp Messenger with a Beeper account, because Apple doesn't let third-party wearables reply to notifications directly.

Not recommended

Fitbit Charge 6 for daily GPS runners. Its 5-hour GPS rating means charging about every 3 days if you record an hour outdoors each day. The Amazfit Active 2 goes about 6.8 days on the same routine and has a full watch screen.
