What the differences mean

  • Minutes per charge = measured usable Wh ÷ the microwave's input watts × 60. Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: 890 ÷ 1,500 × 60 = 35.6 minutes.
  • Reheats: a two-minute reheat at 1,500 W uses 1,500 × 2 ÷ 60 = 50 Wh. 890 ÷ 50 = 17.8, so 17 full reheats.
  • Battery size barely separates them. The four OutdoorGearLab-tested units range from 34.8 to 36.4 minutes at 1,500 W, a spread of 1.6 minutes. The inverter rating is what decides whether your microwave runs at all.
  • Headroom = continuous AC − microwave input. C1000 Gen 2: 2,000 − 1,500 = 500 W. Elite 100 V2, AC180 and DELTA 3: 1,800 − 1,500 = 300 W. Explorer 1000 v2: 1,500 − 1,500 = 0 W.
  • Bigger microwaves cost minutes. At an 1,800 W input the C1000 Gen 2 gives 890 ÷ 1,800 × 60 = 29.7 minutes.

Read the input watts on the microwave's rating label, not the cooking watts printed on the door or in the ad. The input figure is what the power station has to supply.

Measured runtimes at microwave-sized loads

Most runtime tables multiply the rated Wh by an assumed efficiency. The figures above come from tests instead. OutdoorGearLab's capacity test loads each station with "approximately 80% of its rated maximum wattage" using a light-bar jig and runs it from 100% to empty. For these units that is roughly 1,200–1,600 W, which is the range a household microwave draws. Results: 890 Wh from the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2, 890 Wh from the BLUETTI Elite 100 V2, 870 Wh from the EcoFlow DELTA 3 and 910 Wh from the Jackery Explorer 1000 v2.

Timed and high-load tests:

  • The Solar Lab ran the Explorer 1000 v2 at a steady 1,479 W, "just shy of its maximum 1,500W capacity". It lasted 37.5 minutes against an expected 43, which they report as 87.5% efficiency. It held a peak of 1,560 W for about 10 minutes and shut off after a 2,200 W surge lasting a couple of seconds.
  • The Technology Man drew 907 Wh from the C1000 Gen 2 at a 1 kW heater load. That works out to 907 ÷ 1,500 × 60 = 36.3 minutes at 1,500 W. At 2 kW the unit overheated after 26 minutes. The heater was a little close to it, which the reviewer said wouldn't have helped, so keep the station's vents clear of the microwave.
  • OutdoorGearLab found the C1000 Gen 2's fan "frustratingly loud" near its maximum wattage. It said the Elite 100 V2 handled a toaster oven easily.
  • Digital Trends (Simon Sage) measured 976 Wh from the AC180 over a full discharge. He said it was the first station he had tested that could run his high-wattage microwave.

Boost modes don't raise the limit for a microwave. BLUETTI's AC180 manual says its 2,700 W Power Lifting mode is for "devices consisting of heating elements only" while "its rated output power remains 1800W". EcoFlow describes X-Boost as "an intelligent voltage control that marginally reduces voltage". The DELTA 3 reaches 2,200 W only in that mode. Anker's 2,000 W on the C1000 Gen 2 is its plain continuous rating (120 V × 16.66 A). So compare your microwave's label against the continuous figure in the table, not the larger number in each listing.

Not recommended

Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 for a full-size microwave. It measured the most usable energy of the four OutdoorGearLab-tested units (910 Wh). But its continuous AC rating is 1,500 W, and The Solar Lab's sustained test went only about 60 W past that (1,560 W for about 10 minutes, when the testers stopped it). A microwave drawing around 1,500 W leaves no margin. It suits a smaller microwave whose label input is well under 1,500 W.
