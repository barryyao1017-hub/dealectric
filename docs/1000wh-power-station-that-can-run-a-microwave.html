Which 1,000 Wh portable power station can actually run a microwave, and for how long? (2026)

Short answer. A 1,000 Wh power station can run a microwave if its AC output beats the microwave's input watts on the nameplate, not its cooking watts: a "1,000 W" microwave draws about 1,400–1,600 W. The Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 (price on the Amazon page; buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FN7MSY4L?tag=dealectric-20) has the most headroom in the 1,000 Wh class at 2,000 W, and a charge covers roughly 15 two-minute reheats if nothing else runs (our arithmetic).

What to know: no reviewer has metered a microwave on a 1,000 Wh unit yet, and one measured 1,849 W from a microwave on a larger BLUETTI, more than the 1,800 W limit of the EcoFlow DELTA 3, BLUETTI AC180 and ALLPOWERS P1800, so check the label inside the door first. The Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 (1,500 W) suits a 700 W microwave at about 1,050 W input, not a full-size one.

Cooking watts and input watts

A microwave's advertised wattage is the cooking power; the nameplate (inside the door or on the back) gives what it draws from the outlet, about 1.4–1.6 times more. Examples from makers' sheets as quoted by BLUETTI: a 700 W GE (JES1072) 1,050 W input, a 900 W GE (JES1097) 1,050 W, a 1,000 W GE over-the-range (JVM6175) 1,580 W, a 1,200 W Panasonic (NN-SC67NS) 1,400 W. Panasonic's own page lists its inverter model NN-SN966S at 1,250 W cooking and 1,460 W input. An "inverter" microwave varies its power smoothly instead of cycling on and off, but still peaks near its nameplate. Digital Trends measured 1,849 W from a microwave on a BLUETTI AC500, which is why a 1,800 W station can trip on a full-size model.

How many reheats

Two minutes at about 1,500 W is about 50 Wh at the outlet; with about 85–90 % of the rated capacity delivered (907 Wh measured from the C1000 Gen 2 at a steady 1 kW), that is roughly 15–18 two-minute reheats from a 1,024 Wh unit, fewer if a fridge or lights share the charge. Anker's own estimate is about 36 minutes of microwave time on 1,000 Wh at 1,500 W and says it has not measured it. These are arithmetic, not tests.

Before you rely on it

Run the microwave alone on the station with nothing else plugged in, start on a short time and watch the station's output reading; if it shows an overload, a smaller microwave at around 1,000 W input is the fix, not a power-saving mode. Surge ratings (2,700–3,600 W) cover the start, but the continuous rating is what has to hold.
