What the differences mean

The figure that separates these sets is how many watts of panel each one can actually charge from. That sets how much energy comes back each day.

  • Daily solar at 4 full-sun hours (the basis Sun Gold Power uses for its 14.4 kWh figure): 3,600 W × 4 h = 14.4 kWh; 2,000 W × 4 h = 8.0 kWh; 1,000 W × 4 h = 4.0 kWh.
  • Measured panel output: Impulse Gamer measured about 400 W average from one SORA 500 in Australian winter, so 4 panels give about 1,600 W (4 × 400) and 2 panels about 800 W (2 × 400).
  • Idle draw: ESS News measured the Apex 300 at 19.5 W with its inverters on. 19.5 W × 24 h = 468 Wh a day, which is 11.7% of the 1 kW kit's 4.0 kWh day (468 / 4,000) and 5.9% of the 2 kW kit's 8.0 kWh day (468 / 8,000).
  • Battery per dollar, panels included: $4,949 / 10.24 kWh = $483 per kWh; $6,554 / 5.53 kWh = $1,185 per kWh; $3,255 / 2.76 kWh = $1,177 per kWh (kit totals at BLUETTI and Sun Gold Power store prices on 2026-11-13).

Why an Apex 300 takes 1,000 W of panels, not 2,400 W

The Apex 300's two built-in solar ports are XT60 inputs rated 12–60 V and 20 A, 1,200 W each (StorageReview, BLUETTI). BLUETTI's own setup guide says the input is capped at 60 V open-circuit and to plan parallel strings rather than series arrays. BLUETTI's product page lists the 4,000 W solar figure as requiring a SolarX 4K.

  • SORA 500 electrics (Impulse Gamer): 49.1 V open-circuit, 40.92 V at max power, 12.22 A at max power, 13.31 A short-circuit, MC4 plugs.
  • Two in series: 2 × 49.1 = 98.2 V, over the 60 V limit.
  • Two in parallel: 2 × 13.31 = 26.6 A short-circuit on a 20 A port; even at the 20 A cap a port delivers about 20 × 40.92 = 818 W.
  • One per port: 49.1 V and 13.31 A fit, so 2 × 500 W = 1,000 W is the clean maximum on the built-in ports.

The SolarX 4K takes 150–500 V strings and steps them down to below 60 V for the Apex 300 (BLUETTI). BLUETTI's AU guide gives a 150 V start-up voltage, an MPPT range of 120–450 V, 13 A maximum input and 17 A short-circuit.

  • Three SORA 500s in series: 3 × 49.1 = 147.3 V, below the 150 V start-up.
  • Four in series: 4 × 49.1 = 196.4 V open-circuit and 4 × 40.92 = 163.7 V at max power; 163.7 V × 12.22 A ≈ 2,000 W. 12.22 A is under the 13 A input limit and 13.31 A is under the 17 A short-circuit limit.
  • Two parallel strings: 2 × 12.22 = 24.4 A, over the 13 A limit, so stay with one string and add panels in series. Eight in series is 8 × 49.1 = 392.8 V at rated conditions and 4,000 W, the SolarX 4K's output rating. Open-circuit voltage rises on cold mornings, so leave headroom under 500 V.

Notebookcheck's first look at the SolarX 4K says it needs a separately bought PV disconnect (600 V / 20 A) and a ground cable, and must hang vertically on a dry wall because it is not weatherproof. StorageReview lists the Apex 300's charging range as 0–40 °C, so off-grid it belongs indoors in a heated space. BLUETTI's page also states that the Hub D1 ($299) is required for any DC output.

Not recommended

BLUETTI's Apex 300 + 2*B300K + 2*500W bundle ($5,499) puts 8,294.4 Wh of battery behind only 1,000 W of panel on the built-in ports. At 4 sun-hours that is 8,294.4 / 4,000 = 2.1 full days of sun to refill from empty, before the 468 Wh a day the unit draws at idle. The Apex 300 + 2*B300K + SolarX 4K bundle has the same battery for $4,498 and takes up to 4,000 W of panels in series.
