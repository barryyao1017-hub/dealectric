What the differences mean

Every hour figure on this page is arithmetic: the battery's watt-hours divided by the watts the kit's own panels deliver, capped by the station's solar input. Dealectric does not test units. None of the reviewers below timed a full 0–100% solar refill on these kits, so treat the hours as full-sun estimates. They leave out charging losses and the slower top-off near 100%.

  • Same battery, bigger panel: the P1800 with its 600 W panel needs 1,024 ÷ 600 = 1.7 h. With the 400 W SP037 it needs 1,024 ÷ 400 = 2.6 h. That is a third less sun (1 − 400 ÷ 600 = 0.33).
  • Real-sun basis: where a named tester measured the bundled panel, the measured watts are used. Both 400 W panels gave about 360 W, so 1,024 ÷ 360 = 2.8 h. For the other panels the page uses 75% of rating. That was the lowest result OutdoorGearLab measured among major-brand 200 W panels (Bluetti 200W, 150 of 200 W), while EcoFlow, Jackery and Anker panels ran at 83–88%. So 600 × 0.75 = 450 W, and 1,024 ÷ 450 = 2.3 h.
  • Bigger battery, same 400 W of panel: the R2500 V2 needs 1,920 ÷ 360 = 5.3 h and the Elite 200 V2 needs 2,073.6 ÷ 300 = 6.9 h. They store about twice the energy and need about twice the sun.
  • Wiring on the AC180: in series the pair can deliver 400 W, so 1,152 ÷ 400 = 2.9 h. In parallel the 10 A input holds it to at most 10 A × 24.6 V = 246 W, so 1,152 ÷ 246 = 4.7 h or more.

Why the station's amp limit can matter more than the panel's watts

A station's solar input has three limits: watts, a voltage window and amps. A panel delivers volts × amps, so a panel that works at low voltage can hit the amp limit before it reaches its rated watts.

  • BLUETTI AC180: the manual gives 12–60 V, 10 A max and 500 W max, and allows panels in series or parallel. BLUETTI lists its 200 W SP200L at 24.6 V open-circuit and 10.8 A short-circuit. Two in series make 49.2 V, under the 60 V limit, so all 400 W can reach the battery. Each panel's 10.8 A short-circuit current is just above the 10 A cap, so expect slight clipping in full sun. Two in parallel are held to 246 W at most by the 10 A limit. Wire this kit in series.
  • BLUETTI Elite 200 V2: 12–60 V, 20 A, 1,000 W. Two SP200L panels in parallel can reach at most about 20 A × 24.6 V = 492 W, which is more than 400 W, so wiring makes little difference.
  • ALLPOWERS P1800: the unit's label, photographed by Vantour, reads 11–60 V, 13 A max, and ALLPOWERS rates the XT60 input at 650 W. A 600 W panel gets all its watts through only if it works at 46 V or more (600 ÷ 13 = 46.2). At a lower working voltage the input trims it: at 44 V, for example, to 13 × 44 = 572 W. That is still 1,024 ÷ 572 = 1.8 h, the fastest kit here. The SP037 measured 10.4 A at 35 V (Beyond the Tent), under the 13 A cap.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus: StorageReview's spec table lists two solar ports at 11–60 V, 500 W max. One 400 W panel fits under that.

The panel measurements behind these hours: OutdoorGearLab (Sam Schild, updated 2026-07-13) logged 12 panels side by side for 2 h on a clear day at 6,300 ft. Results included EcoFlow NextGen 220W at 193 W (88%), Jackery SolarSaga 200 at 171 W (86%), Anker SOLIX PS200 at 166 W (83%) and Bluetti 200W Portable at 150 W (75%). Beyond the Tent measured the ALLPOWERS SP037 at about 360 W (10.4 A at 35 V) in late-April sun in Minnesota. CleanTechnica measured EcoFlow's 400 W panel at 310 W, then 360 W after aiming it at the sun.

Not recommended

ALLPOWERS S2000 Pro + SP037 400 W, if fast solar refill is why you're buying. On iallpowers.com on 2026-11-15 it cost $848, against $798 for the P1800 + 600 W kit. It comes with the same 400 W panel as the cheaper P1800 kit but a bigger 1,451.5 Wh battery, so it needs 4.0 h at the panel's measured 360 W instead of 2.3 h. ALLPOWERS's own listing for this kit calls the panel polycrystalline and says a 100 W panel of that type puts out about 55–65 W. At 60%, the refill takes 1,451.5 ÷ 240 = 6.0 h. Buy it for the larger battery and the 2,400 W inverter, not for speed.

EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus + 400 W panel as one purchase. On 2026-11-15, EcoFlow's US store listed no DELTA 3 Plus bundle with a 400 W panel, and the 400 W panel itself showed as sold out at $699.
