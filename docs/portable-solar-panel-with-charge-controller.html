The built-in controller only serves a 12 V battery

A suitcase controller sits between the panel and a bare battery. It sets the charge stages and stops overcharging. ACOPOWER says its included controller "must be used to charge 12V batteries". For a power station it says to "use the solar panel connector cables provided and plug them into the cables that came with your power station". Its page also says a 12 V battery and a power station can charge at the same time. BLUETTI says the same thing from the station side: "your BLUETTI station already manages the solar optimization", so no separate controller is needed.

Renogy's suitcase uses the Voyager 20A PWM. Renogy lists it for Gel, AGM and Flooded batteries with a "Lithium option", and adds: "For lithium batteries, it is essential to manually set the voltage." ACOPOWER's 20A controllers list LiFePO4 among eight presets. On an older Renogy suitcase, The Camping Nerd had to add MC4 connectors between the panel and the Voyager before it could charge a Goal Zero Yeti, and the author notes that the change voids the warranty.

The "20A" on these controllers is a limit on the current into the battery, not extra power. It caps how much panel you can add (360 W at 12 V for ACOPOWER) but doesn't raise what the panel produces. If you mainly want to charge a power station, a kit like $489.99 on bluettipower.com, $465.50 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-30-v2-100w-solar-kit avoids paying for a controller you'd bypass.

Not recommended

VEVOR 200W with 30A PWM, for a LiFePO4 battery. It is the cheapest 200 W option at $129.90. But VEVOR's page names no battery types for its controller, and the panel weighs 26.23 lb net. A lithium owner can't confirm a LiFePO4 charge profile before buying. ACOPOWER's ProteusX lists one for $189.
