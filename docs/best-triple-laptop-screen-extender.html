Which MacBooks can run both side screens

A triple extender only works with the laptop open, because the laptop's own screen is the middle one. Apple's lid-closed trick for adding a second display therefore doesn't help. Apple says the M3 MacBook Air runs one external display with the lid open and a second only with the lid closed. It says the M4 MacBook Air runs up to two external displays.

Why one cable is harder on a Mac. Xebec says Apple does not support MST, the DisplayPort feature that lets one cable drive two screens. That is why Xebec requires its Tri-Screen 3 Mac Adapter, which has DisplayLink chips and needs the DisplayLink driver, for all Mac users, even on an M4. That brings a Mac-ready Tri-Screen 3 to $758, $324.51 more than the Trio 3 Pro. The Trio avoids the MST problem by running one USB-C cable per screen. Simon Jary at Macworld quotes its manual: no driver is needed if the laptop has two DisplayPort-enabled USB-C ports.

Power. Jary found the Trio's screens cannot reach their 300-nit maximum on laptop power alone. A power adapter plugged into the Trio gives full brightness, and that same adapter supplies the up-to-65 W pass-through to the laptop. CHIP.pl's spec table lists 11 W draw in use. The FOPO S788 also needs an external power supply. FOPO's driver-based setup is the documented route for running three added screens on a base M1, M2 or M3 Mac. $699.99 on fopomonitor.com, $630 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/fopo-s788-16-inch-triple-laptop-screen-extender
