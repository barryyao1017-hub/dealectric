What the differences mean

  • EverFrost 2 40L: 288 Wh ÷ 51 h = 5.6 W average; 5.6 W × 24 h ≈ 136 Wh a day. Anker's own claim of 52 h (empty, Eco, 39°F inside, 77°F room) works out to 288 ÷ 52 = 5.5 W, so the measured and claimed figures agree.
  • Koolatron W65: its cord is rated 5 A / 60 W, and with no thermostat it runs at full power the whole time: 60 W × 24 h = 1,440 Wh a day, or 1,440 ÷ 136 ≈ 10.6 times the EverFrost 2. A 288 Wh pack lasts 288 ÷ 60 = 4.8 h. It also cools only 40°F below the air around it: 77 − 40 = 37°F in a 77°F room, but 90 − 40 = 50°F on a 90°F day.
  • BLUETTI MultiCooler: Fstoppers got about 60 h from one 716.8 Wh B70 pack at a 34°F setting. That is 716.8 ÷ 60 = 11.9 W, or about 287 Wh a day. The setting was colder than the 39°F used for the Anker, and no room temperature was given. The $569 store price is for the cooler alone, and the B70 ($399) and the $1,098 bundle are both listed as discontinued.
  • VEVOR T60: rated 0.2 kWh per 24 h, which is 200 ÷ 24 = 8.3 W, so about 288 ÷ 8.3 ≈ 35 h on a 288 Wh pack. This is VEVOR's rating with no test conditions.

How long each one runs on a battery

A compressor cooler switches off once it reaches its set temperature, so its average draw ends up far below its rated watts. Koolatron's FAQ says its standard thermoelectric coolers "do not have an adjustable thermostat and run at maximum cold." It gives a typical draw of 4.5 A (54 W) at 12 V. Koolatron sells a separate battery saver that shuts the cooler off below 11 V, and the same FAQ warns that some vehicles cut power to the 12 V socket when the ignition is off.

The EverFrost 2 40L carries its own battery, so it keeps cooling in a parked car or on a trolley. It has two battery bays. A second 288 Wh pack ($359.99, fits the EverFrost 2 40L and 58L only, not the first-generation EverFrost) doubles storage to 576 Wh, and Anker rates that at 4.3 days under the same Eco test. Solar charging (100 W panel) works only with a battery installed. Freezing costs more: in Max mode, The Gadgeteer's unit went down to 17% after 12 h and reached -4.9°F. That is up to 0.83 × 288 ÷ 12 ≈ 20 W.

Two-zone claims also differ. BODEGACOOLER's $269.99 40 qt dual-compartment model lets you set only the main zone; the smaller zone's temperature is "influenced by the primary zone." On the VEVOR T60, the left zone cannot be set lower than the right one.

Not recommended

The Koolatron W65 is a poor fit for anyone running a cooler from a battery or in a parked car. It draws 60 W with no thermostat, so a 288 Wh pack runs it for under 5 h. On a 90°F day it can only reach about 50°F inside, which is warmer than a refrigerator.
