Installing Unraid or TrueNAS on a UGREEN NASync

  • Boot. Plug in HDMI and a keyboard, press F2 or Ctrl+F12 at power-on for the BIOS, put the USB stick (Unraid) or the NVMe/USB installer (TrueNAS SCALE) first in the boot order, and disable the internal UGOS drive (128 GB SSD on the DXP4800 Plus, 32 GB eMMC on the DXP2800) so it cannot boot UGOS. Debian 13 needed "Force UEFI" to boot from NVMe (Debian wiki).
  • Watchdog. Disable the watchdog timer in the BIOS. With UGOS gone nothing feeds it, and the NAS reboots about every three minutes.
  • What works. Community installs report the Intel NIC (2.5GbE and 10GbE), the iGPU for Plex or Jellyfin transcoding, the M.2 slots and the HDMI output working under Unraid and TrueNAS SCALE on the DXP series. Fan curves and the front LEDs are UGOS features; expect default fan behaviour and dark LEDs unless a community driver covers your model.
  • Warranty. UGREEN's representative told NASCompares (update of 26 March 2024) that the warranty "only covers the hardware" and the policy "will not change" with another OS; the Debian wiki and NASCompares' Unraid guide repeat it. A support-chat reply reported on Reddit in 2024 said the opposite, and forum users mention a warranty sticker over the OS drive. Keep the UGOS drive intact so you can put it back.

What the OS adds to the price

Unraid: Starter $49 (6 attached devices, a year of updates), Unleashed $109 (unlimited devices, a year of updates), Lifetime $249; after the year, updates cost $36 a year or the licence keeps working as is. Unraid 7.3 can boot from an internal drive, but a USB stick is still needed to start a new server. TrueNAS Community Edition is free (25.10 stable as of November 2026). So the DXP4800 Plus comes to $668.99 with Unraid Starter or $619.99 with TrueNAS, before drives.

Why not the others

The TerraMaster F4-424 Pro has the stronger CPU (i3-N305, 8 cores) at about the same money, but its 32 GB of RAM is soldered into one slot, it has no 10GbE, and TerraMaster's own bootloader USB sits inside the case. The Asustor Lockerstor 4 Gen2 is the one maker whose warranty terms openly permit another OS, but it is 2021 silicon (N5105, 4 GB DDR4) and TrueNAS leaves its fan at a fixed speed and its LEDs dark without a community driver. QNAP's TS-464 works and costs $639, but QNAP's documentation disclaims unsupported software. Synology's DS925+ is built for DSM and nothing else.
