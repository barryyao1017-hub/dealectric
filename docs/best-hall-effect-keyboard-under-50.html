What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure here is a maker's own or a named reviewer's, read on 2026-11-21.

  • Step size. Redragon's 0.1 mm step is 10 times the 0.01 mm that Gamakay, MCHOSE (Ace 60 Pro) and Epomaker state (0.1 / 0.01 = 10). The standard Aula WIN68 HE and WIN60 HE are 4 times coarser (0.04 / 0.01 = 4) and the Max versions 2 times (0.02 / 0.01 = 2). The MCHOSE Ace 68 states a step 10 times finer again (0.01 / 0.001 = 10).
  • Settings across the travel. The NS68's 3.5 mm switch travel (GamePadSquire) at 0.01 mm gives 350 positions (3.5 / 0.01). The Redragon K617 HE has 33 steps between 0.1 and 3.4 mm ((3.4 - 0.1) / 0.1), against 330 on the Epomaker HE68 Lite over the same range ((3.4 - 0.1) / 0.01).
  • The "0.125 ms" on the box. At 8,000 Hz a keyboard sends one report every 0.125 ms (1,000 ms / 8,000). Gamakay, Epomaker and Redragon print that same interval as latency, so it does not separate their boards. MCHOSE's 0.1 ms for the Ace 60 Pro is shorter than one report interval.
  • Wireless. At 1,000 Hz a report goes out every 1 ms (1,000 ms / 1,000), 8 times the wired interval (1 / 0.125 = 8). That is the NS68 Tri-mode off the cable, per Crafting Worlds and GamePadSquire.
  • Scan rate. Epomaker's 128 kHz is one scan every 0.008 ms (1,000 / 128,000). The NS68's 32 kHz (GamePadSquire) is one every 0.031 ms (1,000 / 32,000). The 0.023 ms gap is under a fifth of one 0.125 ms report (0.023 / 0.125 = 18%).
  • Battery. GamePadSquire lists the NS68 Tri-mode at 4,000 mAh and about 40 h at the dimmest backlight: 10 days at 4 h a day (40 / 4).

The rapid trigger figures, on one basis

Each maker gives the number a different name. Gamakay writes "0.01mm RT Accuracy" and "0.01mm resolution accuracy". MCHOSE writes "0.01mm Precision Rapid Trigger" for the Ace 60 Pro and "Up to 0.001 mm RT Accuracy" for the Ace 68, and on its Ace 68 V2 listing labels the same kind of figure "Adjustment Precision". Epomaker writes "per 0.01mm adjustable RT travel". Redragon says the actuation point "can be 0.1mm precision adjusted between 0.1-3.4 mm". Aula says the Max has "a keystroke accuracy of up to 0.02mm, surpassing the standard version's 0.04mm". In Epomaker's, Redragon's and MCHOSE's wording the number is the smallest change the software lets you set. It is not a lab measurement of how repeatably the sensor registers that distance.

Two named tests sit behind this table. RTINGS bench-tested the MCHOSE Ace 60 Pro (KTEK magnetic switches, review updated 2026-01-06) and wrote: "we have confirmed that the keyboard fully utilizes its 8,000Hz polling rate without any latency-related bottlenecks". It also calls typing noise "fairly loud". HLPlanet's Marcus Richardson measured the MCHOSE Ace 68 at 0.3 ms with OSLTT, 3.75 times the "up to 0.08 ms" MCHOSE states (0.3 / 0.08). The NS68's figures are Gamakay's own plus two reviewers' spec readings, so this page makes no measured-latency claim for it.

On wireless, Crafting Worlds (Tristan) writes that the NS68 runs "up to 8000Hz in wired mode" and that "Wireless is limited to 1000Hz"; GamePadSquire lists the same two rates. Aula's own note on the MINI60 HE reads: "MINI60 HE Max features 8K polling in USB/2.4Ghz mode, while MINI60 HE Standard only features 8K polling in wired mode". HLPlanet's general advice is that "HE keyboards are best used wired to have the best latency" and that a 2.4 GHz dongle "typically adds 20-100% latency". For competitive play on either wireless board, plug in the cable.

Not recommended

Redragon FIZZ K617 HE for rapid trigger. Its 0.1 mm step is the coarsest here, and Redragon's own page warns that "the actual effect of the 0.1-0.2 mm actuation point may be affected by the normal tolerances of the components". Redragon also says its switches are "incompatible with other brands' HE switches". At $39.99 it cost $2.59 more than the MCHOSE Ace 60 Pro on 2026-11-21 (39.99 - 37.40) for a step 10 times coarser.

A standard Aula WIN60 HE or WIN68 HE bought for 0.02 mm. Aula sells the standard and Max versions on one listing (WIN60 HE Series at $33.99, WIN68 HE Series at $42.49 on 2026-11-21). The 0.02 mm figure belongs to the Max; the standard version is 0.04 mm in Aula's own words. Check which version is in the cart before paying.
