What the differences mean

  • AT-LP70XBT over AT-LP60XBT: $299 − $259 = $40 more buys the VM95 cartridge, a J-shaped tonearm and RCA outputs.
  • Victrola Hi-Res Onyx under the AT-LP70XBT: $299 − $199.99 = $99.01 less. The Onyx already comes with the elliptical VM95E. To get the same stylus on the AT-LP70XBT you pay $299 + $52 = $351. The trade-off is that you lower the tonearm by hand on the Onyx.
  • AT-LP3XBT over AT-LP70XBT: $379 − $299 = $80 more for a removable headshell, an adjustable counterweight and anti-skate.
  • Sony PS-LX3BT over AT-LP70XBT: $399.99 − $299 = $100.99 more for a USB output, but it keeps a 3600L-type stylus.
  • Stylus running cost on the AT-LP70XBT: the stock AT-VMN95C costs $32, and Audio-Technica says to replace it after 300 hours. $32 ÷ 300 h ≈ 10.7¢ per hour of play.

Setup note: to pair the AT-LP70XBT with Bluetooth speakers or headphones, Audio-Technica's manual says to set the pre-amplifier selector to LINE and keep the speaker or headphones within 1 m (3.3 ft). Pairing mode ends after about 120 seconds.

Why the cartridge decides it

Bluetooth only carries what the stylus reads from the groove, and the stylus is the part that wears out. Every VM95-series cartridge uses the same body, so you can pull out the stylus by hand and fit another one from the series. On the AT-LP70XBT that means moving from the stock AT-VMN95C conical ($32) to the AT-VMN95E elliptical ($52) or the AT-VMN95EN nude elliptical ($109). Turntable Lab lists all three as fitting the AT-LP70X. The Victrola Hi-Res Onyx and AT-LP3XBT use the same VM95 body.

The AT-LP60XBT and Sony PS-LX3BT use 3600L-type styli. Turntable Lab's fit list puts the ATN3600LE elliptical ($46) at the top of that line for both. Sony's own replacement styli for the PS-LX3BT are listed at Turntable Lab for $74.95 and $79.95, and both were out of stock on 2026-11-13. Neither deck has a detachable headshell, so you can't fit a different cartridge.

Not recommended

JBL Spinner BT. At $439.95 it costs $140.95 more than the AT-LP70XBT ($439.95 − $299). It comes with an Audio-Technica AT3600L, from the same 3600L line as the stylus on the $259 AT-LP60XBT, so the extra money doesn't buy a better cartridge. Lasse Svendsen at LB Tech Reviews measured his review unit at 33.15 rpm (−0.55%) with 0.75% wow and flutter, and wrote that "you can hear a piano note fluctuate in pitch." Its adjustable arm and removable headshell only pay off once you buy a separate cartridge.
