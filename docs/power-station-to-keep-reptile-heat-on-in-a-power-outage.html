What the differences mean

Every runtime above is usable energy ÷ (heater watts + the station's idle draw). With a heat mat that draws 24 W, the station's own draw is close to the heater's, so it decides the hours as much as the battery size does.

  • Elite 200 V2 vs DELTA 3 Max, both about 2 kWh: 1,910 ÷ (24 + 10) ≈ 56 h, against (2,048 × 0.79) ÷ (24 + 27) ≈ 32 h. That is about 24 more hours on a mat for $20 more.
  • How much of the battery heats nothing: Jackery 1000 v2: 18 ÷ (24 + 18) ≈ 43%. Elite 200 V2: 10 ÷ (24 + 10) ≈ 29%.
  • Ceramic heat emitter on a thermostat: 100 W at 50% duty averages 50 W. Elite 200 V2: 1,910 ÷ (50 + 10) ≈ 32 h. AC180: 1,030 ÷ (50 + 15) ≈ 16 h.
  • Emitter or heat lamp running nonstop: Elite 200 V2: 1,910 ÷ (100 + 10) ≈ 17 h.
  • Cost per hour of mat heat (maker stores, 2026-11-08): Elite 200 V2: $899 ÷ 56 h ≈ $16 per hour. DELTA 3 Max: $879 ÷ 32 h ≈ $27 per hour.

Where the reviewers state a test load, the usable-energy figures were measured at 350 W (AC180) to 1 kW (C1000 Gen 2). Inverters lose a larger share at 24–50 W, so treat these hours as upper limits and keep a reserve.

Idle draw and auto-off: the two settings that decide reptile heat runtime

A power station uses power for itself whenever its AC outlet is on, whether or not anything is drawing from it. The measured figures: The Gadgeteer found about 10 W on the Elite 200 V2 with AC and DC on, and The Solar Lab found 9.5 W. The Solar Lab calculated about 27 W for the DELTA 3 Max and about 16 W for the Elite 100 V2. The Technology Man saw an Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 fall from 100% to 80% in 12 h with no load, which is about 17 W. He saw an Elite 30 V2 fall from 100% to 19% in 12 h with AC on, about 19 W. BLUETTI's AC180 manual gives about 15 W, and Jackery lists 18 W standby for the Explorer 1000 v2.

The modes that cut idle draw can also cut the heat. When a thermostat switches a heater off, the outlet's load drops to almost nothing, and that is the condition auto-off modes look for. Before an outage:

  • BLUETTI (AC180, Elite series): turn ECO mode off for AC. The AC180 manual says ECO turns AC off when the load stays below a threshold you set (15–30 W) for 1–4 hours, and a 24 W mat falls inside that range. StorageReview notes that the Elite 200 V2 has ECO settings for its AC and DC outputs.
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: turn Smart AC Output Mode off. The Technology Man reports that by default it switches the inverter off after 15 minutes below 20 W.
  • Jackery Explorer 1000 v2: Jackery says Energy Saving Mode can be turned off and auto power-off can be set in the app.
  • EcoFlow: for the DELTA 3, EcoFlow says an intermittent load can trigger automatic AC shutdown and recommends setting AC timeout to Never. Set the same option on the DELTA 3 Max.

None of these stations has a 0 ms UPS. Jackery says so outright for the 1000 v2 (switchover within 20 ms). Plug the thermostat and heater in through the station's AC outlet, pull the wall plug, and check that the thermostat keeps its setting and the heater comes back on. Not recommended

BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 for an overnight outage. The Technology Man measured 231 Wh usable on AC and about 19 W idle with Eco off. That is 231 ÷ (24 + 19) ≈ 5.4 h on a 24 W mat and about 3 h with a 100 W emitter at half duty. It is fine for a short blackout and one small mat, but it won't last a winter night.
