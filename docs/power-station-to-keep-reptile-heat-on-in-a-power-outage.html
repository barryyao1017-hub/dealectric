How the runtimes are worked out

Usable energy is about 85% of the rating after inverter losses, and the station's own draw (roughly 5–20 W with AC on) counts too. A 24 W heat mat plus about 15 W of station draw is about 40 W: 288 Wh lasts about 6–8 hours on that, 2,073.6 Wh about two days. A 100 W ceramic heat emitter on a thermostat at half duty averages about 50 W, plus the station's draw: about a day from 2,073.6 Wh (all arithmetic; we found no measured reptile run on a power station).

First, without any power

Most pet reptiles handle a short outage in a room in the 60s °F: one vet's guidance for bearded dragons is about a day at 65 °F, and ball python keepers report two-day outages in the low 60s without harm. Cover the enclosure with blankets, move small animals to an insulated container with hand warmers wrapped in cloth, and keep the room door shut. Battery heat is for longer outages and colder rooms.

Mat or lamp on battery

Run the heat mat and switch the basking lamp off: a 100–150 W lamp drains a small battery in a few hours, while a mat on a thermostat lasts all night. Keep the thermostat in line; a heater running flat out on a battery is both wasteful and a burn risk.
