What the differences mean

Two figures decide a travel tripod for a DSLR. The first is how much rated load each pound of tripod gives you. The second is the heaviest camera-plus-lens kit it should carry if you keep its rating at least twice the kit, which is Finding the Universe's rule.

  • Load per pound: the Manfrotto Befree Advanced AS Carbon gives 19.84 ÷ 2.8 = 7.1 and the Peak Design Travel Tripod Carbon 20 ÷ 2.81 = 7.1. The Peak Design Aluminum gives 20 ÷ 3.44 = 5.8, the Befree Advanced AS lever aluminum 19.84 ÷ 3.55 = 5.6, the SIRUI Traveler 7C 17.6 ÷ 3.46 = 5.1 and the Traveler 5C 8.8 ÷ 1.87 = 4.7.
  • Largest kit at 2× is the rating ÷ 2: 10 lb for Peak Design, 9.9 lb for the Befree Advanced AS, 8.8 lb for the Traveler 7C and 7A and 4.4 lb for the Traveler 5C and 5A.
  • Price per pound of rated load: Traveler 7A 119.90 ÷ 17.6 = 6.81 dollars, Traveler 7C 149.90 ÷ 17.6 = 8.52, Traveler 5C 109.90 ÷ 8.8 = 12.49 and Peak Design 399.95 ÷ 20 = 20.00.

Rated load per pound, and what each rating measures

Manfrotto rates the Befree Advanced AS at 9 kg (19.84 lb) as a Safety Payload under UNI/PdR 105:2021. In Manfrotto's description, a support loaded to that figure “is never jeopardised, no matter the position”: the head must not slip out of its seat in any position, and the leg sections, centre column and spider must not collapse. Peak Design states a “20 pound weight capacity” and says the tripod is built for full-frame DSLRs and telephoto lenses, but it names no test method. SIRUI lists its 4 kg and 8 kg figures as max load, also with no method. Manfrotto says a plain max payload is “sometimes not taking safety parameters into account”, so treat the Peak Design and SIRUI figures as the less conservative kind. The 7.1 tie between the Befree Advanced AS Carbon and the Peak Design Carbon therefore favours the Manfrotto.

Here is what the 2× rule leaves for a lens on a real body. Nikon lists the D850 at 915 g (2.02 lb). Under the Traveler 5C's 4.4 lb kit limit that leaves 4.4 − 2.02 = 2.38 lb of lens. Under the Traveler 7C's 8.8 lb limit it leaves 8.8 − 2.02 = 6.78 lb.

Hands-on steadiness points the same way. In GearLab's tests (sideways push, wind and long exposures), Zach Lovell scored stability at 7.4/10 for the Manfrotto Befree Advanced, 7.0 for the Peak Design Travel Tripod and 5.0 for the SIRUI Traveler 5C. GearLab's spec table lists the Befree Advanced it tested as aluminum and plastic and the Peak Design as aluminum.

On plates, Manfrotto says the AS head will “mount any gear with the full Arca-compatible attachment”, which includes L brackets and the collar foot of a telephoto lens. Check for the AS carbon model number, MKBFRTC4FB-BH.

Not recommended

The SIRUI Traveler 5C is not a pick for a full-frame DSLR with a zoom. Its 8.8 lb rating sets a 4.4 lb kit limit, it reaches 65.55 − 54.3 = 11.25 in less height than the Traveler 7C, and GearLab's Zach Lovell found it “quite wobly when fully extended”.
