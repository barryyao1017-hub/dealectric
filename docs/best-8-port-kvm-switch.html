Hotkeys on an 8-port KVM: which keyboard port does the switching

With 8 computers, hotkeys are the quick way to move between them. On TESmart's switches they only work from one place. TESmart's support article says the switch "is designed to support the hotkey function only on these dedicated ports", meaning the keyboard and mouse ports. TESmart's January 2026 hotkey guide adds that standard USB ports "operate in passthrough mode and do not participate in hotkey detection". The trigger keys are Scroll Lock and Right-Ctrl, pressed twice and then followed by the port number.

The catch. TESmart lists keyboards that customers report fail on those dedicated ports: the Dell KB813 and similar keyboards with a card slot, the Logitech MX series, Roccat, and certain Microsoft models. Its guide also flags wireless, Bluetooth and macro-enabled keyboards. TESmart's fix is to plug the keyboard into one of the USB 2.0/3.0 hub ports, which "mimic a direct connection to the computer". The keyboard then types normally, but it can no longer switch computers.

What you switch with instead. The P23, L23 and EB23 all list an IR remote, front-panel buttons, an RS232 port and LAN (TCP/IP) control. RS232 or LAN lets a script or control system pick the port. The M24 lists front-panel buttons, mouse-wheel double-click and an IR remote, plus a LAN port for TCP/IP control. If you plan to keep a Logitech MX or Roccat keyboard, test it in the dedicated port first. If it fails there, plan on the remote or RS232/LAN for switching. The P23's hub is USB 3.0, and a TESmart blog post passes on USB-IF's warning that USB 3.0 can interfere with 2.4 GHz wireless keyboard and mouse receivers. TESmart suggests moving USB 3.0 devices to a port farther from the wireless receiver.

Not recommended

TESmart HKS801-EB23. At $229 it costs $85.01 more than the HKS801-L23 ($229 − $143.99). Its title still says 4K 30 Hz, its listing says USB 2.0, and its 4K 60 Hz claim is qualified with "on compatible devices". For 4K 30 Hz, buy the L23. For 4K 60 Hz, buy the P23.

StarTech R8AD122-KVM-SWITCH for a desk setup. Its spec sheet says "the KVM switch does not include cables". For 8 computers that means buying 8 DisplayPort and 8 USB cables on top of the switch, and its shared hub is USB 2.0. It makes sense only where every computer and the monitor use DisplayPort.
