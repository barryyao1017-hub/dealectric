What the differences mean

  • Energy two charges need at the outlet: a typical 48 V e-bike charger is rated at 85% efficiency or better, so one 700 Wh charge takes 700 ÷ 0.85 = 824 Wh from the station, and two take 1,647 Wh.
  • Hours the inverter actually works: a Bosch 4A charger is rated 36 V × 4 A, about 144 W out, so each 700 Wh charge takes 700 ÷ 144 = 4.9 h and two take about 10 h. That leaves about 38 of a 48-hour weekend with the AC on and nothing plugged in.
  • What 38 idle hours cost: S2000 6 W × 38 = 228 Wh; Elite 200 V2 9.5 W × 38 = 361 Wh; Elite 300 19 W × 38 = 722 Wh; DELTA 3 Max 27 W × 38 = 1,026 Wh.
  • Spare with AC off between charges: measured output minus 1,647 Wh, e.g. S2000 1,900 − 1,647 = 253 Wh; Elite 200 V2 1,910 − 1,647 = 263 Wh; Elite 300 2,650 − 1,647 = 1,003 Wh.

Idle draw: why the AC switch decides the second charge

A power station's inverter draws power whenever its AC output is on, even with nothing plugged in. On a 2 kWh station the spare after two 700 Wh charges is only about 250 Wh, so a weekend of idling can use up the second charge before you plug the bike in.

  • Anker SOLIX S2000: The Solar Lab measured about 6 W with the inverter on and 2 W with it off. Notebookcheck saw just over 10% of the battery gone per 24 hours with AC enabled (about 8 W). Its about 1,900 Wh was measured at a 250 W load, close to what an e-bike charger draws.
  • BLUETTI Elite 200 V2: The Solar Lab measured 9.5 W idle and The Gadgeteer about 10 W with AC and DC left on. StorageReview notes that ECO mode can be switched on for the AC or DC outputs to cut idle use. StorageReview measured 1,910 Wh but didn't state the test load.
  • BLUETTI Elite 300: The Technology Man measured the battery falling from 100% to 90% in 16 hours with AC on and Eco mode off (about 19 W). Its extra kilowatt-hour absorbs that: at a 200 W load Notebookcheck drew about 2,650 Wh.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max: The Solar Lab measured about 27 W idle and estimated the unit would run flat in roughly three days doing nothing.

The habit that makes a 2 kWh station work: turn the AC output on only while the bike charges, or turn on the station's ECO / AC auto-off. With the AC off, each of the 2 kWh stations above except the DELTA 3 Max keeps about 250 Wh in hand.

Not recommended

EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max for two charges. Its rated 2,048 Wh looks like enough, but The Solar Lab measured 79% efficiency (about 1,618 Wh, with the method not stated), which is 29 Wh short of two charges even with the AC off between them. At about 27 W idle, a weekend with the AC left on costs about 1 kWh more, leaving it closer to one charge than two.
