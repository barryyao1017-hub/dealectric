What the differences mean

Every kit is worked out the same way: panel watts × 4 peak sun hours × 0.75. The 0.75 is Dealectric's planning allowance for wiring, heat, inverter and battery losses. Sun Gold Power's own figure of 14.4 kWh a day for the SGR-8K10E is 3,600 W × 4 h with nothing taken off. Days of battery means storage ÷ daily solar. If you keep 20% of the battery in reserve, multiply it by 0.8. Prices are the store's on 2026-11-13.

  • SGR-8K10E: 3,600 W × 4 h × 0.75 = 10.8 kWh/day. 10.24 kWh ÷ 10.8 = 0.95 day. $4,949 ÷ 10.8 = $458 per daily kWh.

Size by daily kWh, then check the panel strings

The inverter's watts set how much can run at once. The daily kWh decides whether the battery is full again by evening. That is why the table leads with daily solar after losses rather than inverter size. For the SGR-8K10E, the 3,600 W of panels use only 33% of the inverter's 11,000 W PV rating (3,600 ÷ 11,000), so you can add panels later without changing the inverter.

Wiring is the next thing to check. The 8 kW inverter's manual says the open-circuit voltage of panels in series must stay below 500 V. The store lists both the 8 kW and 10 kW inverters at 500 V DC open-circuit, the 10 kW at each of its two inputs. The table below uses the SGN-450-BDG5 450 W panel's figures: Voc 35.03 V, Vmp 30.08 V, Isc 16.05 A.

One string of 8 leaves 219.8 V below the 500 V limit (500 − 280.2), so there is room for panel voltage to rise in cold weather. Twelve in series leaves only 79.6 V (500 − 420.4), so check the temperature coefficient on the panel label before wiring the SGR-10K20E as a single string. Two strings of 4 on one input halve the working voltage and double the current to 32.1 A. Compare both against the MPPT voltage window and per-input current limit in your inverter's manual before you use that layout. Each kit ships with one 25 A PV breaker and one 50 ft PV extension set, so using the inverter's second input means buying a second breaker and cable set.

What you still buy separately

The store's lists of what comes in these kits include Z-brackets, a DC breaker, a PV breaker and battery cables. They name no roof or ground racking, grounding hardware, AC disconnect or load center. Budget for those on top of the kit price. Plan for the panel wiring to the inverter and the AC side to be inspected under your local electrical code.
