What the differences mean

A conventional 230 V submersible pump asks for several times its running current for the instant it starts. Franklin Electric's AIM manual lists that locked-rotor current for its 4-inch motors, here as tabulated by Willrun (The Home Battery Report lists the same 2-wire figures): 1/2 HP 23.0 A (3-wire) or 32.2 A (2-wire); 3/4 HP 34.2 A or 40.7 A; 1 HP 41.8 A or 48.7 A; 1.5 HP 51.4 A or 66.2 A. The generator has to deliver that at 240 V or the pump never turns.

  • Surge watts into amps: surge W ÷ 240 V. Jackery 14,400 ÷ 240 = 60 A. EcoFlow and Anker S5000 8,000 ÷ 240 = 33.3 A. OSCAL 7,200 ÷ 240 = 30 A. BLUETTI Apex 300 has no surge rating, so 3,840 ÷ 240 = 16 A continuous is all its page supports.
  • The F3800 Plus is rated differently: Anker's product page lists only 6,000 W and 25 A on the L14-30R outlet. The 80 A figure is the locked-rotor rating in Anker's user guide, as quoted by The Home Battery Report. 80 ÷ 66.2 = 1.21× on a 1.5 HP 2-wire motor.
  • Margins: Jackery 60 ÷ 48.7 = 1.23× on a 1 HP 2-wire. DELTA Pro 3 and S5000 33.3 ÷ 32.2 = 1.04× on a 1/2 HP 2-wire, and 33.3 ÷ 41.8 = 0.80× on a 1 HP 3-wire. OSCAL 30 ÷ 23.0 = 1.30× on a 1/2 HP 3-wire.
  • Gallons per charge: battery Wh × 0.85 ÷ Wh per gallon. F3800 Plus: 3,840 × 0.85 = 3,264 Wh; 3,264 ÷ 2.02 = about 1,620 gallons with a 1 HP pump, 3,264 ÷ 1.117 = about 2,920 gallons with a 1/2 HP pump. The 0.85 usable share is Willrun's allowance.

These are paper comparisons of published ratings, not bench tests, and none of these makers' pages says how long the surge is held. A margin near 1.0× is a maybe.

How much sun and battery a well pump needs

Starting the pump is the hard part. Keeping it supplied from panels is easy, because a pump runs for minutes a day.

  • Energy per gallon: Franklin's full-load figure for a 1 HP motor is 1,210 W. At 10 gallons per minute (600 gallons an hour, the flow in Willrun's household duty cycle), 1,210 ÷ 600 = 2.02 Wh per gallon. A 3/4 HP motor: 940 ÷ 600 = 1.57 Wh. A 1/2 HP motor: 670 ÷ 600 = 1.12 Wh. A pump that delivers fewer gallons per minute uses proportionally more per gallon.
  • A 300-gallon day, 1 HP: 300 ÷ 10 = 30 minutes of pumping. 1,210 W × 0.5 h = 605 Wh, plus the inverter left on all day at 15 W × 24 h = 360 Wh (Willrun's allowance; The Solar Lab measured about 20 W idle on the Apex 300), total 965 Wh.
  • Panel hours: 965 ÷ 400 W = 2.4 hours of rated output from one 400 W panel, or 965 ÷ 500 = 1.9 hours from a 500 W panel. For a 1/2 HP pump: 335 + 360 = 695 Wh, 695 ÷ 400 = 1.7 hours. Panels rarely reach their rated watts, so read these as minimums.
  • Gallons per panel-hour: 400 Wh ÷ 2.02 = about 198 gallons for each hour a 400 W panel holds its rating, with a 1 HP pump.

Charging and pumping at once. Anker states: "The 240V output cannot be used while the device is charging via 120V AC." A 240 V pump is therefore off while an F3800 Plus tops up from a 120 V wall outlet or a 120 V gas generator. Anker's page states that limit for 120 V AC charging only, which makes panels the practical way to refill it while the well stays live.

Measured capacity. OutdoorGearLab measured 3,790 Wh from the DELTA Pro 3's rated 4,096 Wh (92.5%); 3,790 ÷ 1.117 = about 3,390 gallons with a 1/2 HP pump, more than the 85% estimate in the table. The original Anker SOLIX F3800, not the Plus, delivered 2,980 Wh for StorageReview and 2,905 Wh at a steady 260 W for Notebookcheck, 76 to 78% of its 3,840 Wh. If the Plus behaves the same, 2,905 ÷ 2.02 = about 1,440 gallons with a 1 HP pump instead of 1,620.

One hands-on start. Robb Sutton reports that his DELTA Pro 3 started a 1 HP 240 V submersible pump without a soft starter. He does not name the pump or say how the start draw was measured, so it is one pass on one well, against a paper margin of 0.68× to 0.80× for Franklin 1 HP motors.

Not recommended for a 240 V well pump

BLUETTI Apex 300. BLUETTI US was taking orders on Nov 11, 2026, with shipping from early December. Its page publishes 3,840 W of output and a 7,680 W figure called Lifting Power. The Home Battery Report notes BLUETTI does not present that as a motor surge rating. On the published numbers it has 16 A at 240 V against 23.0 A for the smallest Franklin 230 V motor.

A single Growatt HELIOS 3600. Growatt's listing gives one unit a 240 V outlet limited to 2,200 W, and 2,200 ÷ 240 = 9.2 A against 23.0 A for the smallest Franklin 230 V motor. It says 240 V whole-home power is supported when two HELIOS 3600 units are paralleled.
