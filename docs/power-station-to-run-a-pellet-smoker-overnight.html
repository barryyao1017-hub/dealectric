What the differences mean

A pellet smoker draws most of its power in the first few minutes, then very little. Traeger rates its D2 grills at 110 W but lists 0.08–0.17 A once they reach temperature (0.08 A × 120 V ≈ 10 W; 0.17 A × 120 V ≈ 20 W). Its non-WiFIRE grills are rated 300 W and drop to 0.4–0.8 A, which is 48–96 W. Mike Sokol (RVelectricity) measured a Z Grills smoker running on a Jackery Explorer 1000. It drew about 194 W at ignition for several minutes, then 35 W, and used 210 Wh over a 6-hour rib cook (210 Wh ÷ 6 h = 35 W).

  • Smoker energy for a 12-hour night: a D2 Traeger uses 10–20 W × 12 h ≈ 115–245 Wh, a 35 W smoker uses 35 W × 12 h = 420 Wh, and a non-WiFIRE Traeger uses 48–96 W × 12 h = 576–1,152 Wh.
  • The station's inverter draws power all night as well. The Technology Man measured about 17 W on the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: 20% of 1,024 Wh was gone after 12 hours with nothing plugged in, so 17 W × 12 h ≈ 205 Wh. New Atlas gives about 15 W for the AC180 (180 Wh a night) and about 10 W for the Elite 100 V2. The Gadgeteer reads 10 W on the Elite 200 V2 (120 Wh a night).
  • Elite 200 V2 with a 96 W smoker: (96 + 10 W) × 12 h = 1,272 Wh. The Gadgeteer drew about 2,000 Wh from it, so 2,000 ÷ 1,272 ≈ 1.6 nights. With a 35 W smoker: (35 + 10) × 12 = 540 Wh, so 2,000 ÷ 540 ≈ 3.7 nights.
  • AC180 with a 96 W smoker: (96 + 15) × 12 = 1,332 Wh, which is more than its 1,152 Wh battery. With a 35 W smoker: (35 + 15) × 12 = 600 Wh, about half the battery.
  • Anker C1000 Gen 2: (35 + 17) × 12 = 624 Wh fits within the 907 Wh The Technology Man drew from it. (96 + 17) × 12 = 1,356 Wh does not fit.
  • Elite 100 V2: New Atlas works out 850–900 Wh usable. (35 + 10) × 12 = 540 Wh fits; (96 + 10) × 12 = 1,272 Wh does not.
  • Price per watt-hour on BLUETTI's US store today: AC180 $499 ÷ 1,152 Wh ≈ $0.43; Elite 200 V2 $899 ÷ 2,073.6 Wh ≈ $0.43. The rate is the same, and the Elite 200 V2 holds about 1.8 times as much.
  • Igniter watts: every station here has 1,500 W or more of AC output. That is far above the 194 W Sokol measured at ignition and Traeger's 300 W rating.

Why a power station can switch a pellet smoker off overnight

Power stations have an eco or energy-saving mode that turns the AC inverter off when the load is small, so a forgotten inverter doesn't drain the battery. A pellet smoker holding temperature can look like that small load. When the outlet goes off, the auger stops feeding pellets and the fan stops. Traeger lists a power outage as a cause of a WiFIRE grill shutting off mid-cook, and the cook stays stopped until someone restarts it. Here is the rule on each station:

  • BLUETTI AC180: in the manual, ECO mode turns AC off when the load stays under a threshold you set between 15 and 30 W for 1, 2, 3 or 4 hours. A D2 Traeger at 10–20 W sits at or under every setting, so turn AC ECO off in Setting mode. $499 on bluettipower.com, $474.05 through this link with code DEALECTRIC: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-ac180
  • BLUETTI Elite 200 V2: APH Networks says ECO shuts it down after a period with little or no load. The reviews we read give no threshold, so turn ECO off in the app rather than trying to tune it. $899 on bluettipower.com, $854.05 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-200-v2
  • BLUETTI Elite 100 V2: New Atlas (Noel McKeegan) found the AC eco threshold adjustable from 10 to 40 W, and says Eco has to be off for small loads to keep running.
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: The Technology Man reports that by default it turns its inverter off after 15 minutes below 20 W. He turned Smart AC Output off to stop it.
  • Jackery Explorer 1000 v2: the manual says Energy Saving Mode is on by default. It turns all outputs off after 12 hours when nothing is connected or the load is at or below a set value, so a cook longer than 12 hours on a low-draw grill can hit it. Its discharge range is 14–113 °F, which covers a frosty night.

Non-WiFIRE Traegers at 48–96 W sit above all of these thresholds, but turning the setting off costs nothing. Before your first overnight cook, run the smoker from the station for an evening with the setting off and check that the outlet is still on at the end.

Not recommended

The BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 for an overnight cook. BLUETTI lists it at 288 Wh with 4.5 W standby. The 35 W smoker Sokol measured needs 420 Wh for 12 hours before any inverter loss (35 W × 12 h). At 35 + 4.5 W, the battery lasts about 288 ÷ 39.5 ≈ 7.3 hours. That is enough for an evening cook, not a whole night.
