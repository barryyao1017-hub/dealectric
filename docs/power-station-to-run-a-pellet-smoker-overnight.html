Watt-hours for one cook

RVelectricity measured a Z Grills pellet grill on a Jackery Explorer 1000: about 194 W at start-up, 35 W while cooking and 210 Wh over 6 hours. At 35–60 W for 12 hours plus the igniter, an overnight cook is about 450–750 Wh (arithmetic); cold or windy nights push the fan and auger toward the top of that range. Add the station's own inverter draw, roughly 10–20 W.

The igniter

The hot rod pulls 200–400 W for a few minutes at start-up (forum owners; Recteq's support pointed to a 600 W inverter). Any station above 600 W continuous starts it. Re-lighting after a flame-out repeats the start-up.

Inverter type

Pellet controllers are electronics with fans and motors: run them on a pure sine wave inverter, which every station in this table has. Traeger itself sells only a 400 W (900 W peak) 12 V inverter for its grills, a hint at how small the running load is.
