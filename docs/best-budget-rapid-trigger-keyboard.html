What the differences mean

  • Step. Redragon states 0.1 mm steps for the K617 HE and K673 HE; Gamakay, MCHOSE and Epomaker state 0.01 mm. 0.1 ÷ 0.01 = 10, so the Redragon step is ten times coarser. Redragon adds its own warning: "The actual effect of the 0.1-0.2 mm actuation point may be affected by the normal tolerances of the components."
  • Settings you can pick. NS68: (3.7 − 0.01) ÷ 0.01 + 1 = 370 actuation points. Ace 60 Pro and HE68 Lite: (3.4 − 0.1) ÷ 0.01 + 1 = 331. K617 HE: (3.4 − 0.1) ÷ 0.1 + 1 = 34.
  • Report interval. 1 s ÷ 8,000 = 0.125 ms on the cable. Gamakay rates the tri-mode NS68 at 1,000 Hz in wireless: 1 s ÷ 1,000 = 1 ms, eight times longer.

What 0.01 mm and 0.125 ms on the box mean

Rapid trigger, in the words of Wooting, which introduced it, is "dynamically activating and deactivating the key based on travel distance instead of a fixed point"; Wooting describes its own as settable "as sensitive as 0.1mm". The step is how finely the firmware lets you set that travel. It is a setting, and a maker's rating: nobody we read has measured whether any board in the table resolves 0.01 mm at the sensor. What the figure does tell you is how the makers differ on paper, and here they differ by a factor of ten.

The 0.125 ms line is arithmetic. Redragon ties it to polling in its own sentence ("up to 8x faster (0.125ms lowest latency)"), and Epomaker writes "8000Hz polling with a 0.125ms response". Epomaker prints the same 0.125 ms on its HE80, which it rates at a 32,000 Hz scan rate against the HE68 Lite's 128,000 Hz, so the number follows the polling rate and nothing else. MCHOSE's 0.1 ms and the 0.06 ms that Gamakay prints for the NS75 (and in an image on the NS68 page) are shorter than one 8,000 Hz report interval, so they cannot be the time for a key press to reach the PC; neither maker gives a test method.

The measurements that exist:

  • MCHOSE Ace 60 Pro, RTINGS. RTINGS bought a black unit with KTEK switches and wrote: "we have confirmed that the keyboard fully utilizes its 8,000Hz polling rate without any latency-related bottlenecks." It describes single-key and multi-key latency as remarkably low; its millisecond figures are for members only. It is the one board in the table for which we read a lab review.
  • Gamakay NS68 wired, TechXReviews. Moussa Obscur checked the wired board in Keyboard Inspector in May 2025 and found it ran at or very close to 8,000 Hz. He found no polling-rate selector in the web driver, rapid trigger sensitivity adjustable from 0.01 mm to 2 mm with press and release set separately, and top and bottom dead zones adjustable down to 0.0 mm. That is a software check, not a latency measurement.
  • Across rapid trigger boards, RTINGS. In its 2025 research RTINGS timed key release on the boards it had tested and found a range of about 1 ms (Geonworks Venom 60 HE) to about 18 ms (REALFORCE GX1), and concluded "the differences are too small to meaningfully impact performance, even at a highly competitive level". It also recorded one outlier, the Akko MOD007-HE PC at 45.7 ms on release, so a poor implementation is possible on a board no lab has tested.

Taken together: among boards with the same stated step and polling rate, layout, connection and price are what is left to choose on. That is why the wired NS68 leads here, and why the Ace 60 Pro is the alternative if a lab result matters more to you than arrow keys. $36.79: https://dealectric.com/go/gamakay-ns68-wired

Not recommended

  • The tri-mode NS68 for wireless play at 8,000 Hz. The listing title says "8K Polling Rate"; the spec line says "8K Hz Polling Rate (Wired) / 1K Hz (Wireless)". If you will always play on the cable, the wired version does the same job. Gamakay's NS75 page states 8,000 Hz for wired use and gives no wireless polling figure, so treat it the same way.
  • The Redragon K617 HE if you plan to run 0.1-0.2 mm actuation. That is the range Redragon itself says component tolerances may affect, and 0.1 mm is also the smallest step it offers. Redragon lists the board as not hot-swappable and "incompatible with other brands' HE switches".
