Adobe's rules

Adobe's Lightroom Classic FAQ says catalogs must be stored on the computer or a locally connected drive, never on a network drive or in Creative Cloud; original images may live on the computer, an external drive or a networked drive. Smart Previews are lossy DNGs built at import or from Library → Previews, stored beside the catalog as "Catalog Smart Previews.lrdata"; Adobe's own example is 500 RAW files, 14 GB of originals and about 400 MB of previews, and edits sync back to the originals when the drive reconnects. The cloud-based Lightroom is a different product with its own storage model; this page is about Lightroom Classic.

2.5GbE or 10GbE

2.5GbE moves about 260–300 MB/s in real SMB transfers; Fstoppers' review of the DXP4800 Plus measured about 285 MB/s with four 4 TB drives in RAID 10. That is enough to scroll a Library grid and open RAW files from the NAS. 10GbE moves about 1,050–1,150 MB/s, which matters when importing a 200 GB card or copying the archive, and needs a 10GbE port at the other end: a Thunderbolt adapter (Sonnet Solo10G about $169–199, QNAP QNA-T310G1T about $199) or a switch with 10GbE ports (QNAP QSW-2104-2T with two 10GbE and four 2.5GbE ports, around $140–213 in listings). The front SD 3.0 reader on the DXP4800 Plus imports cards straight to the NAS.

RAID is not a backup

RAID survives a drive failure, not deletion, ransomware, theft or fire. Backblaze's 3-2-1 rule: three copies on two kinds of media with one off-site. For 10 TB, Backblaze B2 costs $6.95 per TB per month, about $70 a month or $834 a year, before any egress above three times the stored amount; a second NAS or a pair of external drives rotated off-site is the cheaper second copy.
