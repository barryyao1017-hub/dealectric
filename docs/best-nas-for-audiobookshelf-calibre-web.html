What the differences mean

  • Idle power is a tie between the two 2-bay picks. ComputerBase measured 18.6 W for both the DXP2800 and Synology's DS224+, the J4125 predecessor of the DS225+, with the hard drives spinning (two in the DXP2800; ComputerBase doesn't list the DS224+'s drives). That works out to 18.6 W × 8,760 h = 163 kWh a year, or about $29.59 at the August 2026 US average of 18.16¢/kWh (EIA).
  • Synology wins on deep sleep. With the drives asleep, the DXP2800 measured 7.3 W and the DS224+ 3.5 W. (7.3 − 3.5) W × 8,760 h = 33 kWh, which is about $6 a year in Synology's favour, and only for the hours the drives actually sleep.
  • The DXP2800 is quieter at idle. 33.0 − 30.0 = 3.0 dB(A) quieter in ComputerBase's room, which is half the sound energy. During drive access the gap shrinks to 34.6 − 34.2 = 0.4 dB(A), because the hard drives set the level.
  • RAM. The DXP2800 ships with 8 GB. The DS225+ ships with 2 GB and tops out at 6 GB. At UGREEN's price today, $389.99 / 8 GB = $48.75 per installed GB.
  • Four bays cost watts. In the same chart the DXP4800 Plus idles 51.6 − 18.6 = 33.0 W higher. That is 289 kWh, or about $52 a year, and it costs $619.99 − $389.99 = $230 more at UGREEN. ComputerBase doesn't give that row's drive count, and the article suggests the 4-bay units ran four drives.

Measured watts and noise with drives fitted

Audiobookshelf and Calibre-Web hardly load the CPU. The NAS is on 24/7, though, so what the family lives with is idle power and idle noise. The makers' own numbers aren't measured the same way. Synology rates the DS225+ at 19.6 dB(A) at 1 m with Synology HDDs fitted, but rates the DS425+ at 21.6 dB(A) with SSDs fitted. QNAP gives 18.288 W 'disk standby' for the TS-264-8G 'tested with drives fully populated', without naming the drives. One lab's chart avoids that mismatch:

ComputerBase marks 30.0 dB(A) as below its meter's range, so the DXP2800's idle noise may be lower still. Other reviewers: Dong Knows logged about 275 Wh per 24 hours for the DS225+ with two drives, an average of 275 / 24 = 11.5 W. LinuxLinks measured the AS5402T at 29.2 W with disks idle and 15.4 W hibernating, but its setup used 7200 rpm drives and two NVMe SSDs, which pushes the figure up.

Where the databases live matters too. Audiobookshelf's docs say its /config database must sit on the same machine as the server, because on a network share 'you will eventually encounter database corruption'. On the DXP2800 the M.2 slots can form a separate SSD pool for /config and Calibre-Web's databases. The DS225+ has no M.2 slot, so its databases stay on the hard drives. To protect those databases from a power cut, UGREEN's compatibility page lists the NAS UPS US3000 ($109.99) for its '2-4Bay Series', which includes the DXP2800 $109.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nas-ups-us3000-120w. The cheaper US2000 is 'Only Support DH Series'.

Not recommended

UGREEN NASync DH2300. At $239.99 it is UGREEN's cheapest NAS today, but UGREEN states 'The DH2300 does not support Docker or virtual machines', so it can't run the Audiobookshelf or Calibre-Web containers. If you want an ARM box for less, the DH4300 Plus ($373.99) does support Docker, though LinuxServer's Calibre ebook-conversion mod runs on x86-64 only.
