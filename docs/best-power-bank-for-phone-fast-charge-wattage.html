What the differences mean

The common advice to buy any USB-C PD bank of 20 W or more is right for some phones and wrong for the newest ones. Apple's fast-charge support page (published 22 Oct 2026) says the iPhone 17, 17 Pro and 17 Pro Max reach 50% in about 20 minutes only with a 40 W or higher adapter; the iPhone Air and 17e need 20 W or higher; the iPhone 15 and later need an 18 W or greater USB-PD source. A 20 W bank therefore meets the Air, 17e, 15 and 16 tier and misses the 17 and 17 Pro tier. Samsung's support page adds a second rule: Super Fast Charging switches on only when the source supports USB PD PPS, however high its wattage label, and Samsung's compatibility chart gives 45 W Super Fast Charging 2.0 to the S23+, S23 Ultra, S24+, S24 Ultra, S25+, S25 Ultra, S26+ and S26 Ultra, with the base S23, S24, S25, S26, FE and Edge models on 25 W Super Fast Charging.

Delivered capacity is the same story for every 10,000 mAh bank here. Engadget's tests put about 5,800 mAh into the phone from a 10,000 mAh pack. Against Apple's EU-label capacities that is 5,800 ÷ 3,692 = 1.6 charges of an iPhone 17, 5,800 ÷ 4,252 = 1.4 of a 17 Pro and 5,800 ÷ 5,088 = 1.1 of a 17 Pro Max, so a 5,000 mAh bank would not fill a Pro Max once.

Charging two things at once also changes the answer. Anker's spec for the Nano 10K 45W is 15 W on the cable plus 7.5 W on the port when both are in use, 15 + 7.5 = 22.5 W total, so with earbuds on the port the phone sits below Apple's 18 W threshold. The 45 W and 40 W figures hold only with the phone alone. Belkin's page gives 15 W shared output for the BoostCharge 10K with two devices, below the same threshold.

Not recommended

Belkin BoostCharge 10K with integrated cable is well built but tops out at 20 W for $24.99, which meets Apple's 18 to 20 W tier for the iPhone Air, 17e, 15 and 16 and misses the 40 W iPhone 17 tier and the 45 W Galaxy tier. Its 2.2 in cable also means the phone has to sit on the bank.

Any Anker 10,000 mAh 22.5 W bank with a built-in cable sold without a model number. Notebookcheck reported Anker's 2025 recall of about 481,000 power banks after 33 fire or explosion reports, and the A1257 10,000 mAh model is on that list. Check the model number printed on the back before buying a 22.5 W Anker 10K, and register any A1257 with Anker's recall programme.
