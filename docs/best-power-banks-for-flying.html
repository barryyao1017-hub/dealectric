Any power bank under 100 Wh (about 27,000 mAh) can fly in your carry-on in the US; none may go in checked bags. What changed in 2026 is using it: since 27 March 2026 the ICAO rules say no more than two power banks per person and no recharging them on board, and many airlines now ban using one in flight at all. The FAA has not adopted the ICAO change; US airlines set their own rules (table below).

The rules in one paragraph. FAA: spare lithium batteries, power banks included, go in carry-on only; under 100 Wh there is no FAA limit for personal use; 101–160 Wh needs the airline's approval, two at most; over 160 Wh is forbidden. Watt-hours = mAh × 3.7 ÷ 1,000 (American Airlines' formula): 10,000 mAh ≈ 37 Wh, 20,000 mAh ≈ 74 Wh, 25,000 mAh ≈ 92.5 Wh, 27,000 mAh ≈ 99.9 Wh. Airlines may be stricter than the FAA, and from 2027 IATA's rulebook drops the 100–160 Wh allowance for power banks. Recalled banks may not fly at all; Anker recalled models A1647, A1652, A1681, A1689, A1257 and the PowerCore 10000 in June 2025.

What it means for the choice: on most international carriers the bank charges your devices at the gate and the hotel, not in your seat, so capacity per gram matters more than in-flight convenience. On US carriers that allow use, keep it visible and out of the overhead bin. If you fly abroad with two banks, both must be under 100 Wh on most airlines, and three is too many almost everywhere.
