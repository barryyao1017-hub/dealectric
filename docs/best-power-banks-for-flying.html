What the differences mean

Airlines go by the watt-hours printed on the case. Watt-hours are amp-hours times cell voltage, and makers rate their cells at different voltages, so the mAh figure on the box can mislead:

  • AOHi Future Starship: 21.6 V × 4.6 Ah = 99.36 Wh, which is the same as 27.6 Ah × 3.6 V. At 3.7 V the same mAh would come to 27.6 × 3.7 = 102.1 Wh, over the limit, but the label is what counts.
  • EcoFlow RAPID Pro: 99.54 Wh ÷ 27.65 Ah = 3.60 V. At 3.7 V it would be 27.65 × 3.7 = 102.3 Wh.
  • Anker Prime 26K: 99.75 Wh ÷ 26.25 Ah = 3.80 V. It has 1,400 fewer mAh than the EcoFlow yet holds 99.75 − 99.54 = 0.21 Wh more.
  • Baseus FC41: 91.25 Wh ÷ 25 Ah = 3.65 V. UGREEN rates its 145W bank at 3.6 V, so the same 25,000 mAh prints 25 × 3.6 = 90 Wh.
  • Two banks, the usual per-passenger maximum: 2 × 99.36 = 198.72 Wh with two Starships.

Why the printed Wh decides, and how much you actually get out

From 27 March 2026, ICAO limits passengers to two power banks and bars recharging them during flights. The UK CAA's wording of the same rule adds that a power bank should not be used to charge other devices on board, and that a bank over 100 Wh, up to 160 Wh, needs the airline's approval. Delta caps each bank at 100 Wh with no exceptions and allows at most two. It keeps them out of the overhead bin and bans using one to charge a device during taxi, take-off and landing. In practice the energy you board with is all you have, so charge the laptop from seat power where the plane has it and use the bank between flights. That makes the highest printed Wh under 100 the figure to buy on, ahead of a high wattage rating.

Not all of the printed energy reaches your device: some is lost converting the cells' voltage up to USB voltages. The measured figures:

  • AOHi Future Starship (ChargerLAB): 90.25 Wh at 9 V 3 A, 90.47 Wh at 12 V 3 A, 85.43 Wh at 15 V 3 A and 79.39 Wh at 5 V 3 A. At 20 V 5 A (100 W) its temperature protection stopped the test after 23 minutes, at 35.52 Wh. At 28 V 5 A (140 W) it stopped after 10 minutes, at 24.06 Wh. It is the pick for loads up to 45 W, not for running a 16-inch MacBook Pro flat out.
  • Anker Prime 26K (techtest.org, Michael Barton): 79.6–89.8 Wh delivered; held 140 W constant.
  • EcoFlow RAPID Pro 27,650 mAh (techtest.org): 75.1–87.5 Wh delivered; held 140 W, dropping to 100 W once the cells reach 40 °C.
  • UGREEN Nexode 20,000 mAh 165W with retractable cable (techtest.org): 62.5–66.4 Wh delivered. A 99 Wh bank gives you roughly 15–25 Wh more for the same carry-on allowance.

Not recommended

AOHi's Starship 40,000 mAh is sold for travel, but even at the lowest cell voltage in the table above (3.6 V), 40 Ah × 3.6 V = 144 Wh. That is over 100 Wh, so it needs your airline's approval, and Delta allows no bank over 100 Wh at all. For flying, take the 27,600 mAh Future Starship instead.
