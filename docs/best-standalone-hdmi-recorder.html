Best standalone HDMI recorder

Short answer. The best standalone hdmi recorder is the Zowietek ZowieREC 4K HDMI Video Recorder ($199; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/zowietek-zowierec-4k-hdmi-video-recorder): it records 4K30 in H.265 to a microSD card, USB drive or NAS with no computer, passes 4K60 through to your TV, and costs less than the ClonerAlliance UHD Pro that has the same 4K30 recording ceiling. If you edit in ProRes or DNxHD, the Blackmagic HyperDeck Shuttle HD records those codecs but tops out at 1080p60 and costs about three times as much.

What owners complain about

Five problems come up again and again in owner reviews, maker FAQs and forum threads.

  • HDCP black screen. The most common complaint by far. Streaming sticks, most cable and satellite boxes, and consoles with HDCP left on give a blank screen or an error on every recorder in the table. Consoles let you switch HDCP off in settings; set-top boxes usually do not.
  • 4K60 sources do not record in 4K. Every sub-$300 recorder caps recording at 4K30. The ClonerAlliance drops a 4K60 source to 4K30, the Zowietek drops it to 1080p60. Game consoles default to 60 Hz, so 4K game capture needs the console set to 30 Hz or a PC capture card instead.
  • Files split at 4 GB. FAT32 drives force 4 GB chunks on all of these. The Zowietek and ClonerAlliance both let you format to exFAT and turn splitting off; the Zowietek can also split by time.
  • Audio drifts out of sync. Reported on the ClonerAlliance and on early Zowietek firmware with 59.94 fps sources. Zowietek lists sync fixes in its 2025 firmware; keep the unit updated and feed it 60 or 30 fps when you can.
  • Storage quirks. The AVerMedia does not accept USB flash drives at all, one owner reports its recording partition corrupting, and NAS recording on it runs at a lower bitrate. The Zowietek has a small fan that gets louder in performance mode.

Which one by need

  • Record a camera, PC or console in 4K with no computer: Zowietek ZowieREC. Same 4K30 ceiling as the ClonerAlliance UHD Pro for less money, plus NAS recording, PoE+ power and a 2-year warranty from the maker store.
  • Record scheduled TV shows from a cable or satellite box: AVerMedia EzRecorder 330G, for the IR blaster that wakes the box and changes channels. It records 1080p60 only, and the box must not output HDCP. Check stock before you order; the maker's US store showed it out of stock on 2026-11-06.
  • Pro editing in ProRes or DNxHD: Blackmagic HyperDeck Shuttle HD. It is HD only and costs about $575, but edit-ready codecs and SD or USB media make it the choice for studios.

Not recommended

  • Recording smart TV apps. A TV is not an HDMI source, so no recorder can capture Netflix or YouTube running on the TV itself. Use an external box with HDCP disabled, if it allows that.
  • USB capture cards sold as recorders. Elgato-style cards and no-name 4K capture sticks need a computer running OBS. They are not standalone, whatever the listing says.
  • HyperDeck Shuttle HD for 4K. The HD model has no 4K input; the 4K Pro model costs far more.
