How much solar each kit can grow to

Panels wired in series add their voltages. The string's open-circuit voltage (Voc) has to stay under the station's input ceiling. Cold weather raises Voc, so leave some headroom. After that, the input's amp and watt limits cap what a string can deliver.

  • ALLPOWERS R3500: one solar input rated 12–150 V, 40 A, 2,000 W. The R3500 manual shows panels wired in series and says to keep panel output within 150 V. The SP039 has a Voc of 52.8 V (SP039 manual), so two in series make 105.6 V and 1,200 W rated. A third would reach 158.4 V, over the ceiling. The SP037 has a Voc of 45.3 V (SP037 manual), so three in series make 135.9 V, about 10% under the ceiling. A fourth would reach 181.2 V.
  • BLUETTI Elite 300: 12–60 V, 22 A, 1,200 W (StorageReview). Each string of added panels has to stay under 60 V Voc, so long series strings of large panels won't work on it.
  • BLUETTI Apex 300: two inputs, each rated 1,200 W, 12–60 V, 20 A (StorageReview). 1,200 W ÷ 20 A = 60 V, the very top of the window, so an input reaches its full 1,200 W only with a string working at 60 V. BLUETTI's 4,000 W solar figure requires its separate SolarX 4K controller.
  • Anker SOLIX F3000: a High-PV input rated 11–165 V, 17 A, 1,600 W and a Low-PV input rated 11–60 V, 17 A, 800 W (Anker). The 2,400 W total needs both inputs in use. The kit's single 400 W panel fills one.
