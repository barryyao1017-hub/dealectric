What the differences mean

  • About 71 W of outage load per Enphase microinverter. 0.30 × 5,700 W ÷ 24 IQ8 = 71 W. Sunlight Backup covers a few essential circuits, not the whole house. Enphase says whole-home backup 'is not supported'.
  • Starting a fridge takes about 13 IQ8MC. OutageMath puts a fridge's start surge at about 1,200 W. 1,200 W ÷ 0.30 = 4,000 W of array AC rating, and 4,000 ÷ 320 VA = 12.5, so 13 microinverters.

Which hardware actually runs without a battery

An ordinary grid-tied inverter goes dark in an outage. Under IEEE 1547 it must detect the outage and stop exporting within 2 seconds (5 if the utility extends it), as OutageMath summarizes. To keep running, the inverter has to form its own grid, and something has to cut the house off from the utility lines. Each maker handles this differently.

Enphase: On Enphase's storage compatibility page, Sunlight Backup 'is supported with IQ System Controller 2/3/3G, IQ8 or IQ9 Microinverters and IQ Gateway or IQ Combiner', and 'at least one IQ Load Controller is required' to commission it. IQ6/7 microinverters aren't named, so an IQ7 array doesn't qualify. The same page marks IQ System Controller 2 and IQ Combiner 4/4C as end-of-life, so new installs use the 3 or 3G controller. Without this hardware, the matrix says a solar-only system will 'produce solar energy only when the grid is up'. The Solar Lab saw the same thing in testing: its IQ8MC rig with no Sunlight Backup hardware needed 'an active grid reference' and produced about 800 W from 1,460 W of panels with a simulated grid present. Enphase's user guide says that if loads exceed production, the system shuts down and can take up to two minutes to restart. It then brings loads back on 22 seconds apart. One IQ Load Controller covers 2 × 240 V or 4 × 120 V circuits, so the full 4 × 240 V needs two.

EG4 18kPV: EG4's manual (p. 84) says 'No Battery' 'can be enabled to use solar power to supply load when the grid fails or load-shedding happens'. On p. 50 it says that when off-grid with no battery, 'the Off-Grid energy is supplied by solar and is not stable', and EG4 suggests installing batteries 'to keep the EPS/UPS output voltage stable'. Use it for light daytime loads until a battery goes in.

SMA Secure Power Supply: Northern Arizona Wind & Sun says the outlet has power 'as long as the sun is shining', up to 2,000 W. OutageMath says you switch it on by hand. Both the -40 and -41 Sunny Boy 7.0 are now listed as discontinued, so this suits existing owners more than new buyers.

Sun Gold Power SPH6548P: None of these is a product we list except this one. It is the lowest-priced all-in-one here whose maker listing says it 'supports battery-free' operation, at $159 per rated kW. It is listed for off-grid systems, not for exporting to the grid. Sun Gold's listing doesn't give a wattage for loads run from panels alone, so ask Sun Gold for the battery-free load limit before you count on it for outage power. $1,035: https://dealectric.com/go/sungoldpower-sph6548p-6-5kw-48v-hybrid-inverter

Not recommended

Sol-Ark 15K-2P for outage power without a battery. OutageMath lists Sol-Ark among systems that need a battery to back anything up, so the 13 kW backup rating on Sol-Ark's page doesn't apply without one. The Solar Lab measured the 15K drawing 97 W idle in standby. If it draws that all day and night, 97 W × 8,760 h = about 850 kWh a year. At $6,900 for the pre-wired system, it only makes sense if you're buying the battery too.
