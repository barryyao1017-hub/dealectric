Why a USB mic goes silent on Xbox, and what works on each console

Xbox. Maono's support page says that "the Xbox operating system deliberately does not support the generic UAC standard for audio." UAC (USB Audio Class) is the plug-and-play driver that PCs and the PS5 use for USB mics. For a USB device to work on Xbox, it "must be part of the 'Designed for Xbox' licensing program," which requires an authentication chip. Without that chip, "the Xbox sees an unauthorized device and simply ignores it." Maono notes that the console is designed for a 3.5 mm headset, mic included, plugged straight into the controller. Neither Maono, FIFINE nor any USB desk mic on this page lists Xbox.

EKSA E900 Pro on Xbox. EKSA's manual says to plug the 3.5 mm cable into the newer Xbox One controller's jack. The original Xbox One controller needs a Microsoft adapter, which is not included, and the Xbox 360 is not supported. The manual also says the 7.1 surround driver "only supports Windows," so on any console the E900 Pro works as a stereo headset. The manual doesn't name Xbox Series X|S. If you want the maker to name it, choose the HyperX Cloud III.

PS5. Sony's PS5 FAQ says "most USB and analog headsets will be supported," and that headsets on the controller's 3.5 mm jack are supported. Third-party Bluetooth headsets "will not be supported." Maono and FIFINE both list PS5 for their USB mics, so on PS5 and PC you can choose either a headset or a mic based on voice quality and desk space.

Keeping your headphones. The ModMic Uni 2+ is a clip-on 3.5 mm mic. Antlion says its Y adapter lets a ModMic work through a controller's 3.5 mm port on Xbox, PlayStation, Switch and Steam Deck. Of the mic routes on this page, it is the only one that works on Xbox.
