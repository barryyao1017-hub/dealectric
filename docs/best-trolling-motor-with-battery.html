The run-time math is simple. A 55 lb motor such as the Minn Kota Endura C2 55 draws up to 50 A at full throttle, so a 100 Ah lithium pack lasts about 2 hours flat out and 4 to 5 hours at the 20 to 25 A of half speed. A 30 lb motor draws up to 30 A, so the same pack runs over 3 hours at full. A 50 Ah pack halves those figures. Lithium gives its full rated capacity; a lead-acid battery with the same label gives about half before the voltage sags, which is why owners of 35 Ah lead-acid "kayak" batteries report a 55 lb motor dying in about 30 minutes.

What owners complain about

We read Newport Vessels' battery troubleshooting guide, the In-Depth Outdoors and iBoats lithium threads, Outdoor Life's NK300 test, Panbo's Spirit 1.0 Plus review and Practical Sailor's electric dinghy survey. The pain points, by how often they came up:

  • Cold-weather charge lockout (6 posters). Every LiFePO4 pack blocks charging near 32°F. One owner's two 100 Ah packs refused to charge at about 30°F until he added a heating pad. Discharge still works down to about 4°F, so the fix is charging indoors.
  • Battery failures (5 reports). A 24 V pack that would not take a charge after one trip, two packs with water in the BMS replaced over four years, a Torqeedo battery dead inside a year. Buy from a brand with a written multi-year warranty and keep the receipt.
  • Less run time than expected (4). Undersized batteries make the motor slow at high speed. The Outdoor Life tester ran a 36 V 30 Ah pack flat fighting current and needed 50 Ah for a full day. Size by your motor's amp draw, not by marketing hours.
  • Weight on a kayak (3). Motor, mount and battery added about 40 lb to a Vibe Shearwater 125, and lead-acid packs sit the hull lower. An 11.3 lb 50 Ah or 21.8 lb 100 Ah lithium pack is the fix.
  • Replacing a built-in battery (3). The Spirit 1.0 Plus battery is $1,349 on its own, a Torqeedo owner paid $1,000 for a replacement, and a Bixpy owner budgeted about $500 after three seasons. A standalone 12 V pack costs a fraction of that and fits any motor.
  • Not knowing the charge left (2). Motor-mounted meters are calibrated for lead-acid and misread lithium, and lithium cuts out abruptly at empty. A Bluetooth readout fixes both.

Not recommended

  • 35 Ah sealed lead-acid "kayak" batteries. Newport's own guide says a 55 lb motor runs about 30 minutes at full speed on one, and the plates sulfate if the battery sits discharged.
  • Any lithium pack with a BMS rated below your motor's max draw. A 55 lb motor pulls 50 A; a 40 lb motor pulls about 42 A. A 30 A or 50 A BMS trips at full throttle.
  • Bixpy K-1 Outboard Kit for an all-day angler. $1,399 buys the same 378 Wh as the kLite 750 but the mounting adapter is sold separately, and a Hobie forum owner's main gripes were fixed speed steps and no larger battery option.
  • Torqeedo Travel for buyers who can't absorb a battery replacement. Practical Sailor writes that it has heard of too many Torqeedo failures to recommend them, and a reader paid $1,000 for a replacement pack. ePropulsion's record over six years was cleaner in the same survey.
