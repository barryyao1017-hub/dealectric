Built in or separate: which ones can run from another battery

ePropulsion kLite 750 and eLite. ePropulsion says the kLite 750 "is powered by its integrated battery and cannot be operated directly from an external battery". The eLite also uses a built-in 378 Wh, 25.2 V pack and shares the same kLite/eLite Range Extension Kit (KL-AC01-00). That kit is "compatible with 12V third-party batteries" and connects through the charging port, giving "up to 100W of charging input". A 12 V battery on these motors is therefore an on-water recharger, not extra power. Refilling the 378 Wh pack takes at least 378 Wh ÷ 100 W = 3.8 h, before conversion losses. ePropulsion's own extended-range figure is 378 Wh + an optional 54 Ah (691 Wh) battery = 1,069 Wh.

ePropulsion Spirit 1.0 Plus. This is the ePropulsion motor here that does run from another battery. Its external-battery input accepts 39–60 V, and ePropulsion recommends its E-Series packs (E40 to E175). A 12 V battery is outside that range. The standard Spirit Battery Plus holds 1,276 Wh and charges in about 8.5 h, or 3.5 h with fast charging.

Torqeedo Travel S. The Travel S runs at 48 V nominal on Torqeedo's own Travel batteries. The older 915 Wh, 29.6 V Travel battery ($1,199, currently not available) lists only the "Travel 503/603/903/1003/1103" as compatible, so owners of an older Travel shouldn't plan on moving that pack to the Travel S.

Motors built for a separate battery. A 12 V trolling motor like Newport's Kayak Series ("Runs on a 12V 50Ah deep cycle marine battery") or the Newport NK180 HD with its 24 V 50 Ah pack takes whatever correct-voltage battery you give it. That makes the battery the part to get right. Minn Kota's rule is that a lithium battery must "provide enough continuous discharge amperage to run the motor at its max amp draw". If it can't, the battery's management system restricts power and the motor may shut off or perform erratically. Minn Kota also says to limit brushed motors running on lithium to 85% speed (Speed 8.5).
