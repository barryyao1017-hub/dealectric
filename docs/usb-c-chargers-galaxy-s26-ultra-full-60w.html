Which USB-C chargers charge a Galaxy S26 Ultra at its full 60 W?

The feature is USB PD PPS that reaches 20 V at 3 A or more on the port the phone uses. Android Authority's Robert Triggs found the Galaxy S26 Ultra needs it for its 60 W mode (Super Fast Charging 3.0) and noted that the 20 V mode is less common than PPS itself. The S26+ and S26 do not use it: Samsung's own chart caps them at 45 W and 25 W. Look for it in the maker's PPS line, on Charge-Test's list of chargers confirmed for the S26 Ultra, or in a named reviewer's port table. A headline wattage does not show it. What the differences mean

  • Samsung 60W adapter: 20 V × 3 A = 60 W. ChargerLAB measured 51.54 W into the phone, and 51.54 ÷ 60 = 86% of the tier.
  • UGREEN Nexode Air 65W: 20 V × 3.25 A = 65 W, which is at least 60 W, from its single port. Forest Watch saw Super Fast Charging 3.0 engage but published no watts.
  • Anker Prime 160W: 20 V × 5 A = 100 W of PPS headroom. Anker lists only a 160 W total for two or three devices, so it does not say what the phone's port keeps.
  • Baseus AH22: 20 V × 5 A = 100 W alone. With a second device plugged in, each port drops to 45 W, and 60 − 45 = 15 W short of the S26 Ultra's top tier.
  • How much the top tier buys: Android Authority's 60 W peak lasted about 2 of 42 minutes (2 ÷ 42 ≈ 5%). Charge-Test (Michael Barton) measured 51% after 15 minutes on a 20 V PPS charger against 49% on an 11 V PPS charger, and about 62 minutes from 5% to 100% on both. Without PPS, Charge-Test measured 14.5 W from a 20 W charger. Techtest.org found a 100 W charger without PPS was held to about 23 W.

How to read a charger's label for the 20 V PPS mode

Find the PPS line for the port you will use and take the current listed at 20 V. Multiply it by 20. The result has to be 60 W or more. Neither the voltage span nor the headline watts settles it. Three things do not count. A fixed 20 V/3 A PDO is not PPS. A high-current PPS range that stops at 11 V or 16 V does not reach 20 V either: Samsung's own 60 W adapter also carries 5–11 V at 5 A (55 W) and 5–16 V at 4 A (64 W) ranges, per ChargerLAB, and only its 5–21 V at 3 A range matches the 20 V/3 A mode Android Authority named. On a multi-port charger, read the split table too, because the 20 V PPS figure often holds only with one device plugged in. On cables, Android Authority says the phone's bundled 3 A cable is enough and a 5 A cable does not charge faster, since 20 V × 3 A = 60 W. SamMobile recommends a 5 A cable as the safer choice with non-Samsung chargers.

Not recommended

Samsung 65W Power Adapter Trio: its 65 W comes from a fixed 20 V/3.25 A PDO. In PPS mode, Samsung's own spec gives USB-C1 2.25 A at 20 V, so 20 × 2.25 = 45 W. USB-C2's PPS stops at 11 V, so 11 × 2.25 = 24.75 W at most. Plugging in a second device on C2 drops C1 to 40 W. SamMobile reported the same 45 W limit. Samsung's older 45W adapter lists PPS at 2.25 A across 5–20 V, which is also 45 W, so it cannot run the S26 Ultra's 60 W mode either.
