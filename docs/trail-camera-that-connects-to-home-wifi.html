What the differences mean

  • The $20 saving buys no router mode. E8 3.0 Pro $119.99 − E8 2.0 Pro $99.99 = $20.00. The cheaper camera holds the same 7,800 mAh battery, but it can only send photos to a phone standing within 165 ft of it.
  • The same price can buy a quarter of the reach. The A80 Pro also costs $119.99, but it talks only to your phone, up to 45 ft away: 45 ÷ 165 ≈ 27% of the 3.0 Pro's direct-connect range, and it never reaches your router.
  • E9 or E8 3.0 Pro? Both cost $119.99, so the difference is $0. GardePro lists triple PIR sensors and 30 m no-glow night vision for the E9 3.0 Pro. For the E8 3.0 Pro it lists a 5 MP sensor behind its 4K video, and 3,840 × 2,160 = 8.3 MP, so the 4K is upscaled about 1.66×. That makes the E9 the better buy at the same price.
  • Link range in woods. GardePro's own dense-forest test reached 384 ft. That is 384 ÷ 1,600 = 24% of the 1,600 ft “typical” rating, but still 384 ÷ 150 ≈ 2.6× the 100–150 ft outdoor reach Magnolia Trail Camera gives for a typical router.
  • Solar refill. 7,800 mAh ÷ 1,520 mA (SP1000, 10 W) ≈ 5.1 h, and 7,800 ÷ 530 mA (SP300, 3.5 W) ≈ 14.7 h. Both are at rated output in full sun and ignore charging losses.

How to tell which trail cameras join a home router

GardePro sells two kinds of “Wi-Fi” trail camera that look alike. The 3.0 Pro models (E8 3.0 Pro, E9 3.0 Pro) have a Home WiFi mode. In that mode the camera joins your 2.4 GHz home network and sends motion alerts and photo and video previews to the app wherever you are. You can request HD files and share access. GardePro's own overviews say “Live View is not supported in Home WiFi Mode”: live view works only in Direct Connection mode, with your phone within 165 ft (50 m) in the open. GardePro publishes no camera-to-router distance, so test your router's signal at the mounting spot before you fix the camera. Magnolia Trail Camera puts a typical router's outdoor reach at about 100–150 ft with a clear line of sight, and less through walls and leaves.

The 2.0 generation and the A80 Pro work the other way: the camera broadcasts its own Wi-Fi and your phone joins it. The store listings for the E8 2.0 Pro and E6 2.0 both say “Unable to connect to home router.” The A80 Pro says “no home WiFi/cellular required” and reaches 45 ft.

The Link 1.0 is a third design. Its R3 2.0 Pro camera “cannot connect directly to WiFi.” It talks to a hub over the 802.11ah long-range protocol, which the Wi-Fi Alliance markets as Wi-Fi HaLow and runs below 1 GHz. The hub needs an Ethernet cable to a DHCP-enabled router and power from a PoE adapter. One hub takes up to 16 cameras, and the kit is sold for the US and Canada only. GardePro's test, published March 13, 2026, with stock 2 dB antennas at 1.5 m mounting height gave these ranges:

iPhone owners: GardePro says that on iOS 26 the GardePro app needs Location set to “Always” with Precise Location on, or it may fail to connect to the camera. On availability, GardePro's own store showed the E9 3.0 Pro in stock on 2026-11-10. In October, Magnolia Trail Camera reported an Amazon pre-order date of March 1, 2027.
