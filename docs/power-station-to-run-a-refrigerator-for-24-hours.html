What the differences mean

CNET ran every unit on the same full-size Whirlpool (118 V, rated 7.2 A). Across the units where CNET split out the fridge's draw, it averaged 54–60 W: 57 W × 24 h = about 1.37 kWh a day, or roughly 500 kWh a year. Each result gives two derived figures.

  • Battery Wh per fridge hour = rated Wh ÷ CNET hours. This covers the fridge plus the station's own draw and losses. FOSSiBOT F3600 Pro: 3,840 ÷ 44.65 = 86.0. Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2: 2,048 ÷ 27.40 = 74.7. BLUETTI Elite 200 V2: 2,073.6 ÷ 26.32 = 78.8.
  • Hours on your fridge = rated Wh ÷ (Wh per fridge hour + the watts your fridge needs above CNET's 57 W). A 585 kWh/yr fridge averages 585,000 ÷ 8,760 = 66.8 W, which is 9.8 W more. A 679 kWh/yr fridge averages 77.5 W, which is 20.5 W more. F3600 Pro on the 679 kWh/yr fridge: 3,840 ÷ (86.0 + 20.5) = 36.1 h. Elite 200 V2 on the same fridge: 2,073.6 ÷ (78.8 + 20.5) = 20.9 h.
  • Cost per fridge hour on a 679 kWh/yr fridge: F3600 Pro 1,199 ÷ 36.1 = $33. HomePower 3000 1,499 ÷ 26.4 = $57. SOLIX F3000 1,899.99 ÷ 24.3 = $78.

Why a lab's 27 hours can be 21 hours in your kitchen

A runtime test measures one fridge. CNET's Whirlpool used about 1.30–1.44 kWh a day (54 W × 24 h to 60 W × 24 h), which is 473–526 kWh a year. Home Power Facts puts the median ENERGY STAR full-size fridge at 585 kWh/yr across 668 models. ACEEE's Smarter House lists 550 kWh/yr for an ENERGY STAR side-by-side and 679 kWh/yr for the least efficient side-by-side. Every extra watt the fridge draws comes out of the same battery. So the 2 kWh units that cleared 24 h on CNET's fridge fall short on these.

The rescaling assumes the station's own draw and losses stay at what CNET measured. That flatters every unit slightly, because inverter loss grows with load. Scaling the whole runtime by fridge watts instead (27.40 × 57 ÷ 66.8) gives the C2000 Gen 2 23.4 h on a 585 kWh/yr fridge, not 24.2 h. The F3600 Pro stays well clear either way: 44.65 × 57 ÷ 77.5 = 32.8 h on a 679 kWh/yr fridge. CNET did not split out the fridge's draw for the F3600 Pro, so the same 57 W midpoint is used for it.

Maker fridge claims don't settle it either. Anker says the C2000 Gen 2 runs a dual-door fridge for up to 32 hours but doesn't name the fridge. The footnote on Anker's S2000 names a 700 L fridge in a 77°F room. Working backwards, 2,010 Wh ÷ 35 h − 6 W idle means that fridge averaged at most 51 W, smaller than most full-size fridges. To check your own fridge, divide the kWh/yr on its EnergyGuide label by 8,760 to get its average watts, then use the formula above.
