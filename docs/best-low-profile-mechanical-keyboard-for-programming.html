What the differences mean

Three things decide a programming keyboard: where your remaps and layers are stored (the VIA project's documentation, each maker's page and Keychron's manual), a 75% layout that keeps the function row and arrow keys (all six boards here), and how many 8-hour days a charge lasts (maker ratings). Dealectric does not test units; every figure here is a maker rating or a named reviewer's observation.

  • Layers per system on the Keychron K3 Max: 4 layers ÷ 2 system modes = 2 each (one base layer, one Fn layer). Keychron's manual gives layers 0 and 1 to Mac and layers 2 and 3 to Windows, and warns that Windows-mode changes go on layer 2, not layer 0.
  • Lofree Flow 2 84 with all lights on: 17 h ÷ 8 h = about 2 working days per charge. With the backlight off on Bluetooth: 120 h ÷ 8 h = 15 days.
  • NuPhy Air75 V3 with lights on: 60 to 100 h ÷ 8 h = 7.5 to 12.5 days (NuPhy's rating, relayed by Notebookcheck).
  • Keychron K3 Max: 73 h ÷ 8 h = about 9 days; Keychron's page gives the 73 hours without a backlight condition. K3 Ultra 8K: 550 h ÷ 8 h = about 69 days.

Where a remap is stored, and the setup each board needs

VIA's documentation says it "stores dynamic keymaps, macros, layout options, and other supported settings in the keyboard's non-volatile memory", talks to the firmware over USB, and "by default, VIA provides four dynamic layers" (a maker can build firmware with a different count; Lofree's Flow 2 pages state none). Once a remap is written, it works on any computer with no app running.

Lofree Flow 2 84. Lofree says the Flow 2 is "fully compatible with VIA". Setup is not plug-and-detect. bamka's guide of 2025-09-08 has you connect by cable, download Lofree's Flow2 VIA JSON file, turn on "Show Design tab" in VIA and load the file. Paulo Vargas of Digital Trends (2026-08-09, 84-key Mac version) wrote that VIA would not recognise the board without that file, that Lofree sent it to him directly after he spent most of a day looking, and that the Fn key still cannot be customised. Mattias Inghe of PCWorld (2026-09-23) reports the quick start guide tells you to contact customer service for the file, and Philipp Briel of basic-tutorials (2026-03-31) adds that the touch bar cannot be reconfigured in VIA. On 2026-11-21 Lofree's product page listed no VIA file itself and pointed to its Download Center, so plan on asking Lofree support for the file. Julian van der Merwe of Notebookcheck writes that users on Linux, macOS and Windows can all remap the Flow 2 through VIA; Lofree's own compatibility list names Windows, Android, macOS, iPadOS and iOS.

Typing. RTINGS reviewed the 100-key Flow 2 and notes "somewhat loud typing noise if you choose the tactile switch option", so in a shared room choose the Surfer linear or Void silent version over Pulse tactile. It calls the NuPhy Air75 V3 "well-suited for programming".

Keychron K3 Max and K3 Ultra 8K. Both remap in the Keychron Launcher web app, and Keychron lists macOS, Windows and Linux for each. The K3 Max runs QMK; the K3 Ultra 8K runs ZMK.

NuPhy Air75 V3. MKB Guide writes that the V3 "moved from the V2's QMK/VIA stack to NuPhyIO 2.0". RTINGS says NuPhyIO 2.0 adds an offline client and lets you assign macros to any key.

Not recommended for remapping on Linux

The Logitech MX Mechanical Mini types on Linux, but Mech Key Foundry states its remapping runs through Logi Options+ on Windows and macOS, with no QMK or VIA, so the remaps depend on an app on the computer. Logitech's US store also showed the Graphite version as sold out temporarily on 2026-11-21. Separately, do not buy the NuPhy Air75 V3 expecting the Air75 V2's QMK/VIA support: the V3 uses NuPhy's own software.
