What owners complain about

  • Pump clogs and E4 shutdowns (about 8 mentions). AlorAir's own forum has several E4 threads, and on these units E4 means the water stayed high after the pump ran. The cause is usually a dirty pump or a kinked or blocked hose. Clean the pump and drain line when you change the filter.
  • Warranty hassle (about 5). You register the unit, get a return authorization and ship the unit for repair, so it can be offline for weeks.
  • Drain line freezing outside (about 4). A thin line run out a wall can freeze between pump cycles. Owners suggest draining to an indoor drain, or running the line through a downward-sloped PVC sleeve and insulating it.
  • Refrigerant leaks at 15–18 months (about 4, across AlorAir, AprilAire and Santa Fe). No brand here is free of these reports.
  • Extras cost more (3). AlorAir's wired wall remote is $100, and its MERV-1 filter should be replaced every 3–6 months.
  • Runs nonstop (3). A dirt floor, open vents or a unit too small for the space keep it running. Seal the crawl space first, and size by AHAM pints, not saturation pints.

Which one by need

  • No drain below the unit, up to 1,300 sq ft: AlorAir Sentinel HD55P. The pump is built in, with a 19.6 ft drain height.
  • Small or unheated crawl space up to 1,000 sq ft: AlorAir Sentinel HD35P WiFi. It runs to 33°F and you can check it from your phone.
  • 1,300 to 2,300 sq ft: AlorAir Galaxy 85P, at 85 pints a day.
  • You have a floor drain or sump, or you'll add a pump and want the pro brand: AprilAire E070. It dried fastest in Bob Vila's test. It has no built-in pump, but AprilAire's 4856 pump is rated for 22 ft of lift.
  • Very low clearance: Santa Fe Compact70, which is 12 in tall. Its pump kit is sold separately.

Not recommended

  • Gravity-only units (AlorAir HD55, HD55S) when you have no drain below them. Water can't run uphill, so you'd still need a separate pump.
  • Picking by saturation pints. A unit labeled 120 pints removes about 55 pints a day at the standard 80°F, 60% rating.
