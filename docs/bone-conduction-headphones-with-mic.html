What the differences mean

  • Boom mic vs built-in mic: 79% vs 22% of listeners rated the recording Good or Perfect. 79 ÷ 22 ≈ 3.6, so about 3.6 times as many listeners gave the boom-mic OpenComm2 a top rating.
  • Bad or Awful ratings: OpenComm2 UC 1% + 4% = 5%; OpenMeet UC 3% + 5% = 8%; OpenRun Pro 2 18% + 6% = 24%. About one listener in four rated the OpenRun Pro 2 recording poor.
  • Talk time vs music time: the OpenComm2 is rated 16 h for calls and 8 h for music: 16 ÷ 8 = 2× as long on calls. For music the OpenRun Pro 2's 12 h is 12 ÷ 8 = 1.5× the OpenComm2's rating.
  • A full workday of calls: 16 h talk ÷ 8 h day = 2 workdays per charge for the OpenComm2; 14 ÷ 8 = 1.75 for the OpenMeet.
  • Weight: the OpenMeet's 78 g band is 78 ÷ 35 ≈ 2.2× the OpenComm2's 35 g neckband.
  • Adding a PC adapter later: Shokz sells the Loop120 adapter separately for $39.95. On the OpenMeet, the UC version with the adapter costs $139.95 − $119.95 = $20 more than the headset alone.

How listeners rated each microphone

Bone conduction only changes how you hear. Your voice still reaches the other caller through an ordinary microphone, so the type of mic decides call quality. SoundGuys posts a recording from each headset's mic and asks readers to rate it from Awful to Perfect. The four results:

  • OpenComm2 UC: 1,080 votes: 30% Perfect, 49% Good, 15% Okay, 1% Bad, 4% Awful. Reviewer Jasper Lastoria (SoundGuys) called the boom mic's speech quality 'excellent' and said street noise was 'virtually eliminated' outdoors.
  • OpenMeet UC: 218 votes: 27% Perfect, 44% Good, 22% Okay, 3% Bad, 5% Awful, recorded in ideal conditions. RTINGS calls its mic 'easily the best of our picks', and its OpenComm2 2025 review calls the OpenComm2's mic 'satisfactory for office use'. RTINGS' measured figures are available to members only.
  • OpenRun Pro 2: 1,452 votes: 2% Perfect, 20% Good, 54% Okay, 18% Bad, 6% Awful, from a recording SoundGuys labels non-standardized. Lil Katz (SoundGuys) wrote that the mic 'will suffice when speaking from quiet conditions' but can 'fall flat when relaying my voice in a windy environment'.

SoundGuys tested the original 2023 OpenComm2 UC. Shokz lists the 2025 Upgrade's changes as USB-C charging, a new wireless adapter and software controls, and the microphone isn't among them. The same SoundGuys review measured 17 h 59 min of playback on one charge. That is longer than Shokz's own 8 h music rating, which Shokz measured at 93% volume on an iPhone 12 Pro. SoundGuys doesn't give the volume it used.

Choosing a PC adapter: Shokz says only its communication-series headsets (OpenComm and OpenMeet) work with the Loop120. Order the USB-A version for Windows and USB-A computers, and the USB-C version for Macs and USB-C computers. The plain OpenComm2 (2025) has to be paired with the adapter by hand. The UC version ships with it already paired.

Not recommended

The OpenRun Pro 2 as a call headset. It is a good running headphone, but its built-in mics got the fewest top ratings and the most poor ratings in SoundGuys' polls (22% Good or Perfect, 24% Bad or Awful). It can't use Shokz's USB adapter, and Shokz publishes no talk-time rating for it. RTINGS measured 9 h of battery against Shokz's 12 h rating. If calls are the reason you're buying, choose a boom-mic model.
