Best gaming headset for Xbox

Updated 2026-11-06

Short answer. The best gaming headset for Xbox is the SteelSeries Arctis Nova 7X Gen 2 if you want wireless: its USB-C transmitter has an Xbox mode, it runs Bluetooth from your phone at the same time, and it is rated for 50 hours per charge. The best wired gaming headset for Xbox under $40 is the EKSA Fenrir S ($36.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/eksa-fenrir-s): 50 mm drivers, a 6-foot cable that plugs into the controller's 3.5 mm jack, no battery and no pairing. Check the connection before anything else: Xbox consoles have no Bluetooth audio and reject generic 2.4 GHz dongles, so a wireless headset must say "Xbox Wireless" or ship an Xbox-mode transmitter, while any 3.5 mm headset works through the controller jack.

Between those two sits the Turtle Beach Stealth 600 Gen 3 (Xbox version): 80-hour claimed battery, 83 hours 42 minutes in SoundGuys' test, $109.99 at turtlebeach.com on 2026-11-06. It is the wireless pick for most Xbox owners, with one known weak spot: a firm clamp that SoundGuys scored 3.5 out of 10 for comfort. Microsoft's own Xbox Wireless Headset pairs with no dongle at all, but it lasted 19 hours 18 minutes in the same lab's test, so it needs charging about four times as often.

The Fenrir S is the wired pick because it is the cheapest headset here that reviewers rate well on Xbox. Xbox Tavern scored the same design 8.2 and found the boom mic clear with decent background-noise rejection. Its limits are plain: the cable and mic are fixed, the green LEDs only light when the USB plug is powered (so never from a controller), and EKSA's returns ship to China at your cost. If you want a wired headset with a detachable mic and a 10-year brand track record, the HyperX Cloud III costs more but also plugs into the controller.

What owners complain about

The most common problem is a headset that will not connect at all. Owners of Razer, Corsair and other 2.4 GHz headsets report that the included dongle does nothing on a Series X|S, because Xbox uses Microsoft's own wireless protocol and has no Bluetooth audio. Retailer Q&A pages for the Stealth 600 Gen 3 show the same confusion between the Xbox and PlayStation versions; only the Xbox version works on Xbox. Second is the mic: owners of every wireless model here say teammates hear them as quiet or thin, and the EKSA E900 Pro draws the same "quiet mic" complaint in three reviews. Third is comfort: a tight headband on the Xbox Wireless Headset and a firm clamp on the Stealth 600 Gen 3 cause fatigue after an hour or two. Fourth is reliability: Turtle Beach forum threads describe units that will not power on or stay stuck in pairing, and Xbox Wireless Headset owners report cracked adjustment sliders and peeling ear pads after a year.

Which one by need

  • Best wireless, money no object: SteelSeries Arctis Nova 7X Gen 2. It is the only headset here that plays game audio over the transmitter and phone audio over Bluetooth at once, and the transmitter switch also makes it work on PC and PS5.
  • Best wireless on a budget: Turtle Beach Stealth 600 Gen 3, Xbox version. The battery is the longest tested here and the price was $109.99 at the maker on 2026-11-06. Try the clamp within the return window.
  • No dongle, pairs like a controller: Microsoft Xbox Wireless Headset. Buy it for the convenience and the game/chat dials, not for battery life.
  • Cheapest that works today: EKSA Fenrir S ($36.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/eksa-fenrir-s). Plug it into the controller and you are done. The EKSA E900 Pro adds a detachable mic and two cables for a few dollars more, but reviewers rate its mic quieter.
  • Best wired, step up: HyperX Cloud III. Detachable mic, 3.5 mm into the controller, long-lived build.

Not recommended for Xbox

  • Any headset sold with a plain 2.4 GHz dongle, including EKSA's own E1000 WT, E900WT and Mako BT. EKSA's product pages say their wireless modes are not compatible with any Xbox.
  • The PlayStation versions of the Stealth 600 Gen 3 and the Arctis Nova 7 (sold as the 7P). Same headset, wrong transmitter.
  • Bluetooth-only headphones. The console has no Bluetooth audio output.
  • The USB mode of the EKSA E900 Pro. Use its 3.5 mm cable on Xbox.
