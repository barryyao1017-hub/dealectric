Usable energy and US availability, kit by kit

A battery delivers less than its label. StorageReview drew 1,910 Wh from the Elite 200 V2's 2,073.6 Wh (92%) and 1,030 Wh from the AC180's 1,152 Wh at a 350 W load (89%). Notebookcheck drew about 850 Wh from an EU-model Anker C1000 Gen 2 (83%) at about 250 W. A small overnight load such as 57 W may deliver less than these results, because no named reviewer has measured these units that low. So the hours above are a ceiling, not a promise.

The kits that store solar and feed it back into your home wiring are mostly out of reach in the US. EcoFlow's US page says the STREAM Ultra is sold and installed plug-and-play only in Utah. It also says the unit starts its inverter only after it detects a valid grid signal. In an outage, its two outlets give up to 800 W to appliances that surge below 1,200 W. Stacking 2 to 6 Ultras raises storage to 11.52 kWh, but output to the home stays at 1,200 W. Notebookcheck measured about 90% of a 1.92 kWh STREAM battery's energy coming back out at a steady 500 W, on an EU unit. Zendure's US SolarFlow Kit, which sends up to 900 W to the home, showed as sold out at $3,932 on 2026-11-15. Anker's Solarbank 2 E1600 Plus (1,600 Wh, 800 W to the home) is on Anker's European store, and a search of the US store returned no Solarbank. BLUETTI's Balco 260 (2.56 kWh LFP, up to 2,400 W solar input, 1,200 W AC, per pv magazine's 2026-05-19 launch report) did not appear in a search of BLUETTI's US store on the same day. For how the grid-tied systems work and which states allow them, see balcony solar systems with battery for US buyers.

That leaves the power-station kits as the battery kits a US buyer can order today. They hang a panel off the railing, store the energy and run appliances from their own outlets. They do not lower your bill through home wiring, but they also need no utility approval.

Not recommended

Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 + 100 W. Anker's US store charges $999.99 for it. That works out to $1.18 per measured usable Wh, against $0.78 for the Elite 200 V2 + 2×200 W, which holds more than twice the measured energy. Its single 100 W panel needs 10.2 hours of full sun to fill the battery, so on a balcony it rarely fills in one day.
