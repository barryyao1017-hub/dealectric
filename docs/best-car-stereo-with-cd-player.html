What the differences mean

  • Clean power, Sony vs Pioneer. Both makers rate their units under CTA-2006 at 4 Ω and about 1% THD+N. 20 W / 14 W = 1.43, so the Sony has 43% more power. In volume terms that is 10 × log10(1.43) = 1.5 dB more clean output before distortion. That is small but audible on factory speakers.
  • Why the Pioneer reads '22 W RMS' at some stores. Pioneer's manual gives 22 W × 4 'continuous' measured at 5% THD over 50 Hz–15 kHz. That is a looser test than CTA-2006. 22 / 14 = 1.57, so the looser test adds 57% on paper.
  • Box headlines. 55 W / 20 W = 2.75 times the CTA-2006 rating on the Sony, and 50 W / 14 W = 3.6 times on the Pioneer. The 5 W gap on the box hides a 6 W gap in clean power, and it runs in the Sony's favour.
  • Signal into an add-on amp. The Pioneer's 4.0 V pre-outs against the JVC's listed 2.5 V: 20 × log10(4 / 2.5) = 4.1 dB more signal, so the amp's gain can sit lower and add less hiss.
  • Phone charging from the front USB. The Pioneer supplies 1.5 A and the Sony 1 A (both manuals): 5 V × 1.5 A = 7.5 W against 5 V × 1 A = 5 W.

Power on one standard: why the box numbers mislead

Every CD receiver here prints 50 or 55 W on the box. That 'maximum' figure is a short peak, not what the amp sustains. CTA-2006 is the car-audio standard that pins the rating to continuous output into 4 Ω at no more than 1% distortion. Sony's manual gives the MEX-N5300BT 20 W RMS × 4 under CTA-2006, with an 80 dBA signal-to-noise ratio at 1 W. The manual Pioneer shares across the DEH-S5200BT, DEH-S4200BT and DEH-S4220BT gives 14 W RMS × 4 under CTA-2006. Some retailers list the Pioneer's separate 22 W 'continuous' figure, measured at 5% distortion, as 'RMS'. Sonic Electronix lists the JVC KD-T920BTS at 22 W × 4 RMS without naming the standard, and JVC's own page gives no power figure, so the JVC can't be placed on the same CTA-2006 scale.

Add-ons differ too. Sony's manual lists SiriusXM Connect vehicle tuners SXV100, SXV200 and SXV300 as optional for the MEX-N5300BT, and an RC-SR1 cable for a steering-wheel remote input. JVC's page says the KD-T920BTS is SiriusXM-ready with the tuner sold separately, and that it keeps factory steering-wheel buttons through a third-party adapter, also sold separately. For burned discs, Sony's manual lists MP3, WMA and AAC files from CD-R/CD-RW.
