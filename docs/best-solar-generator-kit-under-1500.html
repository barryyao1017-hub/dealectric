What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. The figures below come from the makers' US stores, read 2026-11-17, and from the named reviewers given beside each number.

  • Price per Wh is the price you pay divided by the rated battery. ALLPOWERS R2500 V2 + 400 W: $913.49 ÷ 1,920 Wh = $0.48. BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 + 2 × 200 W: $1,424.05 ÷ 2,073.6 Wh = $0.69. Jackery Solar Generator 2000 v2: $1,239 ÷ 2,042 Wh = $0.61.
  • Hours of full sun to refill is the battery divided by the kit's panel watts, so it assumes the panels deliver their full rating. ALLPOWERS: 1,920 ÷ 400 = 4.8 h. BLUETTI 2 × 200 W: 2,073.6 ÷ 400 = 5.2 h. Jackery: 2,042 ÷ 200 = 10.2 h.
  • Real panel output is lower. ALLPOWERS's own listing says its polycrystalline SP037 panel gives about 55–65 W per 100 W. At 60%, 1,920 ÷ (400 × 0.6) = 8.0 h, so plan on more than one sunny day to refill from empty.
  • Usable energy. StorageReview measured 1,910 Wh from the Elite 200 V2: $1,424.05 ÷ 1,910 = $0.75 per measured Wh. OutdoorGearLab measured 1,710 Wh from the Jackery Explorer 2000 v2: $1,239 ÷ 1,710 = $0.72. No named lab has published a measured figure for the R2500 V2, so its $0.48 is per rated Wh.

Not recommended

The ALLPOWERS S2000 Pro + SP037 400 W kit looks cheap at $0.63 per rated Wh. StorageReview, however, measured only 920 Wh from the S2000 Pro at a 350 W load, and the unit switched off with 22% still showing. That works out to $917.93 ÷ 920 = $1.00 per measured Wh. The Solar Lab reported that the unit shut off at 55% battery under a 2,250 W load. Both tests used an earlier unit (StorageReview's dates from 2023). For about the same price, the R2500 V2 kit has more battery and the same 400 W panel.
