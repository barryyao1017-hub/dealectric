What women say goes wrong

Trackers too big for small wrists (the theme in every women-specific thread found); heart-rate and sleep readings off when the band sits loose; subscription fatigue (Whoop stops working without its $199–359 a year); cycle predictions that miss for irregular cycles or perimenopause (calendar-based apps are only reliable for steady cycles, PMC8504278); and band breaks or rashes on cheap bands.

Which one by need

Small wrist and a jewelry look: Lily 2 Active, or Fitbit Inspire 3 on a budget. Temperature-based cycle tracking: Apple Watch SE 3 with an iPhone, or Oura Ring 4 if a ring suits you. No fee, under $100, a week or more of battery: Amazfit Active 2. Amazfit's cycle tracking is logging plus calendar prediction, not temperature-based.

Not recommended

Whoop (useless without the subscription), Samsung Galaxy Fit3 for iPhone owners (Android only), and the larger Amazfit Active Max and Balance 2 (48 mm and 47 mm cases) for small wrists.
