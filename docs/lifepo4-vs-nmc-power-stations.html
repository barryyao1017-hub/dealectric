LiFePO4 (LFP) is the better chemistry for almost every portable power station bought today: the makers rate LFP packs at 3,000–6,000 cycles to 80% capacity against about 500 for the NMC packs still on sale, LFP cells tolerate heat better, and every LFP model in the table below carries a 5-year warranty where the NMC models carry 2. NMC's one advantage, more energy per kilogram, barely shows at the product level: the best NMC station here reaches 112 Wh/kg, and three LFP stations (Jackery Explorer 2000 v2, BLUETTI Elite 300, Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2) match or beat it, because the inverter, case and ports weigh the same whatever the cells. The market has already decided: of the 25 current models below, 21 are LFP, and the four NMC ones are either sold out or discontinued on their makers' stores.

What the chemistry changes

LiFePO4 (LFP, lithium iron phosphate)NMC (lithium nickel manganese cobalt)
Cycle life (cell level)2,000–3,000 cycles (PNNL); "2,000 and higher" (Battery University)300–1,000 cycles (PNNL); 1,000–2,000 (Battery University)
Cycle life, as the station makers rate it3,000–6,000 cycles to 80% (BLUETTI, EcoFlow, Anker, ALLPOWERS); 4,000 to 70% (Jackery, DJI)500 cycles (Jackery Explorer 1000, Goal Zero Yeti X); 2,500 claimed by ALLPOWERS for the S2000 Pro
Energy density (cell level)90–120 Wh/kg150–220 Wh/kg
Energy density, whole station53–115 Wh/kg in the table below69–112 Wh/kg in the table below
Thermal runaway onsetabout 270 °Cabout 210 °C; a high state of charge promotes it
Cobalt, nickelNoneBoth
Typical warranty on current stations5 years2 years
Where NMC still winsCold-weather charging below 0 °C (LFP packs refuse to charge; the station's heater or BMS limits apply either way) and the last few hundred grams when the pack dominates the weight, as in small DC-only units.

In practice: a station used daily (van, off-grid cabin, daily UPS) at one full cycle a day wears out an NMC pack rated 500 cycles in under two years and an LFP pack rated 3,500 in about ten. A station used a few weekends a year will not reach either limit; calendar ageing and the warranty matter more, and there LFP's 5 years still wins. The one current NMC model worth considering is the ALLPOWERS S2000 Pro, which ALLPOWERS rates at 2,500 cycles and sells at the lowest price per Wh in the 1.4 kWh class.

How to choose between them

  • Daily use, home backup kept for years, anything on a UPS duty cycle: LFP, and prefer the makers who state the end-of-life capacity with the cycle count (to 80%: BLUETTI, EcoFlow, Anker SOLIX, Goal Zero; to 70%: Jackery, DJI, ALLPOWERS P1800). 4,000 cycles to 70% and 3,000 cycles to 80% describe similar packs.
  • Weight is the constraint: compare Wh/kg at the station level rather than the chemistry. At 1 kWh the lightest are the Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 (99 Wh/kg) and Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 (91), both LFP; at 2 kWh the Jackery Explorer 2000 v2 (114) and Anker C2000 Gen 2 (108). The NMC Yeti 1000X and 1500X are heavier per Wh than every LFP unit of their size.
