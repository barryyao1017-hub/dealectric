What the differences mean

Most LFP power stations stop charging at 32°F. The five above are rated by their makers to charge below it, but the rating says nothing about speed. Dealectric does not test units; every figure here is the maker's or a named tester's, with the date read.

  • Pioneer Na, time to its cold cap at 5°F: 900 Wh × 60% = 540 Wh; 540 Wh ÷ 132 W ≈ 4.1 h from empty (≈ 3.7 h at Zelpio's measured 145 W), before losses.
  • Pioneer Na at 32°F: 540 Wh ÷ 245 W ≈ 2.2 h, against 540 Wh ÷ 1,400 W ≈ 0.4 h at room temperature (ESS News).
  • HomePower 3600 Plus heater: its self-heating switched on at 32°F and drew 50 W, which Outdoor Tech Lab lists as drawn from the battery; at 28°F, 680 W × 3 h = 2,040 Wh (Outdoor Tech Lab).

How charging below freezing actually works

There are two ways a power station charges in the cold. Lithium iron phosphate (LFP) packs need warming first: the HomePower 3600 Plus switched on its self-heating at 32°F, drawing 50 W (Outdoor Tech Lab), and the PECRON E1000LFP heats its pack below 32°F only while AC or DC input is connected (PECRON). A PECRON pack that sits cold-soaked with no input does not heat until charging power arrives. The sodium-ion Pioneer Na takes the charge with the battery still cold, but BLUETTI cuts charge power hard as it gets colder: about 1,400 W at 50-77°F, 245 W at 32°F, 211 W at 23°F, 159 W at 14°F, 132 W at 5°F, and none at −4°F and below (BLUETTI's table, reported by ESS News). Zelpio's freezer test matched that: about 145 W on AC with the battery at 5-6°F, climbing as the battery warmed, and it still ran a full 1,500 W load while ice cold. ESS News also left the unit outdoors overnight below 14°F in a German winter and it discharged normally. Below 14°F it charges only to 60%.

Budget for the cold charge rate, not the temperature floor: at 5°F the Pioneer Na gains roughly 130-145 W whatever is plugged in. $700 on bluettipower.com, $665 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-pioneer-na

Which can charge from solar panels below freezing for winter camping

For a camp where the station sits outside overnight, the Pioneer Na is the one with a measured cold charge: its 500 W solar input (ESS News) is about double what it accepts at 32°F (245 W), and at 14°F and below (159 W) one 200 W panel already exceeds the cold-battery rate, so more panel only helps once the battery warms. ESS News does not say whether BLUETTI's cold table covers solar as well as AC; Zelpio's measurement was on AC. The PECRON E1000LFP's heater runs from DC input too, so solar can warm and then charge it, but its floor is 14°F. Jackery's Explorer 1500 v2 (400 W solar) and 600 v2 (200 W, sold out) are rated to charge at −4°F on Jackery's own spec sheets, with no named cold test published for either. The EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra lists charging to −4°F and a self-heating battery, but its own FAQ also says its batteries cannot be charged in low temperatures and that self-heating costs 10% of each discharge cycle, and the battery plus inverter weigh about 182 lb: a home unit, not a camping one.
