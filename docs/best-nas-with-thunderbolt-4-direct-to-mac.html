What the differences mean

The Thunderbolt link these NAS boxes use is a network link. QNAP states a 20 Gbps maximum, and Liliputing saw Windows report the UGREEN DXP480T Plus link at 20/20 Gbps. The arithmetic below follows from that ceiling.

  • The ceiling: 20 Gbps ÷ 8 = 2,500 MB/s before protocol overhead.
  • TS-h966TX: 2,252 ÷ 2,500 = 90% of the link (QNAP Labs, M4 Pro Mac, U.2 flash).
  • TBS-h574TX: 1,929 ÷ 2,500 = 77% of the raw rate. StorageReview said this 'fully saturated the 20Gbps connection'.
  • DXP480T Plus vs its own 10GbE port: 1.51 ÷ 1.09 = 1.39× faster reading. A 100 GB copy takes 100 ÷ 1.51 = 66 s over Thunderbolt, against 100 ÷ 1.09 = 92 s over 10GbE (Liliputing).
  • DXP6800 Pro with hard drives: its 10GbE port carried 9.5 Gbit/s ÷ 8 ≈ 1,190 MB/s in Notebookcheck's iPerf3 test, about 3× the 396 MB/s its 4-HDD pool delivered over Thunderbolt to an M4 Mac (MacSources). The drives set that speed, not the cable.
  • Price per MB/s of measured Thunderbolt read: DXP480T Plus 849.99 ÷ 1,510 = $0.56; TS-h966TX 1,299 ÷ 2,004 = $0.65; TBS-h574TX 1,799 ÷ 1,850 = $0.97; TVS-h674T 2,499 ÷ 1,740 = $1.44.

What the Thunderbolt link really is, and what reviewers measured on it

A Mac and a NAS are both Thunderbolt hosts, so the cable carries a private IP network. The Mac mounts an SMB share at the NAS's Thunderbolt address and never sees a disk. UGREEN says it plainly: the link 'does not give the computer direct control of the installed drives'. UGREEN supports this on the DXP480T Plus, DXP6800 Pro and DXP8800 Plus with UGOS 1.2.0.2121 or later. It does not combine the two ports into a faster link. UGREEN advertises the port as 40 Gbps, but on the DXP480T Plus Liliputing saw the network link come up at 20/20 Gbps, the same ceiling QNAP prints for its own boxes.

QNAP lists macOS 13.5 or later and Apple silicon support on the TVS-h674T and TBS-h574TX pages. On the TS-h966TX page, QNAP says direct connect is 'fully validated for Intel and AMD-powered USB4 / Thunderbolt 4 systems', yet its own benchmark client on that page was a MacBook Pro M4 Pro on macOS 26.0.

The client matters as much as the NAS. On the same TBS-h574TX and the same 5× E1.S RAID 5 pool, StorageReview measured 1,929 MB/s write from an M1 Max Mac but 1,182 MB/s write and 1,015 MB/s read from a Windows 11 PC. The three all-flash boxes (DXP480T Plus, TBS-h574TX from the Mac, TS-h966TX) measured between 1.42 and 2.25 GB/s over the cable. The one Mac test on hard drives (MacSources, DXP6800 Pro, four HDDs in RAID 10) reached 396 MB/s write and 369 MB/s read. Buy the Thunderbolt link for flash. With hard drives it is a way to skip a switch, not a way to go faster.

Not recommended

Asustor Lockerstor Gen3 and Flashstor Gen2 for this job. They have USB4 ports, but Asustor's knowledge base says the AMD USB4 driver 'only supports external storage devices' and connections to another Lockerstor Gen3 or Flashstor Gen2. A Mac cannot connect to them directly over the cable.
