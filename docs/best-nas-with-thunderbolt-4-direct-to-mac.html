How the Thunderbolt link works

A Thunderbolt NAS does not appear on the Mac as a disk. The cable carries an IP network between the two (Apple's Thunderbolt Bridge, which macOS adds under System Settings → Network, with DHCP or a manual address such as 169.254.1.1 / 169.254.1.2), and the NAS's shares mount over SMB with Finder's Connect to Server. UGREEN's support article lists the DXP480T Plus, DXP6800 Pro and DXP8800 series, says macOS reaches higher speeds than Windows (which needs manual drivers), and one owner reports the NAS reverting a manual Thunderbolt IP after a reboot. Reviewers saw 10–20 Gb/s links depending on the host, so expect 1–2 GB/s, two to four times a 10GbE link, with no switch, adapter or 10GbE card to buy; a Thunderbolt direct-attached array (OWC, LaCie, Promise) mounts as a drive and goes faster, but serves one computer and offers no apps, users or remote access.

Which to buy

For one editing Mac and a flash-only library, the DXP480T Plus at $899.99 undercuts QNAP's TBS-h574TX by roughly $550 with the same processor; for a hard-drive library with Thunderbolt to one Mac and 10GbE to the rest, the DXP6800 Pro ($1,099.99) or DXP8800 Plus ($1,369.99) against QNAP's $1,299 TS-h966TX (with U.2 NVMe bays and QuTS hero's ZFS) and $3,000 TVS-h874T. Two Macs at once on the two ports, and whether the link survives sleep, are not documented by UGREEN.
