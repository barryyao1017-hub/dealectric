How long each bank holds its top speed

The wattage on the box is the peak a bank can reach. What makes it fast is how long it stays there before heat forces it to step down. Smartzone logged the Anker Prime at 53 °C when it dropped from 140 W to 60 W, and at 57 °C when its 280 W two-port mode fell to 100 W. At 100 W it held steady with no drop. ChargerLAB's AOHi stopped on temperature protection at both 140 W (10 min) and 100 W (23 min). Techtest's UGREEN 165W throttled to 65 W because of heat after about half its charge and came back once it cooled. The UGREEN 130W held 100 W until 34% was left, then fell to 65 W (Techtest). ChargerLAB measured a real MacBook Pro 16 (M4 Pro) drawing 133.47 W (27.77 V × 4.81 A) from the AOHi, so the peak is genuine. The problem is how long it lasts. Each lab used its own load setup, so read the minutes as rough comparisons, not exact matches.

140 W only arrives as 28 V × 5 A, the extended power range (EPR) of USB PD 3.1. That needs an EPR (240 W) cable. A 5 A, 100 W cable stops at 20 V, which means 100 W (Texas Instruments' PD summary). Apple fast-charges the 16-inch MacBook Pro at 140 W only over USB-C to MagSafe 3, or over its 240 W USB-C cable on models from November 2023 on. The 14-inch MacBook Pro fast-charges on a 96 W adapter and the MacBook Air on a 70 W one (Apple). That is why the UGREEN 145W bundle with a 240 W cable is the one to buy $84.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-145w-power-bank-240w-cable-bundle, not the same bank bundled with two 100 W cables. UGREEN's current spec lists the 28 V × 5 A, 140 W profile on its first port. The unit Techtest timed in 2023 had a 100 W top port and held 100 W until 29% was left.

Not recommended

The AOHi Starship 240W, if you want long 140 W sessions. ChargerLAB's constant 140 W test ended after 10 minutes and 24.06 Wh on temperature protection, and its 100 W test ended at 23 minutes. It does deliver 79.4–90.5 Wh at 15–45 W loads and ran 118.7 W + 94.2 W from both USB-C ports together (ChargerLAB). That makes it fine for phones, tablets and short laptop top-ups, but not for fast-charging a 16-inch MacBook Pro.
