Best power bank for fast charging

Short answer. The best power bank for fast charging is the AOHi The Future Starship 240W ($119; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/aohi-future-starship-240w-27600mah): 140 W from either USB-C port, a 140 W input that refilled it to 50% in 48 minutes in ChargerLab's bench test, and 27,600 mAh (99.36 Wh), the most you can carry onto a plane. It supports PPS, so Galaxy and Pixel phones get their full 45 W wired speed, which many 100 W banks skip. The outright fastest bank is the Anker Prime Power Bank (26K, 300W): two 140 W ports at the same time and a 250 W dual-cable recharge that Anker rates at 50% in 13 minutes. It costs $229.99 on anker.com (2026-11-06), nearly twice the AOHi, and reviewers say it buys speed, not capacity.

If you only charge a phone, skip the laptop bricks. The Baseus PicoGo AM52 ($59.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/baseus-picogo-am52-qi2-2-10000mah-45w, code AM52 saves $6, pre-applied by the link) weighs 6.9 oz, pushes 45 W by cable (a Galaxy S24 Ultra went from 15% to 90% in 50 minutes in Notebookcheck's test) and 25 W to a Qi2.2 iPhone. If you hate carrying cables, the UGREEN Nexode 165W with built-in cables ($74.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nexode-25000mah-165w-built-in-cables) has a 140 W cable and a 65 W cable attached, recharges at 90 W and costs the least here.

Fast charging has two sides. Output is what the bank gives your device: 140 W for a MacBook Pro 16, 45 W for a Galaxy, 36 W for an iPhone 17. Input is how fast the bank refills itself, and it is the number most listings bury. A 65 W input takes about 2 hours on a 25,000 mAh bank; 140 W takes under an hour to 50%.

What owners complain about

Across reviews, Q&A threads and lab tests, the same eight problems come up. The phone never reaches the advertised speed was the most common: a bank labelled 45 W or 100 W that lacks PPS drops a Galaxy or Pixel to standard 15-25 W charging, and a Nothing Phone owner saw a PPS-labelled 130 W bank fall back to 14 W. Second was the bank itself taking too long to refill: 65 W inputs mean about 2 hours for a 25,000 mAh bank, and one owner reported 48 hours on a weak wall adapter. Third was heat: ChargerLab's constant-load test tripped the AOHi's thermal cutoff after 10 minutes at 140 W, Anker's own FAQ says a drained Prime may refuse to recharge until its cells cool, and the Baseus AM52 slows its wireless pad when the phone gets warm. Port sharing came next: plug in a second device and the first drops, to 100 W on the AOHi and UGREEN 200W, to 120 W total on the UGREEN 165W with three devices, and to 15 W total on the AM52 with all three outputs. Owners also flag weight (the 140 W banks are 1.2-1.4 lb), the need for a 5 A E-marked cable above 100 W, usable capacity around 76-90% of the label, and a handful of dead-on-arrival units for both Anker and AOHi.

Which one by need

Laptop, fastest for the money: AOHi The Future Starship 240W. Same 140 W single-port speed as the Anker Prime, a 140 W recharge, PPS for Android phones, 18-month warranty. Laptop, fastest, price no object: Anker Prime Power Bank (26K, 300W). Two 140 W ports at once and 50% back in 13 minutes with two cables; also the only one with a Bluetooth app to set per-port limits. Two laptops at once: the Anker Prime (140 W + 140 W) or the UGREEN Nexode 200W (100 W + 100 W); the UGREEN costs less but refills at only 65 W. Travel, no cables to pack: UGREEN Nexode 165W with built-in cables, 90 W recharge, cheapest here. Phone only: Baseus PicoGo AM52, 6.9 oz, 45 W wired, 25 W Qi2.2 wireless on iPhone 16 and 17, cool-running in Notebookcheck's test.

Not recommended

10,000 mAh banks with 140 W or 210 W outputs for laptops, including AOHi's own Starship Pro 210W: 36 Wh empties into a MacBook Pro 16 in about 17 minutes. Buy them for phones or not at all. 22.5 W banks for Galaxy or Pixel phones: they trigger standard charging, not Super Fast Charging 2.0; look for PPS on the spec sheet. Any bank with an input under 65 W if you recharge it every night, and the UGREEN Nexode 200W if refill speed is your main concern: its 65 W input is the slowest in this group despite the 200 W output. The Anker Prime charging base bundle adds a lot of cost for a 150 W dock when the bank already takes 250 W by cable.
