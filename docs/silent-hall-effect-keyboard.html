What "45 gf", "0.06 ms" and "0.005 mm" measure

Force. Makers label switches on different points of the press. Gamakay's 45 ± 5 gf for Phoenix V3 is the force at 2.0 mm of travel; the spec table HLPlanet reproduces gives 35 gf minimum at the start and 58 gf maximum at the end. Wooting names its Lekker switches by bottom-out force: the L60 is 40 gf to 60 gf and the L45 is 30 gf to 45 gf. On the same basis the silent Phoenix V3 (35 to 58 gf) sits beside the L60, not the L45, with 0.5 mm less travel.

Latency. An 8 kHz board sends a report every 1 / 8000 s = 0.125 ms. The "0.125 ms" that Wooting and Epomaker print is that interval. Gamakay's "0.06 ms" for the TK75HE V2 is shorter than one report interval, so it cannot be the time from key press to PC. HLPlanet measured the sibling TK75 TMR with the Open Source Latency Testing Tool at 0.60 ms wired and 0.75 ms on 2.4 GHz. That is 0.60 / 0.125 = 4.8 report intervals, ten times the printed 0.06 ms (0.60 / 0.06), and the dongle adds 0.15 / 0.60 = 25%. The measurement is for the TK75 TMR, not the TK75HE V2. Printed latency does not separate the boards in the table: all six are 8 kHz.

Rapid trigger step. 0.005 mm (Gamakay), 0.01 mm (Epomaker) and 0.1 mm (Wooting) are how finely the setting can be typed in, not how small a movement is read reliably. On the TK75 TMR, HLPlanet found release settings below about 0.3 mm could misread neighbouring keys when the plate flexes under a firm press, and suggests about 0.4 mm. That is 0.4 / 0.005 = 80 of Gamakay's steps.

How quiet. None of the sources below publishes a sound-meter reading for these boards, so the quiet claim rests on switch design and on named listeners. AppleInsider's Wesley Hilliard tested the TK75HE V2 with Phoenix Silent switches and wrote that they "live up to the name", giving "a solid 'thunk' instead of a 'clack'"; he rated the board 2.5 out of 5 for its plastics and Mac software, not its noise. HLPlanet's Marcus Richardson called Phoenix V3 in the TK75 TMR "the kind of silent where background noise is louder than the keys", and his guide calls Phoenix V3 in the TK75HE V2 or NS75 "essentially the only mainstream silent magnetic option available".

Not recommended

Epomaker HE68 Lite, if silence is the goal. Its five layers of foam and pads sit under a standard Clear Mag switch, and Epomaker's page names no silent switch for it and no other magnetic switches that fit. It is the lowest-priced board here, but foam damps the case, not the key hitting the bottom of the switch.

Gamakay "Hall Effect v1" Phoenix switches as spares for a V2 board. Gamakay still sells them from $3.99, and its own listing says: "The V1 Magnetic switch- Mercury and Phoenix only work for the TK75HE V1." Spares or a swap set for the TK75HE V2, NS75 or another maker's board need the "Hall Effect V3" listing.
