What the differences mean

Every board in the table carries an 8,000 Hz label, so the label cannot rank them. The figures below are worked from the measurements and the makers' own numbers.

  • 8,000 Hz is an interval, not a latency. 1,000 ms / 8,000 = 0.125 ms between reports. HLPlanet's wired reading for the TK75 TMR is 0.60 ms, and 0.60 / 0.125 = 4.8 report intervals from keycap contact to the PC.
  • The dongle costs time on both measured boards. TK75 TMR: 0.75 / 0.60 = 1.25, so 25% more than on the cable. RT75 Pro: 1.01 / 0.65 = 1.55, so 55% more.
  • The gap between the two measured boards is small. Wired: 0.65 - 0.60 = 0.05 ms. On 2.4 GHz: 1.01 - 0.75 = 0.26 ms. One frame at 240 Hz lasts 1,000 / 240 = 4.17 ms, so 0.26 / 4.17 = 6% of a frame. It decides the pick on paper; it will not change how a match feels.
  • 8,000 Hz did not help the HE75 V2 in KitGuru's test. 15.96 - 14.46 = 1.50 ms slower at 8,000 Hz than the wired 1,000 Hz reading (KitGuru does not state the connection for the 8,000 Hz figure); wireless at 1,000 Hz was 16.54 - 14.46 = 2.08 ms behind the cable. These are end-to-end figures with the PC and display included, so they cannot be set against the OSLTT figures.
  • Makers' latency claims are not key-to-PC times. Gamakay's 0.06 ms is 0.06 / 0.125 = 0.48 of one report interval. Epomaker's 0.125 ms for the HE80 is exactly one interval. Yunzii's own 1 ms wireless figure is 1 / 0.125 = 8 intervals, and HLPlanet's 1.01 ms agrees with it.
  • Step size. The HE80's 0.02 mm step is 0.02 / 0.005 = 4 times coarser than the 0.005 mm stated for the HE75 V2, NS75 and TK75HE V2. The TK75 TMR's 0.01 mm is 2 times coarser than 0.005 mm.

The measurements, collected

Dealectric does not test units. Every latency figure on this page comes from a named reviewer or is marked as the maker's claim.

  • HLPlanet (Marcus Richardson), OSLTT. The Open Source Latency Testing Tool times a press from keycap contact to the signal reaching the computer. TK75 TMR, review dated 2026-05-18: 0.60 ms wired, 0.75 ms on 2.4 GHz; the review does not give a run count. RT75 Pro, review dated 2026-01-02: 0.65 ms wired, 1.01 ms on 2.4 GHz, each an average of 10 tests. Neither review gives a Bluetooth figure, and the two were published four months apart.
  • KitGuru (Carlos Ribeiro), 240 fps camera. HE75 V2, review dated 2026-09-09: 14.46 ms wired and 16.54 ms wireless at 1,000 Hz, 15.96 ms at 8,000 Hz. KitGuru also reports an 8,000 mAh battery and 2.4 GHz, Bluetooth and USB-C connections.
  • HLPlanet on the MCHOSE Jet 75. Review dated 2026-09-24: "<1ms latency & 8KHz polling rate", wired only, with no exact figure.
  • NS75, TK75HE V2 and HE80. The only latency figure for each is its maker's, shown as such in the table.

TMR against Hall. The pick reads its magnetic switches with tunnel magnetoresistance sensors. Epomaker, describing its own TMR board, puts the difference this way: Hall effect sensors rely on voltage, while TMR sensors measure changes in resistance. The TK75 TMR uses the same kind of magnetic switches as the Hall boards here and has rapid trigger. It also accepts 5-pin mechanical switches; HLPlanet notes rapid trigger should be turned off on any key fitted with one. If the sensor must be a Hall sensor, the RT75 Pro is the board here with a bench figure, and the NS75 and TK75HE V2 are the Hall effect boards we link.

Where the line was drawn. Every row cost $100 or less on 2026-11-21, and each maker calls its board 75% (81 keys on the RT75 Pro, 82 on the HE80, 80 keys plus the knob's two on the HE75 V2). The TK75 TMR and TK75HE V2 are under the line at Gamakay's Black Friday prices, which end on 2026-11-30; both list above $100, so check the price after that date. The NS75 lists under $100. Left out for being just over: the Epomaker HE75 V2 TMR at $109.65 (maker, 2026-11-21). AULA's Hero 75 HE and F75 HE are left out because AULA's US store could not be read on 2026-11-21 and this page lists only prices read that day. The Jet 75 price is the amazon.com listing for the Black Topographic version, read the same day.

Not recommended

The older Epomaker HE75 Mag, which is easy to mistake for the HE75 V2. Notebookcheck's review lists it as "8 kHz via wired and 2.4 GHz (1,000 Hz max tested via wireless)" and reports that repeated inputs were frequent on 2.4 GHz and Bluetooth. If you want an Epomaker 75% board with wireless, the HE75 V2 is the one in the table.

Paying extra for an 8K label alone. On the one board here tested at both rates, KitGuru measured 8,000 Hz 1.50 ms slower than 1,000 Hz.
