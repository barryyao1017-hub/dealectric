What the differences mean

  • Battery energy: UGREEN rates the 20,000 mAh bank at 72 Wh and the 25,000 mAh bank at 90 Wh (25,000 mAh × 3.6 V = 90 Wh). Both are under 100 Wh, the line above which an airline must approve a power bank in advance.
  • Laptop recharges: a 13-inch MacBook Air (M5) has a 53.8 Wh battery (Apple), so 72 ÷ 53.8 = 1.3 and 90 ÷ 53.8 = 1.7 full charges at rated capacity, before conversion losses.
  • Bank recharge time: both banks take 65 W in. At best, 72 Wh ÷ 65 W = 1.1 h and 90 Wh ÷ 65 W = 1.4 h, plus the slowdown near full.
  • Laptop port: the 25,000 mAh bank's C1 delivers 28 V × 5 A = 140 W. A 100W cable is limited to 20 V × 5 A = 100 W, which is why that kit uses the bundle with the 240W cable.

Power banks on planes: the 2026 rules change how you pack

From 27 March 2026, the ICAO rules for international aviation allow two power banks per passenger and ban recharging them during flights (ICAO). AirlineRatings summarizes the limits: under 100 Wh needs no special permission, 100–160 Wh needs the airline's approval in advance, over 160 Wh is banned, and power banks can't go in checked bags (TSA also requires them in carry-on). AirlineRatings adds that they can't go in overhead bins either. Airlines can be stricter:

  • Southwest: one portable charger per passenger from April 20, 2026, and it may not be recharged from in-seat power outlets (NBC New York, April 8, 2026).
  • Lufthansa Group (including SWISS, Austrian, Brussels Airlines and Eurowings): since 15 January 2026, power banks may not be used or charged at any point during the flight. Passengers are told to use the seat's USB ports or power outlets instead (AirlineRatings, January 21, 2026).

What this means for any kit: charge the bank to full at the hotel or the gate. Pack the wall charger and both cables in your carry-on, not the checked bag, because on board the seat outlet charges the laptop or phone directly. Each kit here has one bank under 100 Wh, which meets Southwest's one-bank limit and the ICAO two-bank limit with no airline approval.
