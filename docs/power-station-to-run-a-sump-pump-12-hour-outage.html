What a 1/3 HP pump draws

BLUETTI's own sump-pump guide puts a 1/3 HP pump at 600–800 W running and 1,200–2,900 W starting; retailer nameplates give the Zoeller M53 9.7 A running and 25.9 A locked rotor (about 1,160 W and 3,100 W at 120 V) and the Superior Pump 92341 4.1 A running and 19 A starting (about 490 W and 2,300 W). Manufacturers' own locked-rotor figures were not readable, and Wayne CDU790 listings disagree with each other. The start pulse lasts a fraction of a second, but an inverter must supply it: BLUETTI's manuals describe Power Lifting as a resistive-load mode (heaters, kettles) and BLUETTI staff have said inductive surge can be 3–5× running, so size against continuous watts, which is why the AC180 is marginal and the Elite 300 or R4000 are comfortable.

Duty cycle and the runtime table

Pump duty depends on inflow: BLUETTI's pages assume 5 minutes an hour in light rain (8 %) and 10–20 minutes an hour in heavy rain (17–33 %); owners in storms report a cycle every 2–3 minutes. Our table assumes 800 W running, 15 W of station self-draw with the inverter on (BLUETTI's own assumption; one review measured about 10 W) and 85 % of rated watt-hours usable: 12 hours needs about 1,140 Wh at 10 % duty and about 2,580 Wh at 25 %. BLUETTI's own estimate for the Elite 300 is 12–18 hours and for the Elite 200 V2 8–12, which matches the 25 % column. Measure your own pit before a storm season: time the cycles for an hour in rain.

Running it unattended

Leave the station plugged into the wall with the pump plugged into the station; the UPS mode switches over in 8–20 ms, far faster than a motor notices, with no one home. BLUETTI says pass-through charging is safe and heat is the ageing risk, but one owner reported an AC180P shutting down during an outage while in pass-through, so pull the plug once and watch the pump run. A station has no float switch or high-water alarm of its own: keep a separate alarm.
