What the differences mean

A sump pump only draws power while it runs, but the station's inverter stays on for the whole outage. So every hour you pay for the pump's run time at the station's efficiency, plus the idle draw for the minutes it sits waiting. At 25% duty (15 minutes an hour), an 800 W pump averages 200 W:

  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3: 200 ÷ 0.921 + 0.75 × 34 W = 242.7 Wh per hour, and 4,096 Wh ÷ 242.7 = 16.9 h.
  • Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus: 200 ÷ 0.898 + 0.75 × 38 W = 251.2 Wh per hour, and 3,840 Wh ÷ 251.2 = 15.3 h.
  • BLUETTI Elite 300: 200 ÷ 0.886 + 0.75 × 19 W = 240.0 Wh per hour, and 3,014.4 Wh ÷ 240.0 = 12.6 h.
  • PECRON F3000LFP: 200 ÷ 0.83 + 0.75 × 37 W = 268.7 Wh per hour, and 3,072 Wh ÷ 268.7 = 11.4 h. That is short of 12.
  • Highest duty for 12 hours: duty = (rated Wh ÷ 12 − idle) ÷ (pump W ÷ efficiency − idle). For the DELTA Pro 3 that is (341.3 − 34) ÷ (868.6 − 34) = 36.8%, about 22 minutes an hour. The F3800 Plus reaches 33.1% (about 20 min), the Elite 300 26.3% (about 16 min) and the PECRON 23.6% (about 14 min).
  • Some pumps draw more than 800 W. Tested Watts gives the Zoeller M53, a common 1/3 HP pump, at 9.7 A × 115 V = 1,116 W running. At 25% duty, the DELTA Pro 3 lasts 279 ÷ 0.921 + 25.5 = 328.4 Wh per hour, so 4,096 ÷ 328.4 = 12.5 h. The F3800 Plus lasts 11.3 h, the Elite 300 9.2 h and the PECRON 8.4 h. Read the amps off your pump's label before you size the station.
  • A literal 13 hp motor: 13 × 746 W = 9,698 W of shaft power. That is more than the highest continuous output here, the F3800 Plus at 6,000 W, so no station on this page can run it.

The DELTA Pro 3 wins on measured runtime, but EcoFlow's US store lists it as currently unavailable. Anker's store also shows the F3800 Plus sold out. Of the four stations, only the Elite 300 can be ordered from its maker today and still lasts 12 hours on these measurements.

Idle draw and measured usable energy: why rated Wh overstates the hours

Runtime tables usually divide rated Wh by the pump's average draw, take off about 15%, and ignore the inverter. Over a 12-hour outage at 25% duty the pump is off for 9 hours, and the inverter is still drawing power the whole time. At the PECRON's measured 37 W, that is 9 × 37 = 333 Wh, more than 10% of its 3,072 Wh. The Elite 300 draws about half as much at idle (19 W measured by The Technology Man, about 15 W by The Solar Lab), so it loses only 171 Wh over the same 9 hours. That is how a 3,014 Wh station outlasts a 3,072 Wh one.

Measured usable energy also differs from the label. Solar Power Auditor drained the DELTA Pro 3 to 3,772 Wh (92.1%) and the F3800 Plus to 3,448 Wh (89.8%), both on a constant 300 W load. StorageReview drew 2.67 kWh (88.6%) from a US Elite 300, and Notebookcheck got 2,650 Wh from the EU version at about 200 W. The Solar Lab measured the PECRON at 83% efficiency at full load.

Turn the station's power-saving mode off before the storm. Between cycles the pump draws 0 W, and BLUETTI's AC300 manual says its ECO mode switches AC off when the load stays under 30 W for 4 hours. The Technology Man found that the Elite 300's Eco mode turns AC off with no load attached. With Eco on, the outlet can be dead when the float switch closes. With Eco off, the idle draw above comes out of your runtime, which is why the hours on this page include it.

Starting the pump is a separate limit. Tested Watts puts the M53's start at 25.9 A × 115 V = 2,979 W. That is above the Elite 300's 2,400 W rating, and StorageReview says its 4,800 W lifting mode is for resistive loads only, not motors. None of the overload tests above used a pump motor. If your pump's start current times 115 V is over 2,400 W, choose the DELTA Pro 3 or F3800 Plus. On the F3800 Plus, plug the pump into one of the three leftmost 20 A outlets: Anker says only those switch over automatically, in 20 ms.

Not recommended

PECRON F3000LFP. With The Solar Lab's measured 37 W idle draw and 83% efficiency, it lasts 11.4 h with an 800 W pump at 25% duty, short of a 12-hour outage. With a 1,116 W pump it lasts 8.4 h. In The Solar Lab's surge test it held 4,500 W for about 10 seconds, then tripped and had to be restarted. PECRON's store also lists it as a pre-order shipping in early December.
