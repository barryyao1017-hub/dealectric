What the differences mean

Idle energy per day is the idle draw times 24 hours. For the Sigineer APC6024D: 80 W × 24 h = 1,920 Wh. One Redodo 24V 100Ah battery holds 2,560 Wh, so that is 1,920 ÷ 2,560 = 75% of the battery gone before any appliance runs.

Power-saver mode cuts the loss. The Sigineer drops to 15 W × 24 h = 360 Wh (14%). The Sun Gold 6000W 24V drops to 29 W × 24 h = 696 Wh (27%). With saver on, the Sigineer loses about 336 Wh a day less.

Battery current at full output is watts ÷ (24 V × 0.88 rated efficiency). For a 6,000 W unit that is 6,000 ÷ 21.1 ≈ 284 A. A Redodo 24V 100Ah has a 100 A BMS, so running 6,000 W takes three in parallel (300 A). Those three hold 7.68 kWh, and the 1.92 kWh idle loss is 25% of that.

Per leg, Sigineer allows 3,000 W ÷ 120 V = 25 A between each hot and neutral. The full 6,000 W is available only to 240 V loads.

Cost per continuous watt: $949 ÷ 6,000 W = $0.158 for the Sigineer, against $1,220 ÷ 6,000 W = $0.203 for the Sun Gold.

Idle draw: the load that never switches off

All five are low-frequency units built around a large transformer, and each draws power whenever it is on, even with nothing plugged in. On a 48 V bank that loss is spread over a larger battery. A 24 V system is usually one or two batteries, so a 77–80 W idle on a 6,000 W unit takes most of a day's charge. That is why power-saver mode decides the pick. In saver mode the unit sleeps and pulses to look for a load. AIMS says its unit pulses every 30 seconds and wakes only for a load of 25 W or more. Anything that must run all night below that threshold, such as a router or a CPAP, keeps the inverter at full idle draw. Plan for the 'saver off' column in that case.

All of these units also need 240 V to charge. Sun Gold says its 24 V split-phase units 'require 240VAC input'. Sigineer gives a 160–264 V input range, so a 120 V generator is below the 160 V minimum. AIMS says its charger accepts 'any source of 240V AC power'. A 120 V-only inverter generator will not charge any of them.
