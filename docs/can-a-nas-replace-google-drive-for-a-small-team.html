What the differences mean

  • Files per person. DS225+: 100,000 ÷ 5 users = 20,000 files each. DS425+: 500,000 ÷ 5 = 100,000 each.
  • Against a Google shared drive. 100,000 ÷ 500,000 = one-fifth. A DS225+ is rated for one-fifth of the items one Google shared drive can hold. The DS425+ and DS925+ match it.
  • How long history lasts on a busy file. At 10 saves a day, Synology's 32 versions cover 32 ÷ 10 ≈ 3 days. UGREEN's default of 8 covers 8 ÷ 10 = under a day. Google keeps up to 100 newer versions, which is 100 ÷ 10 = 10 days, and its 30-day limit doesn't apply until later.
  • Three years of Workspace. 5 seats × $14 × 36 months = $2,520.
  • Three years of running a DS425+. Power at Synology's access rating: 28.25 W × 8,760 h = 247.5 kWh a year × 18.16¢ = about $45 a year, or $135 over three years. Offsite copy of 1 TB on Backblaze B2: $6.95 × 36 = $250. Before buying the NAS and drives, that totals about $385.
  • Hardware budget before the NAS costs more than Workspace. $2,520 − $385 = about $2,135 for the NAS plus drives, over three years. Workspace also includes email and video calls, which this sum leaves out.

Count your files and save habits before you buy

Synology publishes a Synology Drive rating for each NAS model. The hosted-file count covers every file Synology Drive indexes or hosts. Synology says going over it 'will not block application operations, but may result in longer response time'. The two models that differ most are the DS225+ and DS425+. They have the same Celeron J4125 and 2 GB of memory, yet Synology rates the DS425+ for twice the users (20 against 10) and five times the files (500,000 against 100,000). For a team of five, the file count, not the number of people, decides between them. Google counts files, folders, shortcuts and trashed items toward its 500,000-item limit per shared drive. Count your team's files the same way before you choose.

The products also keep version history differently. Synology Drive keeps up to 32 versions of each file and rotates them over 7, 30, 60 or 120 days, first-in-first-out, or with Intelliversioning, which keeps the significant versions. Admins set this policy per Team Folder. UGREEN keeps 8 versions by default, raisable to 32. When two people edit the same file, UGREEN keeps the newer file and renames the older one rather than merging them. Google keeps an uploaded file's versions for up to 30 days or until there are 100 newer versions, and you can pin any version with 'Keep forever'. Nextcloud's default is time-based instead. It keeps one version per hour for the first day, one per day for 30 days, then one per week, using up to half of the user's free space. On a NAS, frequently saved files need a longer safety net than 32 versions, so add scheduled snapshots: the DS425+ allows 128 per shared folder.

Synology Office spreadsheets are also limited by memory. With less than 4 GB of RAM a sheet can have 2,000,000 cells, and with 4 GB or more it can have 5,000,000. The DS425+ ships with 2 GB, so a team with large Sheets should add a 4 GB module (6 GB maximum).

Not recommended

The Synology DS223j is rated for 5 Synology Drive users and 5 Synology Office users, with 1 GB of memory. A five-person team uses 5 ÷ 5 = 100% of that rating from the first day, with no room for a new hire. Its file rating is also 100,000, the same as the DS225+.
