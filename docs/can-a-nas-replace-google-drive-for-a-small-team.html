Short answer. Files yes, Docs no: a NAS can replace Google Drive for a 5-person remote team's shared files, versions and links, not Docs co-editing, Gmail or Calendar. If the team has an admin, buy the UGREEN NASync DXP2800 ($389.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nasync-dxp2800), $389.99 diskless on 2026-11-03, and run Nextcloud in Docker; a non-technical team gets the polished route from Synology Drive plus Office; a truly remote team should keep Workspace Standard ($2,520 for five over three years), because the host's home upload (20–50 Mbps, 3–7 minutes a gigabyte) is the price of any NAS.

What to know: Workspace Starter ($7, 30 GB a user) cannot hold a 1 TB working set, so Standard (2 TB pooled per user) is the comparison, $2,520 over three years at list; a NAS with two 4 TB drives and Backblaze B2 for the off-site copy ($6.95 per TB a month, about $250 for 1 TB over three years) lands near that once drives are counted, so the saving is small. Synology Drive keeps up to 32 versions per file with on-demand sync on Mac and Windows, Synology Office co-edits docx/xlsx/pptx in real time, and the DS225+ (about $340–365, 2 GB RAM, rated for about 10 Drive users) has immutable snapshots on supported models. On UGOS the sync client does two-way or one-way sync with deletions propagated, snapshots are folder-level Btrfs with no documented immutability, 2FA does not cover SMB logins, UGREENlink relays through UGREEN's servers at an unpublished speed, and Nextcloud (with Collabora for web editing) is a Portainer, DDNS and reverse-proxy build from a third-party guide. Hosted Nextcloud on Hetzner Storage Share is €4.29 a month for 1 TB (about €154 over three years), unlimited users, but Hetzner reportedly cannot run the built-in Collabora, so document editing needs a separate server.

What the team keeps and what it gives up

A NAS gives the team a shared file tree with per-user permissions, sync clients on each laptop, file versions and share links, and a 4 TB mirror for the price of one year of Workspace Standard. It does not give real-time Docs and Sheets editing (Synology Office and Nextcloud with Collabora are the substitutes, and neither is Google Docs), a bundled Gmail and Calendar on the company domain, effortless sharing with outside clients, Vault, or Google's uptime and zero-admin updates. Most teams that move end up on a hybrid: Workspace Starter for mail and Docs, the NAS for the files that fill the quota.

The remote-work penalty

The NAS lives in one person's home, and every colleague's download rides that home's upload link: at 20 Mbps a 1 GB file takes about seven minutes, at 40 Mbps about three, on 500 Mbps fibre about 16 seconds. Vendor relays (QuickConnect, UGREENlink) add their own cap, which is why Synology's own guidance tells Drive users to use DDNS or a direct connection and why Tailscale on every laptop is the usual fix. If no one on the team has fast upload, the NAS is the wrong tool for a distributed team however good the box.

Security is now your job

Turn on 2FA for every account (on UGOS it does not cover SMB), auto-block, and snapshots on a schedule; keep an off-site copy because sync clients propagate deletions and encrypted files to the NAS. Synology's immutable snapshots (DSM 7.2, model-dependent) survive a stolen admin login; UGOS's do not claim to. Someone has to apply firmware updates and replace a failed drive; with Workspace or Hetzner that is the vendor's job.
