What the differences mean

Screen brightness in foot-lamberts (ft-L) is the projector's lumens × the screen's gain ÷ the screen's area in square feet. ProjectorCentral gives the SMPTE dark-room target as 16 ft-L, with 12–22 ft-L allowed. Aurzen's 120-inch 16:9 screen covers 120² × 0.0029674 = 42.7 sq ft at 1.1 gain.

  • Lumens needed for 16 ft-L on that screen: 16 × 42.7 ÷ 1.1 = 621 lm. Even the 12 ft-L floor needs 466 lm.
  • EAZZE D1 MAX: 1,000 × 1.1 ÷ 42.7 = 25.7 ft-L. That is above the 22 ft-L ceiling, so there is headroom for dusk or a porch light. The figure uses Aurzen's rating, not a lab measurement.
  • EAZZE D1: 200 × 1.1 ÷ 42.7 = 5.1 ft-L on the full screen. It reaches 16 ft-L at √(200 × 1.1 ÷ 16 ÷ 0.0029674) = 68 in. Use its 50–100% zoom to shrink the picture to about 70 in.
  • MoGo 4 in Eco: 151 × 1.1 ÷ 42.7 = 3.9 ft-L, about a quarter of the target.
  • Cost on aurzen.com, 2026-11-14: D1 MAX $349.99 + screen $24.99 = $374.98. D1 $149.99 + screen $24.99 = $174.98.

Why battery hours and brightness don't add up

XGIMI rates the MoGo 4 for “up to 2.5 hours video playtime” in Eco Mode and notes that leaving Eco shortens playtime. Its 450 ISO lumen figure is measured in Performance or High Power mode. Nikki Kahl at ProjectorCentral measured 400–404 lm in Performance, 258–270 lm in Standard and 151–154 lm in Eco, and Eco lasted about 2.5 h. That means 151 ÷ 404 = 37% of the plugged-in light for the advertised runtime. The same pattern shows up on other battery projectors. Steve Smith at SlashGear found the Yaber T2 Plus drops its brightness setting from 100 to 60 on battery. Test-Achats measured that projector at 292 ANSI lm in normal mode and 202 in Eco. The Hook Up measured the NEBULA Capsule 3 at 161 lm plugged in and 112 lm in Eco, which lasted 2 h 53 min. The rule: take the Eco-mode lumens, then choose the screen size. At 16 ft-L on a 1.1-gain screen, 151 lm fills about 59 in.

Mounting matters for a grab-and-go setup. Aurzen says the D1 MAX has no 1/4-inch threaded hole and needs a ceiling mount with 4× M5 screws, so outdoors it sits on a table or box, not a tripod. ProjectorCentral lists a 1.28:1 throw ratio. A 120-inch 16:9 image is 104.6 in wide, so the lens sits 1.28 × 104.6 = 134 in (about 11 ft) from the screen. The D1 does fit Aurzen's $39.99 foldable stand, which lists it by name.

Not recommended

A battery projector on a 120-inch screen. In Eco, the MoGo 4 gives 3.9 ft-L and the Capsule 3 gives 2.9 ft-L on that screen, both under a quarter of the 16 ft-L target. Both are fine at 50–60 in. The same goes for running the EAZZE D1 at the full 120 inches (5.1 ft-L): zoom it down to about 70 in or step up to the D1 MAX.
