Best portable projector with screen

Short answer. The best portable projector with screen is the Aurzen EAZZE D1 ($149.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/aurzen-eazze-d1-smart-projector) paired with the Aurzen 120-inch screen ($24.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/aurzen-120-inch-portable-projector-screen): native 1080p, 200 ANSI lumens, licensed Netflix, Prime Video and YouTube built in, auto focus, and a 1.1-gain 16:9 screen, under $175 for the pair. For a backyard crowd at dusk, step up to the Aurzen EAZZE D1 MAX ($349.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/aurzen-eazze-d1-max-google-tv-projector) with the same screen: 1,000 SGS-certified ANSI lumens, Google TV, about $375 for the pair.

The cheap in-the-box bundles that fill Amazon's results, such as the TMY V08 with 80-inch screen, are native 720p, 95 lumens by the maker's own page, and have no streaming apps.

Throw distance for a 120-inch image is about 11.8 ft for the D1 and 11.2 ft for the D1 MAX. For 100 inches, about 9.8 ft and 9.3 ft.

What owners complain about

Across reviews, forum threads and retailer feedback, the same five problems repeat. Dimness leads: budget bundles advertise thousands of "lux" or lumens, but TechGearLab measured a TMY mini at 119 ANSI lumens against a 7,500 claim, and even Aurzen D1 owners say it needs a dark room. Second is the fine print on resolution: most sub-$100 bundles are native 720p with "1080p supported" in the title. Third is the screen itself: fold-flat sheet screens arrive creased, and a cloth screen hung close to a wall lets light through and bounces it back, which blurs the image. Fourth, cheap bundles have no apps, so you add a streaming stick and its power cable. Fifth, fan noise and soft corners after keystone correction, reported on the D1 and the D1 MAX at full brightness.

Which one by need

Dark-room movie nights under $200: the Aurzen EAZZE D1 with the 120-inch screen. It is the cheapest native 1080p projector with licensed Netflix we found, and the 24-month warranty is twice what the D1 MAX gets.

Backyard at dusk or a room with some light: the Aurzen EAZZE D1 MAX with the same screen. Its 1,000 ANSI lumens are SGS-certified, five times the D1, though RTINGS still found it loses to glare and runs a loud fan at maximum brightness. Keep it for after sunset.

No outlet, camping, tailgates: the Yaber T2 Plus, sold on Amazon and yaber.com. It has a built-in battery rated 2.5 hours, native 1080p, dual 8 W JBL speakers and a Google TV dongle, and B&H has sold it as a kit with an 80-inch screen. Note the spec table says 350 ISO lumens while marketing says 450 ANSI, and it weighs 5.5 lb.

Not recommended

The TMY V08 with 80-inch screen and similar in-the-box bundles: the maker's own page lists native 720p and 95 lumens, and there are no apps. The Sharper Image Portable Bluetooth Projector and Screen publishes no brightness or resolution figure at all, which is reason enough to pass. The Aurzen ZIP tri-fold is clever but rated 100 ANSI lumens, and Gizmodo measured about 45 minutes of battery at full brightness, so it cannot fill a 100-inch screen.
