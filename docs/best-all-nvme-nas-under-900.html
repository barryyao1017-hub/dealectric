What the differences mean

A 10GbE port carries 1.25 GB/s (10 Gb/s ÷ 8). One PCIe 3.0 lane moves about 0.985 GB/s and one PCIe 4.0 lane about 1.969 GB/s. These are the per-lane conversions ComputingForGeeks uses.

  • Asustor Flashstor 6 Gen2: 4 + 2 + 2 + 2 + 1 + 1 = 12 Gen4 lanes; 12 × 1.969 ≈ 23.6 GB/s to the drives. Even its x1 slot (1.97 GB/s) is faster than the 10GbE port.
  • UGREEN DXP480T Plus: 2 × 7.9 GB/s (x4 Gen4) + 2 × 1.97 GB/s (x2 Gen3) ≈ 19.7 GB/s. Every slot is faster than 10GbE.
  • TerraMaster F8 SSD Plus: 6 Gen3 lanes × 0.985 ≈ 5.9 GB/s across eight slots. One x1 slot is 0.985 ÷ 1.25 = 79% of 10GbE, so a transfer needs two drives striped to fill the port. Drives 5–8 share one 2-lane uplink: 2 × 0.985 ≈ 1.97 GB/s between them.
  • TerraMaster F4 SSD: 2 × 2 + 2 × 1 = 6 Gen3 lanes ≈ 5.9 GB/s. Its 5GbE port carries 0.625 GB/s, so any single drive fills it.
  • Left on 24/7 at idle: 13.1 W × 8,760 h ≈ 115 kWh a year for the Flashstor 6 Gen2, 19.7 W × 8,760 h ≈ 173 kWh for the F8 SSD Plus with eight drives, 18 W × 8,760 h ≈ 158 kWh for the DXP480T Plus and 9 W × 8,760 h ≈ 79 kWh for the F4 SSD. The reviewers used different drive sets, so treat these as a guide.

Lanes: what the third and fourth drives add

Every M.2 slot needs PCIe lanes, and the processor's lane total sets how many slots can get a wide link. It is the number to check before counting slots.

UGREEN DXP480T Plus. Intel lists 20 PCIe lanes for the Core i5-1235U: the CPU's own lanes are Gen4 and the chipset's are Gen3. For U/P-series chips, Intel puts up to eight PCIe 4.0 lanes on the CPU. Liliputing's lane map for this box is: x4 + x4 for drive slots 1–2, x2 + x2 for slots 3–4, x2 for the OS drive and x4 for the 10GbE port, 18 lanes in all. Slots 1–2 use all eight Gen4 lanes, so the other ten can only come from the Gen3 chipset lanes. By Intel's figures, slots 3–4 therefore run at Gen3 x2, which is what TechRadar reported (as cited by ComputingForGeeks), although Liliputing lists them as Gen4. That still gives each of the third and fourth drives about 1.97 GB/s, more than 10GbE carries. Over the network they are no slower than slots 1–2. The difference shows up only in copies inside the box and over Thunderbolt.

TerraMaster F8 SSD Plus and F4 SSD. Intel lists 9 PCIe 3.0 lanes in total for both the i3-N305 and the N95. On the F8 SSD Plus, Liliputing traced 2 lanes to the 10GbE port, one lane each to drives 1–4, and 2 lanes to an ASM2806 switch that serves drives 5–8. Drives 5–8 add capacity, not throughput, because together they share about 1.97 GB/s. On the F4 SSD, NASCompares reports two slots at Gen3 x2 and two at x1. The third and fourth drives get about 0.985 GB/s each, still more than the 5GbE port carries.

Asustor Flashstor 6 Gen2. Asustor lists every slot as PCIe 4.0: one x4, three x2 and two x1. It does not say which slot number gets which width. The box has two SO-DIMM slots taking up to 64 GB. ECC switches off if any non-ECC module is installed or mixed in. Asustor's datasheet lists the shipped 8 GB module as ECC but its spec page does not say, so check the module's label. Its USB4 ports do not support host-to-host connections. Unlike the DXP480T Plus's Thunderbolt, they cannot serve as a direct cable link to a PC.

Not recommended

TerraMaster F8 SSD Plus for six drives or fewer. On Amazon on 2026-11-17 it was priced the same as the Flashstor 6 Gen2. For that money you get 6 Gen3 lanes feeding the drives instead of 12 Gen4: about 5.9 GB/s against 23.6 GB/s, a quarter of the bandwidth. Its idle draw with eight drives is also higher (19.7 W against 13.1 W). ComputingForGeeks also reports that Debian, Ubuntu and TrueNAS SCALE failed to boot fully with drives on its PCIe switch. Buy it only if you need the seventh and eighth slots.
