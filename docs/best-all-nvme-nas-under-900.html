Why lanes decide an SSD NAS

An NVMe drive is only as fast as the PCIe lanes feeding it: one PCIe 3.0 lane carries about 985 MB/s, two about 2 GB/s, four PCIe 4.0 lanes about 7 GB/s. The N95, N100, N150 and N305 chips in the cheaper boxes have nine PCIe 3.0 lanes in total, so four to eight slots share them at x1 or x2. The Core i5-1235U in the DXP480T Plus has twenty lanes: two slots get a full x4 and two get x2, plus a separate 2242 slot for the 128 GB system drive. Over a single 10GbE link (about 1.1 GB/s) an x1 slot already saturates the network; the extra lanes matter for RAID rebuilds, local transcoding and the Thunderbolt bridge.

Thunderbolt on a NAS

The DXP480T Plus's two Thunderbolt 4 ports run in host mode: a Mac or PC cannot mount the NAS as an external drive. They form an IP link (a Thunderbolt bridge) that reviewers measured at 10 to 20 Gb/s, with one mini PC gaining nothing over 10GbE, and one Mac owner reporting about 2 GB/s reads after a BIOS update. Asustor's Flashstor Gen2 offers USB4 on the same terms.

Heat

All-flash boxes throttle when drives run hot: a review of the first Flashstor generation found unheatsinked drives at 75–85 °C and throttling when fully populated. UGREEN ships thermal pads for the four drives and has Default, Quiet and Full Speed fan modes; a 100 GB copy measured 40–42 dBA and the CPU reached 69 °C, 71 °C with throttling under a synthetic stress test. Buy drives with heatsinks or fit the pads.
