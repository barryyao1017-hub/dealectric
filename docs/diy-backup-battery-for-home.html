What the differences mean

All three kits use the same inverter, so they differ only in how many 5.12 kWh battery packs it runs from. The figures come from four lines of arithmetic:

  • Idle draw per day: 50 W × 24 h = 1,200 Wh; 80 W × 24 h = 1,920 Wh (Inverter Geek's measured range for the SPH6548P).
  • Share of the bank: 1,200–1,920 Wh ÷ 5,120 Wh = 23–38% for one pack; ÷ 10,240 Wh = 12–19% for two; ÷ 20,480 Wh = 6–9% for four.
  • Hours at a 300 W load: 5,120 Wh ÷ (300 + 80 W) = 13.5 h and ÷ (300 + 50 W) = 14.6 h; two packs 10,240 ÷ 380–350 = 27–29 h; four packs 20,480 ÷ 380–350 = 54–59 h. These use nominal capacity, before inverter conversion losses and any reserve you keep, so real runtime is shorter.
  • Battery current at full output: 6,500 W ÷ 51.2 V = 127 A before conversion losses, split across however many packs are connected.

The load you don't plug in: the inverter's own idle draw

An inverter/charger that backs up your home stays switched on around the clock so it can take over the moment the grid drops. James Ndungu at Inverter Geek tested the SPH6548P on a 48V LiFePO4 bank (review updated 2026-04-17) and measured it at 50–80 W continuously just to stay powered on, roughly 1.4–2 kWh a day by his estimate. That energy comes out of the battery before your fridge or router gets any.

The lighter your backup load, the bigger that loss gets. At a 100 W overnight load (router, a few LED lights, a fridge between cycles), one 5.12 kWh pack lasts 5,120 ÷ (100 + 80) = 28 h. Without the idle draw it would last 5,120 ÷ 100 = 51 h, so the inverter takes 44% of the energy. With two packs the same load runs 10,240 ÷ 180 = 57 h. That is why the 10.24 kWh kit is the default here, not the single pack.

Battery count also changes how hard each pack works. At its 6,500 W rating the inverter pulls about 127 A from a 51.2 V bank, and its charger can push up to 140 A back in. Sun Gold Power's own one-battery 6.5 kW kit (SGM-655M) ships with a 160 A DC breaker and 4 AWG cable from the breaker to the inverter. Its two-battery version (SGM-6510M) adds a 250 A busbar and 1/0 AWG battery cables, and the kits here copy that hardware. Before first use, set the inverter's maximum charge current to no more than the limit in the battery manual. This matters most with a single pack.

Not in any cart: a listed transfer switch or protected-loads subpanel, battery cables sized to the run, and a licensed electrician for the panel connection. A battery wired to house circuits must not be able to backfeed the utility, and many areas require a permit for battery storage.

Not recommended

Sun Gold Power 6000W 48V Split Phase Inverter With Charger (low-frequency), if you'll recharge from a 120 V generator or outlet. Its store page states that the split-phase inverter "requires 240VAC input". A 120 V-only source does not meet that requirement, so plan on a 240 V feed or 240 V generator if you choose it. It also cost more than the SPH6548P on 2026-11-13 ($1,220 vs $1,035).
