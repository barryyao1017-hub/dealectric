Same name, two lasers: check the class on the box

A laser's class sets its power ceiling: Class 2 visible lasers are limited to 1 mW and Class 3R to 5 mW. How bright the dot looks is that power times the eye's sensitivity at the laser's wavelength. On the CIE photopic table, that sensitivity is 0.710 at 520 nm (green) and 0.107 at 650 nm (red). At equal power a 520 nm green dot looks 0.710 ÷ 0.107 = 6.6× brighter than a 650 nm red one. Put the class ceilings together and a 5 mW green Class 3R (5 × 0.710 = 3.55) looks about 33× brighter than a 1 mW, 650 nm red (1 × 0.107).

Olight sells the ArkPro Ultra as a Class 1 version (0.39 mW, 226 h of laser) and a Class 3R version (5 mW, 50 h), both under the same model name. Which one you get depends on the SKU. Not recommended

Klein Tools 56074 for long-distance pointing. It is sold as a green-laser work light, but Klein rates the laser Class 2, under 1 mW. That is at most a fifth of a Class 3R rating, enough to point inside a room or across a job box. Fenix E06R if you want the laser to show up: its laser is red, and at equal power a 650 nm red dot looks 6.6× dimmer than a 520 nm green one. Fenix's green-laser model in the same family is the E06R PRO RG ($84.95, 2026-11-08). Never point any of these lasers at eyes, vehicles or aircraft.
