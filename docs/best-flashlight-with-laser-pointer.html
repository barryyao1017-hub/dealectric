Best flashlight with laser pointer

Short answer. The best flashlight with laser pointer is the Wuben X5 (Class 3R version) ($79.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/wuben-x5-3-in-1-class-3r): a 5 mW Class 3R green laser that is visible in daylight, a 1,300-lumen white light that settles to about 400 lumens, and a 2 W 365 nm UV LED in a 4.5 oz flat body. The concern that comes up most with these lights is a sealed battery and a proprietary charger: the X5 charges over plain USB-C, its 1,200 mAh cell is replaceable, and four AAA cells run all three lights when you cannot charge. The Olight ArkPro is the better light if you want more output and a waterproof body: a 1,500-lumen flood plus an 800-lumen spot, 1,200 mW UV, IPX7, a 2,000 mAh sealed cell and both USB-C and magnetic charging, at a higher list price. The Wurkkos HD01 Pro is the budget pick if the laser is for fun rather than work.

A Class 3R laser (up to 5 mW) is the strongest class sold in consumer flashlights in the US. It is bright enough to point out a bolt across a shop or a roof line in daylight, but it must never be aimed at eyes, cars or aircraft. Wuben sells the 3R version only to the US and Canada. For workplaces that ban Class 3R, the 0.39 mW Class 1 X5 ($79.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/wuben-x5-3-in-1-class-1) costs the same and keeps the white light, UV, USB-C and AAA backup.

What owners complain about

  • Sealed batteries and proprietary charging. The most common gripe, raised in five of the reviews and threads we read. Olight's older Arkfeld Pro charges only through a magnetic cable that costs money to replace, and the ArkPro's 2,000 mAh cell cannot be swapped. The X5 answers this with USB-C, a replaceable cell and AAA backup.
  • Turbo does not last. Three independent tests show every flat light in this class dropping to roughly 400 lumens within one to ten minutes. Buy on sustained output, not the box number.
  • The laser is weaker than expected. Three mentions, all about Class 1 lasers or lights sold outside the US. Only a Class 3R laser reads clearly in daylight.
  • Tint and color. Two reviewers measured the X5 at about 65 to 70 CRI with a green cast; Olight does not promise a color temperature either.
  • Laser aim and artifacts. Two reports on the Wurkkos HD01 Pro: a laser dot that sits off from where the beam points, and a second stray dot.
  • Ergonomics. One X5 reviewer wants the mode dial lower on the body because changing modes often takes two hands; one Arkfeld Pro owner bent the clip within a month.

Which one by need

  • Pointing at work, dog play, star-gazing: Wuben X5 Class 3R. Daylight-visible 5 mW laser on its own tail button, runs with the white light or UV, and AAA backup for long jobs away from a charger.
  • Most light and best sealing: Olight ArkPro. Flood and spot beams, the strongest UV here at 1,200 mW, IPX7, and a 2,000 mAh cell that runs about two weeks on moonlight. You give up a replaceable battery and pay more.
  • Class 1 only: Wuben X5 Class 1 version, same price, same light minus the strong laser.
  • Cheapest with UV and a laser: Wurkkos HD01 Pro at $38.99 on wurkkos.com (2026-11-06). Fine for showing the kids a laser and checking hotel sheets; not for precise pointing.

Not recommended

  • Olight Arkfeld Pro. The ArkPro replaced it. It charges only through Olight's magnetic cable, its clip drew complaints, and it has less throw than the ArkPro.
  • Wurkkos HD01 Pro for precise work. Off-aim laser reports, a stray second dot, slippery body and sharp corners.
  • Unbranded 3-AAA "5-in-1" lights with a red laser. A red laser looks far dimmer to the eye than a green one of the same power, and we found no reliable test data on any of them.
