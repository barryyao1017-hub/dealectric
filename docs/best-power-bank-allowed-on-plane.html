What a 99 Wh power bank actually delivers

Airlines check the Wh rating printed on the case. US rules have required that marking on lithium-ion batteries since 10 May 2024 (49 CFR 173.185). AOHi, EcoFlow and UGREEN rate their cells at 3.6 V: AOHi's 27,600 mAh × 3.6 V = 99.36 Wh. Anker's label works out to 3.8 V (99.75 Wh ÷ 26.25 Ah), so mAh alone can't tell you whether a pack is under the line.

Some of that energy never reaches your device. Stepping the cell voltage up to USB-C voltages costs energy, and so does heat. In the best runs about 10% is lost (Anker 90%, EcoFlow 88%). Low loads lose more: Techtest.org measured 75.1 Wh from the EcoFlow at 5 V/2 A against 87.5 Wh at 20 V/3 A.

At laptop loads, heat decides. ChargerLAB ran the AOHi in a 25 °C chamber. At 28 V/5 A its front reached 51.8 °C within 10 minutes, and protection stopped output after 24.1 Wh. At 20 V/5 A it stopped after 23 minutes and 35.5 Wh. Techtest.org tested in a 20–22 °C room, where the EcoFlow ran its 100 W and 140 W loads to empty (84.2 and 85.5 Wh). It also notes that the EcoFlow drops to 100 W once its cells reach 40 °C. These are two different labs in two different rooms, so compare figures within the same lab first.

  • Up to 100 Wh: no airline approval needed. Spares go in carry-on only (49 CFR 175.10).
  • Over 100 Wh, up to 160 Wh: needs the airline's approval, with at most two such spares per person (49 CFR 175.10). International rules from 27 March 2026 keep this band (UK CAA).
  • From 27 March 2026, international: at most two power banks per person. Don't recharge them on board, and they should not be used to charge other devices in flight (UK CAA).
  • Delta: each power bank may be 100 Wh at most, with no exceptions, and you can carry two at most. They can't go in overhead bins or charge devices during taxi, takeoff or landing.

Not recommended

AOHi Starship 40,000 mAh. At the 3.6 V AOHi uses on its 27,600 mAh pack, 40 Ah × 3.6 V = 144 Wh, well over 100 Wh. That means asking each airline for approval, and Delta refuses power banks over 100 Wh with no exceptions. If you want a bank that flies on any airline, stay at 99.75 Wh or below.
