What the differences mean

A power station's own draw adds to the router's. At a small load like a 15 W router, that own draw decides the hours more than the battery size does. Every figure below is the rated capacity (or the measured delivered energy, where a tester measured it) divided by the router's 15 W plus the station's measured own draw. Dealectric does not test units; the measurements are the named testers'. Rated-capacity sums ignore conversion loss, so they are upper bounds.

  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 on DC: 4,096 Wh ÷ (15 + 13) W = 146 h, about 6.1 days. With AC on: 4,096 ÷ (15 + 49) W = 64 h, about 2.7 days. Its own draw is 46% of the battery on DC (13 ÷ 28) and 77% on AC (49 ÷ 64).
  • Anker SOLIX C300 DC: 277.6 Wh delivered at 24 W ÷ 15 W = 18.5 h. Loss at that load: 288 − 277.6 = 10.4 Wh over 11.6 h, under 1 W, conversion included.
  • EcoFlow TRAIL 300 DC: 2 Wh ÷ 24 h = 0.08 W idle; 288 ÷ 15.08 W = 19.1 h at most.
  • EcoFlow RIVER 2: 221.9 Wh from its 12 V port ÷ 15 W = 14.8 h at most; 233 Wh from USB-C ÷ 15 W = 15.5 h at most.
  • BLUETTI Elite 30 V2: about 11 W with DC on (The Technology Man; 0.76 × 288 Wh ÷ 20 h = 10.9 W if the test started full), so 288 ÷ 25.9 W = 11.1 h. With AC on: 0.81 × 288 ÷ 12 h = 19.4 W, so 288 ÷ 34.4 W = 8.4 h. On BLUETTI's 4.5 W standby figure it would be 288 ÷ 19.5 W = 14.8 h.

Which power station loses the least power when you only use the USB and 12 volt ports

A station without an AC inverter. techtest.org measured the Anker SOLIX C300X DC (sold in the US as the C300 DC) delivering 277.6 of its 288 Wh (96%) at 12 V and 2 A, about 24 W, and 271.5 Wh (94%) on USB-C at 9 V and 3 A, about 27 W. That is a load the size of a router plus modem, and the loss does not grow at the lower load. The Solar Lab measured the EcoFlow TRAIL 300 DC using about 2 Wh in 24 hours switched on with nothing plugged in; nobody has yet published its delivered energy under load.

Stations that have an inverter lose more, because the control board stays awake to run the DC side. Measured with DC on and no load: EcoFlow RIVER 2, 1.14 W standby with no outputs on (Gough Lui); BLUETTI EB3A, 16% of 268 Wh in 12 h, about 3.6 W (The Technology Man); BLUETTI Elite 30 V2, about 11 W (The Technology Man, same method), 2.4 times BLUETTI's own 4.5 W standby figure; EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3, about 13 W with DC outputs only (Hobotech). The older EB3A draws about a third of what the newer Elite 30 V2 draws on DC.

The catch: makers describe UPS switchover for the AC outlets. EcoFlow's RIVER 3 Plus page says all three AC outlets have UPS protection and names no USB or car port. Running the router from DC saves the most energy, but treat the USB and 12 V ports as having no written switchover promise: test yours by pulling the wall plug before you rely on it. On the C300 DC, use one of the 140 W USB-C ports or the car socket; the USB-C4 port stops at 15 W.

Not recommended

Running a big station's AC outlets for a router alone. With AC on, the DELTA Pro 3's own 49 W is more than three times a 15 W router and uses 77% of the battery, cutting about 6.1 days on DC to about 2.7. The same holds on a small unit: the Elite 30 V2 drops from about 11.1 h on DC to about 8.4 h on AC. Use AC only when the router has no 12 V or USB-C option, or when it needs the UPS switchover.
