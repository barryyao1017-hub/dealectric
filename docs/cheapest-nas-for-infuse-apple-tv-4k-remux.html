What the differences mean

Infuse decodes the video on the Apple TV, so the NAS only has to send the file faster than it plays. The worst case is a full Ultra HD Blu-ray remux: 144 Mbit/s on 66 GB and 100 GB discs, which is 144 ÷ 8 = 18 MB/s.

  • Synology DS124: 112.53 MB/s × 8 = 900 Mbit/s; 900 ÷ 144 = 6.3×.
  • Synology DS223j: 115 MB/s × 8 = 920 Mbit/s; 920 ÷ 144 = 6.4×.
  • UGREEN DH2300: 109 MB/s × 8 = 872 Mbit/s; 872 ÷ 144 = 6.1×.
  • TerraMaster F2-212: 59 MB/s × 8 = 472 Mbit/s; 472 ÷ 144 = 3.3×. This was a write.
  • UGREEN DXP2800: 170.45 MB/s × 8 = 1,364 Mbit/s, but the Apple TV's port takes at most 1,000 Mbit/s; 1,000 ÷ 144 = 6.9×.
  • DXP2800 against DH2300 on UGREEN's store (2026-11-17): $389.99 − $239.99 = $150.00 more.

Every box clears a remux at least three times over, so speed doesn't separate them. Bays, price and power do.

Measured speed against a 4K remux: where the bottleneck really is

The NAS is rarely what makes a remux stutter in Infuse. The link from the Apple TV usually is. Apple's spec page for the Apple TV 4K (3rd generation) lists Gigabit Ethernet only on the 128 GB Wi-Fi + Ethernet model. The 64 GB Wi-Fi model has no Ethernet port. No current Apple TV 4K has a wired port faster than 1 Gb/s, so a 2.5GbE NAS can't deliver more than a 1GbE one.

Firecore's Infuse team wrote on its forum that "streaming large or high-bitrate files is best done over a wired Gigabit connection." If your Apple TV has to use Wi-Fi, use 5 GHz. Apple lists the 2nd- and 3rd-generation link rate as 1,200 Mbit/s at 5 GHz, which is 8.3× the 144 Mbit/s peak. At 2.4 GHz it is 195 Mbit/s, only 1.35× the peak, and real throughput is lower than the link rate.

One limit no NAS can fix: Firecore staff wrote that "All Profile 7 will fall back to HDR". Ultra HD Blu-ray remuxes that carry Dolby Vision Profile 7 play as HDR on the Apple TV, whichever box serves them.

Not recommended

The UGREEN DXP2800, if Infuse on an Apple TV is the only job. It costs $150.00 more than the DH2300 on UGREEN's store. Its 2.5GbE port runs at the Apple TV's 1 Gb/s, and its Intel N100 transcoding goes unused when Infuse direct-plays. PC-WELT measured 17.0 W idle, against NAS Compares' 9.5 W for the DH2300: 7.5 W × 8,760 h = 65.7 kWh more a year. It is worth buying only if you also run Plex or Jellyfin for devices that need transcoding.
