What the differences mean

“2000 watt” can mean 2,000 Wh of stored energy or 2,000 W of output. Every station here has both except the Anker SOLIX S2000, which stops at 1,500 W. Dealectric does not test units. The figures come from The Solar Lab, Watt Power Station, APH Networks and the makers, each named in its cell, and the derived figures are our arithmetic, shown below.

  • Idle loss per day = idle W × 24 h: BLUETTI 9.5 × 24 = 228 Wh; Pecron 13.6 × 24 = 326 Wh; Jackery 17 × 24 = 408 Wh; Anker C2000 Gen 2 18 × 24 = 432 Wh; Dabbsson 26 × 24 = 624 Wh; EcoFlow 27 × 24 = 648 Wh; Anker S2000 6 × 24 = 144 Wh.
  • Share of the battery = daily idle loss ÷ rated Wh: 228 ÷ 2,073.6 = 11% (BLUETTI); 624 ÷ 2,048 = 30% (Dabbsson); 648 ÷ 2,048 = 32% (EcoFlow).
  • Hours on a steady 100 W load (fridge plus router), best case = rated Wh ÷ (100 W + idle W): BLUETTI 2,073.6 ÷ 109.5 = 18.9 h; Pecron 2,048 ÷ 113.6 = 18.0 h; Dabbsson 2,048 ÷ 126 = 16.3 h; EcoFlow 2,048 ÷ 127 = 16.1 h. Other conversion losses come off every one of these.
  • Cost per usable Wh = maker price on 2026-11-15 ÷ Watt Power Station's measured usable Wh: Dabbsson 649 ÷ 1,887 = $0.34; Pecron 649 ÷ 1,536 = $0.42; BLUETTI 899 ÷ 1,989 = $0.45; Jackery 999 ÷ 1,773 = $0.56; Anker C2000 Gen 2 1,199.99 ÷ 1,818 = $0.66.
  • Runtime at a full 2,000 W = measured usable Wh ÷ 2,000 W: BLUETTI 1,989 ÷ 2,000 = 0.99 h; Dabbsson 0.94 h; Anker C2000 Gen 2 0.91 h; Jackery 0.89 h; Pecron 0.77 h. The S2000 can't hold 2,000 W at all.

Idle draw: the figure that decides an overnight outage

When the AC outlets are switched on, the inverter keeps running even with nothing plugged in, and it drains the battery around the clock. On a short, heavy job like a kettle or a saw, that barely matters. On an outage night running a cycling fridge, a CPAP or a router, it can eat a third of the battery in a day. The Solar Lab measured idle draw on every unit here except the Growatt INFINITY 2000 PRO. The BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 drew 9.5 W, which The Solar Lab calls the lowest it has seen for a station this size. Its review says the unit went through the same tests as every station but does not state whether the inverter was on for that reading. The Pecron E2400LFP fell from 98% to 82% in 24 h with the inverter on, which works out to about 13.6 W. The Jackery Explorer 2000 v2 drew about 17 W. The Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2 drew about 18 W with its outlets on standby; Anker advertises 9 W, but The Solar Lab saw that only with just the screen and USB on. The Dabbsson 2000L drew 26 W and the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max about 27 W. All these idle figures come from one lab, so a second lab could read a few watts differently.

Efficiency under load follows a similar order. The Solar Lab measured 94% for the BLUETTI and about 93% for the Dabbsson's inverter, then 90% for the Jackery, about 89% for the Anker C2000 Gen 2 and 79% for the EcoFlow. The Pecron managed 75% at 2,275 W, and its first run overheated and stopped with about 15% of the battery left. APH Networks ran the BLUETTI down with a heater drawing about 1,000 W and got 2,028 Wh out, 97.8% of its rating. The Dabbsson stays the cheapest energy for short, heavy use, but The Solar Lab could not reach its 3,300 W Power-Boost figure.

Where the BLUETTI loses: at 53.4 lb it is the heaviest here, 12.4 lb more than the Dabbsson. It takes no expansion battery (APH Networks). BLUETTI publishes no surge rating. Its 3,900 W Power Lifting mode is for resistive loads such as heaters, and APH Networks saw the unit shut down when two hair dryers pulled 3,140 W. It also costs $250 more than the Dabbsson or the Pecron. On 2026-11-15, BLUETTI's own store and Walmart (the station alone, sold and shipped by Bluetti Official) both list it at $899. Not recommended

Anker SOLIX S2000 for a 2000 watt job. It stores 2,010 Wh, but its continuous output is 1,500 W. Anker's own S2000 FAQ warns that a 1,500 W microwave plus a fridge compressor's start-up surge can exceed comfortable headroom. It cannot run a 2,000 W load at all. It does win one thing: the lowest idle draw here, about 6 W (The Solar Lab), at 35.7 lb. If every load you plan to run stays under 1,500 W, $649.99: https://dealectric.com/go/anker-solix-s2000 wastes the least energy while idling.

EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max for long outages. About 27 W idle and 79% efficiency (The Solar Lab) leave the least energy for the load of any station here, and the plain DELTA 3 Max cannot take an extra battery. Its strength is a 68-minute recharge from the wall.
