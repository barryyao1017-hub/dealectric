What the differences mean

  • Added pixels: the Trio 3 Pro adds 2 × 2240 × 1400 = 6.27 MP, and the FOPO S7 adds 2 × 1920 × 1200 = 4.61 MP. That gives the Trio 36% more room (6.272 ÷ 4.608 = 1.36).
  • Sharpness: a 14.1" 2240×1400 panel has a √(2240² + 1400²) = 2,642 px diagonal, and 2,642 ÷ 14.1 = 187 ppi. A 14" 1920×1200 panel works out to 2,264 ÷ 14 = 162 ppi, and a 16" 1920×1200 panel to 2,264 ÷ 16 = 142 ppi. Smaller text stays crisper on the 14-inch 1400p pair.
  • Battery: CHIP.pl's spec table gives the Trio 3 Pro an 11 W draw. That alone empties a 13-inch M5 MacBook Air's 53.8 Wh battery in 53.8 ÷ 11 = 4.9 h, before the laptop's own use.
  • Weight: Macworld gives the Trio 4.13 lb. That is 4.13 ÷ 2.7 = 1.53 times a 13-inch MacBook Air.

How many added screens light on your laptop

An extender's two screens reach the laptop by one of two routes. On the native route, the laptop's own graphics sends a separate DisplayPort signal to each screen over a video-capable USB-C (Alt Mode) or HDMI port. On the driver route, software packs both screens into a USB stream. FOPO says of the S1: "Each S1 screen needs its own supported video connection… no driver installation required." The S7 and S11 are also driver-free, and the S9G runs on video-capable USB-C or HDMI.

Apple caps the native route on its entry Macs. The MacBook Air M1 and M2 each drive "one external display". The M3 Air drives one with the lid open and adds a second only with the lid closed, and a screen extender is used with the lid open. So on those Macs a driver-free dual extender lights one of its two screens, and no setting changes that. The current M5 MacBook Air supports two external displays (Apple), so there the FOPO S7 lights both.

The Trio 3 Pro works on both routes. Macworld's Simon Jary quotes the manual: "if your laptop has two DP enabled USB-C ports … driver installation is not necessary." He also writes that plain M1 and M2 Macs need Mobile Pixels' free software, which he compares to DisplayLink, to run both screens. Mobile Pixels' Mac driver page has downloads for macOS 14 and later and says to install the driver before plugging the Trio in.

The driver route has a cost. Plugable documents that a DisplayLink driver on macOS needs the Screen Recording permission, "which disables HDCP video playback system-wide", because macOS doesn't separate DisplayLink-run displays from those run by the Mac's own graphics. Mobile Pixels doesn't say whether its software asks for that permission. On a base M1 or M2 Mac, test protected streaming before relying on it. Laptops that run the Trio natively don't face this.

One more condition from Macworld: on laptop power alone, the Trio can't reach its full 300 nits. A power adapter on one of its USB-C ports fixes that and passes up to 65 W through to the laptop. $367.99: https://dealectric.com/go/mobile-pixels-trio-3-pro
