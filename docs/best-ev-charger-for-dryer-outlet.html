Best EV charger for a dryer outlet

Updated 2026-11-14

Short answer. The best EV charger for a dryer outlet is the EVDANCE Level 2 NEMA 10-30 24A J1772 Portable EV Charger ($239.95; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/evdance-level-2-nema-10-30-24a-j1772, code EVUS25 saves $59.98): it charges at 24 A, the most a 30 A dryer circuit allows for charging (5.76 kW at 240 V). Its 3-prong plug fits the outlet with no adapter, and the cable is 25 ft. The rule that matters most: never plug a 32 A or 40 A charger into a dryer outlet through an adapter. Charging runs for hours, so a 30 A circuit should carry 24 A at most. Going over that limit is behind most hot-plug and melted-outlet reports.

Have a 4-prong outlet? Get the NEMA 14-30 version ($229.95; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/evdance-level-2-nema-14-30-24a-j1772, code EVUS25 saves $57.48), also 24 A. Without a Mobile Connector, the EVDANCE NACS version ($239.95; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/evdance-level-2-nema-10-30-24a-nacs, code EVUS25 saves $59.98) plugs straight into a Tesla. At 24 A, expect roughly 15 to 20 miles of range per hour. A 3-prong 10-30 outlet has no separate ground wire. If a ground wire is already in the box, an electrician can fit a 14-30 receptacle, which is the safer long-term setup.

What owners complain about

  • Too much current (about 8 mentions across forums and guides). People plug 32 A and 40 A chargers into dryer outlets through adapters, and those setups trip breakers and overheat plugs. On the Mach-E forum, five posters in one thread repeat the 24 A limit for a 30 A circuit. The fix is a charger whose plug matches the outlet and tops out at 24 A.
  • Hot or melted plugs (about 7). One Kona owner measured 73 to 74 °C where the cable leaves a 26 A charger's 10-30 plug. Worn or loose receptacles make it worse. Stop charging and call an electrician if the plug face browns or gets too hot to hold.
  • No ground on 10-30 outlets (about 7). Three-prong dryer outlets have no ground wire, and that can cause ground-fault errors, especially through adapters. An upgrade to a 14-30 receptacle fixes it when a ground wire is already in the box.
  • Sharing with the dryer (about 5). Swapping plugs every day wears out the outlet. An automatic splitter, such as NeoCharge's NEMA 10-30 Smart Splitter ($399 on its store, 2026-11-14), switches power between the dryer and the car.
  • EVDANCE support. EVDANCE has a Trustpilot score of 3.9 from 38 reviews. On the first page, all four low-star reviews say replies were slow or never came, and two describe a melted plug or prong after months of use. Those reviews don't name the model.

Which one by need

  • Non-Tesla EV, 4-prong outlet: EVDANCE NEMA 14-30 24A J1772.

  • Tesla with no Mobile Connector: EVDANCE NEMA 10-30 24A NACS. A 14-30 NACS version is sold too.
  • Lowest price, short commute: VEVOR's 16 A NEMA 10-30 charger ($129.99, 2026-11-14) costs less but charges a third slower (3.84 kW). Its listing says it complies with ETL standards; it doesn't say it is ETL listed.
  • Need more than 24 A: A dryer circuit can't supply it. Have an electrician run a dedicated 40 to 50 A circuit for a 14-50 or hardwired charger.

Not recommended

  • A 32 A to 48 A charger on a dryer outlet through a 10-30-to-14-50 or 14-30-to-14-50 adapter, unless the charger is locked at 24 A or less.
  • Household extension cords between the outlet and the charger.
  • Running the dryer and the car on one outlet without an automatic splitter.
