What the differences mean

On a 30 A dryer circuit, every charger above charges at the same speed: 24 A × 240 V = 5.76 kW. In a home supplied at 208 V it is 24 A × 208 V = 4.99 kW, about 13% less.

  • An average day of driving: 13,735 mi a year (FHWA) ÷ 365 = 37.6 mi. A 2026 Model Y Long Range AWD uses 27 kWh/100 mi (EPA), so that day takes 37.6 × 0.27 = 10.2 kWh. At 24 A that is 10.2 ÷ 5.76 = 1.8 hours on the charger.
  • A full 10-hour night: 10 × 5.76 = 57.6 kWh, and 57.6 ÷ 0.27 = 213 miles of range.
  • Cost: 10.2 kWh × 18.16 ¢ (EIA US residential average, August 2026) = $1.85 a day.
  • Price per kW you can actually use on a dryer circuit: EVDANCE 14-30 at $229.95 ÷ 5.76 = $39.92/kW. Grizzl-E Mini at $349.99 ÷ 5.76 = $60.76/kW. The 40 A Grizzl-E charges no faster on this outlet.

3-prong or 4-prong: what the electrical code allows

A 4-prong NEMA 14-30 has two hots, a neutral and a separate ground. An older 3-prong NEMA 10-30 has two hots and a neutral, with no ground. NEC 250.140, titled "Frames of Ranges and Clothes Dryers", covers "frames of electric ranges, wall-mounted ovens, counter-mounted cooking units, clothes dryers, and outlet or junction boxes". Its rule (A) says the circuit "shall include an equipment grounding conductor". The exception in (B) applies "for existing branch-circuit installations only": it lets those listed appliances use the neutral as their ground. An EV charger is not on that list. So a charger plugged into a 10-30 has no ground the code allows, even though the dryer before it did.

Each charger on a 4-prong outlet holds 24 A in a different way. Both EVDANCE 14-30 models top out at 24 A, so no setting can overload a 30 A circuit. Tesla's 14-30 and 10-30 adapters "automatically reduce your charge rate to 24 Amps", according to NeoCharge. NeoCharge also warns that a generic NEMA adapter lets the Mobile Connector draw 32 A, which can trip a 30 A breaker. The Grizzl-E Mini ships set to 40 A, and Plug & Range notes it "can not detect the circuit amperage limit". Its cap is a DIP switch inside the charger, so you have to set it before the first charge.

Ground-fault protection: NEC 625.54 calls for GFCI protection on receptacles installed for EV charging equipment. EC&M reports that a 2026 amendment (TIA 1892), written because the GFCI requirement can cause unwanted tripping, passed its ballot on May 29, 2026. EVDANCE publishes no leakage-trip figure, so ask your electrician whether the dryer breaker needs to be a GFCI and test a full night's charge.

Not recommended

EVDANCE NEMA 10-30 models on a 3-wire outlet. They fit the old 3-prong socket, but with no ground on that circuit they rely on the exception 250.140(B) gives only to dryers and ranges. The 10-30 J1772 listing also describes a 40 A, 14-50 charger in its body text, so don't buy from its specs until EVDANCE fixes the page. Adapter stacks. A 14-30-to-14-50 pigtail on a charger or adapter made for a 14-50 makes the plug fit but doesn't lower the current the charger draws. J+ Booster 2. It costs $749 at J+ Americas, and the maker's adapter list includes no 14-30 or 10-30 adapter.
