What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units; the figures come from the named reviewers, the makers' pages, or the arithmetic shown. Every nights figure uses one formula: rated Wh × 0.85 ÷ ((CPAP watts + idle watts) × 8 hours). The 0.85 covers conversion losses. The idle term is what the station draws just to keep its AC outlets live, and on a big home-backup inverter it is about as large as a CPAP's own draw.

The two CPAP loads are set against ResMed's own measurements of an AirSense 10: 7 W for the machine alone, 22 W with the humidifier, 34 W with the humidifier and ClimateLineAir heated tube, and 5 W in standby. The 40 W column covers the 34 W humidifier-and-heated-tube set-up with room to spare. The 20 W column sits just below ResMed's 22 W humidifier figure and well above its 7 W machine-only figure, so it is not a humidifier-off load: with the humidifier off, a machine like this gets more nights than the 20 W column shows.

The table compares like for like only within the same battery count. Single boxes (one box) are the fair comparison; the DELTA Pro Ultra X, the DELTA Pro Ultra and the Apex 300 figures include the add-on batteries named in the 'Batteries in this figure' column, and any expandable system gets more nights by adding more.

  • Anker SOLIX S5000 (one box): 5,024 × 0.85 = 4,270 ÷ ((40 + 10) × 8 = 400) = 10.7; at 20 W, 4,270 ÷ 240 = 17.8. pv magazine USA saw 4.87 kWh delivered (an app reading, not metered); 4,870 ÷ 400 = 12.2.
  • DELTA Pro Ultra X (inverter + 2 batteries): 12,288 × 0.85 = 10,445 Wh ÷ ((40 + 36) × 8 = 608 Wh) = 17.2 nights; at 20 W, 10,445 ÷ 448 = 23.3.
  • Apex 300 + 2× B300K: 8,294.4 × 0.85 = 7,050 ÷ ((40 + 20) × 8 = 480) = 14.7; at 20 W, 7,050 ÷ 320 = 22.0. This assumes BLUETTI's 20 W idle figure still holds with two packs attached; nobody has published a measurement of that.
  • DELTA Pro Ultra (inverter + 1 battery): 6,144 × 0.85 = 5,222 ÷ ((40 + 45) × 8 = 680) = 7.7, or ÷ ((40 + 60) × 8 = 800) = 6.5.
  • Elite 300: 3,014 × 0.85 = 2,562 ÷ ((40 + 15) × 8 = 440) = 5.8; at 20 W, 2,562 ÷ 280 = 9.1.
  • Explorer 5000 Plus: 5,040 × 0.85 = 4,284 ÷ ((40 + 58) × 8 = 784) = 5.5, or ÷ ((40 + 85) × 8 = 1,000) = 4.3. Its idle alone is 58 × 8 = 464 Wh a night, more than the CPAP's 320 Wh.
  • F3800 Plus: 3,840 × 0.85 = 3,264 ÷ ((40 + 112) × 8 = 1,216) = 2.7.

These figures assume you switch the AC output on at bedtime and off in the morning. If you leave it on all day, the idle runs for 24 hours: the DELTA Pro Ultra X then gives 10,445 ÷ (320 + 36 × 24 = 1,184) = 8.8 nights. The Solar Lab does not say how it measured its 36 W, 45 W, 15 W or 58 W figures.

Why idle draw sets the count, and how a 12 V cord gets around it

An inverter uses power to keep its AC outlets live whether anything is plugged in or not. ResMed measured an AirSense 10 at 7 W on its own and 34 W with the humidifier and heated tube, so on a large inverter the idle draw can match or exceed the CPAP's own draw. That is why the 5,040 Wh Explorer 5000 Plus (58–85 W idle) gets fewer nights than the 3,014 Wh Elite 300 (15 W idle).

You can avoid the inverter by running the CPAP's DC cord from a 12 V socket with the AC output switched off. ResMed's AirSense 11 DC/DC Converter (ref 39231) takes 12 V at 10 A or 24 V at 5 A and puts out 24 V at 3.75 A. ResMed says it can run the AirSense 11 with the HumidAir humidifier and the ClimateLineAir heated tube. ResMed's UK store lists it, so ask your US supplier for the same part. A 12 V socket rated 10 A delivers exactly the 120 W the converter can draw, with nothing to spare.

  • Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus: has a 12 V car socket (The Solar Lab). HOBOTECH measured 0 Wh used over 12 hours with the 12 V and USB outputs switched on and nothing connected. On DC the ceiling is 5,040 × 0.85 ÷ 320 = 13.4 nights at 40 W, less whatever the converter loses (ResMed publishes no efficiency figure). HOBOTECH gives that socket as 10 A, regulated at 13.2 V, which matches the converter's 10 A input with nothing to spare.
  • Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus: 12 V socket rated 10 A (Zelpio, and Anker's product page). On DC the ceiling is 3,840 × 0.85 ÷ 320 = 10.2 nights.
  • BLUETTI Elite 300: has a 12 V car socket (The Solar Lab). Nobody has published its idle draw on DC alone, so the table keeps its AC figure.
  • DELTA Pro Ultra X and Anker SOLIX S5000: no 12 V output (The Solar Lab; Anker), so the CPAP has to run through the inverter. BLUETTI Apex 300: DC outputs need the Hub D1 add-on (BLUETTI).

Before the first night, set the station's AC auto-off timer to never: a CPAP with the humidifier off is a light load (ResMed measured 7 W). Then run one test night at home.

Not recommended

The Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus on its AC outlets. With the inverter on and nothing plugged in, Zelpio measured about 112 W (1,350 Wh over 12 hours), and the unit delivered 83% of its rated capacity on AC. That idle comes to 896 Wh a night, almost three times a 40 W CPAP's 320 Wh, so its 3,840 Wh gives 2.7 nights. Zelpio does not state the firmware version, and the unit is sold out at Anker today. If you already own one, run the CPAP from its 10 A 12 V socket instead.
