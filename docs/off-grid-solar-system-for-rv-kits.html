What the differences mean

  • Stored energy. Victron's example RV uses 720 Wh a day (laptop, 12 V fridge, lights). The RV kit's 48 V battery covers 5,120 ÷ 720 = 7.1 days with no sun. The Elite 200 V2's measured 1,910 Wh covers 1,910 ÷ 720 = 2.7 days. The B1210 adds 12.8 V × 100 Ah = 1,280 Wh on the 12 V side.
  • Solar watts per day. 6 × 100 W = 600 W × 5 peak sun hours = 3,000 Wh at nameplate, against 2 × 200 W = 400 W × 5 = 2,000 Wh. Both are before losses, and both are well above Victron's 144 W minimum for 720 Wh a day (720 ÷ 5).
  • 12 V coach loads. The RV5 supplies 12 V × 100 A = 1,200 W into the coach's DC panel. The Elite 200 V2 gives 12 V × 10 A = 120 W from one car outlet and does not connect to the coach's lights, pump or fans.
  • AC. 5,000 W against 2,600 W. 2,600 W ÷ 120 V = 21.7 A, a 20 A-class load, so the Elite won't carry what a 30 A RV service can (120 × 30 = 3,600 W).

The 12 V side: why the RV kit gets a second battery

The RV5 hub runs the coach's 12 V side by converting the 48 V B4810 down to a regulated 12 V, and BLUETTI rates that output at 100 A. Lights, the water pump and fans stay far below that. Leveling jacks may not. On eTrailer, Jameson C. explains that Lippert's Ground Control system (LC358590) fuses each of its four legs at 35 A, so the whole unit can draw up to 4 × 35 = 140 A at once. That is 40 A more than the hub supplies.

BLUETTI's own RV kit page covers this case: "For 12V auto-leveling systems with startup currents over 100A, we recommend pairing with a dedicated B1210 battery or your vehicle's existing 12V lead-acid battery." The kit's box list includes the hub, the B4810, the Epanel, the Epad and three wiring packs, but no 12 V battery. If you keep the coach's lead-acid battery, it carries the jacks and you can leave the B1210 out. If you are removing it to go all-lithium, the B1210 replaces it and charges below freezing, because its self-heating film turns on below 32 °F.

Wiring each kit's panels. The RV5's solar input has a strict 50 V open-circuit limit (BLUETTI). A PV100 FX panel is 41.4 V Voc, and two in series would give 82.8 V, so every panel goes in parallel. Six in parallel give 6 × 3.05 A = 18.3 A. The kit's box list includes no Y-branch connectors, and BLUETTI's own $39 Y-branch pair was out of stock on 2026-11-13. Plan on MC4 parallel connectors rated above 18.3 A. The RV5's second solar input (PV2) also serves as its alternator charger, so with alternator charging fitted, all the panels go on PV1.

The Elite 200 V2 is the reverse case: its input takes 12–60 V at 20 A. Two SP200L panels in series give 2 × 24.6 V = 49.2 V at 10.8 A, which fits. In parallel they would give 2 × 10.8 A = 21.6 A, over the 20 A limit. A third panel in series would reach 73.8 V, over 60 V, so 400 W is the most this pair of panels can feed it. BLUETTI's 20 A MC4-to-XT60 cable lists the Elite 200 V2 as compatible. On 2026-11-13 the cable's page carried an out-of-stock label while the store listed it as orderable, so check the cart before you check out.
