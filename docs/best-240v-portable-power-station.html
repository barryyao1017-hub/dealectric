What the differences mean

All four make 120/240 V split-phase power from one box with no second unit or hub. The 240 V outlet's label doesn't tell you how much current it can supply. The inverter does:

  • PECRON F5000LFP: 7,200 W ÷ 240 V = 30 A, from 5,120 Wh in one unit.
  • Anker SOLIX S5000: 4,500 W ÷ 240 V = 18.75 A.
  • Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus: Anker states 25 A on the L14-30R. 25 A × 240 V = 6,000 W, the whole inverter.
  • Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus: 7,200 W ÷ 240 V = 30 A on either 240 V outlet.
  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra (inverter + battery, $3,699 at EcoFlow, 2026-11-17): 7,200 W ÷ 240 V = 30 A. $3,699 ÷ 6.144 kWh = $602 per kWh. The DELTA Pro Ultra X bundle ($7,899) reaches 12,000 W ÷ 240 V = 50 A, but EcoFlow sells it as an installed system.

Does 240 V stay on while the station recharges?

This matters most in a multi-day outage, when a well pump or other 240 V load has to keep running while you top the station up from a wall outlet or a 120 V generator.

  • Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus: its AC input is 120 V only (15 A max, 1,800 W charging, 1,440 W bypass), and Anker states that 240 V output 'cannot be used while the device is charging via 120V AC'. On a 120 V generator, Anker's FAQ says output is available on the L1 phase only, with no output on the L2 phase. 240 V pass-through of up to 6,000 W needs a 240 V generator and Anker's Generator Adapter.
  • Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus: its AC input is 120 V, 15 A, 1,750 W. Jackery's spec page lists '240V Ports: No Output' during simultaneous charge and discharge. In that state the 120 V ports are limited to 12 A in Backup UPS mode or 30 A in Online UPS mode.
  • PECRON F5000LFP: Pecron's page says '120V Charging While Using 240V Output', and The Solar Lab confirmed it in its 2026-05-12 review. The BLUETTI Apex 300, which BLUETTI says can 'Charge the Apex 300 at 120V without interrupting 240V output', was out of stock at BLUETTI with no restock date on 2026-11-21.
  • Anker SOLIX S5000: The Gadgeteer (Vincent Nguyen) reports that its '240V output stays available while the station charges from a 120V source'. Anker's own S5000 page lists 1,800 W AC input at 120 V and 4,500 W at 240 V, and says nothing either way about 240 V output while charging.

Not recommended

Jackery HomePower 3600 Plus for one-box 240 V: Jackery's store says a single unit runs 120 V appliances and you need to 'connect two units' for 240 V. Don't confuse it with the HomePower 3600 Pro Max. EcoFlow TITAN 5000 Classic: EcoFlow says it 'supports 120V output only'. For a 240 V load that must survive a long outage with 120 V recharging, also skip the Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus, whose 240 V outlet shuts off while it charges. Buy it only if you will run 240 V on battery and need up to 25 A. Anker's US store showed it as sold out on 2026-11-17.
