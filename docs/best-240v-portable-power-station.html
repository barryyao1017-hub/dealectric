What owners complain about

  • Start-up surge. This was the complaint we saw most. Well pumps and compressors draw several times their running watts for a second or two, and a station that can't supply that shuts off. OSCAL lists 7,200 W of surge for the PowerMax 6000, and StorageReview lists 7,680 W for the Apex 300. Each Mango Power E is rated for 4,500 to 4,800 W for 10 seconds.
  • Not true split-phase. Forum answers keep pointing out that you can't wire two ordinary 120V stations into 240V. The units have to be built to stay in sync. Every setup in the table is.
  • No wall charging while running 240V. One DIY Solar forum owner called this a deal-breaker for long outages. Mango's manual says to unplug both units' wall-charging cables before you connect the mSocket Pro, so plan to recharge between 240V runs.
  • Weight. Each Mango Power E weighs 100.1 lb. The Gadgeteer called it a beast to move around, even with its wheels. StorageReview called the 83.8 lb Apex 300 a two-person job.
  • Extras add up. Mango's 3,000 W charging cable is $79 and the mSocket Pro is $249. Growatt's pairing hub is $139. A transfer switch or interlock to feed your house panel is another cost, and it needs an electrician.

Not recommended

  • A big 120V-only station for a 240V load. More watts don't change the voltage.
  • Two stations joined with a homemade cord when they aren't sold as a split-phase pair.
