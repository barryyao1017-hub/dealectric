What the differences mean

Wireless Moves ran a Starlink Mini for 2 h 45 min on a 72 Wh, 100 W USB-C PD power bank. The dish averaged about 21 W. Every hours figure on this page starts from that test.

  • Measured rate: 72 Wh ÷ 2.75 h = 26.2 Wh of rated capacity used per hour. 21 W × 2.75 h ÷ 72 Wh = 0.80, so about 80% of the bank's rated Wh reached the dish.
  • BLUETTI Elite 100 V2 and Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: 1,024 Wh ÷ 26.2 = 39 h. At Starlink's 40 W top average: 40 W ÷ 0.80 = 50 Wh per hour, and 1,024 ÷ 50 = 20.5 h.
  • BLUETTI Elite 30 V2: 288 ÷ 26.2 = 11.0 h; 288 ÷ 50 = 5.8 h.
  • UGREEN 145W bank: 90 ÷ 26.2 = 3.4 h; 90 ÷ 50 = 1.8 h.

The 80% figure was measured on a power bank. For the power stations we assume their USB-C conversion loses about the same, because nobody has measured their USB-C efficiency with a Mini.

Which USB-C port runs the Starlink Mini, and why 100 W

Starlink's Mini spec sheet lists three numbers that look like they disagree. The dish's input rating is 12–48 V at 60 W. Its average power consumption is 25–40 W. And the USB PD requirement is "100W, 20V/5A Minimum" with Starlink's USB-C to Barrel Jack Cable. The dish never needs 100 W. Starlink's cable asks the charger for 20 V, and the 100 W rule means the port has to offer 5 A at that voltage. Only a 5 A profile leaves room for the boot peak. Wireless Moves saw peaks of about 40 W only in the first minute or two after power-on.

Wireless Moves tested what happens below the rule: an older 60 W USB-C bank left the Mini stuck in a boot loop, while the 100 W bank ran it. A bigger number on the box doesn't add anything either. USB-IF says USB PD tops out at 100 W at 20 V and 5 A, and 140 W needs the newer 28 V level. The Mini asks for 20 V, so a 140 W port gives it the same 100 W a 100 W port does.

Read the per-port profile, not the total wattage on the box. The Anker C1000 Gen 2 has three USB-C ports, but only USB-C2 lists 20V 5A. USB-C1 shares 20 W with the USB-A port. On the UGREEN 145W bank, USB-C2 stops at 20V-3.25A (65 W). On the Nexode 100W charger, USB-C3 gives 22.5 W, and the 100 W on C1 or C2 drops when other ports are in use. Both BLUETTI Elite stations list 20V/5A on both of their USB-C ports.

The cable matters too. A USB-C cable that carries more than 3 A must have an e-marker chip (Granite River Labs). Use Starlink's USB-C cable, and don't put a 3 A USB-C extension or adapter between it and the port.

Each station above also has a 12 V/10 A car outlet. That's 120 W, double the Mini's 60 W input rating. Starlink's Mini Car Adapter works with 12–24 V outlets, so the car outlet is a second way to run the Mini that doesn't depend on USB-C at all. This follows from the two makers' specs; neither maker has tested the pairing.

Not recommended

Skip 60–65 W chargers and power banks for the Mini, including 65 W GaN chargers that are excellent for laptops. Their 20 V level stops at about 3 A, below Starlink's 20V/5A minimum. Wireless Moves' 60 W bank boot-looped. DISHYtech's Colby Baber found a DC supply needed at least 120 W (12 V/10 A) to boot the Mini reliably.
