What the 2200X manual says that the sales page doesn't

Solar input is 1,200 W, not unpublished. The technical specifications in the 2200X user manual list solar input as 600 W × 2 through Anderson-compatible ports. The charging section gives the accepted range: 'The acceptable DC charging voltage range is 12-59 VDC. If any other VDC is used, the unit could be damaged.' With both inputs full, 2,240 Wh ÷ 1,200 W = 1.9 hours, which agrees with the manual's 2-hour DC charge time. That is more than the 1,000 W the Elite 200 V2 and DELTA 2 Max accept. Slow solar charging comes from the single 200 W panel in the bundle, not from the generator. If you add panels to a 2200X, keep each input's string under 59 V.

The 4,000 cycles are measured another way. The manual rates the battery at '>4000' life cycles 'down to 80% DOD'. Depth of discharge is how much of the battery each cycle uses. It says nothing about how much capacity is left when the cycles run out. EcoFlow rates the DELTA 2 Max at 3,000+ cycles until the battery holds 80% of its original capacity, and APH Networks lists the Elite 200 V2 at 6,000+ cycles to 80% of original capacity. So 4,000 against 6,000 is not a like-for-like gap. The 2200X figure simply doesn't say where its battery ends up.

Surge and lifting are different kinds of figure. The manual gives the 2200X a 4,400 W inverter peak for 0.5 seconds, which is the number that matters for starting a compressor or pump motor. BLUETTI's 3,900 W applies to 'pure resistive loads' (heaters, kettles) run at reduced power, and BLUETTI publishes no motor-start surge for the Elite 200 V2. StorageReview lists the DELTA 2 Max at 4,800 W surge. If a hard-starting motor is your main load, check its starting draw before you buy.

It won't charge in the cold. The manual gives 32–104 °F (0–40 °C) for operating and charging. In a winter outage, keep the unit indoors and run the panel outside on the 25-foot solar extension cable the manual mentions.

Not recommended

The 2200X + 200 W bundle at $3,199 for anyone who doesn't need its expansion packs. You pay $1.43 per Wh with a 1-year warranty, and the panel in the box refills the battery at one sixth of the rate the generator can accept. If expansion is the reason you're considering it, the Growatt INFINITY 2000 PRO takes the same 1,200 W of solar and expands to 6,144 Wh at $799 for the station.
