What the differences mean

Why 3,000 W is the line. A 20 A household socket at 120 V carries 20 A × 120 V = 2,400 W (Anker prints that limit for each 5-20R outlet on its F3000). So 3,000 W continuous is the first common rating above one socket's worth: two 1,500 W loads at once (2 × 12.5 A), or 25 of the 30 A an RV's TT-30 shore cord can draw (30 A × 120 V = 3,600 W). If your largest load is under 2,400 W and you run one thing at a time, a 2,400 W station costs less. Every row is rated at 3,000 W or more continuous from one unit by its own maker; surge and boost figures were not counted. Left out for falling short: the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max, sold with a 3,400 W X-Boost figure but rated 2,400 W ($879 at EcoFlow on Nov 27, 2026).

  • Amps. 3,000 W ÷ 120 V = 25 A. That is over a 20 A socket (2,400 W) and under a 30 A TT-30 (3,600 W).
  • Minutes at 3,000 W. Rated Wh × 0.85 ÷ 3,000 W × 60. Dabbsson 3000L: 3,072 × 0.85 ÷ 3,000 × 60 = 52 min. EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max Plus: 2,048 × 0.85 ÷ 3,000 × 60 = 35 min. The 0.85 is an assumption. StorageReview (Dylan Dougherty) measured 1.64 kWh from the EcoFlow, 80% of its rating: 1,640 ÷ 3,000 × 60 = 33 min. AndroidGuys (Scott Webster) measured about 1,740 Wh, which gives 35 min.
  • Cost per watt-hour, rival rows. Dabbsson $969 ÷ 3,072 Wh = $0.32. EcoFlow $1,149 ÷ 2,048 Wh = $0.56. Pecron $899 ÷ 3,072 Wh = $0.29, on a pre-order that ships mid-December.
  • Recharge owed. A 3,000 W load draws about 3,000 ÷ 0.85 = 3,529 W from the battery. On the 1,800 W wall input Dabbsson and Pecron state, that is 3,529 ÷ 1,800 = about 2 minutes of charging per minute of use.

Which outlet carries the 3,000 W

A station's 3,000 W rating is a total across its outlets. What one cord can draw depends on the socket it plugs into.

  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max Plus. EcoFlow's spec page lists four AC ports at 20 A max each. StorageReview's port list confirms four NEMA 5-20R outlets and no TT-30R. Any one socket gives 20 A × 120 V = 2,400 W, so 3,000 W needs two or more sockets. AndroidGuys reports it held a 3,000 W load and tripped only above about 3,800 W.
  • Dabbsson 3000L. Dabbsson lists six AC outlets and one TT-30 for RVs. A TT-30 is a 30 A socket (3,600 W), so the station's own 3,000 W rating is the limit on that outlet. Dabbsson's listing gives no separate watt figure for it.
  • ALLPOWERS R3500. Bernard Ryan at Battery Skills lists five 120 V 20 A outlets and one 30 A TT-30R. The store listing text we read gives the 3,200 W total and no per-outlet figure.
  • BLUETTI Apex 200. BLUETTI states 3,200 W continuous on its own. The 3,600 W in its bundle headlines needs an expansion battery or AC/solar input. It has a TT-30R. $1,149 on bluettipower.com, $1,091.55 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-apex-200
  • Anker SOLIX F3000. Anker's own table is the clearest statement of the rule: 2,400 W max per 5-20R outlet, 3,600 W from the TT-30R. It has no row because Anker's US store showed it sold out on Nov 27 with a 3 to 7 week ship notice.
  • DJI Power 2000. DJI rates the 100 to 120 V model (DYM2000L) at 25 A in total across four AC outputs (25 A × 120 V = 3,000 W) and names no 30 A outlet. It has no row because DJI's US store did not return a price to us on Nov 27.
  • Jackery HomePower 3000. Outage House lists the same split: 2,400 W per household outlet, 3,600 W from the 30 A outlet.

Boost modes are not continuous output. X-Boost (EcoFlow, 3,800 W), P-Boost (Dabbsson, 3,600 W) and Power Lifting (BLUETTI, 5,400 W) are figures each maker lists apart from its rated watts.

Cycle counts use different yardsticks. Dabbsson counts 4,000 cycles to over 80% capacity and Pecron 3,500+ to 80%. BLUETTI counts the Apex 200's cycles to 70%. ALLPOWERS gives 3,500+ with no percentage in the listing we read.

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure here is the maker's, or a named reviewer's measurement, read on Nov 27, 2026.

Not recommended

A "3,400 W" listing for a 3,000 W job. The EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max is rated 2,400 W. The 3,400 W is its X-Boost figure, not its rated output.

Running 3,000 W while plugged into a wall socket. Pass-through is capped by the cord feeding the station. DJI rates the Power 2000 at 12 A in bypass (12 A × 120 V = 1,440 W). Anker's F3000 takes 3,600 W only from a 30 A source, and only for under 3 hours; its continuous input is 24 A. Outage House lists 1,440 W for the Jackery HomePower 3000 with the grid on.
