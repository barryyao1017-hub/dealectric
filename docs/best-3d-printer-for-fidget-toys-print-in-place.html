What the differences mean

  • A1 mini first layer: Bambu's base 0.20 mm profile shrinks the first layer by 0.15 mm, and the A1 mini profile overrides that to 0. That leaves 0.15 − 0 = 0.15 mm of shrink missing, exactly the low end of the −0.15 to −0.2 mm first-layer compensation that 3dprintgearlab gives for print-in-place.
  • A1 first layer: 0.15 − 0.075 = 0.075 mm missing, half of Bambu's own base value.
  • QIDI and Flashforge: 0.15 − 0.15 = 0, so their stock profiles already sit at the recommended value.
  • Plate area: (256 × 256) ÷ (180 × 180) = 65,536 ÷ 32,400 = 2.02×, so the A1 holds about twice as many same-size fidgets per plate as the A1 mini.
  • Long fidgets: the A1 mini's diagonal is 180 × 1.414 = 254.6 mm. A 200 mm articulated dragon (extruly's size, 4–5 h on the A1 mini) fits only laid diagonally, and only if it is no wider than 254.6 − 200 = 54.6 mm. On the A1 it fits straight, since 200 ≤ 256.
  • QIDI price step: the Q2 and Q2C have the same 270 × 270 mm plate. At QIDI's store the gap is 449 − 349 = $100.

Why print-in-place joints fuse at the plate, and the one setting to change

Bambu Studio describes elephant-foot compensation as a setting to "shrink the initial layer on build plate to compensate for elephant foot effect." The first layer is pressed down and spreads, so any gap that starts on the plate gets narrower there. Fidget designs usually leave only 0.2 mm (3dprintgearlab's default clearance). A Fab Academy clearance ladder printed in PLA on an A1 (Mishael Sharaf) showed how little margin that leaves: 0.05–0.10 mm gaps fused, 0.15 mm moved with friction, and 0.20–0.25 mm moved smoothly.

The A1 mini handles fine gaps. IgorsLab (Tim Kutzner) printed Sci3D's clearance fidget spinner in Bambu Basic PLA, and every wheel down to a 0.15 mm gap turned straight off the plate without much force. What the stock profile leaves out is the first-layer shrink. Bambu Studio's A1 mini profiles inherit 0.15 mm from Bambu's base profile and then override it to 0 mm, in both the 0.20 mm Standard and 0.16 mm High Quality presets. The A1 profile overrides it to 0.075 mm. QIDI Studio's Q2 and Q2C profiles and Orca-Flashforge's Adventurer 5M and 5M Pro profiles keep 0.15 mm.

What to do on a Bambu printer: in the process settings, set Elephant foot compensation to 0.15 mm and save it as your own preset before slicing a fidget whose hinges or rings sit on the plate. That is the value Bambu's own base profile uses. With that one change, the A1 mini is the printer to get for most fidget toys, and the A1 is the choice when you need the larger plate.

Not recommended

QIDI Q2 for fidget toys alone. It has the same 270 × 270 mm plate and the same 0.15 mm stock profile as the Q2C, for $100 more at QIDI's store. The extra money pays for a 65 °C heated chamber and a 370 °C nozzle meant for engineering filaments, while extruly recommends matte PLA for articulated fidgets. Buy the Q2 if you also print ABS, ASA or nylon parts.
