What the differences mean

  • The outlet limit. Under the NEC, a load that runs at full current for 3 hours or more is continuous, and the breaker must cover 125% of it. That leaves 80% of the breaker for the load: 15 A × 120 V × 0.8 = 1,440 W (12 A).
  • 18 A chargers. 58.4 V × 18 A = 1,051 W at the very end of the charge, which leaves headroom on a shared 15 A circuit. 100 Ah ÷ 18 A = 5.6 h of constant current, which matches the 5.6 h Redodo quotes for its own 48V 100Ah kit.
  • LiTime 30A. 51.2 V × 30 A = 1,536 W and 58.4 V × 30 A = 1,752 W. Both are above 1,440 W before conversion loss is added, and 100 Ah ÷ 30 A = 3.3 h, which is past the 3-hour mark for a continuous load.
  • Vatrer 20A. Vatrer lists a 13 A maximum AC input, which is above the 12 A continuous figure for a 15 A circuit (15 × 0.8).
  • Lester Summit II 650W. 650 W ÷ 58.4 V = 11.1 A near full charge. Per amp it costs $409 ÷ 13.5 A = $30.30/A, against $149.99 ÷ 18 A = $8.33/A for the Redodo.

Will it run on your garage outlet?

A lithium charger spends most of its cycle in the constant-current stage, drawing close to its full power for hours. The wall has to supply that output plus conversion loss, so DC output is the lowest the wall draw can be. Here is what each maker publishes for the input side:

All five accept 240 V input. On a 240 V circuit the LiTime 30A's 1,752 W works out to about 7.3 A before losses (1,752 ÷ 240), so it is a reasonable fast charger in a garage wired that way. On a shared 15 A, 120 V outlet, an 18 A unit is the fastest charger in this group that stays under the continuous limit. The Lester works differently. Lester's FAQ says the Summit II charges lithium on its standard wet-cell (IEI) profiles, with the battery's own controller capping the voltage, and that its chargers need about 15 VDC at the terminals to start. That suits a cart whose lithium pack talks to the charger. It does not suit a generic drop-in pack whose BMS has disconnected after a deep discharge. Golf Cart Lab sells the 650W with a Club Car 3-pin cord at $690 and a 48-month Lester warranty.

Not recommended

LiTime 30A on a shared 15 A outlet. Its 1,536–1,752 W output is already above the 1,440 W continuous figure before any conversion loss, and it holds that load for more than 3 hours on a 100 Ah pack. LiTime also recommends it for indoor use. Buy it only for a dedicated 20 A or 240 V circuit. Vatrer E15-58.4V20A for now. Vatrer lists it as a pre-order, its program ends in a float stage, and its 13 A maximum input is above 12 A on a 15 A circuit.
