What the differences mean

TMR (tunnel magnetoresistance) is the newest magnetic-switch sensor, and several TMR boards are priced just over $100. On 2026-11-26 MonsGeek's M1 V5 TMR was $134.99 on MonsGeek's site (list $149.99), Womier's K75 TMR was $119.99 at Womier and Epomaker's HE75 V2 TMR was $109.65 at Epomaker. Those three are left out for being just over the line. The sensor name alone is not a reason to buy: Wooting, which sells Hall effect keyboards, wrote on 27 May 2026 that TMR "hasn't taken the lead in either performance or stability" and that firmware and software are the biggest factors for accuracy.

  • Latency against the "0.125 ms" on spec sheets: 1 ÷ 8,000 Hz = 0.125 ms is the gap between reports, not key-to-PC time. HLPlanet's 0.60 ms by cable for the TK75 TMR is 0.60 ÷ 0.125 = 4.8 report intervals. Going wireless cost 0.75 − 0.60 = 0.15 ms.
  • Bluetooth: 1,000 Hz on the TK75 TMR is one report every 1 ms. 125 Hz on the HE65 V2 TMR is one every 1 ÷ 125 = 8 ms.

These tests are not like for like. Only Notebookcheck's figure is tied to 8,000 Hz on 2.4 GHz. APH Networks ran the HE65 V2 TMR at 1,000 Hz, and Smartzone does not say which connection it used. Read 32 ÷ 3 = 10.7 to 56 ÷ 3 = 18.7 times the FUN60's runtime as a direction, not a lab ratio. Smartzone still lists "moderate battery life" among its cons for the TK75 TMR and notes that a lower polling rate adds a little runtime. On the FUN60 Ultra TMR that option is missing: Notebookcheck writes that the driver cannot change the polling rate.

Latency does not separate the boards. HLPlanet's 0.60 ms by cable for the TK75 TMR sits level with the 0.5 to 0.6 ms that GamePadSquire quotes from RTINGS and IGN for the FUN60 Ultra TMR. Those are different test rigs, so the table does not rank across them. The HE65 V2 TMR's 0.125 ms is Epomaker's own figure and equals the 8,000 Hz report interval; neither review read for this page measured it.

Two limits on the pick, both from HLPlanet. With rapid-trigger release set below about 0.3 mm, firm presses can flex the plate and register a release on neighbouring keys; it suggests about 0.4 mm, well above the 0.01 mm on the spec sheet. Keys fitted with a mechanical switch need rapid trigger turned off or they can double-click. The case is plastic, where the FUN60 Ultra TMR's is aluminum.

Not recommended: the look-alike FUN60 Ultra listing

MonsGeek sells the FUN60 Ultra with either sensor. Its combined FUN60 Ultra page showed $69.99 to $89.99 on 2026-11-26, while its TMR page showed $79.99 to $89.99, so the $69.99 belongs to a Hall effect variant, not a TMR board. That page marks mechanical-switch support as "Supported (TMR Sensor)" and "Not Supported (HE Sensor)". Neither page tied a price to each variant, and both carried a pre-order notice and a waitlist form; Notebookcheck gives $79.99 for wired and $89.99 for tri-mode. Choose the TMR variant on MonsGeek's page and read its stock line before paying. For switch swaps, MonsGeek says the FUN60 reads magnets with the N pole facing down, names Akko's Magnetic Creamy Yellow as not compatible, and promises 0.01 mm precision only for the six switches listed in its driver; others may run at 0.1 mm.
