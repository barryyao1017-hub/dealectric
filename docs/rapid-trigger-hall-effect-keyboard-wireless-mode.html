What the differences mean

Polling rate sets the gap between reports to the PC. 1,000 ms ÷ 8,000 Hz = 0.125 ms. 1,000 ms ÷ 1,000 Hz = 1 ms. 1,000 ms ÷ 125 Hz = 8 ms. A board that holds 8,000 Hz on its 2.4 GHz dongle sends 8 reports in the time a 1,000 Hz board sends one, and 64 in the time a 125 Hz Bluetooth link sends one (8,000 ÷ 125).

On the pick, the measured cost of going wireless is small. Marcus Richardson at HLPlanet measured the Gamakay TK75 TMR at 0.60 ms wired and 0.75 ms on 2.4 GHz with OSLTT: 0.75 − 0.60 = 0.15 ms, or 25% more (0.15 ÷ 0.60). One frame on a 240 Hz monitor lasts 1,000 ÷ 240 = 4.17 ms, so 0.15 ms is about 4% of a frame. Bluetooth costs far more: HLPlanet's review of the Epomaker HE75 V2 TMR lists 15 ms on Bluetooth against 0.58 ms on 2.4 GHz, about 26 times as long (15 ÷ 0.58) and 3.6 frames at 240 Hz (15 ÷ 4.17).

Measured: wired against 2.4 GHz and Bluetooth

Rapid trigger is worked out inside the keyboard. Gamakay describes it this way: "This feature allows keys to be activated or deactivated based on their travel distance rather than a fixed point". The Aula listing says rapid trigger "adjusts activation and reset with key movement". The board reads each key's position (HLPlanet's spec table lists a 256,000 Hz scan rate for the TK75 TMR), decides press or release, then sends the result over whichever link is active. The link changes how often and how late that result arrives. It does not change where the key triggers. The maker and seller pages we read list rapid trigger and the wireless modes side by side and give polling by connection. None adds a sentence joining the two, and neither reviewer says rapid trigger was switched on during the 2.4 GHz test. So the evidence that it holds up on a dongle is the measured latency below.

Use Bluetooth for typing and for a second device. For rapid trigger in games, use the dongle or the cable.

Not recommended for wireless rapid trigger

Two listings look like wireless boards and are not. The Aula Hero 68 HE ($69.46 at aulatechstore.com, 2026-11-21) is USB-C only, with no 2.4 GHz or Bluetooth. The Gamakay x NaughShark NS68 is sold in a Wired version ($36.79) and a Tri-mode version ($39.99, both read 2026-11-21) on one product page; the Wired version has no radio, so pick Tri-mode if you want it off the cable. And on any board in the table, Bluetooth is the wrong mode for rapid trigger play: the Aula listing puts its Bluetooth rate at 125 Hz, which is one report every 8 ms.
