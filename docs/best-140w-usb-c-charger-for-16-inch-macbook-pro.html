Among power adapters, Apple names only its 140W USB-C Power Adapter for fast charging a 16-inch MacBook Pro (up to 50% in about 30 minutes). USB-IF ties 140 W to PD 3.1's 28 V level (28 V × 5 A = 140 W), while older chargers stop at 20 V × 5 A = 100 W. A 100 W charger still charges the Mac (Apple's minimum is 94 W), but it cannot fast charge it. Every charger in the table gives 140 W from one port used alone, on its maker's figure. 100 W models, such as the UGREEN Nexode 100W 4-Port, were left out for that reason. The 16-inch M5 Pro and M5 Max ship with Apple's 140 W adapter in the box, so this page is about a second or replacement charger. Dealectric does not test units. What the differences mean

  • With a phone plugged in, 100 W is 71% of fast-charge power (100 ÷ 140 = 0.71). Apple's half charge works out to about 100 W into the battery (0.5 × 99.6 Wh ÷ 0.5 h = 99.6 W). At 100 W, the quickest possible half charge before losses is 29.9 minutes (49.8 Wh ÷ 100 W × 60). At 140 W it is 21.3 minutes (49.8 ÷ 140 × 60).
  • AOHi with a second device: Chongdiantou measured 88.82 W with a USB-A device on the second port. That gives a floor of 33.6 minutes to half charge (49.8 ÷ 88.82 × 60). With a second device on USB-C2 the port measured 62.02 W, a floor of 48.2 minutes (49.8 ÷ 62.02 × 60).
  • Three devices at once: the UGREEN and the Anker Prime keep 100 W on the MacBook port. The Anker 4-Port keeps 100 W when the other two devices use USB-C3 and USB-A, which then share 24 W (Anker's table). With a device on its other 140 W port it falls to 65 W, which is 46% of 140 W (65 ÷ 140 = 0.46).
  • The extra 20 W on a 160 W charger goes to the other ports, not the MacBook. 160 − 140 = 20 W would be left for a phone if the Mac kept 140 W. Neither maker splits it that way: both cut the MacBook port to 100 W.
  • Output drop on the Anker 4-Port: techtest.org read 28 V × 4.28 A = 119.8 W after about 30 minutes. That is 86% of 140 W (119.8 ÷ 140).
  • Weight: the UGREEN, at about 360 g, is 140 g heavier than the Anker Prime at 220 g (360 − 220).

Why the MacBook port falls to 100 W once a second device is plugged in

The 140 W level exists only at 28 V. USB-IF says the new 28 V fixed voltage enables up to 140 W, and that before PD 3.1 the limit was 100 W at 20 V and 5 A. Granite River Labs, a USB test lab, describes how the switch works. The laptop asks the charger to enter EPR mode. If it leaves EPR mode, the voltage drops to 20 V or lower and the old 100 W ceiling applies. A 100 W entry in a maker's split table matches the old 20 V ceiling, though no maker here says which voltage the port then uses (techtest.org saw the Anker 4-Port stay at 28 V when it throttled to 120 W). That is the figure for the Anker 4-Port (Anker's table), the Anker Prime 160W (Anker's split as Notebookcheck lists it) and the UGREEN Nexode Pro 160W 5-Port (UGREEN's figure as Notebookcheck reports it). A 160 W total does not change it. Anker's app can prioritize USB-C1, but Anker does not say any mode holds 140 W while a second port is in use. The only way to keep 140 W with nothing shared is a single-port charger such as Apple's own adapter.

Which cable and port keep 28 V. Apple lists the USB-C to MagSafe 3 cable for every 16-inch model from 2021 on. It lists the 240W USB-C Charge Cable only for the 16-inch from November 2023 or later. Anker's FAQ says the same by chip: M1 and M2 16-inch models need the MagSafe 3 cable, while M3 and M4 models can use an EPR (140 W or 240 W) USB-C cable or MagSafe 3. Granite River Labs notes that the cable's e-marker chip must declare it EPR-capable (50 V, 5 A). A 100 W e-marked cable never gets 28 V, so the Mac stays at 100 W. On the UGREEN, plug the Mac into USB-C1 or USB-C2: USB-C3 is a PD 3.0 port capped at 100 W (Notebookcheck). On the AOHi, only USB-C1 offers 28 V (Chongdiantou's protocol read-out). Notebookcheck's UGREEN box held only the charger and a manual. Plan on the MagSafe 3 cable that came with the Mac.

Unit notes. Chongdiantou tested AOHi's China-market Youth 140W in 2024, so its readings describe that unit, not one bought in the US today. UGREEN's 100 W with up to three devices is the maker's figure. Notebookcheck's own test measured 56–58 W on C1 with all five ports busy, which is in line with UGREEN's 65 W rating for four or five devices.
