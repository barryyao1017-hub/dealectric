What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every watt figure on this page is a named tester's measurement or the maker's stated rating, with the date it was read. Hours to full are arithmetic: capacity divided by the car-side watts, before conversion losses, so a real run takes somewhat longer.

  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 + 800W Alternator Charger: 1,024 Wh / 800 W = 1.3 h.
  • BLUETTI Elite 100 V2 + Charger 2: 1,024 Wh / 640 W = 1.6 h; 1,024 Wh / 500 W = 2.0 h.
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 + Alternator Charger: 1,024 Wh / 600 W = 1.7 h.
  • BLUETTI AC180 + Charger 1: 1,152 Wh / 500 W = 2.3 h.
  • BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 + Charger 1: 2,073.6 Wh / 560 W = 3.7 h; 2,073.6 Wh / 500 W = 4.1 h.
  • A 1 kWh station on the 12 V socket: 1,024 Wh / 108 W = 9.5 h.
  • Speed gap: 800 W / 108 W = 7.4 times the socket rate with EcoFlow's alternator charger.

Measured car charging: what testers saw

An alternator charger is a DC-DC charger wired to the starter battery, so it bypasses the 12 V socket, whose wiring and fuse hold most stations near 100 W. In 4X4 Australia's lab, Colin Dedman measured 98-128 W at 13.2 V for 11 of 13 stations (two outliers lower: Renogy Phoenix 222 at 33 W, Dometic PLB15 at 65 W). The EcoFlow DELTA 3 1500 took 106 W, or 198 W with the XT60i input's centre pin shorted to unlock its full 15 A.

EcoFlow 800W Alternator Charger. The Solar Lab ran it from a Jeep Wrangler into an EcoFlow DELTA Pro: it reached 800 W almost at once and did not drop below 800 W with the headlights, A/C and stereo on. GoRV ran it on a 200 Series LandCruiser into an EcoFlow Delta station: 800 W at idle, 700 W with headlights, high beams and a light bar on, back to 800 W with slightly higher revs, drawing 65-70 A from the vehicle. Neither test used a DELTA 3 itself; EcoFlow lists the 800W charger as a DELTA 3 and DELTA 3 Plus recharging method. The full 800 W runs through the station's extra-battery (XT150) port: EcoFlow says its XT60 output cable limits the charger to 500 W.

BLUETTI Charger 2. BLUETTI rates it at up to 800 W from the alternator and 600 W from solar; the 1,200 W headline needs both. The Solar Lab, testing two units in Canada across several vehicles, never saw more than 500 W from an alternator; a firmware update BLUETTI offered would not install, and the reviewer suggested US units may behave differently. GoRV measured 640 W at most on a LandCruiser and put part of the gap down to its own battery connections and other loads on the alternator.

BLUETTI Charger 1. Rated 560 W. Love Your RV saw about 500 W from a vehicle into a BLUETTI AC70. On the AC180, the station's 500 W DC input is the ceiling anyway.

Anker SOLIX Alternator Charger. Car Watch (Takuya Shoda, 14 May 2026) measured a 597 W peak into a SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 in a kei-class van, just after engine start; it fell with the A/C on. A small van's alternator may be the limit there, but the C1000 Gen 2's own XT60i input tops out at 600 W, so this pairing cannot reach 800 W in any vehicle.

Before wiring any of these in, check that the alternator has spare output for a 65-70 A draw at 12 V, and fuse the cable run as the charger's manual specifies.

Not recommended

Buying BLUETTI Charger 2 for car-only speed. It is rated 800 W from the alternator, but the two named tests measured 500 W and 640 W, no better than Charger 1's 560 W rating, and The Solar Lab could not recommend it at the time of its test. It earns its place only when roof solar feeds through it as well.
