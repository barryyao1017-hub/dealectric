What the differences mean

The battery sizes are all close to 1,000 Wh. The big differences are how much panel comes in the box and how much AC power the station puts out. Dealectric does not test units. Every figure below comes from the maker's store or a named reviewer, and the hours are arithmetic.

  • Refill time. The P1800 + 600 W kit needs 1,024 Wh ÷ 600 W = 1.7 h of full sun. The Elite 100 V2 + 200 W needs 1,024 ÷ 200 = 5.1 h, so three times the panel watts gets the battery full in a third of the sun-hours.
  • Real output is lower than rated. OutdoorGearLab measured 150 W in one hour of sun from BLUETTI's 200W portable panel. At that rate a 1,024 Wh battery needs 1,024 ÷ 150 = 6.8 h, not 5.1 h.
  • Price per Wh. P1800 + 600 W: 718.20 ÷ 1,024 = 0.70. Elite 100 mini + 200 W: 616.55 ÷ 1,004.8 = 0.61. Pioneer Na + 200 W: 949.05 ÷ 900 = 1.05.
  • AC output. The Elite 100 mini's outlets share 700 W in total (BLUETTI), so a 1,500 W kettle or space heater draws 1,500 − 700 = 800 W more than it can supply. The P1800, Elite 100 V2, AC180 and DELTA 3 are rated at 1,800 W.

Solar refill: what the panels in the box can do

A kit refills no faster than the smaller of two numbers: the panel watts in the box and the station's solar input limit. The table divides the battery by the panel watts at rated output, so it is the best case. Clouds, panel angle and heat lower it.

  • ALLPOWERS P1800: ALLPOWERS states up to 650 W of solar through its XT60 input, so the 600 W kit already uses nearly all of it. Adding panels later gains at most 650 − 600 = 50 W.
  • BLUETTI Elite 100 V2: BLUETTI lists a 1,000 W solar input. With the 200 W kit panel connected, 1,000 − 200 = 800 W of input is left for panels you add later. BLUETTI's '1,000W Solar Input: Full charge in 70 mins' line assumes about 1,000 W of panels, five times what this kit ships with. The station is rated for 4,000+ cycles to 80% of original capacity.
  • BLUETTI Elite 100 mini: its input tops out at 350 W, so even the 350 W kit leaves no room to add panels.
  • BLUETTI AC180: the manual caps solar input at 500 W, 12–60 V and 10 A. Two panels wired in parallel add their current together and run into the 10 A limit, so a two-panel AC180 setup needs series wiring to use its full panel rating.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3: it accepts up to 500 W of solar (OutdoorGearLab spec table), so the 220 W kit can take 280 W more panel. OutdoorGearLab measured an energy output of 870 Wh from it.

Where the line was drawn: batteries of 800–1,200 Wh (within 20% of 1,000 Wh), sold with their panels as one listing. Left out: the Elite 100 V2 + 350 W, Elite 100 mini + 350 W and Pioneer Na + 350 W, which are pre-orders whose 350 W panel ships Jan 10 (BLUETTI, 2026-11-26). Also left out: the ALLPOWERS R1500 + SF100, a pre-order whose dispatch date ALLPOWERS listed as to be confirmed on 2026-11-26. Last, the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 + 200 W: Anker's US store says orders are estimated to ship in early December, and the page showed no price when we read it.
