What the differences mean

  • Light in a lit room: the CO-FH02 on mains gives 2,003 ÷ 217 = 9.2× the light the Capsule 3 Laser gives on battery, and 2,003 ÷ 341 = 5.9× its output on AC.
  • Image size: ProjectorScreen.com puts offices and classrooms at 40–59 foot-lamberts. The largest 16:9 image at 40 fL (1.0-gain screen) is √(lumens ÷ 40 × 144 ÷ 0.4273): for the CO-FH02, √(2,003 ÷ 40 × 144 ÷ 0.4273) = 130 in. For the Capsule 3 Laser on battery, √(217 ÷ 40 × 144 ÷ 0.4273) = 43 in, about the size of a TV.
  • Cost per measured lumen: CO-W01 $399.99 ÷ 2,061 = $0.19; CO-FH02 $499.99 ÷ 2,003 = $0.25; Capsule 3 Laser $749.99 ÷ 341 = $2.20.
  • What the extra $100 for the CO-FH02 buys: sharpness, not brightness (TechGearLab measured 2,003 lm against the CO-W01's 2,061). It has (1920 × 1080) ÷ (1280 × 800) = 2.0× the pixels. TechGearLab found "good edge-to-edge clarity" on spreadsheets with the CO-FH02. On the CO-W01 it said clarity "leaves something to be desired, especially on fullscreen spreadsheets".

Rated lumens vs what labs measured

A rating is the most a projector produces under the maker's test. Two things cut it in a meeting: the lab's real-world settings and, for portables, the battery. On mains the two Epson Flex models measured about two-thirds of their 3,000-lumen rating. On battery the minis lose a third or more, and the mode that reaches the advertised runtime is the dimmest.

ProjectorCentral found battery power cut the Capsule 3 Laser's brightness by about 36% in every picture mode (217 ÷ 341 = 0.64). Projector Reviews measured it lower still, at 126 lumens on battery. XGIMI's 2.5-hour battery figure is for ECO mode, which ProjectorCentral measured at 151 lumens. Its Standard mode, at 258 lumens, still lasted through a 90-minute film.

Connections to check before the meeting: Epson lists one HDMI input on the CO-FH02. Its user guide plugs the Android TV streaming player into that same HDMI port, inside the side compartment. To connect a laptop you open the cover and unplug the player, so pack an HDMI cable, plus a USB-C-to-HDMI adapter if your laptop has only USB-C. The PowerLite 1781W has HDMI (MHL), VGA and USB Type-B plug-and-play inputs. Epson gives Miracast mirroring only to the 1785W and 1795F, so wireless on the 1781W goes through the Epson iProjection app. On macOS 10.15 to 14 that app needs Screen Recording permission.

Not recommended

NEBULA Capsule 3 Laser for lit meeting rooms. At $749.99 it costs $250 more than the CO-FH02. It measured 341 lumens on AC, enough for a 54-inch image at office brightness, against the CO-FH02's 130 inches. On battery it measured 192 lumens in Standard. ProjectorCentral measured the MoGo 4 at 258 lumens in Standard, and it lasted a 90-minute film.
