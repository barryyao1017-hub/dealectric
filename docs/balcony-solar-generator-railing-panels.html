What the differences mean

Every yield figure comes from NREL's PVWatts model. The panel faces south and hangs vertically (90° tilt) in Los Angeles, with 14% system losses and no shade from the balcony above, so treat each figure as a ceiling.

  • Two panels double the worst month. In June, 400 W gives 14.64 kWh ÷ 30 days = 0.49 kWh a day, and 200 W gives 7.32 ÷ 30 = 0.24 kWh.
  • Winter is the railing's best season. Two panels give 45.50 kWh ÷ 31 = 1.47 kWh a day; one panel gives 22.75 ÷ 31 = 0.73.
  • June refill time. AC180 with two panels: 1,152 Wh ÷ 488 Wh a day = 2.4 days. With one panel: 1,152 ÷ 244 = 4.7 days. Charging losses and anything plugged in make both longer.
  • What a day's sun runs. Solar.com puts an ENERGY STAR fridge at about 460 kWh a year, or 460 ÷ 365 = 1.26 kWh a day. Two railing panels make 1.47 kWh a day in December, enough to cover it. In June they make 0.49 kWh, which covers 0.49 ÷ 1.26 = 39%.
  • Railing length. Each SP200L is 2,105 mm (82.8 in) long and 608 mm (23.9 in) tall unfolded, so two side by side need 2 × 2,105 = 4.21 m (13.8 ft).
  • Bundle saving. The AC180 ($499) and the 2-panel pack ($798) cost $1,297 bought separately; the bundle is $1,232, which saves $65.

Why a railing panel is a winter panel

A panel hung on a railing stands vertical. Low winter sun hits it almost face-on, while high June sun strikes it at a steep angle. Output therefore peaks in winter and bottoms out in summer, the reverse of a tilted rooftop panel.

How you wire the second panel decides whether its power reaches the battery. The AC180 accepts 12–60 V at up to 10 A and 500 W. Each SP200L is rated 24.6 V open-circuit and 10.8 A short-circuit.

  • Series (panel 1's + to panel 2's −): 2 × 24.6 = 49.2 V, and the current stays at 10.8 A, so the full 400 W can reach the station. The two free leads go into one extension cable, then into the AC180's own 1.5 m solar charging cable.
  • Parallel (MC4 Y-branch): 2 × 10.8 = 21.6 A against a 10 A cap, which allows at most 24.6 V × 10 A = 246 W, and less in practice.

Turn the AC outlets off when nothing needs them. The Solar Lab calculated the Elite 100 V2's idle draw at about 16 W with the AC inverter on and no load. Over a day that is 16 W × 24 h = 384 Wh, more than one 200 W railing panel's 244 Wh a day in a Los Angeles June.

Not recommended

The 3 × 200 W panel pack for an AC180. BLUETTI sells the SP200L as a 3-pack ($1,197). Three in series reach 3 × 24.6 = 73.8 V, over the AC180's 60 V limit. The manual says panels above 60 V open-circuit voltage will damage the unit, and it reports PV over-voltage as error E033. Three in parallel run into the same 10 A cap as two. For the AC180, stop at two panels in series.
