What the differences mean

Immich's own requirements page asks for 6 GB of RAM minimum and 8 GB recommended, and says a 4 GB machine can run it only with machine learning disabled. Home Assistant's installation page says a Container (Docker) install has no Apps, the add-on store, and no out-of-the-box Thread or Z-Wave. To get full Home Assistant on a NAS you run Home Assistant OS as a virtual machine, which Home Assistant specifies at 2 GB of RAM, 2 vCPUs and UEFI boot. Subtracting those two from each enclosure's RAM gives the table's headroom column:

  • UGREEN DXP2800: 8 − 6 − 2 = 0 GB at stock. After the one-slot upgrade to 16 GB, 16 − 8 − 2 = 6 GB, with Immich at its recommended 8 GB.
  • TerraMaster F2-425 Plus: 8 − 6 − 2 = 0 GB at stock. At TerraMaster's 32 GB ceiling, 32 − 8 − 2 = 22 GB. Intel's spec sheet rates the N150 at 16 GB, which gives 16 − 8 − 2 = 6 GB, the same as the DXP2800.
  • TerraMaster F2-425: 4 − 6 = −2 GB before Home Assistant is counted. Immich's no-machine-learning mode needs all 4 GB.
  • Synology DS225+: at its 6 GB maximum, 6 − 8 − 2 = −4 GB. At stock 2 GB it fails Synology's own rule that virtual machines run only on a NAS with more than 2 GB of memory.
  • Asustor AS5402T: 4 − 6 − 2 = −4 GB at stock; 16 − 8 − 2 = 6 GB after filling both slots with 8 GB modules.

Memory budget, VM support and running cost

Immich's requirements page also says the Postgres database should live on a local SSD, never a network share, so the M.2 slots matter: the DXP2800 has two, the F2-425 Plus three (ITPro), and the Synology DS225+ none. Synology's Virtual Machine Manager specification states that VMs run only on a NAS with more than 2 GB of memory and only on Btrfs volumes, and that it imports qcow2 images, which is the format Home Assistant publishes for KVM. Synology's DS225+ page lists 2 GB, one slot and a 6 GB maximum, and warns that non-Synology memory modules void complete warranty and technical support. For UGREEN, the esp32.co.uk guide to installing Home Assistant on NASync (3 October 2026) states that current DXP models get Docker and the Virtual Machine app, the DH4300 Plus gets Docker only, and the DH2300 gets neither; it imports the qcow2 image with UEFI boot. Asustor lists VirtualBox as an official ADM app for the AS5402T. We did not verify VM support in TOS 6 on TerraMaster's own pages, so this page makes no claim about it; the F2-425 Plus figures above are hardware only.

Electricity barely separates the x86 boxes. At the EIA's August 2026 US residential average of 18.16 ¢/kWh, the DXP2800's UGREEN-rated 16.38 W in access is 16.38 × 8.76 = 143.5 kWh, or $26.06 a year. Tim Kutzner at igor'sLAB measured 14.5 W at the wall during transfers with one NVMe SSD and no HDDs, which is 127.0 kWh, or $23.07 a year, and 5.6 W in standby; noise was 33.2 dB(A) at 40 cm, equal to the room floor. Synology's rated 16.98 W for the DS225+ is $27.01 a year. Asustor's rated 22.9 W for the AS5402T is $36.43 a year, about $10 more than the DXP2800.

Not recommended

Synology DS225+ for this workload. Its 6 GB hard ceiling equals Immich's minimum, sits 2 GB under Immich's recommendation, and leaves nothing for the Home Assistant OS VM; at stock 2 GB Virtual Machine Manager will not run at all, and the only approved upgrade route is Synology's own 4 GB module. UGREEN DH2300: no official Docker or VM support in UGOS Pro per the esp32.co.uk guide. TerraMaster F2-425 at stock: 4 GB means Immich without machine learning and no VM.
