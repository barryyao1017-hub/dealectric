Charging while you drive: car socket or alternator charger

BLUETTI's Charger 1 FAQ puts a 12 V cigarette-lighter socket at about 100 W and a 24 V vehicle at about 200 W. StorageReview's spec table lists the Elite 200 V2's car input at 96 W from 12 V and 192 W from 24 V. Charger 1 is a DC-DC charger wired to the van's starter battery. Its box holds a DC input cable and an MC4 output cable, so it feeds the station's solar/DC input at up to 560 W while the engine runs.

The station's input sets the ceiling. The Elite 200 V2 accepts 12–60 V at 20 A, up to 1,000 W, so it takes all 560 W. The AC180's input stops at 500 W and 10 A (BLUETTI spec page), so Charger 1 runs at 500 W at most there: 1,030 Wh ÷ 500 W = 2.1 h. From a 12 V socket, BLUETTI lists the AC180 at 10.1–10.6 h.

One input, one source at a time. APH Networks describes a single front DC input on the Elite 200 V2 for charging from "your car or solar panels". Charger 1 occupies that input, so a roof panel and Charger 1 take turns. BLUETTI's Charger 2 merges alternator and solar into one 1,200 W feed; BLUETTI rates it at 2 kWh in 2.4 h with both sources combined. The Elite 200 V2 + Charger 2 bundle is $1,299 with no panel. EcoFlow says its 800 W Alternator Charger needs an adapter to connect to the DELTA 3 Max Plus, and StorageReview lists two 11–60 V XT60 inputs on that station.

Not recommended: the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 on one panel or the car socket

Anker rates the C1000 Gen 2's input at 600 W only between 29 and 60 V (14.5 A); from 11 to 28 V it is held to 8.2 A. One BLUETTI SP200L 200 W panel runs at 20.52 V (Vmp), so 20.52 V × 8.2 A = 168 W, about 16% under the panel's rating. If car charging goes through the same XT60i input, 8.2 A × 12–14.4 V = 98–118 W, and OutdoorGearLab's measured 890 Wh then takes 7.5–9.1 h of driving. Two such panels in series (about 41 V) clear the limit, so this station suits a two-panel roof, not a single folding panel.
