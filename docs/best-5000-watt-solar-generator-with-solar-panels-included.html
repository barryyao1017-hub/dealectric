What the differences mean

  • A 5,000 W load empties 5 kWh in about an hour. 5,120 Wh / 5,000 W × 60 = 61 minutes on nameplate. BLUETTI's guide gives the B4810 battery 85–89% usable energy: 4,352–4,557 Wh / 5,000 W × 60 = 52–55 minutes.

Solar input and install: what each kit connects to

BLUETTI 5kWh RV Kit. BLUETTI's own guide says the RV5 hub "has a strict 50V VOC (Voltage Open Circuit) limit on the PV input" and that "all panels MUST be wired in PARALLEL, never in series." It recommends high-amp Y-branch adapters and 6-gauge cable for the parallel array. The box holds a drilling template, eight M8 wood screws, eight anchors and wiring packs for the battery, the alternator charger and the distribution panel. BLUETTI's FAQ says most RV owners can install it with basic DIY skills and no electrician. The B4810 battery is 51.2 V, 100 Ah (5,120 Wh), about 101.4 lb, and is rated by BLUETTI for over 6,000 cycles at 0.5C and 77°F.

Sun Gold Power kits. Both arrive as separate parts. The SGM-5K5E listing names a 5,000 W 48 V inverter with 120 V output, six 200 W panels, one 51.2 V 100 Ah battery, Z-brackets, a 160 A DC breaker, a 25 A PV breaker and cables. The SGR-8K10E listing names an 8,000 W 120/240 V inverter with dual MPPT for up to 11,000 W of panels, two 51.2 V 100 Ah rack batteries and eight 450 W panels.

The plug-in options are pre-orders.

  • Jackery. Its store says the Explorer 5000 Plus is sold out and ships Dec 2, with the SolarSaga 500 X panel shipping by Dec 5. StormWatts reads the station's ports as one high-voltage input (135–450 V, 15 A, 4,000 W) and two low-voltage inputs (16–60 V, 10.5 A each). The 500 X panel runs at 41.7 V and 12.0 A, so each low-voltage port passes about 10.5 A × 41.7 V = 438 W. That is about 876 W from two panels rated 1,000 W, and 5,040 Wh / 876 W = 5.8 full-sun hours. StormWatts also reports Jackery's FAQ limit of two 500 X panels per station.
  • PECRON. The F5000LFP solar generator (7,200 W at 240 V, 3,600 W × 2 at 120 V, 5,120 Wh) is listed as a pre-order shipping in early January.
  • EcoFlow. The US store lists the TITAN 5000 Plus with a late-December ship date for orders from November 17. The store search showed no listing that includes panels.

Under the 5,000 W line. The Anker SOLIX S5000 + 2 × 400 W kit $3,599.99: https://dealectric.com/go/anker-solix-s5000-2x400w-solar-kit is 4,500 W rated and 8,000 W peak with a 5 kWh battery. Anker's page gives the full 4,500 W only on the 240 V L14-30R outlet (2,400 W on the 5-20R, 3,600 W on the TT-30R) and says it does not work with the Anker SOLIX Home Power Panel. It suits a shopper who meant 5 kWh of storage. Check the ship window at checkout: Anker's page on 2026-11-18 showed 11–12 weeks for orders placed from November 6. BLUETTI's Apex 300 + B300K kits (3,840 W) are also under the line.

Not recommended

  • The BLUETTI 2 × 100W kit, if the sun is the main way to refill it. 5,120 Wh / 200 W = 25.6 full-sun hours at rated output, and 200 W is 4% of a 5,000 W load. It makes sense when the alternator or shore power does most of the charging.
  • House-roof panels on the BLUETTI RV5 hub. BLUETTI warns that standard residential panels, often 60–80 V open-circuit, "WILL destroy the charge controller if connected directly." Stay under 50 V and wire in parallel.
  • Buying on the "5000" in a name. The Anker S5000's 5,000 is watt-hours; its AC output is 4,500 W, and Anker's FAQ says loads above that trip overload protection.
