What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure below comes from a maker page or a named reviewer, read on 2026-11-21. Three things decide a quiet office keyboard: a typing-noise reading from one lab, whether the switch that ships is a damped silent switch, and whether the layout and connection suit your desk.

  • Where 47.6 dBA sits. RTINGS says 50 dBA is too loud for a quiet keyboard, about 45 dBA is quiet and 40 dBA or lower is very quiet. 47.6 − 45 = 2.6 dBA above its quiet mark; 50 − 47.6 = 2.4 dBA below its too-loud mark. RTINGS adds that a gap of about 10 dBA sounds roughly twice as loud.
  • Travel. The Void switch has 2.8 mm of travel and Cherry's MX2A Silent Red has 3.7 mm: 3.7 − 2.8 = 0.9 mm less key movement on the Lofree. Gamakay's Phoenix V3 silent and NuPhy's Gateron low-profile 3.0 switches both sit at 3.5 mm.
  • Force does not separate them. Cherry states 45 cN, and 45 × 1.0197 = 45.9 gf. Void is 40 ± 10 gf (30 to 50 gf) and Phoenix V3 is 45 ± 5 gf (40 to 50 gf), so the three ranges overlap.
  • Charging. MKB Guide gives Lofree's rating for the Flow 2 84 as up to roughly 90 hours with lighting off and around 16 hours with lighting on: 90 ÷ 8 = 11.25 eight-hour workdays unlit, 16 ÷ 8 = 2 lit. MKB Guide gives NuPhy's rating for the Air75 V3 as 60 to 100 hours with lighting: 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5 and 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5 workdays. The Cherry is wired and has no battery.

Which quiet keyboards have a measured noise figure

Only one board in the table has a typing-noise figure in the reviews we read. MKB Guide puts the reason for caution plainly: 'published decibel readings from different sources cannot be ranked against each other'. A reading counts only inside one lab's setup, so the table keeps measurements and descriptions apart.

  • NuPhy Air75 V3. RTINGS bought a Nebula Dark unit with Gateron Nano Blush switches and writes: 'We measured only 47.6 dBA while typing, which is very quiet and likely won't bother people around you.' It is RTINGS' Best Quiet Mechanical Keyboard (page updated 2026-10-26).
  • Lofree Flow 2. RTINGS names it Best Enthusiast Office Keyboard but 'tested the keyboard with tactile Kailh Pulse switches, which are on the louder side'. Any loudness value in that review belongs to the Pulse unit; RTINGS says the Void version is 'much quieter' without a figure. PCWorld's Mattias Inghe tested the 100-key Flow 2 100 in its Silent Edition and wrote 'Even a heavy keystroke produces only minimal clatter', with no decibel reading. MKB Guide reports owner complaints of key chatter in 2026.
  • Gamakay Phoenix V3 silent. HLPlanet's Marcus Richardson tested the switch in an NS75: it 'maintains a relatively crisp feel while significantly reducing acoustic output'. His table gives the silent Phoenix V3 the same 3.5 mm travel as the non-silent Mercury V3. Smartzone's Max Drechsel found the TK75 TMR with these switches clearly quieter than a rubber-dome office keyboard. Neither review gives a decibel reading. The $79.19: https://dealectric.com/go/gamakay-tk75he-v2 is offered with the same switch.
  • Cherry KC 200 MX. Cherry says the Silent Red stem has 'integrated, patented noise reduction technology' and gives no decibel figure.

These are magnetic (Hall effect or TMR) boards in Gamakay's case: the key is sensed by a magnet instead of a metal contact, and the silent version adds damping. In an office that matters less than the switch choice at checkout: pick Phoenix V3 on the Gamakay boards and Void on the Lofree, because the other switch options are not silent.

Not recommended as the quiet pick

  • Keychron V1 Max as it ships. Keychron's store lists it from $85.99 (2026-11-21) with Gateron Jupiter Red, Brown or Banana switches, none of them a silent switch. It needs a separate silent set, and Keychron's own Silent K Pro set was out of stock on 2026-11-21.
  • Wobkey Rainy 75 Pro, Silent SHHH edition. $169.99 at wobkey.com on 2026-11-21, and Wobkey's own page says 'It is not completely silent'.
  • Logitech MX Mechanical Mini for a silent desk. 'Quiet' is the switch's name. Logitech's US page gives no force, travel or noise figure for it, and MKB Guide notes it is not a factory-damped silent switch. It is a fair choice for switching between three computers.
