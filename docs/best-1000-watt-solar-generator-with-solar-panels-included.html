What the differences mean

Two columns are our arithmetic, not test results. Refill hours = battery Wh ÷ panel watts at the panel's rated output. For the P1800 + 600 W kit that is 1,024 ÷ 600 = 1.7 h. For the Elite 100 V2 + 200 W kit it is 1,024 ÷ 200 = 5.1 h. Price per Wh = the price you pay through our link ÷ battery Wh. P1800 + 600 W: 718.20 ÷ 1,024 = $0.70. Elite 100 V2 + 200 W: 692.55 ÷ 1,024 = $0.68. Pioneer Na + 200 W: 949.05 ÷ 900 = $1.05.

Put together: the P1800 kit that ships now costs about 2 cents more per Wh than the Elite 100 V2 pre-order and comes with three times the panel watts (600 ÷ 200 = 3). The Jackery kit has the same 1,024 Wh at $0.83/Wh (849 ÷ 1,024), and its 200 W panel won't ship until Nov 28.

Panel watts against the station's solar limit

A kit refills only as fast as the smaller of two numbers: what its panel delivers and what the station's solar input accepts. Here is each kit's panel against its station's limit. ALLPOWERS P1800 takes up to 650 W through one XT60 input (ALLPOWERS). The 600 W kit panel already uses 600 ÷ 650 ≈ 92% of that, so plan on this kit as full on solar, with about 50 W of headroom. BLUETTI Elite 100 V2 takes 1,000 W at 12–60 V and 20 A at most (BLUETTI). Reaching 1,000 W needs panels wired to about 50 V or more (1,000 ÷ 20). Panels at about 20 V top out near 400 W (20 × 20). Pioneer Na is listed at 500 W of solar (BLUETTI). AC180 is 500 W (Trek4Tech). Jackery HomePower 1000 Plus v2 is 400 W (StormWatts). Every kit here ships with panels under its station's limit, so the table's hours are not cut short by the station.

Real sun gives less than rated output. OutdoorGearLab charged the AC180 to full in 6–8 hours with one 200 W panel under ideal conditions. Our arithmetic gives 5.8 h, so expect roughly 1.0–1.4× the table's hours on a good day (6 ÷ 5.8 and 8 ÷ 5.8). On usable energy, OutdoorGearLab measured 950 Wh out of the AC180 and 890 Wh out of the Elite 100 V2. We know of no named lab measurement of the P1800's output yet, so its 1,024 Wh is ALLPOWERS' rating. ALLPOWERS rates the P1800 for over 3,000 charge cycles, while BLUETTI rates the Elite 100 V2 for 4,000+ cycles to 80%. That is the trade for the P1800's faster solar refill and earlier shipping.

We drew the 1,000 W class at 1,000–1,800 W of AC output. That leaves out the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 kit, which Anker rates at 2,000 W and which belongs to the next size up. Dealectric does not test units: the figures above come from the makers and the named reviewers.

Not recommended

A 100 W panel on a 1 kWh station. The BLUETTI Elite 100 V2 + 100 W kit ($645.05 through our link, pre-order to mid-December) needs 1,024 ÷ 100 = 10.2 hours of full sun at rated output. On the 4.5 sun-hour day that StormWatts uses for planning, that is more than two days (10.2 ÷ 4.5 = 2.3). The ALLPOWERS R1500 + SF100 kit is slower still at 1,152 ÷ 100 = 11.5 h. If you plan to recharge from solar, buy at least the 400 W kit.
