Best small UPS for a home NAS, with a safe shutdown (2026)

Updated 2026-11-08

Short answer. If your NAS is a UGREEN NASync 2- or 4-bay (DXP2800, DXP4800 Plus, DXP4800 Pro, DXP480T Plus, DH2300, DH4300 Plus), buy the UGREEN NAS UPS US3000 ($109.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nas-ups-us3000-120w), $109.99 on 2026-11-03: a 120 W lithium battery that sits inline on the NAS's own DC lead, switches over in 0 seconds, and tells UGOS over USB to shut down at once, after a set time, or before the battery runs out; UGREEN quotes about 10 minutes of runtime. For any other NAS, the small AC unit to buy is the APC Back-UPS BE600M1 (600 VA / 330 W, USB shutdown, about $135–140): it is on UGOS's own supported list and on Synology's and QNAP's.

Two things to know. The US3000 powers only the NAS, not the router or switch, so remote access drops during the outage even though the data stays safe; and no lab has measured its runtime, the figures are UGREEN's 10 minutes and reviewers' 7–10 minute estimates at 35–55 W. For a NAS with an active-PFC power supply, or a 6- or 8-bay that the US3000 does not support, the CyberPower CP850PFCLCD (850 VA / 510 W, pure sine wave on battery, three-year warranty) is the safe AC pick.

DC inline or AC box

UGREEN's DC UPS takes the NAS's own power brick as input (12 V 10 A, 19 V 7.9 A or 20 V 7 A), feeds the NAS from its four 18650 cells and reports over USB; there is no inverter and no switchover gap. It supports only UGREEN's DC-powered 2- and 4-bay NASync units (the 6- and 8-bay DXP6800 and DXP8800 use internal AC supplies and are listed as not supported), powers nothing else, and its battery replacement path is not published. An AC UPS backs up the NAS plus the router and switch, so the NAS stays reachable and NUT can share one UPS with several machines; it needs a model on your NAS's compatibility list for the USB shutdown to work, and its lead-acid battery lasts roughly three to five years.

What each NAS OS does

  • UGOS Pro: detects a USB UPS under Control Panel → Hardware & Power → UPS, with Auto Shutdown (immediate, logged within two seconds in NASCompares' test), Timed shutdown and Standby (shut down before the battery is empty) modes, restart on power return, and a NUT server toggle. UGREEN's supported list names the US3000, APC BE600M1, APC BK650M2-CH and SANTAK TG-BOX850; other USB units may or may not be recognised.
  • Synology DSM: USB, SNMP and network UPS with one NAS as server and others on an allow-list, plus a standby-mode trigger; Synology advises a real outage test.
  • QNAP QTS: USB, SNMP and network master with up to 20 clients; QNAP suggests a 3–5 minute shutdown delay and keeps a per-model list.
  • TrueNAS and Unraid: NUT; the US3000 needs manual configuration there, and one TrueNAS SCALE 25.10 user could not get NUT to see it.

Why bother

A NAS that loses power mid-write can leave a RAID array resyncing for hours or a file system to repair; the UPS's job is the clean shutdown, not hours of runtime. Ten minutes is enough if the shutdown is automatic; pick Auto Shutdown on UGOS, or a short delay on DSM and QTS, and test it by pulling the plug once.
