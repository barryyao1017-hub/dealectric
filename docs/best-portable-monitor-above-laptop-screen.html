What the differences mean

  • Price: the cheapest Xebec setup we could price that mounts above the screen costs $884 − $309.99 = $574.01 more than the Float 2 Pro, which is 309.99 ÷ 884 = 35% of the Xebec price. Xebec's store search only lists the Stack-Up Mount inside bundles.
  • Pixels: 2560 × 1600 = 4,096,000 against 1920 × 1080 = 2,073,600. The Float 2 Pro shows 1.98× as much.
  • What the lid carries: the Float 2 Pro's 2.9 lb stands on its own kickstand on the desk. The Xebec puts 1 lb of screen plus a 1.5 lb bracket on the lid (1 + 1.5 = 2.5 lb), and the Stack-Up Mount adds weight that Xebec doesn't state.
  • Battery when unplugged: in Limited Power Mode the Float 2 Pro draws 7.5 W. That is 7.5 ÷ 99.6 Wh = 7.5% of a 16-inch MacBook Pro's battery per hour. Liliputing reported about 16.5 W per Xebec screen at its 2023 launch. That is 16.5 ÷ 53.8 Wh = 31% of a 13-inch MacBook Air per hour.
  • Charging through the screen: Xebec's pass-through accepts 45–100 W, but the 16-inch MacBook Pro ships with a 140 W adapter. 140 − 100 = 40 W over the limit, so that laptop needs a second, smaller charger.

Both say USB-C, but the video link is different

The Duex Float 2 Pro takes video over USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode. James Smythe of Mighty Gadget found "no drivers or software to install on either Windows or macOS". Mini-HDMI is the fallback for laptops whose USB-C port lacks DP Alt Mode. Mobile Pixels' installation page says one USB-C cable is enough only for a laptop with a "USB-C port with DP ALT mode". Without it you need a USB-C cable plus HDMI. With a wall charger, either USB-C port passes up to 65 W back to the laptop (Macworld, Mighty Gadget). Simon Jary of Macworld calls it "a great fit for owners of the 16-inch MacBook Pro in particular". He also notes that a laptop stand has to go, because the Float would sit lower than a laptop on a stand.

The Xebec Snap carries its video differently. Xebec says it "embedded the DisplayLink chip directly into the Snap Screen". USB-A laptops need the free DisplayLink driver, and the Snap "is not compatible with laptops unable to support Mac OS11+". Xebec's help centre warns that while DisplayLink screens are connected, some protected content in iTunes, HBO, Amazon Prime, Netflix, Disney+, YouTube TV or Udemy may not play: the picture stays black while the sound plays. Its workarounds are to unplug all DisplayLink screens, switch browser, or turn off browser hardware acceleration. The Stack-Up Mount fits only the bracket's left wing in the closed position, one mount per bracket, so you get one screen above the laptop, not two.

Not recommended

Skip the Xebec Snap Stack Bundle if you watch streaming video on the upper screen or carry a 16-inch MacBook Pro. Xebec itself warns that protected video may stay black on it. Its pass-through charging may not start with the 140 W charger that comes with that laptop. And it costs $884 against $309.99 for $263.49: https://dealectric.com/go/mobile-pixels-duex-float-2-pro. It still makes sense for a 13–14-inch laptop that stays plugged in and only runs work apps. If you want screens beside the laptop rather than above it, the $433.49: https://dealectric.com/go/mobile-pixels-trio-3-pro gives you one on each side.
