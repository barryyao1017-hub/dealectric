Best build plate for PLA

Short answer. The best build plate for PLA is the BIQU Panda CryoGrip Pro Frostbite ($30.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/biqu-panda-cryogrip-pro-frostbite-256): it holds PLA at a 30–50°C bed and PETG at 50–70°C, where a textured PEI plate wants 45–60°C for PLA, so corners stay down on open-frame printers and with the side fan running. The most common owner complaint is prints sticking too hard, and the fix is simple: run PLA at about 40°C, skip brims, let the plate cool before flexing, and never clean it with alcohol, which strips the coating. If you also print nylon, PC or TPU on the same plate, the Bambu Textured PEI Plate is the better buy; the Frostbite is rated for PLA, PETG and ABS only.

Three kinds of shopper type this query. Most are fighting lifted corners or small parts popping loose on an open printer such as a Bambu A1, A1 mini or P1P, or on any machine with an auxiliary fan; the Frostbite wins there because it grips PLA cold. The A1 mini size is $23.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/biqu-panda-cryogrip-pro-frostbite-a1-mini. Shoppers who print PLA, PETG and also ABS, ASA or nylon, and who clean with alcohol, do better with the milder CryoGrip Pro Glacier ($23.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/biqu-panda-cryogrip-pro-glacier-256), which runs PLA at 45–55°C and is alcohol-safe. Bambu X1 owners who want a cold plate their printer recognizes without switching detection off should look at Bambu's own Cool Plate SuperTack, with the caveat that it is PLA and PETG only and must not be used with TPU.

What owners complain about

Across the Bambu Lab forum, the Prusa forum, two Reddit threads and three reviews, the same five problems come up. Too much grip is the most common (about eight mentions): thin parts, brims and small supports bond hard, and one H2C owner could not flex a PLA-HT part off at all. Owners solve it by printing PLA at 35–45°C, skipping brims, cooling the plate to room temperature (one puts it in the fridge for 10 minutes) and using a plastic scraper. Coating damage from heat (four mentions): PLA at 65°C, wood PLA at textured-plate temperatures, or a PETG preset left on the stock profile pulled coating off with the part. Alcohol ruins the Frostbite (a 167-point Reddit warning plus three forum reports); the Glacier version tolerates alcohol. No auto-detection (four mentions): P2S, X2D and H2 series printers throw a plate error unless detection is turned off, and the default textured-PEI profile sets the bed too hot and the wrong Z offset, which one A1 owner blamed for elephant's foot at 65°C. Plate flex (two mentions): the sheet is lighter than Bambu's, and a full-bed PETG print can bow it off the magnet. One buyer also waited 6.5 weeks for a BIQU store order; the Amazon listing ships from Amazon.

Which one by need

  • Lifted corners on an open-frame printer, PLA and PETG only: Frostbite. It is the only plate here rated to hold PLA at a 30°C bed, and a German tester on an H2S had zero failed PLA or PETG prints at 40°C and 60°C.
  • One cooler-running plate for PLA plus ABS, ASA or nylon, and you clean with alcohol: Glacier. Prusa Core One owners call it a cheaper stand-in for the Satin sheet; a P2S owner says it holds PC that slid off textured PEI.
  • You print TPU, PC or nylon often, or want the printer to recognize the plate: Bambu's Textured PEI Plate. It covers every common filament from 35°C to 110°C and needs no profile changes.
  • X1 owner who wants a cold plate the lidar reads: Bambu Cool Plate SuperTack, but not for TPU or silk PLA, and the Bambu forum has as many PLA first-layer complaints as praise for it.

Not recommended

Do not buy the Frostbite for TPU, nylon or PC; it is rated for PLA, PETG and ABS and TPU can weld to it. Do not pay extra for the Matte Orange Frostbite unless you want the color; the coating and temperatures are the same as the blue plate. Do not leave the stock textured-PEI profile in place on any cold plate; the bed temperature it sets is what strips the coating. And do not put Nano Polymer Adhesive or other glues on a CryoGrip surface; a Prusa owner tore the sheet removing a part that way.
