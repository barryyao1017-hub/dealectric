What the differences mean

  • Frostbite's margin: at the bottom of its PLA range it sits 45 − 30 = 15 °C under PLA's softening point. At Bambu Studio's Cool Plate PLA default of 35 °C the margin is 45 − 35 = 10 °C.
  • Textured PEI on a Bambu: Bambu Studio's PLA default on the Textured PEI plate is 55 °C, so 55 − 45 = 10 °C over. Prusa's PLA recommendation of 60 °C is 60 − 45 = 15 °C over.
  • Glacier sits right on the line: its PLA floor is 45 °C, and Bambu Studio's warning fires at 'equal to or greater than' the softening value. Glacier can't stay under it anywhere in its rated range.
  • Prusa sheet prices: Textured costs 33.99 − 29.74 = $4.25 more than Smooth, and Satin costs 39.94 − 29.74 = $10.20 more.
  • Cost per printable side: BIQU's CryoGrip Pro listing starts at $19.99 for a double-sided plate, so 19.99 ÷ 2 ≈ $10 per side (that's the cheapest variant). Prusa's double-sided Smooth sheet works out to 29.74 ÷ 2 ≈ $14.87 per side.

Why bed temperature decides it: PLA's 45 °C softening point

Bambu Studio's base PLA profile sets a 'Softening temperature' of 45 °C for PLA. Bambu's PLA Basic preset inherits that value without changing it. The slicer's tooltip for this setting reads: 'The material softens at this temperature, so when the bed temperature is equal to or greater than it, it's highly recommended to open the front door and/or remove the upper glass to avoid cloggings.' The same profile sets PLA's bed defaults at 35 °C on the Cool Plate, 55 °C on the Textured PEI plate and 55 °C on the Smooth PEI / High Temp plate. PLA isn't supported on the Engineering plate (set to 0). So on an enclosed printer, textured PEI at its normal PLA setting comes with advice to open the enclosure. A cold-grip plate run below 45 °C doesn't.

Set the bed by hand. BIQU's listing tells you to choose 'Smooth PEI Plate / High Temp Plate' in the slicer. That plate type defaults PLA to 55 °C, which is above Frostbite's 50 °C ceiling and above the softening point. After you pick it, set the bed between 30 and 44 °C.

Plate detection and size. Frostbite carries an AR code that the X1C recognises. On other Bambu printers, turn off Build Plate Detection (MatterHackers). The 256 mm plate is for the A1, P and X series. The H series needs the separate 350 × 325 mm size.

Release. Hackaday's Dave Rowntree summarised Printing Perspective's hands-on test. He called the CryoGrip's cold adhesion 'immense' and said flexing the plate becomes 'even more essential'. Thin prints were the trouble case. Prusa says prints come off its textured sheet on their own once it cools. On an open printer like a Prusa MK4 the softening limit doesn't come into play, and Prusa's PLA guide says 'Satin or Smooth PEI sheet will provide the best adhesion' at 60 °C.

Not recommended

  • Frostbite if you also print ABS, TPU or nylon. MatterHackers' compatibility table marks all three 'Not Compatible' and says Frostbite is only for PLA and PETG. 3DJake's listing names ABS but gives no ABS bed temperature. Take Glacier instead.
  • Bare glass for PLA in an enclosure. Spoerk et al. at Montanuniversität Leoben report PLA's highest adhesion on glass at a 70 °C platform temperature. That's 70 − 45 = 25 °C over the softening point.
  • Prusa's HighTemp sheet for PLA. Prusa says 'Basic materials such as PLA or PETG do not adhere very well' to it.
