What each camera really costs in year one

Every camera on this list streams live video. They differ in whether recorded clips need a subscription and how long that subscription locks you in.

Petcube sells the Bites 2 Lite two ways. Bought outright at $99.99, it keeps live streaming on the free Basic tier, which Petcube says comes with every camera. Care Optimal adds 3-day history and Smart Alerts for $59.88 a year. The Care Bundle gives you the camera for $0, but the plan must renew for at least 12 months. Cancel early or miss a payment and you owe $99.95 for the camera. After 12 months, cancelling turns off history and Smart Alerts, and live streaming keeps working. WIRED's tester, Molly Higgins, notes that the Bites 2 Lite lets you set both the amount and the distance of each toss. $99.99: https://dealectric.com/go/petcube-bites-2-lite

Furbo lists the Mini 360° at $35 (crossed-out $99), but the cart adds Nanny Standard billed at $83.92. Furbo's terms set a minimum of three continuous months, say subscription payments are nonrefundable, and say that once a plan ends you only get the real-time stream, with no past recordings. Furbo's help center adds that Subscription-Required units lock live view until a plan is linked. WIRED's Molly Higgins found the Mini 360° can't schedule feedings or change the amount dispensed.

Not recommended

Furbo Mini 360° at its $35 headline price. For a $100 budget it comes to $118.92 at Furbo's checkout. Its hopper holds up to 10 small treats, round and 0.25–0.5 in (Furbo), and the plan can't be refunded for the first three months. It fits only a shopper who already wants a Furbo Nanny plan and uses small cat-sized treats.
