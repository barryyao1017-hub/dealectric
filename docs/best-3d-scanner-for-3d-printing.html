What the differences mean

  • Moose vs POP 3 Plus on the same printed part: 0.66 mm ÷ 0.10 mm = 6.6 times the scatter. The worst outliers spread 14.08 mm on the POP 3 Plus and 1.36 mm on the Moose.
  • Moose vs MetroX on the printed part: both have a 0.10 mm standard deviation. The MetroX's stray points spread 8.04 mm against the Moose's 1.36 mm, and 8.04 ÷ 1.36 = 5.9 times as much stray material to clean off before printing.
  • Moose vs MetroX on a flat plate: 0.60 mm ÷ 0.29 mm = 2.1, so the Moose scattered about twice as much on a featureless flat surface.
  • Ferret Pro on Notebookcheck's gauge block: 0.335 mm ÷ 150 mm = 0.22 % of the part's length.
  • Seal on 3D Printing Industry's block: 0.16 mm ÷ 0.02 mm = 8 times larger on one axis than on the other two.
  • Price: the Moose and Seal both list at $419. The MetroX Standard Edition is $999 − $419 = $580 more and was sold out at Revopoint on 2026-11-17.

What the scanners measured against a reference

Kersten, Marsmann and Przybilla at HafenCity University Hamburg scanned five objects with the handheld scanners and compared every scan with a ZEISS ATOS 5 reference. One object was a 100 × 100 × 100 mm staircase with five 20 mm steps, printed in PLA on a Prusa, which is the kind of part a shopper scans to reprint. The Moose was used in handheld EasyScan mode. The Revopoint scanners were used in turntable mode with markers placed nearby. The paper's conclusion: the Moose "proved to be the most accurate system" among the low-cost scanners.

Not recommended

Revopoint POP 3 Plus, for parts that have to fit. Of the scanners tested at HafenCity University Hamburg, it scattered the most on the printed part, the bust, the porous figure and the granite plate: 0.66 mm on the printed part and 1.26 mm on the bust. At $526 it costs $107 more than the Moose, which scattered 0.10 mm on the same part. It still makes sense if you want color scans and the printed copy doesn't need to fit anything.
