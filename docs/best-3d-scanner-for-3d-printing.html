Best 3D scanner for 3D printing

Updated 2026-11-06

Short answer. The best 3D scanner for 3D printing is the Revopoint INSPIRE 2 ($494; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/revopoint-inspire-2-3d-scanner): 0.05 mm single-frame accuracy, a 190 g body you can run from a phone over Wi-Fi 6, and an 11-line infrared laser mode that reads black and matte-metal parts without scanning spray. Lost tracking is the top complaint about every scanner under $1,000. The INSPIRE 2 answers it with marker and global-marker tracking modes; a pack of 3 mm and 6 mm reflective markers is $19.90 on the Revopoint store (seen 2026-11-06), and it scans objects as small as 20 × 20 × 20 mm.

If your parts are mostly black or shiny metal and you need CAD-grade edges, step up to the Revopoint MetroX ($749; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/revopoint-metrox-3d-scanner): 0.03 mm accuracy, 14 cross-line blue laser, 10 mm minimum object. For miniatures and parts under 2 inches, the 3DMakerpro Seal ($454; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/3dmakerpro-seal-3d-scanner, code Save15 saves $15) wins on 0.01 mm accuracy. If budget is not a concern, the standalone Einstar Vega at $1,999 is the easiest scanner we found below $2,000, but it costs four INSPIRE 2s.

One honest warning from owners: no scanner in this price range gives a print-ready STL by itself. Expect 15 to 30 minutes of mesh cleanup per part, and measure critical holes and fits with calipers before printing.

What owners complain about

We read 11 owner threads and hands-on reviews across the Prusa forum, a Lemmy 3D printing thread, the Revopoint and Creality forums, and reviews of the INSPIRE 2, Moose, MetroX and Otter. Lost tracking came up in 8 of them: plain, symmetrical or black parts give the camera nothing to lock onto, and the scan tears or duplicates. The fix every time was reflective markers, a marker mat, or small objects placed around the part. Scans not being print-ready came up in 7: broken plastic parts deform before they break, so a scan of a broken part prints a broken part, and every mesh needs cleanup or a CAD rebuild. Black, chrome or clear surfaces needing spray came up in 7. Clunky or crashing software came up in 5, with one INSPIRE 2 owner whose laser mode stopped working after a week. Heavy PC requirements or a cable and power brick came up in 4. Trouble with very small parts came up in 3.

Which one by need

Household parts, figures and color models: Revopoint INSPIRE 2. It is the only pick here under 200 g, scans from a phone, and its laser mode covers black brackets and matte metal that trip up structured-light scanners. Buy the Standard Edition and add the $99 Mobile Kit later if you want cordless phone scanning.

Shiny metal and machined parts for CAD: Revopoint MetroX. Its cross-line blue laser is the mode reviewers used on polished metal without spray, and the 10 mm minimum object and 0.01 mm precision give clean edges to model over. Budget time for markers.

Miniatures, jewelry and parts under 2 inches: 3DMakerpro Seal at 0.01 mm accuracy. The Revopoint MINI 2 ($829; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/revopoint-mini-2-3d-scanner) is the other small-object choice at 0.02 mm, but it costs more.

Easiest workflow, money no object: Einstar Vega. It is a standalone unit with its own screen and processor, so there is no PC spec to meet, and the Prusa forum owner with six scanners called it the easiest of them. It is $1,999 and we earn nothing on it.

Not recommended

Creality CR-Scan Otter at $899. The 0.02 mm spec is real, but it is PC-only over USB 3.0 with no phone option, weighs 390 g, and Otter owners on r/3DScanning report losing tracking on black parts even with IR stickers and having to mix their own spray.

3DMakerpro Moose for dark or shiny parts. It scans well on matte objects, but it has no marker mode, so anything dark or glossy needs spray, and it needs a separate power supply and short cable at the desk.

First-generation Revopoint INSPIRE or POP 2, new or used. They are 0.2 mm class scanners that need spray on any black surface; the INSPIRE 2 is four times more accurate for about the same money.

Phone LiDAR apps for printable parts. Fine for rooms and furniture, not for a part that has to fit.
