What each battery rating actually measures

A price per hour only compares fairly when the hours are measured the same way, and these makers don't all measure them the same way:

  • Lights off: EKSA rates the E900WT and E1000 WT with their LEDs off. The E900WT drops from 80 h to 30 h with the LEDs on.
  • Split by mode: Corsair gives the HS55 Wireless 24 h on the 2.4 GHz dongle and 37 h on Bluetooth. You'll use the dongle for games, and it lasts 24 ÷ 37 = 65% as long. HyperX's spec gives the Cloud Jet up to 20 h on the dongle, below its up-to-25-h headline. The table uses the dongle figures.
  • No condition stated: Logitech's G435 (18 h) and G321 (20+ h) pages and EKSA's E900 BT page (50 h) give no volume, mode or lighting condition.
  • Measured by a reviewer: SoundGuys ran the Cloud Jet for 19 h 37 min on its 2.4 GHz dongle at about 50% volume, 19.6 ÷ 20 = 98% of HyperX's dongle rating. SoundGuys reported the G435 at over 24 h in its testing, more than Logitech's 18 h.

Xbox is a separate limit. EKSA says neither of the E900WT's wireless modes works with any Xbox. SoundGuys says the Cloud Jet doesn't support Xbox consoles at all. EKSA's E1000 BT and G19 BT pages say Xbox connects only by 3.5 mm cable. The E900 BT comes with that cable. The E1000 WT, G435, Cloud Jet, G321 and HS55 don't come with one.

Not recommended

Logitech G321 LIGHTSPEED. It's sold as a budget wireless headset, but Logitech's own store has it at $54.99 (from $59.99), over a $50 budget. Its 20+ h rating works out to 6.7 evenings per charge, against 16.7 for the $49.99 E900 BT. Logitech lists only PS5, PC and Switch / Switch 2, and it has no cable mode for anything else.
