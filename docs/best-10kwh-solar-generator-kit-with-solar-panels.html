What the differences mean

Two figures separate these kits: what each stored watt-hour costs, and how long the kit's own panels take to put those watt-hours back. Both are arithmetic from the store listings read on 2026-11-18. Refill hours use the panels' rated watts; real output is lower, so read them as a floor.

  • Sun Gold Power SGR-8K10E: $4,949 / 10,240 Wh = $0.48 per Wh. 10,240 Wh / 3,600 W = 2.8 h. It is the only kit here whose own panels can refill the battery inside one sunny day.

Price per Wh and refill hours, kit by kit

Where the line was drawn. A kit is in the table if it is one listing at one price, includes panels, and holds 8,000 to 12,000 Wh (10 kWh give or take 20%). That leaves out BLUETTI's Apex 300 + B500K + 500 W (7,884.8 Wh) and Apex 200 + B500K + 2× 500 W (7,680 Wh), which fall just under, and EcoFlow's DELTA Pro Ultra X standard bundle, which at 2 × 6,144 Wh = 12,288 Wh falls just over and is sold on EcoFlow's US store without panels. Sun Gold Power's 16.07 kWh option of the same kit ($5,969) is also over the line.

The pick is a different kind of kit. The SGR-8K10E is an 8,000 W 48 V inverter, two SG48100P rack batteries and eight rigid 450 W panels, with mounting brackets, solar extension cables and PV and battery breakers in the listing. It is installed and wired, not rolled into a room and plugged in, and its panels are mounted, not unfolded. In return its inverter accepts up to 11,000 W of solar, so the 3,600 W in the box is under a third of what it can take. Sun Gold Power states 7,000+ deep cycles for the batteries and lists them as UL1973 and UL9540A certified. If you need a station on wheels with outlets on the front, the plug-in rows are your list, and none of them was listed by its maker as shipping before December on the day we read them.

Sunny days, not just hours. At 4.5 peak sun-hours a day and 80% charging efficiency, with nothing running: Sun Gold's 3,600 W makes 12.96 kWh a day, so 10.24 kWh takes 0.8 of a day. BLUETTI's 1,000 W makes 3.6 kWh a day, so 8,294.4 Wh takes 2.3 days. Jackery's 1,000 W takes 2.8 days for 10.08 kWh. Anker's 800 W makes 2.88 kWh a day, about 3.5 days for 10 kWh. BLUETTI's 400 W makes 1.44 kWh a day, 5.8 days.

Jackery's panels may deliver less than their label on this station. StormWatts lists the Explorer 5000 Plus's two low-voltage solar ports at 16-60 V and 10.5 A each, with one SolarSaga 500 X per port topping out near 438 W. On those figures the kit's two panels give 876 W, and 10,080 Wh / 876 W = 11.5 h.

Anker's capacity and the price check it allows. Anker gives 5,024 Wh for the S5000 and 30 kWh for the station with five expansion batteries, about 5 kWh per unit, so this page uses 10 kWh for the two-unit kit. On Anker's US store the station plus one expansion battery with no panels is $6,099.99, and the expansion battery alone is $2,999.99. An S5000 kit described as "5-10 kWh" and priced below $6,099.99 is therefore worth checking for the second battery in its box list before you compare it with the kits here.

Two notes on the BLUETTI kits. BLUETTI's listing for the 2× 200 W kit carries the subtitle "5,529.6Wh", which is the one-B300K figure; the kit's title lists two B300K, and 3 × 2,764.8 Wh = 8,294.4 Wh, the figure on the sibling 2× 500 W listing. That kit also cost $104 more than the same parts bought separately at BLUETTI's store on 2026-11-17. On adding panels later: BLUETTI's kit page footnotes its "up to 4,000W solar input" with "Requires a SolarX 4K", a $699 controller that is in neither kit.

Not recommended

A 10 kWh-class battery with 400 W of panels, if you expect the sun to refill it. The BLUETTI 2× 200 W kit is the cheapest plug-in battery per Wh here, but 400 W needs 20.7 rated sun-hours, close to six sunny days, to refill 8,294.4 Wh. It makes sense only if the wall or a fuel generator does most of the charging. The same arithmetic applies to any 12 kWh bundle sold with a single 400 W panel: 12,288 Wh / 400 W = 30.7 h. If solar is the point, buy at least 1,000 W with a battery this size, and closer to 3,000 W if you want it back in a day.
