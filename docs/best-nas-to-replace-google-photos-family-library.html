What the differences mean

  • Synology DS225+ / DS425+ ceiling: 2 GB on board + one 4 GB D4NS01-4G module = 6 GB. That equals Immich's minimum: 8 − 6 = 2 GB short of the recommendation, with no slot left to close the gap.
  • Synology DS925+ path to 8 GB: 4 GB + a second, identical 4 GB D4ES04-4G = 8 GB. Synology requires both modules to share the same model number.
  • Immich alone outgrows a stock 2 GB box: ComputingForGeeks measured Immich at 2,430 MiB idle with its library imported and 2,620 MiB at peak with face detection running at concurrency 4. 2,620 MiB ≈ 2.56 GiB, which is more than the DS225+'s entire 2 GB before DSM itself is counted.
  • Intel vs ARM face indexing: In Mighty Gadget's test, 220,000 photos took 12 h on the Intel DXP4800 Plus (≈18,300 photos an hour) and 36 h on the ARM DH4300 Plus (≈6,100 an hour), so 36 ÷ 12 = 3× as long. At those rates a 50,000-photo family library would take about 2.7 h vs 8.2 h. That is an extrapolation, not a measurement.
  • Storage headroom for Immich: thumbnails and transcodes add 10–20%, so a 2 TB library needs 2 × 1.1 to 2 × 1.2 = 2.2–2.4 TB.
  • Price steps (UGREEN store, 2026-11-13): DXP4800 Plus $619.99 − DXP2800 $389.99 = $230.00 for two more bays. The DH4300 Plus at $373.99 is $16.00 under the DXP2800 and has four bays, but its ARM chip took 3× as long as the Intel DXP4800 Plus in the face-recognition run above.

How much memory each photo app needs, and what each box ships with

Immich. Immich's own requirements page sets a minimum of 6 GB and recommends 8 GB, with at least 2 CPU cores (4 recommended). Systems with only 4 GB can run Immich with machine learning turned off, and machine learning is what does face recognition and smart search. Immich's database should sit on local SSD storage and never on a network share. Since Immich v3, the machine-learning container on x86 needs an x86-64-v2 processor.

Synology Photos. On the DS225+ and DS425+, Synology's compare page footnotes that object recognition needs at least 4 GB of memory. Both ship with 2 GB, so the upgrade comes first. Synology's DS225+ page says non-Synology memory voids full warranty and technical support coverage, so the upgrade should be Synology's own D4NS01-4G. The DS925+ ships with 4 GB, and needtoknowit notes that object recognition works in its default configuration.

QNAP QuMagie. QNAP recommends 4 GB models for full AI Core image recognition. needtoknowit says semantic phrase search needs an x86 QNAP with at least 8 GB, which the TS-264-8G has as shipped.

UGREEN UGOS Photos. UGREEN lists face grouping, pet recognition, OCR text search and semantic phrase search, and says the matching AI models have to be downloaded and enabled. On the DH4300 Plus, UGREEN says its fixed 8 GB is shared by UGOS, indexing and Docker containers. The DH2300 has 4 GB of LPDDR4X.

Not recommended

A stock Synology DS225+ or DS425+ as an Immich server. At 2 GB it sits below even Immich's 4 GB machine-learning-off tier. Fully upgraded to 6 GB it only reaches Immich's minimum, so it can never reach the recommended 8 GB. Both are good Synology Photos boxes once the 4 GB Synology module is fitted, but they are not Immich boxes.

The UGREEN DH2300 for a Google Photos-style library. needtoknowit reports that it does not officially support Docker, so Immich is off the table, and its 4 GB is Immich's machine-learning-off tier. It suits a family that only wants phones backed up to UGOS Photos.
