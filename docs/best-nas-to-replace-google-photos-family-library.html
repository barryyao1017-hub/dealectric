What UGOS Photos does (UGREEN's own description)

  • Recognition: people grouped by face with matches you confirm before merging; scenes, objects and pets after the AI models are downloaded and enabled; text in photos searchable (OCR) and "semantic" descriptions. UGREEN's support notes say some Photos features are DXP-only and list custom classification, pet recognition and sensitive-content detection as unsupported on DH Plus models.
  • Library: timeline and folder views without moving files, map view from GPS data, albums (standard, conditional, baby, personal, shared), exact and similar-duplicate finder, RAW previews (CR2, CR3, NEF, ARW, RAF, RW2, DNG), HEIC and Live Photos (both halves must upload).
  • Phones: the UGREEN mobile app backs up new photos in the background, off-peak or over cellular by choice; iCloud "optimize storage" phones upload the smaller local versions.
  • Sharing: share links and photo-request links for guests (requests can convert HEIC to JPEG and drop the Live Photo video).
  • Maturity: TechPowerUp, ITPro and Cult of Mac call UGOS rougher than Apple or Google Photos; face recognition is praised, the map and mobile app called basic.

Immich on the same box

Immich runs in Docker on the DXP2800 (UGREEN publishes a setup guide) and adds the strongest open-source feature set: face recognition, CLIP "sunset at the beach" search, a map, shared and partner albums, duplicate detection, Live and Motion Photos, and a Google Takeout importer (immich-go). It wants 6 GB minimum and 8 GB recommended with 4 cores, its database on an SSD, and the first machine-learning pass over a large library takes hours to days on an N100 (turn ML off for the initial import). The DH2300's 4 GB and ARM chip rule it out.

Moving out of Google Photos

Export with Google Takeout (links expire in about seven days; each album duplicates its files), copy the folders onto the NAS and add them to the photo app's library; metadata sidecars need a tool to restore dates on Synology, and immich-go handles the ZIPs directly. Keep the phones backing up to the NAS afterward, since Takeout is one-time. Size: a 100,000-photo HEIC library is roughly 300–500 GB, 1–2 TB with videos, plus 10–20% for thumbnails and transcodes; two 4 TB drives in RAID 1 cover it, and the off-site copy is the part the NAS does not provide.
