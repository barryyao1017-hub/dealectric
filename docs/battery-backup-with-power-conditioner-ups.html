What the differences mean

  • Always-on conditioning costs power. The double-conversion SRT1500XLA draws 34.64 W with nothing plugged in, and the CyberPower draws 4 W. That is 34.64 − 4 = 30.64 W extra, and 30.64 W × 8,760 h ≈ 268 kWh a year before any load is attached.
  • Losses under load. At a 300 W desk load, using each unit's weighted efficiency: 300 × (1 ÷ 0.987 − 1) ≈ 4 W lost in the CyberPower, and 300 × (1 ÷ 0.916 − 1) ≈ 28 W lost in the SRT1500XLA running double conversion.
  • Runtime. ENERGY STAR certifies the SMT1500C at 23 min at 500 W and the CyberPower at 11 min at 450 W. 23 ÷ 11 ≈ 2.1×, so the APC runs about twice as long while carrying 50 W more.
  • Switchover. Custom PC Review measured the CyberPower at 8–11 ms and the earlier APC SMT1500 at 0.8–9.4 ms. The worst cases are 11 − 9.4 = 1.6 ms apart.

Measured: how each unit handles bad power

A line-interactive UPS passes wall power through and adjusts it with AVR. It only makes its own sine wave once it drops to battery. Dell's listing gives the CP1500PFCLCD single-boost AVR, which lifts sagging voltage. CyberPower's manual says the unit moves to battery when the line drops below 90 V or rises above 140 V. APC's FAQ FA164358 describes the same boost-only behavior on its SMC series, calls it normal, and suggests the SMT or SMX series for anyone who needs both boost and trim. A double-conversion unit such as the SRT1500XLA rebuilds every watt, and APC says this gives zero transfer time.

On the bench, Sam Chen of Custom PC Review put the CP1500PFCLCD on an oscilloscope (April 2017). Switchover measured 8–11 ms against CyberPower's 4 ms rating. The battery output was a sine wave "identical to power coming from the utility company," and the unit ran 24 minutes at 200 W. The same reviewer measured the earlier APC Smart-UPS SMT1500 (June 2017) at 0.8–9.4 ms against APC's 8 ms maximum, with 1–2 hours of runtime at 100–200 W.

ENERGY STAR certifies the Tripp Lite SMART1500LCDT at 3.87 W idle, 99.4% weighted efficiency and 10 min at 450 W, so it sits in the same efficiency class as the CyberPower and APC line-interactive units.

Setup notes from CyberPower's manual: plug the UPS straight into the wall, because the warranty prohibits extension cords, outlet strips and surge strips. Keep laser printers, paper shredders, copiers, space heaters, vacuums and sump pumps off the battery outlets.

Not recommended

APC Smart-UPS C SMC1500C ($726.08 at Dell, 2026-11-14). APC's own FAQ says the SMC series is boost-only and switches to battery when voltage runs high, which is the same kind of conditioning the CyberPower offers. Dell lists a 10 ms transfer time and 900 W of output, against 4 ms and 1000 W on Dell's CyberPower listing. If you are paying APC prices for conditioning, the SMT1500C is the model with boost and trim.
