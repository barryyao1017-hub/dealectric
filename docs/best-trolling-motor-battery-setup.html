The charger and the 60 A check

Charger. Minn Kota states that its chargers 'are not compatible with single 24V and 36V Lithium Batteries' and that they 'are designed for 12 Volt battery systems only'. Each bank charges one 12V battery, and batteries wired in series can't be charged on one bank. If you keep two or three 12V batteries in series, a Minn Kota multi-bank charger still works, one bank per battery. Its maximum is 14.5 V, so check that against the battery maker's charge voltage. If you switch to one 24V or 36V lithium battery, you also need a 29.2 V or 43.8 V LiFePO4 charger. That's why each 24V kit above includes one.

Current. Minn Kota says QUEST motors 'are designed to not pull more than 60 amps on high speed' and that breakers larger than 60 A should not be used on them. It also says a lithium battery without enough continuous current shows up as a motor that runs for a short time and then shuts off. The battery's continuous rating, not its peak, has to cover the motor's max draw. Put a 60 A breaker such as Minn Kota's MKR-27 between the battery and the motor. Redodo doesn't sell one, so buy it separately.

For a 36V QUEST or a 112 lb motor, Redodo sells no single 36V battery rated for 60 A continuous. The practical route is three 12V lithium batteries in series on a 3-bank charger, one bank per battery. Before you buy, check that each battery's continuous rating covers the motor's max draw and that its maker allows series wiring.

Not recommended

Redodo 36V 50Ah on a 36V QUEST or a 112 lb motor. Redodo markets it for 100–120 lb motors, but rates it at 50 A continuous and 60 A for up to 30 minutes. That is below the 60 A a QUEST can pull and the 52 A Minn Kota lists for a 112 lb motor, so hard Spot-Lock or full throttle can trip the BMS. It suits a 101 lb, 46 A motor.
