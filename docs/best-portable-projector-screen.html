What the differences mean

Every figure in the table comes from three lines of arithmetic. Elite's formula is brightness = lumens × gain ÷ area.

  • Picture area of a 16:9 screen = 0.4273 × diagonal² ÷ 144 sq ft. For a 105-inch screen: 0.4273 × 11,025 ÷ 144 = 32.7 sq ft.
  • Brightness from a 200-lumen projector on the Yard Master 2 at 105 inches: 200 × 1.3 ÷ 32.7 = 7.9 fL. That is below SMPTE's 12 fL minimum.
  • Lumens for 16 fL = 16 × area ÷ gain. Yard Master 2 at 105 inches: 16 × 32.7 ÷ 1.3 = 403. Aurzen at 120 inches: 16 × 42.7 ÷ 1.1 = 622.
  • Gain advantage: 1.3 ÷ 1.1 = 1.18. At the same size and lumens, the Yard Master 2 is 18% brighter than any 1.1-gain screen here.
  • Size: the XGIMI's 70 inches has 4,900 ÷ 10,000 = 49% of the area of a 100-inch screen. Its $99 buys less picture than VEVOR's $90.99 100-inch, but a dim projector lights that smaller area well.
  • Price per square foot: Aurzen 120-inch, 24.99 ÷ 42.7 = $0.59. ProjectorVerdict listed the 105-inch Yard Master 2 at $273 on 2026-10-31, which works out to 273 ÷ 32.7 = $8.35. Most of that extra cost goes into the tensioned frame.

How bright a portable screen gets: lumens ÷ area × gain

Elite Screens' tech note gives the method. Divide the projector's lumens by the screen area in square feet to get foot-lamberts, then multiply by the screen's gain. Elite's own example is 1,000 lumens on a 100-inch 16:9 screen (about 30 sq ft): 33 fL at 1.0 gain and about 50 fL at 1.5. Elite cites the SMPTE dark-room range of 12 fL minimum, 16 fL ideal and 22 fL maximum. It recommends about 50 fL when there is ambient light.

Run that formula backwards and you get the largest 16:9 screen a 200-lumen portable projector can light to 16 fL. At 1.3 gain that is about 74 inches. At 1.1 gain it is about 68 inches, or about 79 inches if you accept the 12 fL minimum. At 0.8 gain it is about 58 inches. So 100 to 120 inches only makes sense with a projector of roughly 400 to 620 lumens. Eco and accurate-color modes lower a projector's output, so those modes need a smaller screen still.

Gain ratings are maker claims, so it matters whether they hold up. The Hook Up measured Elite's CineWhite UHD-B material at 1.27 gain (409 nits against 322.8 nits from a 1.0-gain Stewart StudioTek 100 reference). That sample was a 100-inch fixed-frame screen, not the folding Yard Master 2, but it uses the same material. The same tests found only 14–20% ambient light rejection, so outdoors this is a screen for dusk or later. Elite rates the Yard Master 2 for a light breeze up to 7 mph on its store page, while its FAQ says below 10 mph. Use the lower figure.

Not recommended

The Yard Master 2 WraithVeil Dual is a poor match for a battery portable projector. Its 0.8 gain means a 120-inch screen needs about 16 × 42.7 ÷ 0.8 = 855 lumens for 16 fL. A 450-lumen projector gets only 450 × 0.8 ÷ 42.7 = 8.4 fL. Buy it only if you need to project from behind the screen. Otherwise the standard 1.3-gain Yard Master 2 is brighter and cheaper.
