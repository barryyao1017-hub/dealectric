What the differences mean

  • Weight gap: 23.8 − 23.6 = 0.2 lb between the Elite 100 mini and the Jackery Explorer 1000 v2. Next is the Anker at 24.9 − 23.6 = 1.3 lb more, then the Elite 100 V2 at 25 − 23.6 = 1.4 lb more and the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus at 27.6 − 23.6 = 4.0 lb more.
  • Energy per pound: 1,004.8 ÷ 23.6 = 42.6 Wh/lb for the Elite 100 mini against 1,070 ÷ 23.8 = 45.0 for the Explorer. The Explorer carries 65 Wh more (1,070 − 1,004.8), which is why it leads on Wh per pound while being 0.2 lb heavier.
  • Measured on a scale: OutdoorGearLab (Kenji Mowrey) weighed the Explorer 1000 v2 at 24.4 lb and got 910 Wh out: 910 ÷ 24.4 = 37.3 usable Wh/lb. For the BLUETTI it was 890 ÷ 25.0 = 35.6 and for the Anker 890 ÷ 25.2 = 35.3. The Elite 100 mini's 23.6 lb is BLUETTI's figure (10.7 kg in its manual); The Solar Lab repeats it but didn't weigh it.
  • Refilling from the car: the Elite 100 mini takes up to 96 W from a 12 V socket and 192 W from a 24 V one (BLUETTI manual): 1,004.8 Wh ÷ 96 W = 10.5 h at 12 V. The Explorer caps its car input at 8 A, so 12 V × 8 A = 96 W and 1,070 ÷ 96 = 11.1 h, close to Jackery's 'about 12 h'. A day of driving refills most of either one. A 2-hour drive adds roughly 2 × 96 = 190 Wh.
  • Idle draw with the AC inverter on: The Solar Lab measured about 13.5 W on the Elite 100 mini and Solar Power Auditor (Scott Caine) measured 16 W on the Explorer. Over a night that is 13.5 × 10 = 135 Wh against 16 × 10 = 160 Wh. Run a 12 V fridge from the DC port and switch the inverter off.

What the Elite 100 mini charges from: solar, the car socket and cold nights

BLUETTI's manual lists one XT60 solar input at 12–60 V and 10 A, up to 350 W. The Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 has two DC8020 inputs that take 16–60 V and share a 21 A, 400 W ceiling, with one port capped at 200 W, so its 400 W figure needs a matched panel on each port.

The Elite 100 mini's car input runs at up to 96 W from a 12 V socket or 192 W from a 24 V one, and BLUETTI puts the car charging cable in the box. The Explorer's car socket feeds a DC8020 port capped at 8 A at 12–16 V, so a 24 V truck socket is outside its range; Jackery's car fast charger cuts its refill to about 3 h, and solar and car charging can't run at the same time.
