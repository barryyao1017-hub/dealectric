What the differences mean

  • Weight gap: 23.8 − 23.4 = 0.4 lb (10.8 − 10.6 = 0.2 kg) between the two Jackerys. Next is the Anker at 24.9 − 23.4 = 1.5 lb more, then the BLUETTI at 25 − 23.4 = 1.6 lb more and the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus at 27.6 − 23.4 = 4.2 lb more.
  • Energy per pound: 1,024 ÷ 23.4 = 43.8 Wh/lb for the HomePower against 1,070 ÷ 23.8 = 45.0 for the Explorer. The Explorer carries 46 Wh more (1,070 − 1,024), which is why it still leads on Wh per pound while being 0.4 lb heavier.
  • Measured on a scale: OutdoorGearLab (Kenji Mowrey) weighed the Explorer 1000 v2 at 24.4 lb and got 910 Wh out: 910 ÷ 24.4 = 37.3 usable Wh/lb. For the BLUETTI it was 890 ÷ 25.0 = 35.6 and for the Anker 890 ÷ 25.2 = 35.3. The HomePower's 23.4 lb is Jackery's figure. Neither The Solar Lab nor CleanTechnica weighed it.
  • Refilling from the car: both Jackerys cap the car input at 8 A, so 12 V × 8 A = 96 W. 1,024 Wh ÷ 96 W = 10.7 h, which matches Jackery's 'about 11 h' for the HomePower. 1,070 ÷ 96 = 11.1 h, close to Jackery's 'about 12 h' for the Explorer. A day of driving refills most of either one. A 2-hour drive adds roughly 2 × 96 = 190 Wh.
  • Idle draw with the AC inverter on: The Solar Lab measured 22 W on the HomePower and Solar Power Auditor (Scott Caine) measured 16 W on the Explorer. Over a night that is 22 × 10 = 220 Wh against 16 × 10 = 160 Wh. Run a 12 V fridge from the DC port and switch the inverter off.

What the HomePower 1000 v2 charges from: solar, the car socket and cold nights

Jackery sells the HomePower 1000 v2 as home backup, but its help-center spec sheet shows the same charging hardware as the Explorer 1000 v2. Both have two DC8020 inputs that take 16–60 V and share a 21 A, 400 W ceiling. The HomePower allows up to 12 A on one port, and the Explorer caps one port at 200 W. Either way, the 400 W figure needs a panel on each port, and Jackery requires the two panels to match. CleanTechnica (Kyle Field) refilled the HomePower in 3.3 h from two 200 W folding panels.

The car socket feeds the same DC8020 port, capped at 8 A. Jackery lists 11–16 V for the HomePower and 12–16 V for the Explorer, so a 24 V truck socket is outside both ranges. Jackery's car fast charger cuts the refill to about 2.7 h on the HomePower and about 3 h on the Explorer. On both units, solar and car charging can't run at the same time. The Solar Lab notes that the HomePower doesn't come with a car charging cable.

The cold-charging limit is the difference that shows up on a frosty campsite. After a night below freezing, the Explorer and the Anker won't take solar or car charge until they warm to 32°F. The HomePower keeps charging down to 14°F. Two caveats on the HomePower: on 2026-11-14 Jackery's US store page said the black version was sold out, and its 22 W idle draw (The Solar Lab) is higher than the Explorer's 16 W. Dabbsson's 1000L (24 lb, 1,008 Wh) isn't in the table because Dabbsson's US page doesn't name its cell chemistry.
