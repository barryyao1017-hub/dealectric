Why the label on a 1.5V lithium AA overstates it

A 1.5V lithium AA is a 3.6V Li-ion cell with a converter that holds the output near 1.5V (XTAR). The mWh printed on the label is not what reaches your device. The XTAR 4150 shows how this works. 4,150 mWh ÷ 1.5 V would be 2,767 mAh, yet XTAR rates the cell at only 2,500 mAh. Gough Lui measured 2,422 mAh at 0.5 A, at an average of 1.42 V: 2,422 × 1.42 = 3,439 mWh. That agrees with Charge-Test's separate 3,420 mWh. The mAh rating was close to the truth; the mWh rating was the one inflated. The EBL runs the other way: it carries no mAh rating, and Light And Matter measured more energy than its 3,000 mWh label.

Current matters too. On XTAR's own test-results page, Gough Lui's CLR 4300 cells gave 2,599–2,739 mAh at 1 A but only 2,298–2,438 mAh at 2 A. For the XTAR 4150, Gough Lui advises staying under 1.25 A.

Lifespan is the other number missing from the label. Charge-Test says its ranking does not cover service life. Gough Lui ran the XTAR 4150 through 100 cycles in the lab and measured a loss of 0.85–0.89 mWh per cycle. That projects to 80% of its energy at about 843 cycles, against XTAR's 1,200-cycle rating (843 ÷ 1,200 = 70%). XTAR supplied the samples. Of the cells on this page, the 4150 is the only one with a published cycle-wear test, which makes it the pick if you want measured durability as well as near-top energy: buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CF8WTYX4?tag=dealectric-20.

Charging is the catch. The cells manage their own charging and need a charger that supplies 4.75–5.5 V. A NiMH-only charger won't work, and XTAR says its older VX4 V1 cannot charge the CLR 4300. The XTAR L4 Pro (15.90 dollars at XTAR on 10 Nov 2026) charges both 1.5V Li-ion and NiMH AA/AAA cells.

Not recommended

PUJIMAX AA (4,255 mWh label). It has the biggest number on the shelf and delivered about 2,255 mWh in Charge-Test's lab, 53% of its label (2,255 ÷ 4,255). The Hixon 3500 (3,339 mWh) and UseNiy 3600 (3,162 mWh) also tested below both XTAR cells. EBL for Apple mice and keyboards, or flashes: an EBL store listing says its 1.5V lithium AAs can't be used in Apple mice and keyboards, and Light And Matter measured flash recycle times up to 8 times slower than NiMH.
