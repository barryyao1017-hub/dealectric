What the differences mean

  • The NX3000D's 1,500 W is its 2 Ω figure. Most home passive subs are 4 Ω or 8 Ω, and at 4 Ω Behringer rates it at 900 W per channel. At 20 Hz Audio Science Review measured 268 W: 268 ÷ 900 = 30% of the rating.
  • The Fosi M03 is limited by its power brick. A bridged Class D output can swing at most the supply voltage across the speaker, so clean power tops out at V² ÷ (2 × R). On the included 32 V brick that is 32² ÷ (2 × 4) = 128 W into 4 Ω and 32² ÷ (2 × 8) = 64 W into 8 Ω. The brick itself supplies 32 V × 5 A = 160 W, which is below the 200 W in the spec table.
  • The distortion level changes the number. TI rates the TPA3255 chip inside the M03 at 600 W at 10% THD+N but 480 W at 1%: 480 ÷ 600 = 80% of the headline. Dayton's 250 W is quoted at under 0.1%.
  • The Crown can be bridged for a bigger sub. One channel gives 350 W into 4 Ω or 215 W into 8 Ω. Bridged, it gives 1,100 W into 4 Ω or 700 W into 8 Ω (Thomann listing), about 3× the single-channel figure: 1,100 ÷ 350 = 3.1.

Which watt figure to believe

A watt rating means little without its conditions: the load, the distortion level, the frequency band and how many channels were driven. Read the figure at your sub's own impedance, and trust it more when the maker states how it was measured or a named reviewer has measured it.

  • Dayton SPA250DSP: 250 W RMS into 4 Ω at THD+N under 0.1%. It is the only candidate whose maker page states load and distortion together. Dayton rates no other load. buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B073ZNT8HJ?tag=dealectric-20
  • Crown XLS 1002: Crown's page gives 350 W per channel at 4 Ω and lists THD < 0.5% without a power level. Amir at Audio Science Review (review dated 2020-09-25) called its 4 Ω power 'healthy' and put it on his recommended list 'for non-critical applications or for driving subwoofers'. He heard none of its fans over his PC during testing. He found that a limiter holds burst power to the continuous level, and that one channel measured much worse than the other.
  • Behringer NX3000D: Behringer lists 2 × 1,500 W at 2 Ω, 2 × 900 W at 4 Ω and 3,000 W bridged into 4 Ω, with no conditions. Amir at Audio Science Review (2020-07-06) measured about 400 W per channel into 4 Ω with both channels driven, falling to 268 W at 20 Hz. He wrote that the rated numbers only make sense with one channel driven.
  • Fosi Audio M03: Fosi's page gives 200 W RMS at 4 Ω in its spec table and 150 W RMS at 4 Ω in its description, with no supply voltage for either. Fosi states: 'The package comes with a 32V/5A power supply, you need a more powerful power supply to get the MAX power output.' Size it to a sub rated around 125 W at 4 Ω on the stock brick. $76.49: https://dealectric.com/go/fosi-audio-m03-subwoofer-amplifier

The Dayton and Fosi figures above are the makers' own. We know of no bench test of either by a named reviewer.

Not recommended

Behringer NX3000D for a living room. Its fan 'is not temperature sensitive and wails away upon power up' (Audio Science Review). It has only XLR / 1/4 in combo inputs, so an AV receiver's RCA sub out needs an adapter. At 20 Hz it measured 268 W against its 900 W rating. Amir concluded it is 'not my cup of tea for home use so I can't recommend it'. It suits a PA rig, where fan noise doesn't matter, better than a home theater.
