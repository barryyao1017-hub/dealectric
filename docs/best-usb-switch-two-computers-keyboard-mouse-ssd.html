What the differences mean

  • Power left over on the CM691 from a USB-C computer: 1.3 A. The USB 3 limit for a high-power SSD is 0.9 A, and a keyboard and mouse at one USB 2.0 unit load each draw 0.1 A apiece: 2.4 − (0.9 + 0.1 + 0.1) = 1.3 A spare. A keyboard or mouse can declare up to 0.5 A, which would use up more of that margin.
  • From a USB-A computer, power depends on the computer's port. UGREEN says the CM691 gives 5 V 2 A over USB-A, but a USB 3 port is only required to supply 0.9 A. A port that gives only the minimum leaves the switch 1.1 − 0.9 = 0.2 A short of a keyboard, mouse and SSD.
  • The ATEN's device ports get less power than the CM691's. ATEN's manual caps all four ports at 10 W: 10 W ÷ 5 V = 2 A, which is 0.4 A less than the CM691 gets from a USB-C computer.
  • 10 Gbps vs 5 Gbps, measured. ssd-tester.com measured 1,048 MB/s read on a 10Gbps link, and Binary Tides measured about 400 MB/s on a 5Gbps link. Copying 100 GB therefore takes 100,000 ÷ 1,048 ≈ 95 s at 10Gbps against 100,000 ÷ 400 = 250 s at 5Gbps. The two tests used different drives, so the comparison is between link speeds rather than drives.
  • Cost per 10Gbps port on the CM691: $40.99 ÷ 4 = $10.25.

USB-C or USB-A computer: how much power reaches the SSD

A sharing switch with no power jack passes on whatever the active computer's port supplies. UGREEN's manual lists the CM691's only supply as 'USB-C 5.0V 2.4A Max'. On its US store UGREEN says the switch can output up to 2.4 A when connected to a USB-C laptop port, and 5 V 2 A when connected to a USB-A port. A USB-C port gives a 900 mA baseline and supplies 1.5 A or 3 A only if it advertises that current, so a laptop that offers only the baseline gives the switch less than UGREEN's figure.

Other points from UGREEN for the CM691: with a USB-A computer, use your own USB-A-to-C data cable no longer than 0.8 m, because longer cables may lower the speed. If both computers are connected, PC 1 gets priority. On macOS 13.3 or later, set System Settings > Privacy & Security > 'Allow accessories to connect' to 'Always'. The switch carries data only, with no video and no charging.

ATEN asks for more from the computer: the US3342 needs a host that supplies at least 5 V 3 A for basic USB functions, and its manual says the four device ports share 10 W in total. Its USB-C PD input is for charging the laptop at up to 85 W. Cable Matters recommends using the 201160's optional power port so that power-hungry devices don't lose their connection when the host's power falls short.

Samsung says its T7 Shield complies with USB power specifications, so a USB-spec portable SSD of that kind stays within the 0.9 A ceiling used above. Eject the SSD before you switch: switching disconnects it from one computer and connects it to the other.

The UGREEN USB 3.0 Switch for two computers is a 5Gbps switch. It costs $35.99 at UGREEN US and was out of stock there on 2026-11-08. It saves only $40.99 − $35.99 = $5.00 over the CM691 but runs at half the link speed, which works out to about 400 MB/s against about 1,048 MB/s in the measurements above.
