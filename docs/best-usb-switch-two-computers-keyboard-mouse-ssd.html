Switch versus KVM

A USB switch hands one set of USB devices to whichever computer is selected; it carries no video. A monitor with two inputs does the display half (its own input button, or a monitor with a built-in KVM), and the USB switch does the keyboard, mouse, webcam and SSD. A KVM does both in one box and costs more, and the cheap "USB-C KVM" listings are usually video switches with 5Gbps or USB 2.0 ports.

Drives: eject first

Switching is electrically the same as unplugging the drive from one computer and plugging it into the other. Plugable's knowledge base says to eject storage before switching and does not recommend its switch for drives in use; ATEN's FAQ describes the same behaviour. No switch here ejects for you. Keep the SSD's writes finished (or the file closed) before pressing the button, and expect Windows to re-scan the drive on each switch.

Speed and power

USB 3.2 Gen 2 is 10Gbps, about 1,000 MB/s to a fast external SSD; USB 3.2 Gen 1 (5Gbps) tops out near 450 MB/s. On the UGREEN switch the four ports share each computer's single 10Gbps uplink, and UGREEN says to keep the uplink cables within 0.8 m or speed drops. It is bus-powered: up to 2.4 A from a USB-C host, 1.8 A from a USB-A host (which needs a USB-A to C cable, not included), enough for a keyboard, mouse and a bus-powered SSD; a spinning drive with its own power supply is safer. UGREEN also advises against 2.4 GHz wireless receivers on USB 3 switches because of interference, a USB 3 problem on any brand.

Software instead of hardware

Logitech's Easy-Switch (F1 to F3 on a paired keyboard) and Flow (cursor and clipboard across computers over the network, in Logi Options+) move a Logitech keyboard and mouse between machines with no box at all, but neither moves an SSD or any other USB device.
