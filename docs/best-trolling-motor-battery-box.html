What the differences mean

Three questions decide the box: is there a breaker sized for your motor, does the maker accept your battery chemistry, and does the battery fit under the lid?

  • Newport's breaker vs. Newport's motors: the box's motor breaker is 60 A. Newport specifies 40 A for its 36 lb motor and 50 A for its 40, 46, 55 and 86 lb motors, so only the 62 lb motor can run on the box breaker alone (60 A needed = 60 A fitted). Newport: “Failure to use the correct circuit breaker with the Smart Battery Box can damage your motor.”
  • Minn Kota motors in a 60 A box: Minn Kota's sizing table calls for 30 A at 30 lb, 50 A at 40–45 lb and 60 A at 50–55 lb (12 V). On a 45 lb motor the built-in breaker is 60 − 50 = 10 A oversized.
  • LiTime's breaker vs. motor draw: its only breaker trips above 16 A. Even the smallest motor above calls for a 30 A breaker, 30 − 16 = 14 A more than that breaker can pass, so it can only protect the sockets. The motor needs its own breaker.
  • Group 31 AGM (Optima D31M, 9.38 in tall): Newport 10 − 9.38 = 0.62 in of headroom; NOCO BG31 9.7 − 9.38 = 0.32 in; LiTime 8.66 − 9.38 = −0.72 in, so the lid won't close.
  • 12V 100Ah Group 31 LiFePO4 (LiTime, 8.43 in tall): LiTime box 8.66 − 8.43 = 0.23 in of headroom.
  • 24V 100Ah Group 31 LiFePO4 (LiTime or Redodo, 9.45 in tall): LiTime box 8.66 − 9.45 = −0.79 in, so it doesn't fit.
  • Newport USB port: 5 V × 0.5 A = 2.5 W, enough to top up a phone slowly.

Lithium and the breaker: what each maker states

Newport. The Smart Battery Box listing on Newport's store ends with “*Not compatible with lithium batteries”. Newport's FAQ narrows the issue to the LED charge meter, which “is not compatible with Lithium batteries”, and Newport's meter article says the LED meters on its trolling motors read incoming voltage and “will not work” with lithium. We go by the store listing: if you run LiFePO4, Newport doesn't support this box with it.

LiTime. The box is sold as “suitable for Lithium, Gel, Sealed, and Flooded” batteries up to 66 lb. It connects with 6 AWG silicone cable and M8 ring terminals and is rated for 100 A continuous through the box. Its one breaker, though, is 16 A (“can be cut off ... as the DC is over 16A”), so it isn't a trolling-motor breaker. Minn Kota says the motor breaker goes on the positive lead, “as close as possible to the battery”, and that QUEST motors draw no more than 60 A, so a breaker larger than 60 A shouldn't be used.

Minn Kota. On its discontinued Power Center, Minn Kota says “Lithium batteries can be used”, but its battery meter “will always show fully charged”. It holds Group 24 and 27 only.

24 V Group 31 lithium. Redodo's 24V 100Ah Group 31 listing says it “fits Group 31 trays only; not compatible with standard Group 31 battery boxes”, and LiTime tells buyers of its 24V 100Ah Group 31 to verify enclosed-box clearance. At 9.45 in tall, neither fits the LiTime box.

Not recommended

Minn Kota Trolling Motor Battery Power Center. Minn Kota no longer makes it, and the lowest US listing we opened (Boatersland, $94.99) shows it backordered. It has the same 60 A and 10 A breakers as the Newport but takes only Group 24 and 27, so a Group 31 battery is out. The Newport covers the same 12 V lead-acid use and has 10 in of interior height.
