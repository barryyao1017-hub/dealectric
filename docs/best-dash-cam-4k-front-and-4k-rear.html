What the differences mean

All four cameras record 4K on both channels, so resolution does not separate them. The differences are in how much data each camera writes per second, the rear frame rate, and how long a card lasts before the oldest footage is overwritten. Dealectric does not test units. The figures below come from the makers and from the named DashCamTalk reviewers.

  • Rear detail: the A229 Ultra's rear writes 35 Mbps against 24 Mbps on the S1 Pro Max. 35 ÷ 24 = 1.46, so it writes about 46% more data per second from the same IMX678 sensor at the same 30 fps.
  • Rear smoothness: the A900 Ultra's rear runs at 24 fps. 24 ÷ 30 = 0.8, so it captures 80% of the frames per second that the other three record from their rear cameras.
  • Loop length on 512 GB (decimal gigabytes, before file-system overhead):
    • S1 Pro Max: (32 + 24) Mbps × 60 ÷ 8 = 420 MB per minute, and 512,000 ÷ 420 ≈ 1,219 min ≈ 20.3 h.
    • A900 Ultra: 67.1 Mbps ≈ 503 MB per minute, so about 17.0 h.
    • A229 Ultra: 430 MB front + 250 MB rear per minute = 680 MB, and 512,000 ÷ 680 ≈ 753 min ≈ 12.5 h.
    • Elite 10-2CH: 11 h 25 min at Extreme, from BlackVue's own table.
  • Camera plus parking kit:
    • A229 Ultra 2CH: $409.99 + HK4 $17.99 = $427.98.
    • Elite 10-2CH: $603.99 + Hardwire Kit $19.99 = $623.98.
    • A900 Ultra: $499.99 + UP12/UP11 $129.99 = $629.98.
    These are maker-store prices read 2026-11-21. The S1 Pro Max 4K + 4K was $239.99 through our link on the same date.

What the 4K rear actually records when both cameras run

VIOFO A229 Ultra 2CH. In a test post of Aug 10, 2025 on DashCamTalk, Chuck McCoy lists both cameras at 4K 30 fps in H.264. The post gives the 2-channel maximum as 60 Mbps (430 MB per minute) front and 35 Mbps (250 MB per minute) rear, and notes the camera runs on a supercapacitor rather than a lithium battery. Vortex Radar measured the front at 59.87 Mb/s with the front camera alone and 36.11 Mb/s with the 4K rear connected (Aug 19–20, 2025). Chuck McCoy replied that a firmware update should restore 60 Mbps to the front in the 2-channel setup. Either way, the rear holds 35 Mbps. VIOFO's 4K rear camera (RNE800) works only with the A229 Ultra front unit. An A229 Pro (4K + 2K) cannot be upgraded to dual 4K by adding a rear camera, so you need the Ultra kit itself.

Vantrue S1 Pro Max 4K + 4K. Vantrue lists dual Sony STARVIS 2 IMX678 sensors with 155° HDR lenses. DashCamTalk's spec table gives 4K 30 fps on both cameras at 32 Mbps front and 24 Mbps rear (H.265), a Novatek NT98529 processor, capacitor power and cards up to 1 TB. Reviewer TonyM measured 24.6 Mbps front and 23.8 Mbps rear with both cameras running (H.265, PlatePix on, HDR off). TonyM also writes that the rear camera supports HDR, which differs from the spec table's 'No'. Parking modes need a hardwire kit sold separately. $239.99: https://dealectric.com/go/vantrue-s1-pro-max-4k-4k

BlackVue ELITE 10-2CH. BlackVue's manual lists 4K at 30 fps front and rear. It gives the bitrate as 60 + 30 Mbps at Extreme (H.265), 40 + 30 at Extreme (H.264) and 30 + 30 at High. BlackVue's store lists HDR on the front camera only.

70mai A900 Ultra. rcg530 tested it on firmware 1.0.65ww (DashCamTalk, Oct 1, 2026). The test measured the front at 3840×2160, 30 fps, 33.55 Mbps and the rear at 3840×2160, 24 fps, 33.55 Mbps, and notes that the marketing states 25 fps for the rear.

Not recommended

For a shopper asking for 4K front and 4K rear, the 70mai A900 Ultra is the weakest buy here. It is a true 4K + 4K camera on paper, but its rear records at 24 fps in rcg530's test. Its parking kit (UP12/UP11, 4G) costs $129.99. It sells for $499.99 at 70mai's store (2026-11-21), which is more than the A229 Ultra 2CH. The VIOFO records its rear at 30 fps and 35 Mbps, with HDR on both channels.
