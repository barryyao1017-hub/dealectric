What the differences mean

Why 5,000 W is the line. 5,000 W at 120 V is 41.7 A (5,000 ÷ 120). The largest 120 V outlet on the two 6,000 W units here is the TT-30R at 25 A and 3,000 W, per Nature's Generator's manual and Anker's spec table. So a station that delivers 5,000 W continuous from one unit is a 120/240 V unit, and the load draws 20.8 A at 240 V (5,000 ÷ 240). This is the class for what EcoFlow calls "240V appliances like central AC, a well pump, or an electric dryer", connected through a transfer switch. Every row meets 5,000 W continuous on its maker's own figure. Left out for falling short: EcoFlow's TITAN 5000 Classic, which EcoFlow rates at 4,000 W at 120 V.

Inside the line, the ranking uses cost per watt-hour and what is left above a 5,000 W load. All prices are maker-store prices read on 2026-11-27 unless a date says otherwise.

  • Cost per Wh = store price ÷ rated watt-hours. L6000: $2,249.99 ÷ 3,840 = 58.6¢. OUPES Guardian 6000 V2: $2,699 ÷ 4,608 = 58.6¢. Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus, at Jackery's own US store (from $3,499): ÷ 5,040 = 69.4¢. Pecron F5000LFP: $2,399 ÷ 5,120 = 46.9¢. Anker F3800 Plus: $2,999.99 (read 2026-11-21) ÷ 3,840 = 78.1¢.
  • Cost per continuous watt = store price ÷ rated watts. Pecron $2,399 ÷ 7,200 = 33.3¢. L6000 ÷ 6,000 = 37.5¢. OUPES $2,699 ÷ 6,000 = 45.0¢. Jackery $3,499 ÷ 7,200 = 48.6¢. Anker ÷ 6,000 = 50.0¢.
  • Minutes at 5,000 W = Wh × 0.85 ÷ 5,000 × 60. The 0.85 is an assumed usable share, not a measurement. L6000: 3,840 × 0.85 ÷ 5,000 × 60 = 39. Jackery: 5,040 gives 51; HOBOTECH's measured 4,450 Wh gives 53 (4,450 ÷ 5,000 × 60).
  • Headroom above the load. The L6000 and the F3800 Plus cap their 240 V outlet at 25 A: 6,000 − 5,000 = 1,000 W spare, or 4.2 A. The 7,200 W units leave 2,200 W.
  • What the Jackery's premium buys. Jackery's store price is $1,249.01 above the L6000's for 1,200 Wh more: $1.04 per extra Wh, plus 1,200 W more output. The L6000's own Power Pod adds 3,840 Wh for $1,499.99, which is 39.1¢ per Wh. The L6000 with one Power Pod is $3,749.99 for 7,680 Wh: 48.8¢ per Wh and about 78 minutes at 5,000 W (7,680 × 0.85 ÷ 5,000 × 60).
  • Putting an hour back. One hour at 5,000 W takes 2.8 hours to refill from a 120 V socket at 1,800 W (5,000 ÷ 1,800), on the L6000 and on the Anker. At 3,600 W on 240 V it takes 1.4 hours. The L6000 needs the separately sold Super Charging Cable ($119.99) and a NEMA 14-50R outlet for that.
  • The pick depends on the sale price. At the crossed-out list price of $2,999.99 the L6000 works out to 78.1¢ per Wh (2,999.99 ÷ 3,840), behind the OUPES and the Jackery. Nature's Generator's site showed a Black Friday banner with no end date when read on 2026-11-27.

What 5,000 W costs at each maker's store

Dealectric does not test units. The figures below are the makers' own unless a reviewer is named.

Nature's Generator L6000. The store showed $2,249.99 against a crossed-out $2,999.99 on 2026-11-27. The manual gives 6,000 W continuous and 12,000 W peak, 3,840 Wh (51.2 V, 75 Ah) LiFePO4, more than 3,000 cycles and 112.4 lb. Outlet by outlet: the L14-30R gives "up to 120/240Vac 25A 6000W", the TT-30R 120 V at 25 A and 3,000 W, and each 5-20R 120 V at 20 A and 2,400 W. A 5,000 W load therefore has to be a 240 V load on the L14-30R, or go through a transfer switch fed from it. Wall charging is 800 W or 1,800 W at 120 V, or 3,600 W at 240 V. Solar input is two inputs of 1,200 W at 12 to 60 V. Plugged into the wall on the included 120 V cord it passes through 1,800 W and switches to battery within 10 ms; the Super Charging Cable raises pass-through to 6,000 W. Two units joined by the $199.99 Combiner Cable give 12,000 W at 50 A. The maker also sells it bundled with a 30 A or 50 A transfer switch, manual or automatic. Warranty needs a check before ordering: the store says "Up to 5-year warranty", while the manual's warranty section says twelve months for shipping, parts and labor. No named reviewer's measurement is cited here for the L6000.

Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus. Jackery's US store listed it from $3,499 with 7,200 W rated output, a 14,400 W surge figure and 5,040 Wh. HOBOTECH (posted 2024-11-25) measured 4,450 Wh from the AC outlets at 120 V, 88% of the rating. It held 3,600 W at 120 V and 7,200 W at 240 V for 5 minutes, peaked at 9,300 W at 240 V for under 5 seconds, and drew 85 W with the inverter on and nothing plugged in (515 Wh over 6 hours). The Watt Guide reports from Jackery's manual that the 240 V outlets are rated 30 A and the 120 V sockets are split into two 3,600 W banks.

OUPES Guardian 6000 V2. $2,699 and listed available. OUPES states "240V/6,000W (Boost 7,200W) & 120V/3600W", so it too reaches 5,000 W only at 240 V. The continuous rating is the 6,000 W figure.

Pecron F5000LFP. $2,399 as a pre-order shipping early January. Pecron lists 3,600 W × 2 at 120 V and 7,200 W max at 240 V, 5,120 Wh LiFePO4, 4,000+ cycles to 80% and 3,600 W AC charging with a 30 A cable. It has the lowest cost per Wh and per watt of the five, but it ships more than four weeks out, so it is not the pick today.

Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus. Anker's page showed "Sold Out" with no price loaded on 2026-11-27. Its spec table gives the L14-30R as 120/240 V, 25 A max and 6,000 W max, with 3,840 Wh, 136.7 lb, 1,800 W max AC charging and a 5-year warranty.

EcoFlow. EcoFlow's US store showed the DELTA Pro Ultra as sold out, and the TITAN 5000 Plus as a pre-order shipping late January with only a deposit price, so neither has a cost-per-Wh figure here. HOBOTECH (posted 2024-01-11) measured the DELTA Pro Ultra at 5,390 Wh from the AC outlets, 88% of its 6,144 Wh battery.

Not recommended

EcoFlow TITAN 5000 Classic, for this job. The "5000" in its name is watt-hours. EcoFlow rates it at 4,000 W at 120 V and says it is "not designed for 240V appliances like central AC, a well pump, or an electric dryer"; the TITAN 5000 and TITAN 5000 Plus are the models EcoFlow lists at 7.2 kW and 120/240 V.

Waiting for the Anker F3800 Plus at its listed price. It has the same 3,840 Wh and the same 25 A limit as the L6000 and works out to 78.1¢ per Wh against 58.6¢, and Anker's US store had none to ship on 2026-11-27.
