What the differences mean

How much of the start each unit supplies. TechGearLab measured a 2010 Ford Explorer's 4.0 L V6 starter drawing 301 A. A 100 A engine start covers 100 ÷ 301 = 33% of that, a 200 A unit 200 ÷ 301 = 66%, and a 50 A unit 50 ÷ 301 = 17%. The car's own battery has to supply the rest.

Recharge time. A battery half-discharged from 50 Ah is missing 25 Ah. 25 Ah ÷ 1 A = 25 h for the Charge N Start 1100 and 1120. 25 Ah ÷ 4 A = 6.3 h for the 4120. 25 Ah ÷ 6 A = 4.2 h for any of the 6 A Schumacher units, before charging losses; Schumacher's own manual table gives 5 h at 6 A for a half-discharged 50 Ah battery. The boost rate doesn't shorten this much: the SC1281 manual says boost runs only during the first 10 minutes.

The 1100 vs the 1120. $149.95 − $139.95 = $10 buys 200 more peak amps and gas-engine coverage up to 6.0 L instead of 5.0 L.

Why a 100 A engine start can't start a car by itself

A plug-in charger's engine start mode draws its power from the wall, and it can supply only part of what a starter pulls. Schumacher's SC1309 manual says engine start provides additional amps for cranking an engine with a weak or run-down battery. Always use in combination with a battery. The SC1281 manual warns that using engine start with no battery in the vehicle could damage its electrical system.

These chargers also make you wait. Per the SC1281 manual:

  • The unit charges for 2 minutes before it will crank, or 5 minutes if the battery is below 2 V or it is extremely cold.
  • Each crank lasts up to 7 seconds.
  • Repeated cranking triggers a mandatory 180-second cool-down.
  • It needs a grounded 120 V outlet, and Schumacher does not recommend extension cords.

The SC1281 troubleshooting table adds that if the engine draws more than the engine-start rating, the crank can last less than 3 seconds. A big engine therefore gets the shortest help from the smallest unit.

A lithium pack carries its own energy. TechGearLab's NOCO GB40 started a 4.0 L V6 with a completely drained battery (no clicks, no dash lights) three times in a row at 33 °F. Battery Tender's Charge N Start packs crank for up to 5 seconds per attempt, with 30 seconds between attempts, and need no outlet. One caution about pack claims: TechGearLab measured the GB40's usable capacity at 15.57 Wh against the 24 Wh on the box.

Don't compare the two kinds of amp figure directly. Schumacher rates its own plug-in SC1667 in cranking amps (250 A) and its SL1672 lithium pack in peak amps (1,000 A). A 1,200 A pack next to a 100 A charger is not 12 times the starting power on the same scale.

Battery type matters too. Schumacher added 12 V LiFePO4 charging to 13 models on July 30, 2026, including the SC1281L and SC1305. The older SC1281 and the SC1309 aren't on that list, so check the model number on the listing.

Not recommended

The Schumacher SC1305 as a jump starter. Its 50 A engine start is 17% of the 301 A TechGearLab measured on a 4.0 L V6, so nearly all the cranking current has to come from the battery you're trying to rescue. It's a sound 6 A charger with LiFePO4 support. Buy it for charging, not for starting a car.
