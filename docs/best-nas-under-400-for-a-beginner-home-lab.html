What the differences mean

In a Docker and VM lab, RAM runs out before anything else. So divide each box's price by the RAM it ships with:

  • UGREEN DXP2800: $389.99 ÷ 8 GB = $48.75 per GB.
  • TerraMaster F2-425 Plus: $399.49 ÷ 8 GB = $49.94 per GB, with 51 cents to spare under $400.
  • TerraMaster F2-424: $379.99 ÷ 8 GB = $47.50 per GB, but TerraMaster's store shows it sold out.
  • TerraMaster F2-425: $299.99 ÷ 4 GB = $75.00 per GB. It's the cheapest box but has the most expensive RAM.
  • CPU: PassMark gives the DXP2800's Intel N100 5,305 and the F2-425's Celeron N5095 4,003. 5,305 ÷ 4,003 = 1.33, so the N100 is about 33% faster.

What RAM costs in 2026, and why to buy it in the box

The usual budget advice is to buy the cheaper NAS and add RAM later. Maker prices on 2026-11-08 don't support that:

  • TerraMaster lists its own 16 GB DDR5-4800 stick (A-SRAMD5-16G) at $469.99. The stick fits the F2-424, and $469.99 ÷ $379.99 = 1.24 times the price of the NAS. Its 32 GB stick, the only one that lists the F2-425 Plus, is $699.99 and sold out.
  • The plain F2-425 appears on none of TerraMaster's memory listings. Starry Hope gives it a 16 GB ceiling, so any upgrade means a third-party module.
  • The older F2-423 uses cheaper DDR4-3200. TerraMaster sells 8 GB ($169.99) and 16 GB ($299.99) sticks for it, both sold out, and its US store no longer lists the F2-423 itself.
  • UGREEN sells no memory in its NAS store. Starry Hope says the DXP2800 has one SO-DIMM slot with a 16 GB maximum. Going to 16 GB therefore means replacing the 8 GB stick, not adding a second one. Plan to live with the 8 GB it ships with. That's enough for Docker Compose and a handful of self-hosted containers.
  • Beelink ME Mini: The DIY Life lists its 12 GB LPDDR5 as soldered and not upgradeable, so 12 GB is the most it will ever have. That rules out growing into a Proxmox VM lab.

To learn Linux itself, the DXP2800 is a tested model on the Debian wiki. Disable the watchdog service in the BIOS before you boot the Debian installer. In its DXP4800 notes, the wiki also suggests disabling the eMMC controller in the BIOS before installing to an NVMe drive, so the original UGREEN installer partition stays intact. The same page quotes UGREEN's policy: "The official hardware warranty policy will remain unchanged" if you install a third-party system. $389.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nasync-dxp2800

Not recommended

TerraMaster F2-425 for a Docker learning lab. It costs $299.99 for 4 GB, the most per GB here ($75.00). TerraMaster sells no memory for it, and its N5095 is about 25% slower than the N100 on PassMark. It works fine as a file and media server. A beginner who wants room for containers and VMs should spend the extra $90 on the DXP2800's 8 GB and N100.
