What the differences mean

"Triple" is sold two ways. Most triple extenders add two screens, making three with the laptop's own. A few add three, making four. The table says which for each row. The figures below are arithmetic on each maker's stated size and resolution. Dealectric does not test units.

  • Added pixels. Two 2240×1400 screens: 2 × 2240 × 1400 = 6,272,000 (6.27 MP). Three 1920×1200 screens: 3 × 1920 × 1200 = 6,912,000 (6.91 MP). 6.912 ÷ 6.272 = 1.10, so the third screen brings 10% more pixels. Two 1920×1200 screens give 4,608,000 (4.61 MP); 4.608 ÷ 6.272 = 0.73, or 27% fewer than the Trio 3 Pro's pair.
  • Added area. A 16:10 panel's area is 0.4494 × diagonal². 14.1-inch: 89.35 sq in, × 2 = 178.7. 16-inch: 115.06 sq in, × 3 = 345.2; 345.2 ÷ 178.7 = 1.93, so 93% more area. 14-inch: 88.09 sq in, × 3 = 264.3; 264.3 ÷ 178.7 = 1.48, so 48% more.
  • Pixel density. √(2240² + 1400²) = 2,642 px; ÷ 14.1 in = 187 ppi. √(1920² + 1200²) = 2,264 px; ÷ 16 in = 142 ppi, ÷ 14 in = 162 ppi. The same text is drawn with fewer pixels on the larger 1920×1200 panels.
  • What "2K" and "QHD" cover. UPERFECT's own guide prints "2K" for both 2160×1440 (3.11 MP) and 2560×1600 (4.10 MP); 4.096 ÷ 3.110 = 1.32, a 32% spread. The Trio 3 Pro's 2240×1400 is 3.14 MP per screen; 3.136 ÷ 3.686 = 0.85, so 15% fewer pixels than a standard 2560×1440 QHD panel. Compare pixel counts, not labels.

Aura's listings describe the two Triple Go models as Full HD+ at 16:10, so their rows carry Aura's label and the area that follows from size and shape.

What a third screen buys under $600

It buys area and window count, not sharpness. Every in-stock three-screen extender we read under $600 on FOPO's and Aura's stores on 2026-11-21 is listed as Full HD or Full HD+. The nearest sharper one sits just over the line: Aura's Triple Aero Pro Max 16" Ultra, three QHD 2.5K screens, was $619 on Aura's US store that day.

The third screen also changes how video reaches the screens. The Trio 3 Pro takes one USB-C cable per screen. Its manual, quoted by Macworld's Simon Jary, says driver installation is not necessary if the laptop has two DP-enabled USB-C ports. Jary writes that plain M1 and M2 Macs and the MacBook Neo, which run one external monitor, need Mobile Pixels' free software. Mobile Pixels' downloads page lists Trio drivers for Windows and macOS. He also notes the screens cannot reach full brightness on laptop power alone; a power adapter on the Trio fixes that and passes up to 65 W to the laptop.

The three-screen rows all run through a driver. FOPO's S788 needs its UsbDisplay driver on every laptop, with the Screen Recording permission on macOS, and needs an external power supply. Aura's Triple Go models need a driver install on first use, a USB-C port with video output and the included 30 W adapter.

On price, Aura's Triple Go Max 16" was $489 on Aura's US store on 2026-11-21, below the S788's $540 through our link for the same screen count and size. The S788 is the 16-inch three-screen row we link, and it also connects over USB-A; it is not the lower-priced of the two.

The UPERFECT BE14BF ties the Trio 3 Pro on pixels and was $389.99 on uperfect.com on 2026-11-21, against $407.99 for the Trio. UPERFECT's guide lists it at 1.54 kg; Macworld gives the Trio 4.13 lb (1.9 kg). We put the Trio first because its driver-free condition is documented, while UPERFECT's guide and store listing disagree on whether the single cable needs a driver. Mobile Pixels' store price moves with timed sales: it was $367.99 on 2026-11-18.

Where the line was drawn: every row cost $600 or less on the date read, and for the rows we link that is the price through our link. Left out for being over it: Aura's Triple Go Studio 18.5" ($609) and Triple Aero Pro Max 16" Ultra ($619), FOPO's S999 ($1,099.99), all read on 2026-11-21, and Xebec's Tri-Screen 3 ($699 on Amazon, 2026-11-21).
