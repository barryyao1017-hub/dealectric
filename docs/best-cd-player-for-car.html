What the differences mean

  • FiiO DM13 BT, discs per charge: FiiO rates the battery at 10 h, so 10 × 60 ÷ 74 min ≈ 8.1 full-length CDs. Sending over Bluetooth, HiFi Oasis timed just over 8 h, so 8 × 60 ÷ 74 ≈ 6.5 discs. On a long drive, D.MODE runs it from USB power without drawing the battery.
  • FiiO DM13 BT, charging: FiiO asks for 5 V at 2 A or more (10 W) and quotes about 2.5 h to full. 10 h ÷ 2.5 h = 4 h of play for each hour on a 2 A car charger.
  • In-dash power, continuous vs peak: the Pioneer's continuous rating is 22 ÷ 50 = 44% of its peak, and the Sony's is 20 ÷ 55 = 36%. The Sony's bigger peak number comes with the lowest continuous rating, but all three decks are within 2 W RMS of each other, so power shouldn't decide.
  • Preamp outputs (these matter only if you add an amplifier): Sony has 3 pairs at 4 V, Pioneer 2 pairs at 4 V and Kenwood 1 pair at 2.5 V (Sonic Electronix). 4 ÷ 2.5 = 1.6× the signal voltage into an amp.
  • Ensight universal vs vehicle kit: $299 − $150 = $149 more for the Bluetooth unit, which fits any car with Bluetooth audio, than for a kit matched to one car.

Will a USB CD player work in your car? The .WAV test

A plug-and-play USB CD player doesn't send sound to the car. It reads the disc and hands the car's radio the tracks as files on what looks like a USB drive, and the radio's own media player has to play them. Aerpro's ADVUSBCD makes this explicit. It needs a head unit 'running on Android 4.4 or with USB media player that supports .WAV file playback', and its distributor Connects2 adds that it 'will work only with USB with 5V + 1.5A power supply'.

The test, before you buy: load 3 .WAV songs onto a USB stick and plug it into the factory USB port. If they play without skipping and next/previous track works, Aerpro says the CD player will most likely work. If they don't play, the plug-in USB route is out for your car.

Support differs even within one brand. Hyundai's Gen5W infotainment manual lists only .mp3, .wma and .flac, while another Hyundai owner's manual says .mp3, .wma and .wav files are recognised. The Gen5W manual also warns that hubs and extension cables can stop a USB device being recognised, so plug in directly. Ensight avoids the guesswork by matching each kit to a specific radio (for GM trucks, IO5/6 radios only) and lists WMA support.

Other quirks Aerpro lists: the radio may not see the player until a disc is in, which can take 10 to 20 seconds. Some radios show only track numbers, not song names. Tracks longer than 30 minutes won't play, which rules out many audiobooks.

The FiiO DM13 BT can't be used as one of these plug-in players. Its USB-C port powers the unit and rips CDs to a FAT32 flash drive, so in a car it plays over Bluetooth or the 3.5 mm line out (2.5 Vrms) into AUX. The in-dash decks don't need the test at all. Pioneer's manual lists mass-storage (MSC), MTP and Android Open Accessory (AOA 2.0) connections for its USB port, and the deck plays discs in its own slot.

Not recommended

The standard FiiO DM13 (non-BT) for a car without AUX. FiiO's page says the 'Standard version cannot transmit Bluetooth', so it can't reach a car stereo except by wire. Buy the BT version.

Portable CD players whose Bluetooth only receives. itechguides quotes the Sunoony listing's 'No USB audio output to vehicle' and MEGATEK's 'Not compatible with Bluetooth car stereos'. Sunoony's listing points to its AUX cable or FM transmitter for the car, and MEGATEK's to its FM transmitter.
