Best budget electric hunting bike

Short answer. The best budget electric hunting bike is the Himiway D5 Pro ($1,999; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/himiway-d5-pro, code 50DEAL saves $50, pre-applied by the link): a 500 W mid-drive with 130 Nm of torque, a 960 Wh battery and a 400 lb load rating, at 79 lb with the battery. Climbing with a load is the deciding worry. A mid-drive turns the chain, so the bike's 9-speed, 11-36T gearing multiplies its torque on steep grades, and a hub motor can't use the gears. The lightest cheap pick is the Lectric XPeak2 ($1,399 on 2026-11-17, 75 lb with battery), but its 330 lb rider limit and front-only suspension suit flatter ground.

What owners and testers complain about

  • Weight. Most hunting e-bikes weigh 75 to 92 lb. Reviewers note that bikes this heavy are hard to load into a truck bed alone and too heavy for many hitch bike racks. The D5 Pro is 79 lb, and the D5 2.0 is 92 lb.
  • Real range. Claimed ranges assume flat ground and low assist. Electric Bike Report got 54 miles at full assist and 70 miles at the lowest level from Himiway's 960 Wh Zebra, against a 60-80 mile claim. Hills, a trailer, a heavy rider and cold weather all cut that. A spare battery is the usual fix.
  • Brakes. webBikeWorld saw fade on repeated steep descents with the D5 Pro's 2-piston brakes and 180 mm rotors. The D5 2.0 Camo has 4-piston calipers and a 203 mm front rotor.
  • Support. webBikeWorld waited nearly five months for a Himiway warranty part. Rambo sells through hunting dealers, which helps if you want local service.
  • Noise. We found no measured noise tests, and dealers disagree about whether hub or mid-drive motors are quieter. Low assist and a slow final approach matter more than the motor type.

Which one by need

  • Steep ground, budget under $2,000: Himiway D5 Pro, for the mid-drive, 130 Nm and 960 Wh.
  • Rolling ground, long or cold days: Himiway D5 2.0, for full suspension and the spare battery while Himiway lists it at $0.
  • Camo and easy mounting: Himiway D5 2.0 Camo, with a step-thru frame and 4-piston brakes.
  • Heavy riders or big loads: Velotric Nomad 2X, rated for 560 lb with air suspension.
  • Hunting-brand mid-drive with dealer support: Rambo Roamer 2.0.
  • Lightest and cheapest, flat ground: Lectric XPeak2.

Not recommended

  • The D5 2.0 if you load the bike into a truck alone often: at 92 lb, the D5 Pro or XPeak2 is easier.
  • The Lectric XPeak2 if you weigh more than 330 lb, or with a loaded rack: its rack is rated for 60 lb.
  • The D5 Pro for long, steep descents with heavy loads unless you plan a brake upgrade.
