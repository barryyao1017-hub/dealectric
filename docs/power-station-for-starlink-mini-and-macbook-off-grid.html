What the differences mean

  • One light workday is 195 Wh. 8 h × (21 W Starlink Mini + 3.3 W MacBook Air) = 195 Wh.
  • The worst-case workday is 381 Wh. 8 h × (40 W Mini, the top of Starlink's 25–40 W average + 7.6 W MacBook Air at full brightness) = 381 Wh.
  • Leaving the dish on for 24 hours uses 531 Wh a day. 21 W × 24 h + 3.3 W × 8 h = 531 Wh. The Elite 100 V2 then lasts 819 ÷ 531 = 1.5 days and the Elite 200 V2 lasts 1,910 ÷ 531 = 3.6 days.
  • Elite 100 V2 days per charge. 1,024 Wh × 0.80 = 819 Wh usable. 819 ÷ 195 = 4.2 light days, and 819 ÷ 381 = 2.1 worst-case days.
  • Running the inverter costs most of a workday. The Technology Man measured about 17 W idle on the Anker C1000 Gen 2 with AC on and nothing plugged in, and no drain with only DC on. 17 W × 8 h = 136 Wh, or 70% of a light workday.
  • Storage cost per light workday, BLUETTI store prices. Elite 100 V2: 499 ÷ 4.2 = about 119 dollars. AC180: 499 ÷ 5.1 = about 98 dollars, but its capacity was measured at a higher load. Elite 200 V2: 899 ÷ 9.8 = about 92 dollars.
  • Solar refill time. 195 Wh ÷ 200 W = about 1 h of a 200 W panel at its rated output. Real panels deliver less than their rating.

Measured, not rated: where the 195 Wh comes from

Starlink's Mini spec sheet gives an average draw of 25–40 W. The Mini needs a USB-C PD source of at least 100 W at 20 V/5 A when you use Starlink's USB-C to barrel jack cable. Its barrel input also takes 12–48 V DC at 60 W. Martin Sauter of Wirelessmoves ran a Mini from an Anker power station and saw an average of about 21 W. Draw stayed between 21 and 40 W, with peaks above 40 W only in the first minute or two after power-on. A 60 W USB-C power bank left the Mini stuck in a boot loop, while a 100 W PD bank ran it well. That is why every port in the table is judged against 20 V/5 A.

Notebookcheck measured the base MacBook Air 13 M4 running 16 h 13 min on Wi-Fi at 150 nits, and 7 h 05 min at maximum brightness, from a 53.8 Wh battery. That works out to 53.8 ÷ 16.22 = 3.3 W for light work and 53.8 ÷ 7.08 = 7.6 W at full brightness. Under full load it averaged 29.9 W. These are battery-side figures, and charging from USB-C adds a conversion loss, so plan on the worst-case column if you want margin.

A power station loses more of its energy at low loads. techreviewer.de (Timo Altmeyer) drew 946 Wh from the Elite 100 V2 at a constant 500 W. At loads in the double digits, which is what a Mini and a laptop draw, self-consumption brought usable energy down to about 80%. The AC180 and Anker figures were measured at an 8 A DC load, about four times this setup's draw, so their light-day counts are probably a little high. Keep the AC inverter off. Once it is off, the station you choose matters less than the 136 Wh a day an idle inverter would take.
