The Starlink Mini's power rules

Starlink lists the Mini at 12–48 V DC and 60 W, averaging 20–40 W with idle about 15 W (router included); meters show about 21 W average with 40 W peaks for the first minute or two after power-on, and 17–23 W idle for the antenna alone. The US help page requires a 100 W (20 V/5 A) USB-C PD source and says it will not run on 65 W supplies; a reviewer's 60 W bank boot-looped it. Starlink's own car adapter takes 12–24 V, so a station's 12 V socket is the other inverter-free route (not tested here). Snow-melt mode draws more; no measured Mini figure exists. The Mini was $149 at starlink.com in October 2026 (MSRP $249), Roam plans $55 (100 GB), $80 (300 GB) and $175 (unlimited) a month, and a Mini 2 is rumoured, not announced.

The arithmetic

A MacBook Air M4 idles at a few watts and charges at up to about 72 W; a 14-inch MacBook Pro M4 Pro sustains about 40 W under full load. Call it 10–20 W for an Air on office work and 20–40 W for a Pro. With the Mini on 8 hours the day is about 250–360 Wh; with 70 W combined, 560 Wh; with the Mini on 24 hours, about 720 Wh. Usable capacity is the measured AC figure (which already includes inverter loss), so the USB-C path only does better: the Elite 200 V2 idles at about 9.5 W with its inverter on and the AC180 at about 15 W, which is why the AC180's single USB-C port costs it a day over a long weekend compared with the two-port units.

Refilling

A 200 W panel yields about 150 W in full sun and 600–750 Wh over 4–5 peak-sun hours; the 400 W ALLPOWERS SP037 about 1,000–1,600 Wh. Kits as sold: P1800 + SF200 $531, AC180 + 200 W $729, Elite 200 V2 + 200 W $1,199, S2000 Pro + 400 W $848 (2026-11-03). Car charging: 96 W on the Elite 200 V2 (192 W at 24 V), about 100 W on the AC180, 200 W on the R600. The S2000 Pro caps its inverter near 2,050 W below 70 % charge, irrelevant at 60 W loads but worth knowing.
