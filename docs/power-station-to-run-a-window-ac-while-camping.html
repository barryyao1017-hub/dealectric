What the differences mean

A 5,000 BTU window AC running nonstop draws 400–470 W. TechGearLab metered four of them for an hour at full power and recorded 0.40 kWh (Midea EasyCool MAW05M1BWT and Frigidaire FFRA051WAE), 0.45 kWh (GE AWCS05BWA) and 0.47 kWh (Frigidaire FHWC054TE1). The arithmetic below uses that range.

  • One 8-hour night, nonstop: 8 h × 400–470 W = 3,200–3,760 Wh. No single 3 kWh station delivers that. The Elite 300 measured 2,650–2,819 Wh.
  • Elite 300, nonstop: 2,650 Wh ÷ 470 W = 5.6 h; 2,819 Wh ÷ 400 W = 7.0 h.
  • Elite 300, what lasts 8 h: 2,650 ÷ 8 = 331 W average, which on a 470 W unit is 70 % compressor time (331 ÷ 470); 2,819 ÷ 8 = 352 W, which on a 400 W unit is 88 % (352 ÷ 400).
  • 2 kWh class, nonstop: 1,910 Wh ÷ 470 W = 4.1 h (Elite 200 V2); 1,800 Wh ÷ 470 W = 3.8 h (DELTA 2 Max).
  • Idle overhead: The Technology Man measured about 19 W with the Elite 300's AC outlet on and nothing plugged in. 19 W × 8 h = 152 Wh, so keep the outlet off until you need it.

Measured, not calculated: the AC's draw and each battery's real output

Most runtime charts divide rated Wh by a draw calculated from the AC's efficiency rating. The numbers here come from meters. TechGearLab ran each AC at its highest power and fan setting for one hour in a 10 × 14 ft room with a 12 ft ceiling, so 400–470 W is the draw with the compressor on. Over a whole night a regular AC averages less whenever its thermostat switches the compressor off.

Elite 300 battery: Notebookcheck got about 2,650 Wh out at a load just over 200 W (just under 88 % of 3,014.4 Wh). The Technology Man got 2,819 Wh in 2 h 46 min on a 1 kW heater. StorageReview measured 2.67 kWh. Plan with the low end. Other stations: StorageReview measured 1,800 Wh from the DELTA 2 Max at 350 W, 1.91 kWh from the Elite 200 V2, and 1,030 Wh from the AC180 at 350 W.

Starting the compressor: The Elite 300's 4,800 W figure is 'Power Lifting'. StorageReview's reading of BLUETTI's manual says it is only for resistive loads and is not a startup allowance for motors or compressors, so the AC has to start within the 2,400 W continuous rating. StorageReview quotes BLUETTI's FAQ as saying compressors can draw two to four times their rated power at startup. For a 470 W unit that's at most 4 × 470 W = 1,880 W, which is under 2,400 W. Leave Power Lifting off for the AC.

Why an inverter AC changes the answer: LG says its DUAL Inverter window unit (the 6,000 BTU LW6023IVSM) adjusts compressor speed to hold the temperature, while a non-inverter unit switches its compressor fully on or off. A compressor that slows down at the set temperature instead of running at full power is how a night's average gets under the Elite 300's 331 W budget.

Plugging in: GE's owner's manual for its AEM05 5,000 BTU unit says not to use an extension cord, surge protector, multi-outlet adaptor or adapter plug, and to plug the AC into its own outlet. With a power station, put the station inside, within reach of the AC's own cord, with nothing else on that outlet.

Not recommended

The BLUETTI AC180 for running an AC. Its 1,800 W output covers a 5,000 BTU unit's running draw, but StorageReview measured 1,030 Wh usable at 350 W, and 1,030 ÷ 470 W = 2.2 h of nonstop cooling. It's a good station for fans and phones, but it won't run an AC through the night.
