A "solar generator with panels included" is a portable power station sold together with one or more folding panels. Judge it on four numbers: the battery (Wh, and LiFePO4 if you want thousands of cycles), the inverter (W it can run), the panel watts in the box, and the station's maximum solar input, which caps how much more panel you can add later. A folding panel delivers roughly 60–75% of its rating in good sun, so a 200 W panel puts back about 600–750 Wh on a clear day with five peak-sun hours.

How to pick

  • Size the battery to the load, then the panel to the battery. A fridge drawing about 1–1.5 kWh a day needs a 2 kWh station to ride through a night, and 400 W or more of panel to refill it in a day; a CPAP, phones and lights fit a 256–500 Wh station and a 100 W panel.
  • Check the voltage, not only the watts. Small stations with 12–28 V solar inputs (BLUETTI Elite 30 V2, Goal Zero Yeti 500) cannot take the big 40 V+ folding panels; the kits pair them with low-voltage panels for that reason.
  • Room to grow. The station's solar input is the ceiling: the Elite 200 V2 takes 1,000 W, the AC180 500 W, Jackery's 1000 v2 and 2000 v2 400 W.
  • Codes. Our rows carry the code that worked in each store's cart; Jackery, EcoFlow, Anker and Goal Zero rows show the store's own price with no code.
