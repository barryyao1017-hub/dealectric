What the differences mean

Usable share of the rated battery. Elite 200 V2: 1,910 ÷ 2,073.6 = 92% (StorageReview). Explorer 2000 v2: 1,710 ÷ 2,042 = 84%. DELTA 3: 870 ÷ 1,024 = 85%. AC180: 950 ÷ 1,152 = 82% (all three OutdoorGearLab, measured at the AC outlets). StorageReview doesn't say whether its discharge ran through the AC outlets. Even if it lost the same 15% as OutdoorGearLab's AC tests, the Elite 200 V2 would deliver 2,073.6 × 0.85 = 1,763 Wh. That is still the most of any kit here.

Price per usable Wh. Elite 200 V2 + 2 × 200 W: 1,499 ÷ 1,910 = $0.78. Jackery Solar Generator 2000 v2: 1,239 ÷ 1,710 = $0.72. Elite 200 V2 + 200 W: 1,199 ÷ 1,910 = $0.63. AC180 + 200 W: 769 ÷ 950 = $0.81. DELTA 3 + 220 W: 729 ÷ 870 = $0.84. AC180 + 2 × 200 W: 1,232 ÷ 950 = $1.30. The Jackery costs less per usable Wh, but Jackery's store lists it as sold out and on pre-order, shipping Nov 16. BLUETTI's store takes orders for the Elite 200 V2 kits now but says their 200 W panels ship from Nov 18.

Hours of full sun to refill at the panels' rated watts. Elite 200 V2 + 2 × 200 W: 2,073.6 ÷ 400 = 5.2 h. Elite 200 V2 + 200 W: 2,073.6 ÷ 200 = 10.4 h. Jackery 2000 v2: 2,042 ÷ 400 = 5.1 h. AC180 + 200 W: 1,152 ÷ 200 = 5.8 h. DELTA 3 + 220 W: 1,024 ÷ 220 = 4.7 h. AC180 + 2 × 200 W: 1,152 ÷ 400 = 2.9 h. These figures don't count charging losses, and losses are real: StorageReview's wall recharge of the Elite 200 V2 drew 2,440 Wh, which is 2,440 ÷ 2,073.6 = 18% more than the rated battery. Expect solar refills to take longer than the figures above.

What the panels in the box actually measured

OutdoorGearLab (Sam Schild, updated 2026-07-13) used each panel to charge the same 1,070 Wh Jackery 1000 for two hours on a clear day at 6,300 ft and 55–65°F, logging input every 30 minutes. Results were: EcoFlow NextGen 220W, 193 W of 220 W (88%); Jackery SolarSaga 200, 171 W of 200 W (86%); Anker SOLIX PS200, 166 W of 200 W (83%); Bluetti 200W Portable, 150 W of 200 W (75%).

Read those numbers against the panel your kit actually ships. The Bluetti panel OutdoorGearLab tested weighed 18.9 lb and folds to 23.9 × 23.9 in. That doesn't match the 17.15 lb SP200L that BLUETTI's Elite 200 V2 kit listings point to (their store addresses end in sp200l), so the 150 W result says nothing about the SP200L. Before you count on 193 W from an EcoFlow kit, check that the listing names the NextGen 220W. For Jackery, the SolarSaga 200 result applies directly. Two of them at 171 W each make 342 W, so 2,042 ÷ 342 = 6.0 h of full sun for the 2000 v2.

Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 + PS200 bifacial. OutdoorGearLab measured 890 Wh of usable energy from the 1,024 Wh station (87%). Anker lists the solar input as 11–28 V at 8.2 A and 29–60 V at 14.5 A, with a 600 W maximum. The bundled PS200 bifacial is a 48 V, 4.16 A panel, so it charges in the full-current band. A typical 18–20 V panel would fall into the 8.2 A band and top out around 8.2 × 20 = 164 W. Two PS200s in series reach 96 V, which is over the 60 V limit, so any added panels must go in parallel. Three in parallel come to 3 × 4.16 = 12.5 A, about 599 W.

Not recommended

BLUETTI AC180 + 2 × 200 W at $1,232. On BLUETTI's own store the Elite 200 V2 + 2 × 200 W costs $267 more ($1,499) for the same 400 W of panels and roughly twice the energy: 1,910 Wh measured against 950 Wh, and 2,073.6 Wh rated against 1,152 Wh. The two panels also cost more than the battery they charge: the AC180 alone is $499, so the panels add 1,232 − 499 = $733. If you want an AC180, the single-panel $769 kit is the one that makes sense.
