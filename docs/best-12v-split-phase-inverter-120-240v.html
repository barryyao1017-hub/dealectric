What the differences mean

  • Battery current at full load: 4,000 W ÷ (0.88 efficiency × 12 V) ≈ 379 A from the bank for any of the 4,000 W units. Size the cables, fuse and battery for that current.
  • Cost per continuous watt: Sun Gold Power $936 ÷ 4,000 W = $0.23/W. AIMS 240VAC-input $1,695 ÷ 4,000 = $0.42/W. AIMS 120VAC-input $1,949 ÷ 4,000 = $0.49/W. AIMS 3000W inverter-only $880 ÷ 3,000 = $0.29/W. The Victron 12/4k is $1,190 ÷ 3,400 = $0.35/W, but that buys 120 V only.
  • Bulk recharge of 320 Ah (a 400 Ah bank from 20% to 100%, before the charger tapers): 320 ÷ 120 A ≈ 2.7 h on the Sun Gold, 320 ÷ 115 A ≈ 2.8 h on the AIMS 240VAC-input unit, and 320 ÷ 70 A ≈ 4.6 h on the AIMS 120VAC-input unit.
  • Idle drain on a 12.8 V LiFePO4 bank: Sun Gold 45 W × 24 h ÷ 12.8 V ≈ 84 Ah a day, or 18 W × 24 ÷ 12.8 ≈ 34 Ah in power-saver mode. The AIMS 120VAC-input unit is listed at 50 W × 24 ÷ 12.8 ≈ 94 Ah a day, or 21 W ≈ 39 Ah in power-saver mode. Turn power saver on for any bank that sits on all day.
  • Surge headroom: the low-frequency units hold 3× their rating for 20 s (12,000 ÷ 4,000). Victron's 12/4k peak is 6,000 ÷ 3,400 ≈ 1.8× with no stated duration.

Why the AC input voltage decides which 12V split phase inverter fits

Every inverter-charger here is fixed to one charging-input voltage, and a model's name rarely tells you which. Sun Gold Power says its 4000W 12V split-phase model "requires 240VAC input and can provide 120VAC or 240VAC output power." Its authorized dealer Solaris lists the input range as 140–270 VAC or 184–253 VAC, set by switch SW2. A 120 V source sits below the 140 V minimum, so a 30 A RV pedestal or a 120 V portable generator will neither charge the battery nor pass through to the loads. Sun Gold's 4000W 12V single-phase sibling ($918) does take 120 VAC, but it outputs 120 V only.

AIMS Power covers both cases with two versions of its 4000 W 12V split-phase inverter-charger. The 240VAC-input unit has a 115 A charger and costs $1,695 at Outbound Power. The 120VAC-input unit has a 70 A charger and costs $1,949. AIMS's own page lists "120 VAC Input" for that model. Both make 120/240 V split phase and surge to 12,000 W for 20 s. Paying more for the 120VAC-input version gets you charging from a 120 V source, but its charger is 45 A smaller.

Victron goes the other way. Its MultiPlus-II 2x120V manual says that in invert mode the unit "connects both output lines (L1 and L2) together to provide 120VAC to loads on either line". Victron's product page says "240VAC is only available when the MultiPlus is supplied by a split phase 120/240VAC source." To get true split phase from Victron, its help-me-choose page points to two 120 V models configured as a split-phase pair, designed by a qualified installer.

Not recommended

A single Victron MultiPlus-II 12V 2x120V as a 240 V inverter. It keeps both 120 V halves of a 50 A RV panel running, and its 160 A charger is the largest here. But it cannot run a well pump, dryer or other 240 V load from the 12V battery. If 240 V on battery is the reason you searched, buy one of the low-frequency split-phase units above.
