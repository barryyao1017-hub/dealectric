What the differences mean

A full-size US hair dryer such as the Conair 141WRW draws 1,875 W at 120 V. That is 7.5 times the TESSAN TC03's limit (1,875 / 250 = 7.5) and 9.4 times the Ceptics PU-200C's (1,875 / 200 = 9.4). No 200–250 W travel adapter can run it.

The Ceptics VC-S2000 leaves only a small margin over that dryer: 2,000 / 1,875 = 1.07.

Dyson support costs extra: VC-S2000 vs LX-C2000 is $129.99 − $49.99 = $80.

Cost per converted watt: TC03 $43.99 / 250 W = $0.176/W. LX-C2000 dryer outlet $49.99 / 2,000 W = $0.025/W. The cheap 2000 W outlet is cheap because it is built for simple heating loads.

Which hair dryers a 2000 W outlet will run

The wattage on a travel converter does not tell you which dryer it can run. Ceptics says the 2000 W outlet on its LX-C2000 "will work with electric hair dryers that are controlled by a mechanical button or knob". It adds that it "is NOT compatible with digital temperature control and auto shut off hair dryers with electronic circuit boards such as Dyson and Shark Flexstyle products."

BESTEK draws the same line on its 2000 W EU-plug converter: hair dryers with non-DC motors only, "not available for Dyson". Culture Column explains why: many inexpensive travel converters are built for resistive heating loads and "produce a modified waveform".

The Ceptics VC-S2000 is the pure sine wave model in the line. It is also the only one whose listing tells you how to run a Dyson: with nothing else connected to the other outlets.

TESSAN's 2000W/200W combo lists hair dryers for its converter area but says nothing about digital dryers. Treat a Dyson as unsupported on it.

At the low end, BESTEK says not to run any hair dryer on its 300 W converter "regardless of their wattage". Techaeris (Alex Hernandez, 2026-10-02) lists hair dryers, flat irons, curling irons and kettles as loads that trip the TC03's protection. In the same review he ran camera battery chargers, laptop bricks and electric trimmers on the TC03 at IFA 2026 "without buzzing, whining, or emitting excessive heat". That makes it the right buy for electronics: $43.99: https://dealectric.com/go/tessan-250w-voltage-converter-travel-adapter-tc03.

Not recommended

Don't plug a 110 V-only device into the 200 W outlet of a 2000W/200W combo. Ceptics states that the LX2-M2000's "200W port DOES NOT CONVERT VOLTAGE", so the device gets the full 220–240 V from the wall. BESTEK says the same of the 200 W outlet and USB ports on its 2000 W EU unit. TESSAN describes the 200 W area of its 4-USB combo as for 100–240 V devices.

On these units, a 110 V-only device belongs only in an outlet marked as converting. If you just need to run small US electronics, a converter whose AC outlets all convert, like the TC03, avoids the mix-up.
