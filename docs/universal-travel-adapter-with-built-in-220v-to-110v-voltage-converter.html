Do you need a converter at all?

Almost certainly not for electronics. Wirecutter's travel-adapter guide (2020, republished by Engadget) says most people only need a plug adapter because modern chargers adjust to the local voltage, and TESSAN's own blog says the same. The exceptions are heating and motor appliances sold for the US market only: hair dryers, straighteners, curling irons, some shavers, CPAP machines and older gadgets with a "120 V" label. A dual-voltage hair dryer or a travel-sized one bought locally is cheaper and safer than any converter.

What 250 W does and does not run

Runs: shavers, toothbrush chargers, sound machines, small CPAPs (check the label: many are 100–240 V anyway), sewing machines, older game consoles and small fans. Does not run: anything that heats. A hair dryer is 1,200–1,875 W, a straightener 40–200 W at best but often more, a kettle 1,500 W; TESSAN warns against heating appliances on the product page, and BESTEK's and Foval's listings carry the same warning.

Why TESSAN costs more than BESTEK

Two AC outlets instead of one, two USB-C ports including Power Delivery, and the universal retractable plugs built in rather than a bag of adapter heads. If you only ever travel to one region and only need USB-A, BESTEK's $25–34 unit does the job for less.
