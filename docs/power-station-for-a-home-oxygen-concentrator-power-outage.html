What the differences mean

Each hours figure divides the energy a reviewer actually measured at the outlets by the EverFlo's 350 W average draw, as listed by Philips. Inverter losses are therefore already included.

  • BLUETTI Apex 300: 2,390 Wh ÷ 350 W = 6.8 h on its own. With two B300K packs, StorageReview measured 7,380 Wh, and 7,380 ÷ 350 = 21.1 h.
  • BLUETTI Elite 300: 2,670 Wh ÷ 350 W = 7.6 h. An 8-hour night needs 350 × 8 = 2,800 Wh, so it runs out about 130 Wh (roughly 22 minutes) early.
  • BLUETTI Apex 200 + B300K: no lab measurement is published, so this uses the usual 85%: (2,560 + 2,764.8) × 0.85 ÷ 350 = 12.9 h.
  • BLUETTI Elite 200 V2: 1,910 ÷ 350 = 5.5 h.
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: 850 ÷ 350 = 2.4 h. Notebookcheck measured the 850 Wh at a 250 W load.
  • Switchover compared with the EverFlo's tested dropout: half a cycle at 60 Hz is 1,000 ÷ 60 ÷ 2 = 8.3 ms. A 15 ms switch is therefore 15 ÷ 8.3 = 1.8 times as long, and 16 ms is 1.9 times.

Why the switchover matters as much as battery size

When the power goes out, a station plugged into the wall in UPS mode has to move the concentrator from grid power to its own inverter. Appendix A (EMC information) of the Philips EverFlo / EverFlo Q user manual lists the mains dips the unit was tested against under IEC 61000-4-11: a total (over 95%) dropout for 0.5 cycle, mains at 40% for 5 cycles, mains at 70% for 25 cycles, and a 5-second interruption. The table does not promise oxygen output at any of those levels. Philips adds: "If the user of the device required continued operation during power mains interruptions, it is recommended that the device be powered from an uninterruptible power supply or a battery."

Half a cycle is 8.3 ms. Here is how the stations compare:

  • BLUETTI Elite 300: 8 ms from grid to battery, measured by StorageReview (Dylan Bryan) with a Quarch power analyzer, and 8.9 ms measured by The Technology Man on an oscilloscope. BLUETTI rates it at 10 ms. Both measurements sit on the 8.3 ms line.
  • BLUETTI Apex 300: 16 ms on its standard outlets in 120 V mode, at both 100 W and about 700 W, measured by StorageReview (Kevin OBrien). BLUETTI rates the two left-side AC outlets at 0 ms when the unit is set to 240 V and the grid comes in through the 15 A AC input. StorageReview describes that path as 240 V mode on a 120 V input and did not measure it. BLUETTI's own article also mentions connecting to a 240 V wall outlet. Ask BLUETTI which input your home needs before you rely on the 0 ms outlets.
  • BLUETTI Apex 200 and Elite 200 V2: 15 ms (BLUETTI).
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: under 10 ms (Anker).

No lab has published a test of a home oxygen concentrator running through any of these switchovers. Test your own setup while the user is on another oxygen source: run the concentrator from the station, unplug the station from the wall, and check whether the concentrator keeps running or stops. Whichever station you choose, arrange backup oxygen cylinders with your oxygen supplier first. Anker's own medical-device guide says never to rely on a portable power station as the sole backup for life-sustaining equipment.

Not recommended

BLUETTI AC200L: on 2026-11-14, BLUETTI's US store listed it and its bundles as Discontinued, so any listing elsewhere is old stock. If you want a 2 kWh-class unit that can add a B300K, the Apex 200 can be ordered at BLUETTI's US store, which expects it to ship in mid-January.
