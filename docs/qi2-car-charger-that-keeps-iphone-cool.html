What the differences mean

MobileReviews Eh! charged a bare iPhone 17 Pro from about 15% for 30 minutes on each mount. Every mount ran from the same Apple 140 W USB-C charger, and the reviewer recorded the average watts reaching the battery and the mount's peak surface temperature. Here is what those figures work out to:

  • Share of the rated 25 W: ESR 19.1 ÷ 25 = 76%; UGREEN MagFlow (no fan) 13.5 ÷ 25 = 54%; Anker A2933 11.7 ÷ 25 = 47%.
  • Energy into the battery in half an hour: ESR 19.1 W × 0.5 h ≈ 9.6 Wh; Anker 11.7 W × 0.5 h ≈ 5.9 Wh, about 39% less.
  • What cooling bought: the fan-cooled ESR peaked at 35.3 °C. UGREEN's fanless MagFlow peaked at 42.5 °C, 7.2 °C hotter, and delivered 5.6 W less.
  • Price per watt delivered, at today's maker prices: ESR $62.99 ÷ 19.1 W = $3.30/W; UGREEN MagFlow $39.99 ÷ 13.5 W = $2.96/W; Anker $89.99 ÷ 11.7 W = $7.69/W.

Apple says a warm battery can make iOS stop charging above 80% or pause charging until the battery cools. Keeping the phone out of that state is what the fan is for. Apple also notes that a car's built-in USB ports may charge more slowly than a dedicated USB-C charger, so use the adapter in the box or a 45 W+ USB-C PD unit.

Independent tests of the cooled mounts

Only two named reviewers have published measured results for these mounts. MobileReviews Eh! (Aaron Ho, 7 November 2026) ran the bench test above. It did not state the room temperature, and it measured the mount's surface, not the inside of the phone.

Gizmodo Japan (SUMA-KIYO, 29 August 2026) tested in a real car with the A/C on low, starting an iPhone 17 Pro at 20%. On UGREEN's fan-cooled MagFlow Pro the phone reached 74% at 30 minutes and 95% at 60 minutes. On UGREEN's fanless MagFlow 25W it reached 61% and 86%. That is 13 points more at 30 minutes and 9 more at 60 minutes. Gizmodo Japan reported no temperatures, and it measured battery percent rather than watts, so its numbers can't be ranked against MobileReviews' figures.

The UGREEN MagFlow Pro has one good result and one failed one. MobileReviews Eh! tested a UGREEN TEC + fan vent mount it lists as model W778, Amazon listing B0G5XYDFST. That is the same Amazon listing Gizmodo Japan links in its MagFlow Pro review. On the bench, that mount delivered 1.4 W and was disqualified. The reviewer gives no explanation and no retest. Until another test settles it, the ESR OmniLock is the cooled mount with the stronger measured record. If you buy the $49.99 on us.ugreen.com, $39.99 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-magflow-pro-qi2-25w-car-charger-cooling, check the charging rate in the first week and give it a 45 W+ USB-C adapter, which isn't in the box.

Two more mounts from the same bench test are worth knowing. LISEN's 2E7179 came within 0.1 W of the ESR (19.0 W, 36.2 °C peak, listed at $39.95 by the reviewer when tested). Spigen's Peltier-cooled Cryomax MagFit delivered 18.8 W but ran hottest of the top three at 44.1 °C, 8.8 °C above the ESR.

Not recommended

The Anker Prime Wireless Car Charger AirCool (A2933) costs the most here at $89.99 on anker.com today. It pairs thermoelectric cooling with a fan, and Anker says it needs a 45 W input. In MobileReviews Eh!'s test it delivered the least of the nine mounts that charged: 11.7 W from 21.9 W drawn. Its mount surface peaked at 37.4 °C, above the 35 °C (95 °F) phone temperature Anker's Australian store promises. Anker also lists Tesla and Mercedes-Benz cars, and Honda honeycomb vents, as incompatible.
