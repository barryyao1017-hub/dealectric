Why car mounts throttle, in Apple's words

Apple rates iPhones for use between 0 and 35 °C ambient, says not to leave the device in a car, and states that when a device gets too warm "charging, including wireless charging, slows or stops"; since iOS 16 the phone shows Charging On Hold and resumes when it cools. Wireless charging adds its own heat on top of a sun-warmed dashboard and a running navigation app, which is why an uncooled magnetic mount in summer often sits at a trickle or pauses; a fan or thermoelectric plate pulls heat off the back of the phone. Keep the case thin or off, point an air-conditioning vent at the mount (a vent mount that blocks the vent helps less), and keep the phone out of direct sun.

What the claims are worth

ESR says CryoBoost keeps phones under 35 °C against 40 °C-plus for competitors, Anker says under 89 °F, LISEN 31–36 °C and 10–15 °C cooler than passive mounts; all are the makers' own figures, and no review from ChargerLAB, Macworld, Wired, 9to5Mac or MacRumors logs delivered watts over a drive for a cooled mount against an uncooled one. The one reviewer note is that a fan "may struggle in direct sun unless the AC blows on it". Buy a cooled mount for the mechanism, not for a number.

Getting 25 W

Qi2.2's 25 W reaches only the iPhone 17 series and iPhone 16 series on iOS 26; every older iPhone charges at 15 W. The mount's own electronics plus the cooler need a USB-C PD car adapter of about 35–45 W (UGREEN says 45 W or more for maximum cooling; ESR's OmniLock needs 35 W and ships a 40 W adapter); a 20 W adapter gives a slow charge and a warm phone whatever the mount claims.
