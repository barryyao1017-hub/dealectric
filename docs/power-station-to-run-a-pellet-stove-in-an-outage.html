What the differences mean

Each runtime here is the station's measured output divided by the stove's running draw. A measured figure already includes the station's inverter losses and its own idle draw. Elite 300: 2,650 Wh ÷ 200 W = 13.3 h. Apex 300: 2,290 ÷ 200 = 11.5 h. Elite 200 V2: 1,910 ÷ 150 = 12.7 h. DELTA 2 Max: 1,800 ÷ 150 = 12.0 h. HELIOS 3600: 3,686 × 0.85 = 3,133 Wh, and 3,133 ÷ 200 = 15.7 h. That last one is worked from the rating, not measured.

Restarts use energy too. Power Fallback, quoting the US Stove 5660 manual, gives a 346 W igniter that runs for 10–12 minutes, or 58–69 Wh per start. Three restarts use about 207 Wh, so the Elite 300 at 200 W gives (2,650 − 207) ÷ 200 = 12.2 h. Cost per delivered Wh: Elite 300, 1,199 ÷ 2,650 = $0.45. Elite 200 V2, 899 ÷ 1,910 = $0.47. DELTA 2 Max, 1,029 ÷ 1,800 = $0.57. Apex 300, 1,499 ÷ 2,290 = $0.65. The Elite 300 is the cheapest per delivered Wh and the only measured unit in the table that clears 12 h at 200 W: $1,199 on bluettipower.com, $1,139.05 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-300

Measured output versus the number on the box

A flat 85 % rule is wrong in both directions. At a constant load just over 200 W, Marcus Schwarten of Notebookcheck measured about 2,650 Wh from the Elite 300's 3,014.4 Wh, just under 88 %. At 1,800 W the figure rose to just over 92 %. Dylan Bryan of StorageReview got 2,670 Wh from a US unit. StorageReview measured 1.91 kWh from the 2,073.6 Wh Elite 200 V2 (92 %). OutdoorGearLab measured 3,790 Wh from the 4,096 Wh EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 (93 %). At the low end, StorageReview measured 2,980 Wh from the 3,840 Wh Anker SOLIX F3800 (78 %). On paper that stove night is 3,840 ÷ 200 = 19.2 h; measured, it is 2,980 ÷ 200 = 14.9 h.

Why efficiency falls at stove-sized loads: the inverter's own draw stays the same while the load gets smaller. The Technology Man estimated about 19 W with the Elite 300's AC output on and nothing plugged in, from a 10 % drop over 16 hours. Over 12 hours that is about 230 Wh. A test at 200 W already includes that draw, so don't subtract idle draw a second time. Notebookcheck tested a 230 V European unit, which it says differs slightly from the US model.

Keeping the fire going through the moment the power cuts is a separate question. StorageReview measured the Elite 300 switching to battery in 8 ms. BLUETTI rates the Elite 200 V2 at 15 ms, Growatt rates the HELIOS 3600 at within 20 ms, and EcoFlow rates the DELTA 2 Max at 30 ms. Power Fallback, quoting a Harman brochure, says Harman controls are pre-set for quick shutdown when power fails, and on touch-control stoves a dealer must select Continue Operation before a backup keeps the fire burning (knob-control boards use dip switch #4). Check your stove's power-failure setting with your dealer before you rely on pass-through.

Not recommended

Skip the BLUETTI Apex 300 for this job. It costs $300 more than the Elite 300, yet Laura Lancaster of Outdoor Life measured only 2,290 Wh from its 2,764.8 Wh (83 %) on a dehumidifier metered with a Kill-A-Watt. That is 2,290 ÷ 200 = 11.5 h, short of a 12-hour night at 200 W. Its 3,840 W output and 0 ms UPS mode suit backing up whole circuits, not a single stove.
