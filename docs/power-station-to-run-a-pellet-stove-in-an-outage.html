What a pellet stove draws, from labels and plug meters

Harman P43 label: 115 V, start 4.2 A, run 2.8 A, normal operation 1.1 A. Owners' Kill-A-Watt readings: a P43 at 390 W starting (blower, auger and igniter) and 140 W running with fans, 175 W with the auger; a P68 at 396 W at ignition, 209 W on the distribution blower and about 215 W time-weighted; an XXV at about 400 W to start and 102–170 W running; an Accentra's igniter just over 300 W then 60–120 W. A retailer's table lists the P43 at 460 W maximum and 200 W running. The igniter runs 5–15 minutes per light; a thermostat-controlled stove may relight two to four times a night, about 70 Wh each. Standby and controller draw were not published anywhere we could read. Englander, Pelpro, Castle and Quadra-Fire figures were not retrievable, so print nothing for them; the P43's label is the one maker number here.

The hookup: UPS mode, pure sine, ECO off

Plug the stove into the station and the station into the wall so the switchover (10–20 ms) carries the stove through the cut; owners on line-interactive UPS units report stoves riding through transfers, and a stove that loses power mid-burn can push smoke into the room, which is what Harman's small battery backup exists to prevent by running a clean shutdown. Turn ECO mode off so a stove idling at standby does not trip the station's auto-off. Harman advises the manual igniter setting on battery power so the stove does not cycle on and off; check your own manual for its backup guidance. Pure sine is what every maker-affiliated source recommends and every station here provides. Keep the station indoors: the R4000 is rated to discharge at −20 °C and charge at 0 °C; BLUETTI publishes no range on these pages.

What owners actually got

A P43 ran 9 hours on two 100 Ah AGM batteries and a 1,250 W pure sine inverter; an XXV ran 7.2 hours on one 100 Ah AGM at 102 W and 4.3 hours at 116 W; a P68 ran about 12 hours on two RV deep-cycle batteries with the fan at minimum; a 960 Wh Ryobi rig gave 6–7 hours of stove time per charge through a three-day outage in the teens; a Yamaha EF1000iS generator ran a stove 24 hours on a gallon of gas. None of these is a BLUETTI or ALLPOWERS run, and the UPS-only figures (65 minutes on a 1500 VA CyberPower at 100 W) show why a computer UPS is a shutdown bridge, not a night of heat.
