What the differences mean

  • Nano Charger Neo: 20 V × 3.25 A = 65 W on a 5 A cable, and 20 V × 3 A = 60 W on a 3 A cable (USB Connection Information's read of its profiles). Its measured draw into a MacBook Air M5 was 19 V × 3.15 A = 59.8 W. Anker says it lowers power after about 20 minutes at 65 W.
  • Nexode Air 45W: 20 V × 2.25 A = 45 W fixed. Its PPS ceiling is 11 V × 4.5 A = 49.5 W, so the 45 W rating is the real limit. smartzone.de saw 45 W drop to 40 W after 30 minutes: 5 ÷ 45 = 11% less.
  • Nano 30W: 20 V × 1.5 A = 30 W. Its top PPS step is 16 V × 2 A = 32 W, capped at 30 W. TechGearLab measured 29.4 W, and Android Authority lists 27 W over PPS.
  • Dollars per watt at the makers' prices: Neo 15.99 ÷ 65 = $0.25 (15.99 ÷ 45 = $0.36 once it settles); Nexode Air 45W 15.19 ÷ 45 = $0.34; Belkin 14.99 ÷ 30 = $0.50; Nano 30W 15.99 ÷ 30 = $0.53.

iPhone 18 Pro's 60 W tier: AVS decides it, not watts

Apple's support page lists two ways to charge an iPhone 18 Pro or 18 Pro Max to about 50% in about 15 minutes. One is Apple's 40W Dynamic Power Adapter with 60W Max. The other is a third-party adapter that is 60W-capable, uses Adjustable Voltage Supply (AVS) and supports USB-PD 3.2. A 30 W charger misses that line on watts whatever modes it supports. The Nexode Air 45W misses it too, even though smartzone.de reports it supports SPR AVS.

The Nano Charger Neo is the only charger here whose maker claims both. Anker's Canadian product page says it “supports AVS charging and delivers up to 60W for iPhone 18 Pro, reaching 50% in 15 minutes”. USB Connection Information read the box label as AVS 9–15 V at 4.3 A and 15–20 V at 3.25 A. There are three limits. No named reviewer has measured it on an iPhone 18 Pro. The MacBook that Connection Info tested with negotiated USB PD 3.0, so AVS did not show there. And Anker's spec sheet lists PD3.1, while Apple's main text names 3.2 (Apple's footnote for these phones accepts USB PD 3.1 or better with AVS). Check the model number when you buy: Anker's FAQ says AVS and PPS belong to the A120M, and the A121D Nano Charger (45W, Smart Display) is a different model. buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0H7C7BH83?tag=dealectric-20

For Galaxy and Pixel phones the deciding figure is the PPS ceiling. All four chargers stop at 11 V or 16 V, so none gives the 20 V PPS that the Galaxy S26 Ultra's 60 W mode needs. For the 45 W class, smartzone.de expects 40–45 W from the Nexode Air 45W on recent Samsung PPS phones. That is a reviewer's estimate, not a measurement, and the same test read 71 °C on the housing under sustained load. $15.19: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nexode-air-45w-gan-charger

Not recommended

Belkin's 30W Compact charger is not a good pick for a Galaxy or Pixel owner. Belkin's own page lists USB-C PD 3.1 but no PPS and no voltage table, so you cannot count on Samsung's PPS fast-charge mode. For an iPhone 17e or iPhone Air, which Apple says need 20 W or more, it is fine. Android owners spending about the same should buy the Nano 30W, whose maker table lists PPS to 16 V.
