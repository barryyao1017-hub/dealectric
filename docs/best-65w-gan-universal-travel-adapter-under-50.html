What 65 W buys you

A USB-C laptop that ships with a 30–67 W charger (MacBook Air, most 13–14-inch Windows ultrabooks, iPad Pro) charges at full speed from the WTA07's USB-C 3 port. A 14-inch MacBook Pro charges, more slowly than its 70–96 W brick; a 16-inch MacBook Pro (140 W) or a gaming laptop will trickle or not charge under load. TESSAN's figure for a phone is 0–80% in 40 minutes on that port alone.

The port split

Only USB-C 3 is 65 W. USB-C 1, 2 and the two USB-A ports are 15 W each, so the adapter charges six devices at once but only one of them fast. Epicka's $24.99 TA-105C also has three USB-C ports; Best Buy's listing does not state its wattage, and no result we read put it at 65 W.

Not a converter

Every adapter on this list passes the local voltage straight through. Phone, laptop and camera chargers are 100–240 V and need nothing more; a single-voltage hair dryer or straightener needs a dual-voltage replacement, not an adapter, and TESSAN's own 250 W converter cannot run one either.

Fuses, certification and returns

TESSAN lists double 10 A fuses on the WTA07; it does not list a UL or ETL mark on this product page, and we could not confirm one for any rival either. TESSAN's FAQ gives 7 days from delivery for returns, unused and in the original packaging, with a 12–24-month warranty depending on the product.
