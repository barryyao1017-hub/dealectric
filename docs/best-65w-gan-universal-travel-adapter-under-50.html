Why the cable decides whether you actually get 65W

A USB-C laptop charges at 20 V, and watts are volts times amps. 65 W at 20 V needs 3.25 A, and 70 W needs 3.5 A. A USB-C cable without an e-marker chip is limited to 3 A. Anker's support page says it directly: "To achieve output over 60W, an E-Marker cable must be used; without it, only 3A current is supported." Satechi says the same from the cable side: once a charger puts out more than 3 A, "a 5A wire with an E-Mark chip will be required." So every adapter in the table stops at 60 W on an ordinary 3 A cable, however it is labelled.

The fix costs less than a meal. Pack a cable rated 100 W or more: 100 W at 20 V is 5 A. Baseus's 100 W USB-C cable is listed at $9.99 on Baseus's store (2026-11-13) $9.99: https://dealectric.com/go/baseus-usb-c-cable-100w. Baseus's 240 W cable ($12.99, same store and date) says on its listing that it has an E-marker chip. If the cable you already own is printed 60 W or 3 A, that is where your laptop will stop.

Port choice matters as much as the cable. On both TESSANs, only USB-C3 reaches 65 W. TESSAN rates the other four ports at 15 W max each, and it states the 65 W for single-port use.
