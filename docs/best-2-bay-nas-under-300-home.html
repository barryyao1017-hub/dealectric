ARM or x86 at $240

At this price the processor decides what the box can do. The DH2300's Rockchip chip has a neural unit, so UGOS tags faces, scenes and text in the photo library (Cult of Mac found it good, if less smooth than Apple Photos) and the whole unit idles near 10 W and 31 dBA; it cannot run x86 containers, UGREEN removed Docker from the DH series, and Plex or Jellyfin play files without transcoding. TerraMaster's F2-425 spends the same money on an Intel Celeron with Docker and Quick Sync graphics, a 2.5GbE port and a 15 W chip, but its photo AI arrived only on the newer F2-425 Plus. Synology's and QNAP's ARM boxes at this price are 1GbE with 1–2 GB of RAM: backup and file sharing, not apps.

What the DH2300 does well

  • Photos: automatic backup from the UGREEN mobile app, albums by face, scene and text, shared family spaces; the AI jobs keep the drives spinning while they run.
  • Mac backups: Time Machine over SMB with Bonjour; Cult of Mac called setup a breeze after enabling Bonjour.
  • Quiet and cheap to run: NASCompares measured 31–32 dBA at idle on the low fan setting, 44–45 dBA at the maximum, and 9.5–13 W with two 4 TB drives.
  • HDMI: a 4K60 port that plays video started from the phone app (a beta feature, not a TV interface).

Drives

Two 4 TB NAS drives in RAID 1 give 4 TB and survive one failure; two 8 TB give 8 TB. The DH2300 takes up to 32 TB per bay. Prices for 4 TB NAS drives ran about $100–130 each in 2026 listings; check on the day.
