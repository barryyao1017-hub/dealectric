What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure here names the maker or reviewer it comes from. For a student, three figures decide the pick: how many added screens the laptop actually extends to, how much the screen weighs to carry, and how bright it is under library lighting.

  • Screens that work on a base Air. Each FOPO S1 panel is 14 in at 16:9, so 14 × 16/18.36 = 12.20 in wide by 14 × 9/18.36 = 6.86 in tall, or 83.7 sq in. Two panels make 167.5 sq in, but a base M1–M3 Air with the lid open extends to one of them: 83.7 sq in.
  • Brightness. ITPro measured the Dell P1425 at 486.75 cd/m². Parkablogs measured the original Duex Plus, the 2022 model before the DS, at 152 nits. That makes the Dell 486.75 ÷ 152 = 3.2 times as bright. No named reviewer has published a measurement of either Duex DS model, so their 300-nit figure is Mobile Pixels' rating.
  • Weight. The Trio 3 Pro weighs 4.13 lb (Macworld) and the Dell 1.26 lb without its stand (Dell): 4.13 ÷ 1.26 = 3.3 times the weight in a backpack.
  • Battery. Mobile Pixels rates the original Duex Max at 4.5 W. Over a 3-hour class block that is 4.5 W × 3 h = 13.5 Wh taken from the laptop through its USB-C cable.
  • Sharpness. The Trio's 2240×1400 panel has a 2,641-pixel diagonal over 14.1 in, or 187 ppi. The Dell and the FOPO S7 (1920×1200 on 14 in) both work out to 162 ppi. The Duex Max DS (1920×1080 on 14.1 in) works out to 156 ppi.

How many added screens your laptop actually runs

A screen extender works with the laptop lid open, so the number that counts is how many external displays the laptop drives with its lid open. That number is often lower than the number of screens on the box.

  • MacBook Air M1 and M2: one external display. Apple's M2 Air specs say 'One external display with up to 6K resolution at 60Hz'. Macworld gives the same one-monitor limit for plain M1 and M2 Macs.
  • MacBook Air M3: one with the lid open. Apple supports a second external display only with the lid closed, plus an external keyboard, mouse or trackpad, and power. If you open the lid with both connected, Apple says the second display goes dark or disconnects.
  • MacBook Air M4 and M5: up to two external displays alongside the built-in one (Apple), so a dual extender can extend both screens if each gets its own video path.
  • MacBook Neo: one external display (Apple). Only the USB 3 port carries DisplayPort; the second USB-C port is USB 2 with no video. Plug the extender into the USB 3 port. Apple also says devices that use only Thunderbolt are not compatible.
  • One cable, two screens, on a Mac: macOS does not support DisplayPort MST, so two screens split off one cable can at best mirror each other (CalDigit). On a Mac, a dual extender needs one video cable per screen, or a display driver.
  • Drivers: on base M1/M2 Macs and the Neo, the Trio 3 Pro needs Mobile Pixels' free display software, which Macworld calls 'rather like DisplayLink'. Mobile Pixels says to install that driver before you plug the screen into the Mac.
  • Windows laptops: count the USB-C ports that carry video. Each added screen without a driver needs one. Mobile Pixels states this for the Trio 3 Pro, and Dell's P1425 runs on DisplayPort Alt Mode only.

For a student on a base Air or a Neo, a dual extender extends no more screens than a single one does without a driver. The single Duex DS screen costs less, weighs less on the lid and runs without software. Choose the Duex Plus DS for a 13-inch laptop and the Duex Max DS for 14 inches or more (iTechGuides' fit guidance). One honest limit: the only named measurement of a Duex Plus, the original model, is 152 nits. If you study in bright rooms, the freestanding Dell measured over three times that. Mobile Pixels' store prices change with timed sales.

Not recommended

Mobile Pixels Trio 3 Pro, for students with 13-inch laptops. Mobile Pixels states a 15–16-inch laptop fit range for it. It weighs 4.13 lb (Macworld), and on a base M1–M3 Air its second screen only extends with Mobile Pixels' display software installed. At $407.99, it suits a 15- or 16-inch laptop on a desk, not a 13-inch Air carried between classes.
