Short answer. Yes, for one screen. A MacBook Air M3 supports one external display with the lid open (two only with the lid closed), so the Mobile Pixels Duex Max DS works over USB-C with no software. On a dual screen extender, 1 of 2 screens extends unless a DisplayLink-type driver runs the other.

What the differences mean

  • Extended screens without a driver. Apple's spec for the MacBook Air M3 allows one external display alongside the built-in screen. Without a driver, the number of extended screens is the smaller of the added screens and 1: a single screen gets min(1, 1) = 1, and a dual gets min(2, 1) = 1, which is 1 of 2.
  • Lid closed does not help here. Apple's second external display (up to 5K at 60 Hz) works only with the lid shut, and it needs macOS Sonoma 14.3 or later plus an external keyboard and mouse or trackpad. An extender hangs on that lid, so shutting it to unlock a second display also turns off the Air's own screen and folds the extender away.
  • Added weight. The Duex Max DS weighs 1.8 lb (Magnetic; FutureFive). The 13-inch Air weighs 2.7 lb (Apple): 1.8 ÷ 2.7 = 67% more to carry, about 4.5 lb in all.
  • Ports. The Air has two Thunderbolt / USB 4 ports that carry DisplayPort, plus MagSafe 3 (Apple). A screen that takes one USB-C cable per screen uses both ports, and the Air then charges through MagSafe.

Why a driver-free dual extender gives one screen on this Mac

A dual extender can send video to a Mac in two ways, and neither gets past the M3 Air's limit without a driver. If both panels run from one cable over DisplayPort MST, Plugable's knowledge base says that 'MST is not supported on macOS for extended displays' and 'MST-connected displays are mirrored, not extended.' If each panel takes its own USB-C cable, Apple's limit of one external display with the lid open still applies. Apple also says that a display on a hub that can only mirror should be plugged into the Mac directly.

Extra screens past the first need a USB-graphics driver, which macOS counts separately from its native display limit. For DisplayLink products, DisplayLink Manager must be installed and given Screen Recording permission, then restarted. DisplayLink says macOS supports at most 4 virtual displays of any technology, and DisplayLink screens do not show the login screen when FileVault is on. Mobile Pixels says the dock workarounds for Macs that drive only one external display bring '60Hz refresh rate caps and potential latency'.

The Trio 3 Pro uses Mobile Pixels' own macOS graphics driver. It has a separate build for macOS 14 and later, and Mobile Pixels says not to plug the Trio in before installing it. In his April 2, 2026 review, Macworld's Simon Jary writes that a standard M1, M2 or M3 Mac can set up both Trio screens without the download. He tested on a 14-inch MacBook Pro. Apple's M3 Air spec allows one external display with the lid open, so on this Air, plan on installing the driver. Jary also found that the Trio's screens can't reach full brightness on laptop power alone. A charger plugged into the Trio's USB-C port fixes this and passes up to 65 W through to the laptop. The Trio was $407.99 through our link on 2026-11-21. Mobile Pixels' prices change with timed sales: it was $367.99 on 2026-11-18.

Both hands-on reviews of the Duex Max DS ran it on a MacBook Pro, not a 13-inch Air. FutureFive's Owen McCarthy (February 19, 2024) used an M1 MacBook Pro 14-inch. Magnetic found that 'USB-C with DisplayPort Alt Mode handled both video and power with a single cable.' It is $215.99 through our link.

Not recommended

The FOPO S1, S7, S10 and S11 are sold as driver-free duals. That suits Windows laptops, but on a MacBook Air M3 with the lid open, only one of the two panels shows its own desktop. Skip them for this Mac unless one extended screen is all you need, in which case a single-screen extender costs less and weighs less. The S9G is the exception if you add a DisplayLink adapter to its HDMI input.
