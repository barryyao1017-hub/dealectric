Why a TV gets less than the label says

An inverter draws a fixed number of watts just to stay on, whatever is plugged in. With a 500 W load that overhead hardly matters. With a 50 W TV it is a quarter of the draw. Michael Barton at techtest.org measured the AC70 at 81% of rated capacity at 50 W, 91% at 250 W and 92% at 500 W. He measured the RIVER 2 Max at 71.5% at 50 W, 72.9% at 150 W and 73.6% at 500 W. Timo Altmeyer at Techreviewer.de got 946 Wh (93%) from the Elite 100 V2 at 500 W, but only about 80% at loads in the double digits. Notebookcheck's 850 Wh for the C1000 Gen 2 was measured at about 250 W, so a small TV will get less than 850 ÷ 50 hours. The techtest, Techreviewer and Notebookcheck tests used 230 V units, and US 120 V units can measure differently.

Two settings can switch the TV off. BLUETTI's AC70 manual says ECO Mode is on by default and turns AC output off when the load stays under 40 W. You can turn ECO Mode off. Gidon at The Technology Man found that the C1000 Gen 2, by default, turns its inverter off after 15 minutes under 20 W. A dimmed small TV or a set in standby can fall below either limit, so switch these modes off before an outage. buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FN7MSY4L?tag=dealectric-20
