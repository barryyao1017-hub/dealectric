What the differences mean

Apple lists ProRes 422 HQ at 220 Mb/s for 1080p at 29.97 fps. UHD has four times the pixels, so 220 × 4 = 880 Mb/s, or 110 MB/s per stream. The same scaling puts ProRes 4444 at 165 MB/s and 4444 XQ at 250 MB/s. A 10GbE line carries 1,250 MB/s, so 1,250 ÷ 110 = 11 streams is the most any single 10GbE path can do, and a 2.5GbE port (312 MB/s) manages 312 ÷ 110 = 2. Stream counts below are whole streams with no headroom for scrubbing.

  • DXP480T Plus: 1,059 ÷ 110 = 9 streams over 10GbE; Liliputing's 1,510 MB/s Thunderbolt copy gives 1,510 ÷ 110 = 13. At $899.99 that is $899.99 ÷ 1,059 = $0.85 per measured MB/s.
  • QNAP TBS-h574TX: 1,850 ÷ 110 = 16 streams from a Mac over Thunderbolt, but 1,015 ÷ 110 = 9 from Windows and 868 ÷ 110 = 7 over 10GbE. $1,799 ÷ 1,850 = $0.97 per MB/s on a Mac, $2.07 on 10GbE.
  • DXP6800 Pro: 421 ÷ 110 = 3 streams from six HDDs in RAID 5, 762 ÷ 110 = 6 from its NVMe volume. ComputerBase's 25 GB Thunderbolt copy from the HDD pool took 59 seconds, 25,000 ÷ 59 = 424 MB/s, so the cable does not lift an HDD pool. $1,099.99 ÷ 421 = $2.61 per MB/s on HDDs, $1.44 on NVMe.
  • TVS-h874T: QNAP caps Thunderbolt Networking at 20 Gb/s, or 2,500 MB/s, so 2,500 ÷ 110 = 22 is a ceiling, not a measurement; $3,089 before a 10GbE card.
  • DS1825+: 312 ÷ 110 = 2 streams per port as shipped; add the $129.99 E10G30-T1 card to reach the 10GbE line rate.

Thunderbolt and 10GbE: what the measured paths show

Every Thunderbolt NAS in the table is a network share over an IP link, not direct-attached storage. UGREEN says its NAS "remains a network storage system when connected through Thunderbolt", gives the computer no direct control of the drives, uses one computer and one NAS port with no bonding of the two ports, and needs UGOS 1.2.0.2121 or later and a certified Thunderbolt cable. QNAP caps Thunderbolt Networking on the TBS-h574TX and TVS-h874T at 20 Gb/s (2,500 MB/s raw) despite the 40 Gb/s port rating, and requires macOS 13.5 or later. So Premiere and Resolve see a network volume either way; keep media cache, previews and the Resolve project library on a local SSD.

The host matters more than the port. StorageReview's TBS-h574TX figures split 1,850 MB/s on an M1 Max Mac against 1,015 MB/s on a Windows 11 Thunderbolt 4 PC, barely above its 868 MB/s 10GbE result. Liliputing's DXP480T Plus Thunderbolt copy on a NUC 13 Extreme reached 1,510 MB/s, about 40 percent above its 1,090 MB/s 10GbE copy, with its Thunderbolt network speed tests varying from 9.6 to 22.5 Gb/s across host PCs. On a Mac, Thunderbolt pays; on Windows, buy on 10GbE and treat Thunderbolt as a bonus.

Asustor's Lockerstor Gen3 and Flashstor Gen2 carry "USB4" ports rated 40 Gb/s, but Asustor's knowledge base says they connect only external storage or another Asustor NAS: "AMD USB4 at the moment only supports external storage devices and direct connection between another Lockerstor Gen3 or Flashstor Gen2 series NAS." Their host link is 10GbE only. Liliputing also measured the DXP480T Plus at about 18 W idle and 42 W during a 100 GB read.

Not recommended

The Synology DS1825+ as shipped. Its two 2.5GbE ports give 2 streams of 4K ProRes 422 HQ each, and Synology's 2,239 MB/s figure is not tied to any network setup on its page. It becomes a sound 10GbE box only after the $129.99 E10G30-T1 card, against $899.99 for the DXP480T Plus or $1,099.99 for the DXP6800 Pro with two 10GbE ports and Thunderbolt 4 included. Likewise, do not buy an Asustor Flashstor or Lockerstor for its USB4 ports expecting a cable link to a Mac.
