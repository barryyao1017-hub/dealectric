What the differences mean

A US P1S is a small load. On 120 V, forum member holmes4 measured about 350 W while the bed heats and 100–130 W once the printer is up to temperature. The bed runs straight off line voltage, which is why, on 220–240 V, holmes4 says the heat-up draw can exceed 1,000 W, and US owners do not see that. Every unit here clears the 350 W peak: 1,800 W ÷ 350 W = 5.1× headroom, and 600 W ÷ 350 W = 1.7×.

Printing time assumes 85% of rated capacity is usable and a 130 W draw: 1,024 Wh × 0.85 ÷ 130 W ≈ 6.7 h; 1,152 Wh × 0.85 ÷ 130 W ≈ 7.5 h; 2,073.6 Wh × 0.85 ÷ 130 W ≈ 13.6 h; 288 Wh × 0.85 ÷ 130 W ≈ 1.9 h. The 85% is on the cautious side. StorageReview drew 910 of 1,024 Wh from a DELTA 3 Plus (89%), 1,030 of 1,152 Wh from an AC180 (89%) and 1,910 of 2,073.6 Wh from an Elite 200 V2 (92%).

The CyberPower's battery is a different class. CyberPower rates it at 10 min at half load, which is 500 W × 10 ÷ 60 h ≈ 83 Wh. That is about a tenth of the ≈870 Wh a 1 kWh station delivers (1,024 Wh × 0.85).

The Elite 100 V2 and the AC180 both cost $499 at BLUETTI's US store. The AC180's rated gap is twice as long (20 ms ÷ 10 ms = 2×). In exchange it holds 128 Wh more, about 0.8 h more printing (128 Wh × 0.85 ÷ 130 W ≈ 0.84 h). EcoFlow makes the same 10 ms, 1,024 Wh and 1,800 W claims for the DELTA 3 Classic at $479, $20 less. BLUETTI states 4,000+ cycles for the Elite 100 V2; EcoFlow's Classic listing gives no cycle count. On switchover time the two are equal.

Why the switchover time decides it

The P1S splits its power. A 24 V, 108 W internal supply runs everything except the heated bed (Bambu spare part FAC059, as listed by 3DJake), and the bed takes line voltage directly. When wall power drops, a power station's relay hands the load to its inverter. During that gap the controller, motors and hotend run only on the energy stored in that 108 W supply. Bambu publishes no hold-up time for the supply, and no reviewer has timed a P1S through a 20 ms transfer. A 10 ms rating is therefore margin, not proof that a 20 ms unit fails.

Here are the rated and measured times. BLUETTI rates the Elite 100 V2 at 10 ms, the Elite 30 V2 at 10 ms or less and the AC180 at 20 ms or less (AC180 manual). EcoFlow rates the DELTA 3 Classic at 10 ms. StorageReview timed the DELTA 3 Plus with a Quarch mains power analysis module and measured just over 8 ms against a rating of under 10 ms. It is the only station here with a lab-timed switchover.

For flickers, a line-interactive UPS has one more advantage. CyberPower's datasheet says its AVR 'increases the AC power and maintains a safe voltage level without switching to battery mode', so a voltage sag never causes a transfer at all. For a full drop, its 4 ms typical transfer applies.

Before relying on a station, check its setup. BLUETTI's AC180 manual says to turn Grid Enhancement mode off for UPS use, because otherwise the switch is slower. It also caps UPS power at 1,440 W and rates UPS use at 10–30 °C (50–86 °F), which rules out a cold garage. Then test it: pull the station's wall plug during a print you can afford to lose, once while the bed is heating and once mid-print.
