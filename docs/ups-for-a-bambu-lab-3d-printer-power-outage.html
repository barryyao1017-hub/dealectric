What the printer draws

Bambu's power-consumption page lists the P1S and X1C at a 1,000 W maximum on 220 V and 350 W on 110 V, averages of 105 W (PLA) and 140–150 W (ABS) over a short test print that includes warm-up, and 5–10 W standby; the A1 is 350 W at 110 V and 95–200 W average, the A1 mini 150 W. Owners on 120 V log a 300–350 W peak while the bed heats and 100–130 W printing; one second-by-second log on 220 V saw 850 W for the first minute then 30–70 W on PLA, which shows how much depends on bed temperature and plate. Power factor is about 0.45–0.63, so a "1,500 VA" UPS is a 900–1,000 W one and the printer's watts are what count. A 600 W APC tripped on an X1C's bed heat-up at 220 V; on 120 V the 350 W spike is inside every unit here except the T320.

Power-loss recovery, and why a UPS still matters

When power drops, the P1S's capacitors keep the board alive long enough to save axis positions, the G-code line, temperatures and AMS state; on power-up it offers to resume, rehomes X and Y, keeps the stored Z, reheats and returns to the spot. Bambu says it does not work during the first layer, there is almost always a visible mark, cooled PLA can detach and ABS or PC can warp, and a blob can form at the nozzle. Owner reports match: a 10-second flicker left one P1S unresponsive until resumed from the app with a line and a defect; another resumed only from Bambu Studio after reheating, with warped flat parts. A UPS turns those into non-events, and some owners argue a P-series printer barely needs one; both views are in Bambu's forum.

Using a station as the UPS

Plug the printer into the station and the station into the wall. BLUETTI quotes ≤10 ms on the Elite 30 V2, ≤15 ms on the Elite 200 V2 and 20 ms on the AC180; no owner has published a P1S on a BLUETTI switchover, and one forum member warns a slow transfer can reset a printer, so pull the wall plug on a test print first. Turn ECO off (one owner set a 17 W threshold with a 1-hour delay, which an idle printer at 5–6 W would trip) and, on the AC180P, Grid Enhancement Mode off. Pure sine is not something Bambu requires (its supplies are switching units), but every station here is pure sine anyway. EcoFlow and Bambu ran a joint promotion in December 2023 with EcoFlow stations positioned as printer UPSs, and one owner reports a RIVER 3 Plus working on a P1S.
