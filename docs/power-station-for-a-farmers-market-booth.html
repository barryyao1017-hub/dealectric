What the differences mean

Here is an example booth day, item by item:

  • Mini fridge: 100 W while the compressor runs (EcoFlow's small-cooler figure) × 4–6 compressor hours = 400–600 Wh
  • LED lights: 10–40 W × 6 h = 60–240 Wh
  • Card reader and phones: about 60 Wh
  • Blender: 1,500 W × 30 s × 10 blends = 125 Wh
  • Total: 645–1,025 Wh, so plan on 1.0 kWh
  • Reserve = measured output − 1,000 Wh. Elite 200 V2: 1,910 − 1,000 = +910 Wh. AC180: 950 − 1,000 = −50 Wh to 1,030 − 1,000 = +30 Wh. Elite 100 V2: 890 − 1,000 = −110 Wh.
  • Cost per measured Wh (BLUETTI US, 2026-11-13): Elite 200 V2 $899 / 1,910 = $0.47. AC180 $499 / 1,030 to $499 / 950 = $0.48–0.53. Elite 100 V2 $499 / 890 = $0.56.
  • Blender plus fridge start: EcoFlow says a small cooler needs 700–1,000 W as it kicks on, so the worst case is 1,500 + 1,000 = 2,500 W. Elite 200 V2: 2,600 − 2,500 = 100 W spare. AC180: 1,800 − 2,500 = −700 W. BLUETTI also caps the AC180 at 1,500 W from 86 to 104 °F, and the blender alone uses all of that. Jackery Explorer 1000 v2: 1,500 − 1,500 = 0 W spare.
  • Power Lifting does not cover motors. BLUETTI lists the AC180's 2,700 W and the Elite 200 V2's 3,900 W Power Lifting for pure resistive (heating) loads only. The AC180 manual warns that a motor or compressor 'may start at 2-4 times the rated power, which can easily overload the AC180.'
  • Carrying: the Elite 200 V2's second day of energy costs 53.4 − 36 = 17.4 lb more than the AC180.

The AC180 is the only 1 kWh-class station with a named-lab result at or above 1,000 Wh, and its second test came in 50 Wh short. A 1 kWh unit gets a full booth through six hours only on a cool day with a light fridge load. If the fridge works hard and you blend ten times, the Elite 200 V2 finishes the day with about 910 Wh left. That is most of a second market day.

Not recommended
