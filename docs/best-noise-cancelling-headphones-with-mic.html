Two kinds of noise cancelling, measured

Noise cancelling headphones with a mic deal with noise in two directions. ANC lowers the noise you hear. SoundGuys scores it from how much the headphones cut the perceived loudness of outside noise, which is how the Space 2 Pro reaches 8.8/10 (an average cut of over 88%) and the Sony and Bose reach 8.7/10. The mic array lowers the noise your callers hear. On the Space 2 Pro, 6 of its 8 microphones handle calls (SoundGuys). In SoundGuys' recordings Adam Birney 'could barely hear any background noise at all', and in wind 'my voice stays steady and clear'. Christian Thomas wrote that the Sony's mics 'do a fantastic job of handling outside noise like wind', and that on the Bose 'some incidental noise does make its way through, it's fairly well mitigated'. On the Space One, Chase Bernath found 'you can hear some background noise in our simulated environments, you can still understand what is being said'.

Where the Space 2 Pro gives ground: SoundGuys lists shorter battery life than the cheaper Space 2 and no IP rating. soundcore lists its codecs as AAC, SBC and LDAC only, so laptop calls run over standard Bluetooth. The Sony adds LC3 for LE Audio (SoundGuys), and the Jabra has its own USB adapter.

Not recommended

soundcore Space One Pro for calls. soundcore's store sells it for $199.99, the same price as the Space 2 Pro. It scores lower on noise cancelling (8.5 against 8.8) and weighs 21 g more (283 g against 262 g, both SoundGuys), and its store listing makes no call-mic claim. At the same price, the Space 2 Pro is the better buy for calls.
