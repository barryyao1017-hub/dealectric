What the differences mean

Where the line was drawn: every row cost $250 or less on 2026-11-21 at the maker's US store, and for the Vantrue and DDPAI rows that is the price through our link. Two well-known 4K front + 2K rear kits were left out for being over it on their makers' stores that day: the VIOFO A229 Pro 2CH and the ROVE R2-4K Dual Pro, each listed at $349.99 (viofo.com and rovedashcam.com). We read the makers' stores for those two, not Amazon. Dealectric does not test units; every measurement on this page carries the name of the person who took it.

  • Why the rear camera decides it. 3840 × 2160 = 8,294,400 pixels a frame; 2560 × 1440 = 3,686,400; 8,294,400 ÷ 3,686,400 = 2.25. A rear camera needs an 8 MP sensor to fill a 4K frame. RoadGearLab gives Sony's IMX678 as 8.29 MP; VIOFO and ROVE state 5 MP for the IMX675 behind their 2K rear cameras.
  • Data per pixel on the rear channel. Vantrue 4K rear: 23,800,000 ÷ (8,294,400 × 30) = 0.096 bits per pixel. VIOFO A229 Pro 2K rear: 25,000,000 ÷ (3,686,400 × 30) = 0.226 (DashCamTalk's 25 Mbps). The 4K rear spreads about the same data over 2.25 times the pixels, so its advantage is fine detail in good light, such as a plate further back. The two figures come from different testers, so treat the comparison as rough.
  • Card hours with both cameras recording. DashCamTalk lists 32 Mb/s front + 24 Mb/s rear = 56 Mb/s for the Vantrue. 56 × 3,600 ÷ 8 ÷ 1,000 = 25.2 GB an hour; 128 ÷ 25.2 = 5.1 hours on a 128 GB card and 256 ÷ 25.2 = 10.2 hours on 256 GB before the loop overwrites. Vantrue states cards from 32 GB to 1 TB.
  • Parked draw. EricSan measured about 4.2 W in the Vantrue's low-bitrate parking mode (1080p at 15 fps, screen off). 4.2 × 24 = about 101 Wh a day; 101 ÷ 12 = about 8 Ah from a 12 V battery, which is why a hardwire kit with a voltage cut-off matters.

Four details on the Vantrue from the same testers. Frame pacing: TonyM wrote, "There are 30 frames per second, but I don't think they are all equally spaced." Rear HDR: DashCamTalk's spec table lists HDR on the front only, while TonyM wrote that the rear camera supports HDR but not PlatePix, so do not buy it for rear HDR on the strength of either line. Wake time when parked: EricSan measured about 2.3 s to start recording in Quick Start collision detection and about 10 s in Power Saving mode, with 30-second clips. Heat: EricSan read 54 °C on the rear camera's back panel in a 22 °C room; DashCamTalk lists a capacitor instead of a battery and an operating range of -20 to 70 °C.

The two DDPAI rows carry DDPAI's own figures. Both are 4K on the front channel with a 1440p rear, so they answer the question only if a 4K front with a rear camera is what you mean.

Not recommended

The Miofive S1 Ultra, if the reason for buying it is 4K at 30 fps from both cameras. It is sold as 4K + 4K, but rcg530 measured its 4K rear at 25 fps against the manual's 30, and at 24 fps after a July 2024 firmware. In the same tests the rear camera took about 31 seconds to start recording on entering time-lapse parking mode, and after an impact the front started after 11 seconds and the rear after 14. Those are 2024 firmware readings. It has no row in the price table because Miofive's US store did not load on 2026-11-21, so no price was read for it that day.
