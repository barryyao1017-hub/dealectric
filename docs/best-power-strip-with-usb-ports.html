What the differences mean

Each refill time is Apple's 53.8 Wh battery for the 13-inch MacBook Air divided by the port's watts. That is a floor: charging slows near full, and some energy is lost in conversion. Apple ships the Air with a 40 W adapter, so a 20 W port gives it half of its stock charger.

  • 20 W ports (Anker 351, TESSAN PS07): 53.8 ÷ 20 = 2.7 h. On the 351 this holds only while both USB-A ports are empty, per Anker's own note.
  • Anker 12-in-1: 53.8 ÷ 35 = 1.5 h with nothing else plugged in. Once a phone goes in, the laptop port drops to 20 W: 53.8 ÷ 20 = 2.7 h.
  • TESSAN 65 W hub: 53.8 ÷ 65 = 0.83 h. Per USB-C watt it costs $29.99 ÷ 65 = $0.46, against $28.99 ÷ 20 = $1.45 for the Anker 351.
  • Anker Nano 67 W: 53.8 ÷ 67 = 0.80 h alone. With one USB-A device: 53.8 ÷ 45 = 1.2 h. With all four USB ports busy: 53.8 ÷ 30 = 1.8 h.
  • AC side: 1,250 W ÷ 120 V ≈ 10.4 A on the TESSAN PS07 and hub, against 1,875 W (15 A) on the Anker strips. A 1,500 W space heater or kettle alone is over the TESSAN limit.

How long each USB-C port takes to refill a laptop

Shared budgets set the real speed. The headline wattage is a one-port figure. Anker's port table for the Nano 67 W cuts the best USB-C port to 45 W as soon as one USB-A device is plugged in. With two USB-C devices it splits 20 W + 45 W. Anker also says USB-C 2 takes priority over USB-C 1, so plug the laptop into USB-C 2. The Prime 140 W holds 65 W + 65 W on two laptops, but Anker says it needs a 5 A e-marker cable at 60 W or above.

Some laptops won't charge at all from a 20 W port. The Anker 351 lists 5 V 3 A / 9 V 2.22 A / 12 V 1.67 A, so it tops out at 12 V. Renesas's summary of the USB PD power rules puts 20 V only in the tiers above 45 W. Lenovo's ThinkBook 16p Gen 4 guide says a source below 20 V 'Cannot provide power to the computer or charge the battery'. For a laptop like that, the 2.7 h above doesn't apply: the port won't charge it at all. The Nano 67 W lists 20 V at 3.35 A, so it can.

Collected measurements. ChargerLAB metered a Baseus PowerCombo 30 W strip, which shares 15 W across its USB ports and is rated for 200–240 V input. With all three USB ports in use it read 3.06 W, 4.67 W and 6.04 W per port, against 28.87 W from one USB-C port alone. LaptopBee timed an iPhone 15 from 0 to 50% in about 26 minutes on a 20 W Anker strip, so 20 W is enough for phones. The joule figures in the table are each maker's own claim. InspectandTest notes that strip listings don't state a UL 1449 clamping voltage (VPR), and the Anker 351 page doesn't either.

Not recommended

Anker USB Power Strip 12-in-1 (35 W). It costs $29.99, $1 more than the Anker 351, and is rated 900 J against 2,100 J. Its 35 W port drops to 20 W as soon as a second device is plugged in, the same as the 351. That second-device limit makes its 35 W figure misleading for a desk where a phone and a laptop charge together.
