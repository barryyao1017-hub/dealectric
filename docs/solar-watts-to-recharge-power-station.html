How many watts of solar does it take to recharge a 1,000 Wh power station?

Rule of thumb: 300 W of rated panel refills a 1,000 Wh station in one good day; 600 W in half a day. A portable panel delivers about 70–80% of its rated watts in real sun (Jackery's own figure; Goal Zero and Anker say the same without a number), so a 200 W panel gives about 140 W and needs seven hours of strong sun for 1,000 Wh, a 400 W panel about 3.6 hours. Two limits cap the answer: the station's maximum solar input (400 W on a Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 or DJI Power 1000, 500–650 W on a BLUETTI AC180, EcoFlow DELTA 3 or ALLPOWERS P1800, 1,000 W on a DELTA 3 Plus) and its input voltage range: a 400 W panel's open-circuit voltage is 45–48 V, fine for a 60 V input and too high for the DJI Power 1000 (30 V) or the small 300 Wh stations (24–28 V).

Short answer. One panel, one day: the ALLPOWERS SP037 400 W ($299, Voc 45.3 V, MC4 with XT60 and DC adapters included), the cheapest 400 W panel here and within the input range of every 60 V station; the EcoFlow 400W and Anker SOLIX PS400 Bifacial do the same job at $600–700. Lighter, slower: a 200–220 W panel (EcoFlow 220W Bifacial, Jackery SolarSaga 200, Anker PS200, BLUETTI SP200L) at 6.5–7 hours for 1,000 Wh, or two of them in parallel if the station's input allows. A 100 W panel takes two days.

How the hours are computed: hours = 1,000 Wh ÷ (rated W × 0.7). That is hours of strong, direct sun with the panel facing it; a day gives roughly four to six such hours in most of the US in summer and fewer in winter, and PVWatts (pvwatts.nlr.gov) gives the figure for a location. Add about 10% for the station's charging loss. The makers' own recharge-time claims are in the second table for comparison.

Matching panel to station

  • Voltage first, watts second. The panel's open-circuit voltage (Voc, cold morning, no load) must sit inside the station's input range. A 400 W panel at 45–48 V Voc fits every 60 V input above (ALLPOWERS, BLUETTI AC180, EcoFlow, Anker, Jackery) and the Yeti 1000X's 50 V, but not the DJI Power 1000 (30 V) or the 300 Wh stations with 24–28 V inputs, which take a 100–200 W panel at 21–23 V. Panels in series add their Voc; in parallel they add current, so check the amp limit too.
  • Connectors. MC4 is the common ground (ALLPOWERS, EcoFlow, BLUETTI, Anker's cables terminate in XT60); Jackery uses DC8020, Goal Zero its HPP plug, DJI a proprietary plug that needs the adapter module. Adapters exist for all of them; the ALLPOWERS SP037 ships with XT60, DC5525 and DC8020 adapters, which covers most stations in this table out of the box.
  • The makers' recharge claims assume full sun and the maximum input. BLUETTI says so in its footnote ("prime sunshine, ideal orientation and low temperature"); EcoFlow's are "internal testing". Goal Zero's ranges (6–12 h for 200 W into 1,000 Wh) match the 70% rule within a factor of two, which is the realistic spread between a clear June day and a hazy October one.
  • Weight versus speed. The two 400 W fold-outs from ALLPOWERS and EcoFlow weigh 16–18.5 kg; Anker's PS400 Bifacial does 400 W at 9.9 kg. At 200 W, 4.8–8 kg is the norm and the 19 kg Boulder 200 is a fixed-site panel.
