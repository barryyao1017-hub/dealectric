What the differences mean

Every kit here has at least 3,000 W of rated AC output, and each is sold as one listing with its panels. Dealectric does not test units. The figures come from the makers' stores and from the reviewers named below.

  • R3500 + 600 W: $1,753.93 ÷ 3,168 Wh = $0.554 per Wh; 3,168 Wh ÷ 600 W = 5.3 h of full sun.
  • R3500 + 400 W: $1,586.73 ÷ 3,168 Wh = $0.501 per Wh; 3,168 Wh ÷ 400 W = 7.9 h.
  • Moving from the 400 W to the 600 W panel: $1,753.93 − $1,586.73 = $167.20 more, and the refill drops by 7.9 − 5.3 = 2.6 h.
  • Apex 300 + 3× 200 W: $2,279.05 ÷ 2,764.8 Wh = $0.824 per Wh; 2,764.8 ÷ 600 = 4.6 h. It costs $2,279.05 − $1,753.93 = $525.12 more than the R3500 600 W kit and holds 3,168 − 2,764.8 = 403.2 Wh less. On BLUETTI's store on 18 November 2026 the kit ($2,399) cost $297 less than the same parts bought separately ($1,499 + 3× $399 = $2,696).
  • Apex 300 + 2× 200 W: $2,089.05 ÷ 2,764.8 Wh = $0.756 per Wh; 2,764.8 ÷ 400 = 6.9 h.
  • Apex 200 + 2× 200 W: $1,709.05 ÷ 2,560 Wh = $0.668 per Wh; 2,560 ÷ 400 = 6.4 h. It costs $44.88 less than the R3500 600 W kit and holds 608 Wh less.
  • Anker F3000 + 400 W: 3,072 ÷ 400 = 7.7 h. Jackery 3600 Plus + 2× 200 W: 3,584 ÷ 400 = 9.0 h.

Refill hours are arithmetic at the panels' rated output, and real output is lower. On its SP037 400 W kit listing, ALLPOWERS says one of its 100 W polycrystalline panels gives about 55–65 W. On that basis 400 W gives about 220–260 W, so refilling 3,168 Wh takes about 12–14 h (3,168 ÷ 260 = 12.2; 3,168 ÷ 220 = 14.4).

Price per Wh, refill time and what each kit can take

Why the R3500 600 W kit wins. Of the kits that ship now, it has the most battery per dollar, and its panel is 600 W, compared with 400 W in the Anker and Jackery kits. ALLPOWERS's listing rates the R3500 for up to 2,000 W of solar input, so the station has room for more panels later. In Batteryskills' test (Bernard Ryan), the R3500 charged from the wall from 0 to 100% in 2 h 15 min at a steady 1,550 W, reached a 780 W peak solar input in noon sun and started a 120 V well pump with a 3,800 W surge. ALLPOWERS rates the R3500 for over 3,500 charging cycles but gives no end capacity. That figure therefore can't be compared directly with BLUETTI's claim of 6,000 cycles to 80% for the Apex 300.

Apex 300 solar inputs. According to Notebookcheck, the Apex 300 has two XT60 solar inputs, each taking 12–60 V and up to 20 A. That is up to 1,200 W per input and 2,400 W in total without add-ons. BLUETTI's US kit page says solar input above that, up to 4,000 W, needs the separate SolarX 4K. Because of the 60 V cap, BLUETTI's own setup guide says to wire extra panels in parallel strings, not in long series strings. The same guide says the Apex 300 leaves everyday ports to add-on blocks and calls the Hub D1 the mandatory bridge for 12 V DC loads.

Anker's 3,600 W headline is a total. On Anker's F3000 page, each NEMA 5-20R household outlet gives at most 2,400 W. One appliance can draw the full 3,600 W only from the TT-30R RV outlet. Anker rates the F3000 for 4,000 cycles and gives no end capacity.

Where the line was drawn. Kits with less than 3,000 W of AC output are left out even when the battery is about 3 kWh, for example the BLUETTI Elite 300 (2,400 W) and Elite 400 (2,600 W). The R3500 kits with 100 W and 200 W panels are also left out: 3,168 Wh would take 31.7 h or 15.8 h of full sun to refill, and their listed dispatch date of Nov 9 has passed without a current state on the store page. Jackery's HomePower 3000 + 2× 200 W kit and EcoFlow's DELTA 3 Ultra + 2× 220 W kit are not in the table because neither store showed a kit price when we checked on 18 November 2026. On Jackery's store that day, its 200 W panel was listed as a pre-order shipping by Nov 28.
