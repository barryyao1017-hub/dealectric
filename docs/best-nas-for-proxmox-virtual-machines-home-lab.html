What the differences mean

  • Threads per dollar. N5 Pro: $899 ÷ 24 = $37.46 per thread. DXP6800 Pro: $1,099.99 ÷ 12 = $91.67. DXP4800 Plus: $619.99 ÷ 6 = $103.33.
  • RAM left for VMs once ZFS takes its share. Proxmox asks for 2 GB for the host plus about 1 GB per TB of used ZFS storage. With 40 TB of data in the pool, the N5 Pro has 96 − 2 − 40 = 54 GB left for guests. A 64 GB UGREEN has 64 − 2 − 40 = 22 GB, enough for five 4 GB VMs.
  • A year of idle, no hard drives. N5 Pro: 12.3 W average × 8,760 h = 108 kWh. DXP6800 Pro: up to 22.4 W × 8,760 h = up to 196 kWh. MS-01: 25–29 W × 8,760 h = 219–254 kWh.
  • N5 Pro NVMe speed. The three M.2 slots share four PCIe 4.0 lanes, split x1/x1/x2. At about 1.97 GB/s per lane, any two-drive mirror across these slots includes an x1 slot, so it writes at x1 speed, about 2 GB/s. For a fast VM pool, use an NVMe card in the x16-length slot, which ServeTheHome found is wired x4 (about 7.9 GB/s).

Without hard drives, the N5 Pro's highest idle reading is 22.4 − 15.9 = 6.5 W below the DXP6800 Pro's, even though it has twice the threads. Once hard drives go in, they dominate the reading: ServeTheHome's five 28 TB drives took the N5 Pro to 45–49 W, and Notebookcheck's four 4 TB drives took the DXP6800 Pro to 36.6–41.9 W. Those are different drives in different labs, so judge the boxes on their no-drive figures and add your own drives' rated idle on top. A 10GbE link also costs power: KitGuru measured 30.45 − 24.25 = 6.2 W more at idle on the DXP4800 Plus. Splitting the job across an MS-01 and a DXP4800 Plus draws about 25–29 + 24.25 = 49.25–53.25 W across two boxes before either does any work. By comparison, one N5 Pro drew 45–49 W with five drives. None of these labs measured power with VMs running.
