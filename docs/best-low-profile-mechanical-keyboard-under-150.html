What the differences mean

Every hour figure in the table is a maker's rating except the Flow Lite84's, which Notebookcheck measured. The makers rate under different conditions, so each figure is divided out the same way. Dealectric does not test units.

  • Workdays per charge = rated hours ÷ 8. Air75 V3 with all backlighting on: 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5 to 100 ÷ 8 = 12.5 workdays. Flow 2 84 with the backlight on: 14 ÷ 8 = 1.75 workdays.
  • Implied average draw = battery ÷ rated hours. Air75 V3: 4,000 mAh ÷ 1,200 h = 3.3 mA unlit, 4,000 ÷ 100 = 40 mA to 4,000 ÷ 60 = 67 mA backlit. K3 Ultra 8K: 2,600 ÷ 550 = 4.7 mA. K3 Max: 1,550 ÷ 73 = 21 mA. Flow Lite 2 84: 3,000 ÷ 100 = 30 mA. Flow 2 84: 3,000 ÷ 120 = 25 mA unlit, 3,000 ÷ 14 = 214 mA backlit.
  • Backlit, like for like: 60 ÷ 14 = 4.3 to 100 ÷ 14 = 7.1, so the Air75 V3 is rated for 4 to 7 times as long between charges as the Flow 2 84 with the lights on.
  • The two Keychrons: 550 ÷ 73 = 7.5 times the rated hours for $98.99 − $87.49 = $11.50 more on Keychron's store on 2026-11-21. Per rated hour that is $98.99 ÷ 550 = $0.18 against $87.49 ÷ 73 = $1.20.

Hot-swappable does not mean interchangeable. NuPhy's support page says the Air75 V3 is "compatible only with Gateron Low-profile 3.0 Switches" and its own Panda nano switches, "due to pin structure limitations", while the Air V1 and V2 take only Gateron Low-profile 2.0 and NuPhy's Cowberry, Moss, Aloe and Wisteria switches. Switches from an Air75 V2 therefore do not fit a V3. Keychron limits the K3 Max's hot-swap to the RGB hot-swappable version. Tushar Mehta at Digital Trends found the original Lofree Flow's switches can only be replaced by another switch in Kailh's Shadow range because of the low profile and the smaller-than-usual distance between the terminals.

Why the printed battery hours cannot be compared as they stand

Each maker picks its own test condition. NuPhy's 1,200 hours is an estimate with no RGB. Lofree's 100 hours for the Flow Lite 2 84 is with all lights off on Bluetooth. Keychron's listings for the K3 Max (73 hours) and K3 Ultra 8K (550 hours) give no condition at all. Only NuPhy and Lofree publish a backlight-on figure.

The backlight is what drains these boards. On NuPhy's own figures the Air75 V3 draws 12 to 20 times as much lit as unlit (40 ÷ 3.3 and 67 ÷ 3.3). On Lofree's, the Flow 2 84 draws 8.6 times as much (214 ÷ 25).

The one open measurement agrees. Julian van der Merwe at Notebookcheck tracked the Lofree Flow Lite84 (2,000 mAh) over several workdays:

  • Bluetooth, backlight off: 1% per hour, about 100 hours (2,000 mAh × 1% = 20 mA).
  • Bluetooth, maximum backlight: 6% per hour, about 16.6 hours (120 mA).
  • 2.4 GHz, maximum backlight: 10% per hour, about 10 hours (200 mA).

So the dongle at 1,000 Hz cost 10 ÷ 6 = 1.7 times the Bluetooth drain at the same brightness. A rated figure taken on Bluetooth with the lights off is the best case, not a working day on the dongle.

Not recommended

The original Lofree Flow84, for anyone who wants a dongle or long gaps between charges. It has Bluetooth and USB-C only. Lofree rates its 2,000 mAh battery at 40 hours (2,000 ÷ 40 = 50 mA, 5 workdays). RTINGS words its wireless performance "Exceptionally poor". The Flow Lite 2 84 and Flow 2 84 both add 2.4 GHz and a 3,000 mAh battery.

The Keychron K3 Max at its "from" price. On Keychron's store on 2026-11-21 every $72.49 and $79.99 version was sold out. The versions in stock (Black, RGB, hot-swappable, Red or Brown switches) were $87.49, which is $11.50 below the K3 Ultra 8K and its 2,600 mAh battery.
