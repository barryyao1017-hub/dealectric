What the differences mean

RAM headroom against Immich's 8 GB recommendation. Immich asks for at least 6 GB and recommends 8 GB. With only 4 GB it runs only if machine learning is turned off.

  • DXP4800 Plus: 8 − 8 = 0 GB as shipped, and 64 − 8 = +56 GB of room at its maximum.
  • DXP2800: 8 − 8 = 0 GB as shipped, and 16 − 8 = +8 GB at its maximum.
  • TS-464-8G: 8 − 8 = 0 GB as shipped, and 16 − 8 = +8 GB at its maximum.
  • DS925+: 4 − 8 = −4 GB as shipped, so it needs a memory upgrade before the first sync. 32 − 8 = +24 GB once upgraded.

Where the database goes. Immich says its Postgres database should sit on local SSD storage, never a network share. On the two UGREEN boxes the M.2 slots can be used for extra storage, so an NVMe drive in them can hold the database. QNAP's two slots each run on a single PCIe Gen 3 lane. On the DS925+, an M.2 storage pool needs a Synology-verified SSD, so an ordinary NVMe drive won't do for the database there.

Why iPhone video decides the box

The trigger. Apple's iPhone User Guide says the iPhone 'records video in Dolby Vision HDR' on supported models. You turn this off at Settings > Camera > Record Video. Immich's default transcode policy is 'required'. Under it, Immich re-encodes a video when the video is HDR, when it isn't in yuv420p, or when its codec isn't on the accepted list. The default output is h264 at 720p. So with default settings on both sides, nearly every clip your iPhone uploads gets a second, transcoded copy.

Who does the work. Immich can hand encoding to Intel Quick Sync. To do that, pass /dev/dri into the immich-server container and choose QSV. Immich's docs add that 'by default, only encoding is currently hardware accelerated': the CPU still does the decoding and tone-mapping. Turn on hardware decoding (accelDecode) to move decoding off the CPU too.

Chip by chip. The Pentium Gold 8505 (DXP4800 Plus) and N100 (DXP2800) are Alder Lake parts with Quick Sync, both newer than Immich's note that 11th-gen-or-older Intel chips may need Low-Power mode. The N5095 in the TS-464-8G is Jasper Lake, and Immich says Jasper Lake needs Constant quality mode set to CQP. The DS925+'s Ryzen V1500B has no hardware transcoding (Android Central), so its four cores do all of this work alongside face recognition and thumbnails.

The iPhone side, on any box. Immich's mobile-backup guide says Background App Refresh must be on and that iOS, not the app, decides when the background upload runs. Open the app during the first big upload. Photos stored only in iCloud are first downloaded to a temporary cache on the phone, which uses extra data and storage.

Remote access. Immich's remote-access guide lists a VPN (WireGuard or OpenVPN), Tailscale (no router port needed) or a reverse proxy with HTTPS. It warns: 'Never forward port 2283 directly to the internet without additional configuration.'

Not recommended

Synology DS925+ for a library full of iPhone video. It has no hardware transcoding, so every HDR clip is re-encoded on the CPU. It ships 4 GB short of Immich's 8 GB recommendation. And it won't build a database pool on an ordinary NVMe drive. DSM's backup tools are good, but for Immich the Intel boxes above do this work with less setup. If you keep the DS925+, upgrade the RAM, and either turn off HDR Video on the iPhone or change Immich's transcode policy.
