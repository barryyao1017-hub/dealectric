What the differences mean

  • 8,000 Hz against 1,000 Hz. 1 s / 8,000 = 0.125 ms between reports; 1 s / 1,000 = 1 ms. The longest a report can wait shrinks by 1 − 0.125 = 0.875 ms, which is 21% of one frame on a 240 Hz monitor (1,000 / 240 = 4.167 ms; 0.875 / 4.167 = 0.21).
  • The makers' latency numbers. The 0.125 ms printed for the NS68, HE68 Lite and HE80 is the 8,000 Hz interval restated. Epomaker's own HE80 wording shows it: "8x quicker than conventional 1k polling keyboard" is 1 ms / 8 = 0.125 ms. MCHOSE's 0.1 ms and Gamakay's 0.06 ms for the NS75 are shorter than one interval, so neither can be the time from key press to PC. The measured figure in the table, HLPlanet's 0.60 ms by cable for the TK75 TMR, is 0.60 / 0.125 = 4.8 intervals.
  • Scan rate. The NS75's 16K rating is 1 / 16,000 = 0.0625 ms per scan; the HE68 Lite's 128K per-key rating is 1 / 128,000 = 0.0078 ms. The gap is 0.0625 − 0.0078 = 0.055 ms, or 1.3% of a 240 Hz frame (0.055 / 4.167). The NS68 (32,000 Hz, GamePadSquire) and the HE80 (32,000, Epomaker) sit at 1 / 32,000 = 0.031 ms. These are ratings, and too small to rank the boards.
  • Going wireless. HLPlanet measured the TK75 TMR at 0.75 ms on its 2.4 GHz dongle against 0.60 ms by cable: 0.75 − 0.60 = 0.15 ms. A board that falls to 1,000 Hz on wireless can add up to the 0.875 ms above.
  • Rapid trigger step. The HE80's 0.02 mm is twice the 0.01 mm of the NS68, Ace 60 Pro and HE68 Lite, and four times the NS75's 0.005 mm (0.02 / 0.005 = 4). All are maker ratings.

Why 8,000 Hz needs a USB High-Speed link

USB schedules traffic in fixed slots. Microsoft's USB driver documentation gives the two sizes: on a Full-Speed link the bus interval is a frame of 1 millisecond, and on a High-Speed link it is a microframe of 125 microseconds. 1 s / 1 ms = 1,000 slots a second; 1 s / 125 µs = 8,000. An 8K keyboard therefore has to connect as a High-Speed device. Wooting says the same of its own boards: USB Hi-Speed "is required for our keyboards to support the 8000 polling rate", and choosing 1,000 Hz "changes the USB standard to USB Full-Speed".

This is why the 8K figure belongs to the cable on every board in the table, and why what sits between keyboard and PC matters:

  • Plug into the motherboard. Corsair's guidance for its high-polling devices is that "hubs, docks, and 3rd party chipsets may not be able to provide enough stability" and that the port should be "directly linked to the motherboard chipset".
  • If the keyboard is not detected at start-up. Wooting notes that "some motherboards' USB controllers may have compatibility issues" at 8,000 Hz. Its fixes are another USB port or dropping to 1,000 Hz.
  • Check the processor. Corsair sets a floor of an Intel Core i7 9th generation or AMD Ryzen 7 2nd generation (or equivalent) for 8,000 Hz on its devices.
  • Read the wireless rate per mode. A 2.4 GHz mode keeps 8K only where the maker or a reviewer says so: the FUN60 Ultra (MonsGeek) and the TK75 TMR (HLPlanet's spec table) do; the NS68 tri-mode runs at 1,000 Hz wireless (GamePadSquire); Bluetooth on the TK75 TMR is 1,000 Hz.

Wooting and Corsair wrote these notes about their own keyboards. The slot arithmetic is the same for any USB device, and none of the makers in the table publishes its own port or hub guidance on the pages read.
