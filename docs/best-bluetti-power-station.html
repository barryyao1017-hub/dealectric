What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Each energy figure here was measured by the named tester. Prices come from BLUETTI's US store on 2026-11-18. The per-Wh and per-pound figures are our arithmetic:

  • Elite 300: $1,199 ÷ 2,670 Wh = $0.45 per usable Wh. 2,670 Wh ÷ 57.98 lb = 46.0 Wh/lb.
  • Elite 200 V2: $899 ÷ 1,910 Wh = $0.47. 1,910 ÷ 53.4 lb = 35.8. On APH Networks' 2,028 Wh: $899 ÷ 2,028 = $0.44 and 2,028 ÷ 53.4 = 38.0.
  • Stepping up from the Elite 200 V2 to the Elite 300 in StorageReview's tests costs $300 more and gets 760 Wh more (2,670 − 1,910). That works out to $0.39 per extra Wh for 4.6 lb more weight.
  • Elite 100 V2: $499 ÷ 890 Wh = $0.56. 890 ÷ 25.0 lb = 35.6.
  • Elite 100 mini: $429 ÷ 903 Wh = $0.48. 903 ÷ 23.6 lb = 38.3. Compared with the Elite 100 V2, it costs $70 less, gives 13 Wh more and weighs 1.4 lb less. Its AC output is 700 W against 1,800 W.
  • AC180: $549 ÷ 950 Wh = $0.58. 950 ÷ 36 lb = 26.4. It costs $50 more than the Elite 100 V2 (it was $499 until mid-November) and gives 60 Wh more (+7%), but weighs 11 lb more (+44%).
  • Apex 200: $1,149 ÷ 2,304 Wh = $0.50. 2,304 ÷ 60 lb = 38.4.
  • Pioneer Na: no named tester has published a usable-Wh figure, so these use its 900 Wh rating. $700 ÷ 900 Wh = $0.78 per rated Wh. 900 ÷ 35 lb = 25.7 rated Wh/lb.
  • Elite 30 V2: $299.99 ÷ 231 Wh = $1.30. 231 ÷ 9.48 lb (4.3 kg) = 24.4.
  • Kits: $489.99 ÷ 231 Wh = $2.12 (Elite 30 V2 + 100 W). $1,199 ÷ 1,910 Wh = $0.63 (Elite 200 V2 + 200 W).

The testers used different loads, and some didn't state the load. Compare within one lab: the Elite 100 V2 and AC180 were both tested by Kenji Mowrey at OutdoorGearLab, and the Elite 200 V2, Elite 300 and Apex 300 were all tested by StorageReview. The Elite 100 mini's figure comes from the reviewer There's A Trick For That, as reported by The Cool Down.

How much energy each BLUETTI actually delivers

No station gives out its full rating. Testers measured these shares: Elite 300 89% (2,670 of 3,014 Wh), Elite 200 V2 92% (StorageReview) or 97.8% (APH Networks, steady 987 W heater), Elite 100 mini 90% (903 of 1,004.8 Wh, There's A Trick For That), Elite 100 V2 87%, Apex 300 86%, AC180 82%, and Elite 30 V2 80% on AC and 85% on DC. The Solar Lab gives efficiency percentages for the Elite 100 mini (97%) and the Pioneer Na (86%) but no usable Wh, so we don't use them in the arithmetic. StorageReview also logged how much energy each unit drew from the wall to refill. Our arithmetic gives these round-trip figures, which StorageReview did not publish: Elite 200 V2 1.91 ÷ 2.44 kWh = 78%, Elite 300 2.67 ÷ 3.49 = 77%, and Apex 300 2.39 ÷ 3.30 = 72%.

The big surge numbers on BLUETTI pages are Power Lifting figures. That mode runs resistive loads such as kettles and heaters at reduced voltage. It is not a rating for starting motors. StorageReview says this of the Elite 300's 4,800 W. BLUETTI's Elite 200 V2 page limits its 3,900 W to pure resistive loads. APH Networks ran two hair dryers drawing 3,140 W on the Elite 200 V2, and it shut down after about 5 seconds. A 450 W air duster's start of about 2,300 W overloaded the Elite 30 V2 (The Technology Man). Size fridges and pumps on the rated AC output.

UPS switchover times also differ:

  • Elite 300: 8 ms, measured by StorageReview.
  • Elite 30 V2: under 10 ms, measured on an oscilloscope by The Technology Man.
  • Elite 100 V2: 10 ms, BLUETTI's figure.
  • Elite 200 V2: 15 ms, BLUETTI's claim. StorageReview's running servers did not reboot.
  • Apex 300: 16 ms measured in 120 V mode. Its 0 ms figure applies only to two front outlets in 240 V mode on a 120 V input (StorageReview).
  • AC180: advertises 20 ms. OutdoorGearLab would hesitate to use it as a UPS for sensitive electronics.

BLUETTI quotes 4.5 W standby for the Elite 30 V2. With AC on and Eco off, The Technology Man saw it drop from 100% to 19% in 12 hours with nothing plugged in, about 19 W.

Where other brands do better: OutdoorGearLab measured the same 890 Wh from the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 (25.2 lb) as from the Elite 100 V2, but the Anker charged at 1,097 Wh/hr against 512. The EcoFlow DELTA 3 charged at 1,229 Wh/hr. The Jackery Explorer 2000 v2 gave 1,710 Wh at 38.9 lb, which is 44.0 usable Wh/lb. That is close to the Elite 300 and 14.5 lb lighter than the Elite 200 V2.

Other models on BLUETTI's US store:

  • Apex 200 ($1,099): no named usable-energy test to cite, so it isn't ranked here.
