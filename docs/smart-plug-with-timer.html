What the differences mean

  • Price per plug: the Tapo P125 2-pack is $21.99 ÷ 2 = $11.00 a plug, against $11.99 for one P125M. You save about $1 a plug and give up Matter, but keep Apple HomeKit.
  • The '15 A' headline covers lamps and heaters, not motors: 1,800 W ÷ 120 V = 15 A for resistive loads. Motors are rated separately. 1 HP = 746 W, so 1/6 HP ≈ 746 ÷ 6 ≈ 124 W of motor output on the P125M, P125 and EP10. The EP40's 1/2 HP is 746 ÷ 2 = 373 W, three times as much (1/2 ÷ 1/6 = 3).
  • 32 rules per plug: if every on and every off is its own rule, 32 ÷ 2 = 16 on/off windows per plug. That is plenty for a lamp, tight for a plug you want to switch many times a day.
  • When features overlap: TP-Link's order is Timer > Schedule > Away Mode, so a countdown you start overrides that day's schedule.

What a timer plug does when the internet or the power goes out

TP-Link's FAQ on schedules, timers and Away Mode covers the EP10, EP40, Tapo P125 and Tapo P125M. It says these features do work without internet access, because the plug stores and runs them itself. Turning your router off won't stop a lamp schedule.

A power cut is different. In the same FAQ, TP-Link says that after an outage the devices must reconnect to the internet to resync their time, and until then schedules, timers and Away Mode stop working. On TP-Link's community forum, TP-Link staff member Wayne-TP put it plainly: "if the smart plug experiences a power cut, the schedule will not continue until the smart plug reconnects to the internet to synchronize device time." At home, the router usually comes back within minutes and the schedule picks up again. In a cabin, or anywhere the internet stays down after the power returns, the plug keeps no time at all. For an aquarium light or a pump there, choose the plug's power-on Default State so it powers up in the state you need. Wayne-TP also suggests adding repeat OFF schedules so a missed one is caught later.

Multi-device automations work differently from single-plug timers. A Kasa Smart Action, which is how a Kasa EP10 or EP40 turns itself off after each switch-on, needs the devices connected to the internet. Tapo's plug FAQ says Smart Actions aren't available without a hub. That is why built-in Auto-Off counts for more than it seems: on every Tapo plug it is a switch in the plug's Device Settings, not an automation. If you add the P125M to Apple Home through Matter, Tapo's Matter FAQ says Apple Home needs a home hub such as an Apple TV for remote control and automations. The Tapo app runs the plug's own schedules with no hub.

Not recommended

Kasa EP10 for the 'turn off after every use' job. TP-Link's list of plugs with the built-in Auto-Off Timer covers all Tapo plugs plus the Kasa EP40M, KP125M and EP25 V2. The EP10 isn't on it. On the EP10, that job takes a Smart Action, which stops working when the internet does. Fixed daily schedules and one-off countdowns still work on the EP10. Also not for most readers: the Minoston MP22Z outdoor plug ($26.35, maker, 2026-11-10) has schedules and a countdown timer and is IP65-rated, but it needs a Z-Wave hub to set them up.
