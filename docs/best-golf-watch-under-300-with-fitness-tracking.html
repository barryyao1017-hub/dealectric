What the differences mean

Rounds per charge divide each watch's GPS hours by a 4.5-hour round, the low end of Panxis's 4.5–5.5 h estimate for a busy weekend 18 holes:

  • Amazfit Balance 2: 33 h ÷ 4.5 h = 7.3 rounds.
  • Garmin vívoactive 5 and 6: 17 h ÷ 4.5 h = 3.8 rounds with all satellite systems on; 21 h ÷ 4.5 h = 4.7 rounds on GPS only.
  • Garmin Approach S44: 15 h ÷ 4.5 h = 3.3 rounds.
  • Balance 2 against vívoactive: 7.3 ÷ 3.8 = 1.9 times the rounds per charge.
  • Price: $299.99 − $169.99 = $130 more for the Balance 2 or vívoactive 6 than for the vívoactive 5 today.

Days between charges assume two rounds a week (9 h of GPS) plus everyday wear: 7 ÷ (9 ÷ GPS hours + 7 ÷ smartwatch days). For the Balance 2 that is 7 ÷ (9/33 + 7/21) = 11.6 days. For the vívoactive 5 and 6 it is 7 ÷ (9/17 + 7/11) = 6.0 days. For the Approach S44 it is 7 ÷ (9/15 + 7/10) = 5.4 days. These figures run slightly low, because the golf hours are also counted inside the 7 days of everyday wear.

Rounds per charge: why the smartwatch-days figure misleads golfers

The headline battery number on a box (21 days for the Balance 2, 11 for the vívoactive 5 and 6, 10 for the Approach S44) counts days with GPS off. A round keeps GPS on for four to five hours, and GPS is what drains the battery. Rank the watches by GPS hours and the order changes. Garmin's golf-only S44 is rated for less course time (15 h) than Garmin's own fitness watches (17 h with all satellite systems, 21 h GPS-only). The Balance 2 ran for about 33 h of full-accuracy GPS in the5krunner's testing, and the reviewer got two to three weeks of daily use from it.

The measurements support this. OutdoorGearLab measured 20 h of GPS-only recording on the vívoactive 6, against Garmin's 21 h rating, and timed a full recharge at 118 minutes. Keep in mind that the Balance 2 figure is a reviewer's result while Garmin's are lab ratings. With 5.5-hour rounds every figure drops by about 18%: the Balance 2 gets 6.0 rounds, the vívoactive 5 and 6 get 3.1, and the S44 gets 2.7.

Battery is not the whole decision. the5krunner says the Balance 2's heart rate 'might fluctuate more than I'd hope', while OutdoorGearLab measured the vívoactive 6 within 1.0 BPM median of a Polar armband. On golf, Amazfit's blog quotes MyGolfSpy's tester putting the Balance 2 'within 2–3 yards of a rangefinder', with hazards and a driver-range arc on the map. If you play twice a week and want one watch for the course and the gym, the Balance 2 needs charging about every 12 days instead of every 6: $299.99: https://dealectric.com/go/amazfit-balance-2.

Not recommended

The Garmin Approach S44 costs $299.99 but has no wrist heart-rate sensor or sleep tracking. In Garmin's launch release, those features belong only to the Approach S50, which costs $399.99 and is over budget. The S44 also gets the fewest rounds per charge here (3.3), and Enhanced CourseView and green contours need a Garmin Golf membership ($9.99 a month or $99.99 a year). As a golf watch that also tracks fitness, it loses to the vívoactive 5, which costs $130 less.
