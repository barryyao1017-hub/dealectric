What to know: Starlink's router blocks every inbound port and hands out 100.64.0.0/10 addresses, a public IPv4 needs a Priority plan, and its IPv6 is inbound-blocked too, so DDNS and port forwarding are off the table on any NAS. Relays (QuickConnect, UGREENlink, myQNAPcloud Link, EZ-Connect) try a direct path first and fall back to the vendor's servers, which are bandwidth-limited (QNAP quotes 1–2 Mbps at peak; Synology and UGREEN publish no figure) and fine for photos and documents, poor for 4K video. What CGNAT actually blocks

Starlink residential routers do not support port forwarding, block all inbound ports and give the dish a 100.64.0.0/10 address shared with other customers; a public IPv4 is sold only with Local or Global Priority plans (no standalone price published). IPv6 is delegated, but the Starlink router has no inbound firewall settings, so a direct IPv6 path to the NAS needs your own router, which rules it out for most homes. T-Mobile 5G Home Internet behaves the same way. That leaves two kinds of answer: the vendor's relay, which the NAS dials out to, or an overlay network like Tailscale, which punches through NAT with UDP and falls back to its DERP relays while keeping the payload encrypted end to end.

Relay or Tailscale

A relay is zero-setup and gives the family the vendor's own apps, at the cost of a login page that is reachable from the internet (turn on 2FA and auto-block: DSM 7.4 enforces 2FA per user and blocks DSM, SSH, SMB, FTP and app logins after failed attempts; UGOS Pro offers OTP-app 2FA; neither lists passkeys) and of the vendor's bandwidth cap. Tailscale puts nothing on the public internet, runs at line speed when the hole-punch succeeds, and needs its app on each phone and a Compose project on the NAS (on UGOS: Docker app, official tailscale image, auth key). UGREEN's third-party guide recommends Tailscale over UGREENlink for anything confidential, because the relay traffic passes through UGREEN's infrastructure. Cloudflare Tunnel is not a fit for photo and video libraries under Cloudflare's terms.
