What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. The measured figures below come from Outdoor Life (Laura Lancaster), The Solar Lab and Bob Vila (Mark Wolfe). The rest are OUPES's and BLUETTI's own US listings, read on 2026-11-15.

  • Mega 1 maximum capacity: 1,024 Wh + 2 × 2,048 Wh (B2) = 5,120 Wh, which matches OUPES's 'Expandable Up to 5.12kWh with B2*2'.
  • Mega 1 '36-minute' charge: OUPES quotes it with '2200W Max. Input'. That is 1,400 W AC + 800 W solar = 2,200 W (both limits from the manual), so on the wall alone it takes longer. Outdoor Life measured 43 min, and Bob Vila measured 'less than 1.5 hours'.
  • Mega 1 delivered energy, Outdoor Life: 610 Wh ÷ 1,024 Wh = 60% at a steady 34 W load. At 380 W it gave 780 Wh ÷ 1,024 Wh = 76% on the first run and 940 Wh ÷ 1,024 Wh = 92% on a rerun after the storage test.
  • Mega 1 at near-full load, The Solar Lab: 1,024 Wh ÷ 1,779 W × 60 = 34.5 min ideal. It ran 27 min, and 27 ÷ 34.5 = 78% (the site rounds to 79%).
  • Exodus 1200 at full load, The Solar Lab: 992 Wh ÷ 1,198 W × 60 = 49.7 min ideal. It ran 38.5 min, and 38.5 ÷ 49.7 = 77%.
  • Energy per pound: Exodus 700 576 ÷ 13.9 = 41.4 Wh/lb. Mega 1 1,024 ÷ 27.8 = 36.8 Wh/lb. Exodus 350 288 ÷ 9.15 = 31.5 Wh/lb.

Which OUPES add-ons fit which station

OUPES sells its stations in two families, and its add-ons only fit one of them.

  • B2 Extra Battery (2,048 Wh, $578 at oupes.com): OUPES lists it as 'Compatible with the Mega 1/2/3 & Titan 3 Power Station'. The limits are 2 on the Mega 1 (5.12 kWh), 4 on the Mega 2 (10.24 kWh) and 6 on the Mega 3 (15.36 kWh). The Mega 2 and Mega 3 listings were marked '(Sold Out)' on 2026-11-15.
  • Mega 1 Lite: its listing has no 'Expandable' line, and the B2 list does not name it. Treat it as a fixed 1,024 Wh unit.
  • 800 W Alternator Charger ($250 at oupes.com): 'Made for Mega & Guardian Series. Not for Exodus Series or other Brands.' An Exodus charges from a car only through its 12 V socket.
  • Exodus 350, 700 and 1200: none of them appears on the B2 or B5 battery lists, so each stays the size you buy.

The Mega 1's weak points, measured. Outdoor Life measured its AC port and inverter drawing 50 W on their own, against 10 W for the EcoFlow DELTA 2 and 25 W for the Anker SOLIX C1000. That is why it delivered only 60% at a 34 W load, so run small overnight loads like a router or CPAP from its DC or USB ports. The Solar Lab found it 'pulls voltage' during surges rather than exceeding 2,000 W, and called the fans 'very, very loud'. Bob Vila also found the fan louder than other stations it has tested. Its UPS switchover is 20 ms (OUPES manual), against 10 ms on the BLUETTI Elite 100 V2. On the plus side, Outdoor Life measured 90% of rated capacity after three months in storage, and Bob Vila ran a refrigerator and a microwave from it.

Where to buy the Mega 1. On 2026-11-15 Amazon listed the Mega 1 below OUPES's own store price, and at the same price as the Mega 1 Lite. At Amazon, the Mega 1 with its 13 outputs and B2 expansion is the better buy of the two: buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DG8JQNS4?tag=dealectric-20. Check that the listing title says 'Mega 1' and not 'Mega 1 Lite'.

Not recommended

OUPES Exodus 1200. OUPES lists it at $549 with '(Sold Out)' in the title. In The Solar Lab's test, its '1,500 W boost' gave about 1,300 W for about 5 minutes. It never charged above 400 W from the wall, took over 4 hours to fill, drained about 16.5 Wh an hour sitting idle, and shut off at 6–7% remaining. It also takes neither the B2 battery nor the alternator charger.
