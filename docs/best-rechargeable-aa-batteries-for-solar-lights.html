Best rechargeable AA batteries for solar lights (2026)

Updated 2026-11-05

Short answer. For outdoor solar lights, replace the cells with 1.2 V NiMH AA in the 1,000–1,300 mAh range: our pick is Tenergy Solla 1000 mAh ($16.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/tenergy-solla-aa-1000mah-12-pack), rated 2,000 cycles (EBL's solar AA: 1,200) and shipped charged. It is a narrow win: EBL's 1,300 mAh 12-pack costs less on Amazon. Bigger is not better: a small path-light panel may put only about 150 mAh into a cell on a sunny day, so 2,000 mAh cells can sit half-empty.

The light, not the battery, usually sets the runtime: a cell only gives back what the panel put in that day, and in winter every NiMH gives shorter nights (eneloop is rated to -4 °F with reduced runtime). Lights built for 3.2 V LiFePO4 14500 cells cannot take any AA.

Why new batteries still don't last the night

  • The panel is the limit. One measured path light charged at about 20 mA, about 150 mAh on a sunny day; its owner's 600 mAh replacements never filled (All About Circuits forum). A bigger cell helps only if the panel can fill it, or if it banks several sunny days.
  • Dead cells in the pack show up in reviews of every brand here. Charge new cells once in a wall charger and swap a weak one before blaming the light.
  • NiCd lights: NiMH 1.2 V replaces NiCd 1.2 V; keep NiCd only where the light's manual asks for it.
  • Replace every 1–2 years. Cheap solar lights have no overcharge protection, so cells in full sun age fastest.

When a bigger cell is worth it

Lights with larger panels in all-day sun can fill 1,300 mAh (Tenergy Solla Premium). If you top cells up on a wall charger, eneloop's 2,000 mAh cells are the best AA made, at about three times the price per cell.
