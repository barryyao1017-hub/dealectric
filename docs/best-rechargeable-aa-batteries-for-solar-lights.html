What the differences mean

  • Daily harvest. Sunco lists its path light at 2 V 100 mA, with a 600 mAh NiMH AA that charges in 6–8 h of sun: 100 mA × 6 h = 600 mAh per sunny day.
  • Nightly draw. The same light runs 8–10 h on that 600 mAh: 600 ÷ 10 = 60 mA to 600 ÷ 8 = 75 mA. One night uses about one sunny day's harvest.
  • Refill time. 1,000 ÷ 600 = 1.7 days; 1,300 ÷ 600 = 2.2 days; 1,900 ÷ 600 = 3.2 days. These figures ignore NiMH charging losses, and winter days give fewer sun hours, so real refill times are longer.
  • Cost per cell (Tenergy store, 2026-11-17). Solla 1000 12-pack: $16.99 ÷ 12 = $1.42. Solla 1000 24-pack: $25.99 ÷ 24 = $1.08, which is 24% less. Solla Premium 1300 20-pack: $27.49 ÷ 20 = $1.37. How much capacity a solar light can actually refill

A solar light's battery only stores what its panel puts in during the day. The LED draws the same current whatever size of cell you fit, so a bigger battery does not make the light brighter. It also adds runtime only if the panel can fill it. Capacity above one day's harvest acts as a buffer for cloudy nights. On a typical 100 mA path-light panel, a 1,000 mAh cell refills in under two sunny days. A 2,000 mAh cell needs more than three, so through short winter days it mostly sits partly charged.

Some lights have much weaker panels. A user on the All About Circuits forum (OldNewb) measured only 20 mA of charge current in one garden light that came with a 150 mAh 2/3 AA cell. Forum member KeithWalker estimated that such a panel puts in only about 150 mAh on a good sunny day, and OldNewb found that 600 mAh replacements 'don't even charge up enough to even turn on at dark'. At 20 mA, a 1,000 mAh cell needs 50 hours of full sun to fill (1,000 ÷ 20). The rule that works: read the capacity printed on your light's original cell, or the battery line in its manual. Replace it with 1.2 V NiMH of the same capacity, or one step up. Costco's GTX manual, for example, says 'Only use AA 1.2V 1000mAh NiMh rechargeable batteries' and bans alkaline, carbon-zinc and NiCd. The manual also says to charge new cells for about 12 hours of sun before first use.

Not recommended

1.5 V lithium rechargeable AAs, such as the XTAR 4,150 mWh. Inside is a 3.6 V Li-ion cell, and XTAR states that 'the charging voltage range is 4.75-5.5V'. Tom's Circuits took apart a single-cell garden light and measured its solar cell at about 2 V open-circuit in bright sun. The YX8018 driver chip used in many of these lights is specified for 'one 1.2V rechargeable battery'. The light's panel therefore can't reach the voltage this cell needs, so it would run down and stay flat. Also avoid alkaline cells, and lights that came with 3.2 V LiFePO4 cells: they need a LiFePO4 replacement, and Tenergy states the Solla Premium is 'NOT a replacement for LifePO4 batteries'.
