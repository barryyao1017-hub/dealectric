The rules, as the FDA and the labels state them

  • Unopened: refrigerate at approximately 36–46 °F (2–8 °C) and it works to its expiry date (FDA; Lilly; Novo).
  • Out of the fridge: vials or cartridges, opened or unopened, between 59 and 86 °F for up to 28 days (FDA's current emergency page); Humalog in-use pens below 86 °F, thrown away 28 days after first use; NovoLog unopened or in use at room temperature up to 86 °F for 28 days, in-use pens not refrigerated; Tresiba in use up to 86 °F or refrigerated for 8 weeks. Day limits vary by product: follow your label.
  • Heat: above 86 °F the FDA says insulin may still be needed in an emergency; keep it as cool as you can and replace exposed vials once stored supply is available; pump insulin above 98.6 °F should be discarded.
  • Cold: never freeze it, and do not use insulin that has frozen; with ice, keep the insulin off the ice.

Two days in a house that stays under 86 °F is inside every label for the pen or vial in use. The outage problem is the unopened boxes in the refrigerator door, and a closed household fridge holds food temperature only about 4 hours (FDA), so that is what the cooler is for.

The cooler, not the station, sets the runtime

A 12 V compressor cooler holds a real 36–46 °F in a warm room and cycles: Dometic's CFX3 25 spec is 0.92 Ah an hour at 12 V in a 90 °F ambient (about 265 Wh a day), BougeRV's 22 L lists 118 kWh a year (about 320 Wh a day), and owner tests of Alpicool and ICECO units run 450–620 Wh a day, so plan 265–500 Wh. Run it from the station's 12 V socket (a 6.5 A rated cooler fits a 10 A port; put nothing else on a shared DC bus) and leave the AC inverter off, which saves its idle draw. Thermoelectric boxes draw about 54 W all the time and only reach about 40 °F below ambient, so they cannot promise 46 °F in a warm house. Recharge from a car at about 100 W or a 100–200 W panel (the AC180 takes up to 500 W of solar).

Caveats to keep

Follow the label and your prescriber; day limits and in-use rules differ by product. Test the whole setup before an outage, with a thermometer in the cooler. Contact the manufacturer with product questions (the FDA page lists their numbers). This page is about keeping a cold chain, not medical advice.
