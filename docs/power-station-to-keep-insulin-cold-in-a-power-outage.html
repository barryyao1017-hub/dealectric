What the differences mean

Each runtime below is measured usable energy divided by the fridge's measured draw. The first 6 hours use GearLab's measured 50 Wh for that period.

  • AC180 + BougeRV 23 qt: (950 − 50) Wh ÷ 8.3 W + 6 h ≈ 114 h, about 66 h more than two days.
  • RIVER 2 Pro + BougeRV 23 qt: (600 − 50) ÷ 8.3 + 6 ≈ 72 h.
  • Hot room (Dometic's 90 °F rating): 0.92 Ah/h × 12 V = 11.04 W, and × 48 h = 530 Wh. AC180: 950 ÷ 11.04 ≈ 86 h. RIVER 2 Pro: 600 ÷ 11.04 ≈ 54 h, only 6 h spare.
  • Rated vs measured: 950 ÷ 1,152 = 82% for the AC180 and 600 ÷ 768 = 78% for the RIVER 2 Pro. Both are below the 85% that runtime calculators often assume.
  • Elite 100 V2 (estimate): 1,024 × 0.82 ≈ 840 Wh, so (840 − 50) ÷ 8.3 + 6 ≈ 101 h, or 840 ÷ 11.04 ≈ 76 h in a hot room.
  • EverFrost 2 40L: 288 Wh ÷ 52 h ≈ 5.5 W from Anker's one-battery figure (Anker's footnoted runtime test used an empty cooler at 77 °F), leaving 52 − 48 = 4 h spare.

Measured, not rated: what the tests and labels say

What insulin needs. The FDA recommends storing insulin in a refrigerator at about 36–46 °F. Unopened insulin kept that way stays effective until the expiration date. Do not use insulin that has been frozen. Labels differ: the FDA notes that Awiqli pens allow up to 12 weeks below 86 °F, so check your own carton.

Station capacity. GearLab drained the AC180 at 80% load on its light-bar test jig and got 950 Wh of the rated 1,152 Wh. Digital Trends got 976 Wh. GearLab measured the RIVER 2 Pro at 600 Wh in its text and 620 Wh in its spec table, and the figures above use the lower number.

Fridge draw. GearLab metered each cooler's DC energy on identical batteries for 6 hours. The BougeRV 23 qt drew 48.9 W while cooling to 35 °F, then held a steady 8.3 W and used 50 Wh in the six hours. GearLab puts its weekend need at 400 Wh. The Wagan 12V 24L thermoelectric cooler drew 48.0 W.

Display error. Set to 35 °F, the BougeRV measured 32 °F inside on GearLab's iButton loggers, and its display was off by 3.0 °F on average. For insulin, set the fridge to 39–41 °F and keep a separate thermometer next to the pens.

The AC180 settings that matter. BLUETTI's manual says that in ECO mode the AC and DC outputs turn off by themselves under low or no load. The DC threshold can be set from 5 to 10 W, for 1, 2, 3 or 4 hours. GearLab's measured 8.3 W average falls inside that band, so turn DC ECO off. To get to the setting, turn the screen on and the AC output off, then hold the AC and DC buttons for about 2 s to enter Setting mode, or use the BLUETTI app. The manual also gives about 15 W of self-consumption, and 15 W × 48 h = 720 Wh. So leave the AC inverter off and plug the fridge into the 12 V/10 A car port; Dometic rates the CFX3 25 at 6.5 A at 12 V. Before an outage, run a 48-hour test with no insulin inside and the thermometer in the fridge.

Not recommended

300 Wh stations for two days. The BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 is rated at 288 Wh. Even if every watt-hour came out, it would give (288 − 50) ÷ 8.3 + 6 ≈ 35 h with the BougeRV and 288 ÷ 11.04 ≈ 26 h at Dometic's hot-room rating. It covers one day, not two.

Thermoelectric coolers. At GearLab's measured 48.0 W, the Wagan 24 L would use 48 × 48 = 2,304 Wh over two days. The AC180's measured 950 Wh would run it for about 20 h.
