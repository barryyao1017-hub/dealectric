Short answer. If you only want the cameras recorded, buy Reolink's own RLN8-410 NVR ($349.99 with a 2 TB drive on 2026-11-03): eight PoE ports, no NAS, no subscription. If you want a NAS anyway, buy the UGREEN NASync DXP2800 ($389.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nasync-dxp2800), $389.99 diskless: UGOS's Surveillance Center records ONVIF/RTSP cameras 24/7 or on events with no published camera fee, but it is a 2026 first-version app, so the NAS, not the recorder, is the reason to pick it.

What to know: Synology's DS225+ (about $365) has the mature recorder, Surveillance Station, but only 2 free camera licences, so four Reolinks need a CLP4 pack (about $295 on Amazon), about $660 in all; QNAP's QVR Elite is 2 free channels then a subscription; Asustor gives 4 free. The technical route on the DXP2800 is Frigate in Docker with OpenVINO on the N100. Four 8MP Reolinks at their 6 Mbps default write about 7.8 TB a month continuous, so buy 6 TB surveillance drives and expect roughly three weeks of retention per 6 TB.

How Reolink cameras reach a NAS

  • RTSP/ONVIF: RLC-series PoE cameras (RLC-810A 8MP, RLC-510A 5MP, RLC-1212A, Duo 3) expose RTSP and ONVIF; the ports may be off by default and are enabled in the Reolink client. That is what UGOS Surveillance Center, Synology, QNAP, Asustor and Frigate consume.
  • FTP upload: every Reolink PoE camera (add-on cameras excepted) can upload 24/7, motion-only or timed video to an FTP server; SFTP is not supported and Reolink documents no SMB or NFS recording, so this route needs an FTP server on the NAS and gives no playback app.
  • Reolink's own NVR or Home Hub 2: the no-NAS route; the RLN16-410 for more cameras ran about $261–460 across Amazon listings.

Storage math

GB per day is about 10.8 × total Mbps. Four 8MP Reolinks at the 6,144 Kbps default write about 259 GB a day (7.8 TB a month); at 8 Mbps 346 GB a day; four 5MP at 3 Mbps about 130 GB a day (3.9 TB a month). Those are bitrate ceilings, and H.265 on a quiet scene writes less. A 6 TB drive holds about 23 days of four 8MP cameras at 6 Mbps or 46 days of four 5MP at 3 Mbps; the RLN8-410's 2 TB about 8 or 15 days. Event-only recording cuts it several-fold. Prices read on Amazon 2026-11-03, mostly third-party sellers: WD Purple 4 TB $199–235, Seagate SkyHawk 4 TB $214.99 (from $149.99), SkyHawk 6 TB $296–330; volatile, recheck before buying.

PoE

Reolink PoE cameras are 802.3af (about 12 W each), so a 65 W four-port PoE+ switch covers four: TP-Link TL-SG1005P $39.90, Tenda TEG1105P $29.99, TP-Link TL-SG1008MP (8 ports, 126 W) $99.99 on 2026-11-03. The Reolink NVR has its PoE ports built in.

What we could not verify

UGREEN's Surveillance Center guide (support article 866) is readable only in a browser; its camera cap, licence terms, H.265 playback in the web UI and mobile-app live view are unconfirmed, and it has not been tested on the ARM DH2300. Reolink's RTSP table does not name the RLC-1212A, Duo 3 or CX410 individually.
