What the differences mean

  • Daily storage: Reolink's 4K RLC-810A defaults to 6,144 kbps H.265. 6.144 Mb/s × 86,400 s ÷ 8 = 66.4 GB per camera per day, × 4 = 265.4 GB a day. That is 3.77 days per usable TB.
  • RLN8-410 as shipped: 2,000 GB ÷ 265.4 = 7.5 days on the included 2 TB drive.
  • RLN8-410 at its ceiling: its product page says 8 TB per drive (8,000 ÷ 265.4 = 30.1 days), and Reolink's RLN8-410 FAQ says 6 TB via SATA (6,000 ÷ 265.4 = 22.6 days). An eSATA drive raises this to 45.2 days (12 TB) or 60.3 days (16 TB), with no redundancy.
  • DS225+ or DXP2800 with 2 × 20 TB mirrored: 20,000 ÷ 265.4 = 75.4 days, and one drive can fail without losing footage.
  • DS425+ with 4 × 24 TB in RAID 5: 3 × 24 = 72 TB usable; 72,000 ÷ 265.4 = 271 days.
  • Synology licences: 4 cameras − 2 included = 2 to buy, once. A TrackMix recorded on both lenses uses 2 licences (Reolink), so four of them need 8 − 2 = 6.
  • Synology headroom: 4 cameras × 25 fps = 100 of the 200 FPS the DS225+ is rated for at 4K H.265, which is 50%.
  • Running all year: DS225+ 16.98 W × 8,760 h = 148.7 kWh. DXP2800 18.6 W × 8,760 h = 162.9 kWh (ComputerBase). DS425+ 28.25 W → 247.5 kWh. TS-264 29.1 W → 254.8 kWh. Drives never sleep during 24/7 recording, so these spinning-drive figures apply, not the hibernation ones.

How many days of 24/7 footage each box holds, and why the NVR runs out first

Retention is set by two numbers: the cameras' bitrate and the usable disk space. Reolink's 4K PoE cameras such as the RLC-810A record H.265 at 6,144 kbps by default (adjustable from 4,096 to 8,192 kbps). Four of them fill 265 GB a day. Change the bitrate and the days scale with it: at 8,192 kbps, 20 TB lasts 56.5 days.

The Reolink RLN8-410 is limited by drive size, not by camera count. Its US product page lists up to 16 TB, at most 8 TB per drive (one internal SATA plus one eSATA). Reolink's own FAQ for the same model says the version currently on sale takes at most 6 TB via SATA and 6 TB via eSATA. Check the hardware version on the unit's label before you buy an 8 TB drive. On the internal drive alone, that is 22.6 to 30.1 days, and the NVR has no mirroring. The RLN8-410 is sold as 12 channels, but it takes at most 8 cameras, and a dual-lens camera uses 2 of them.

A two-bay NAS takes larger drives and mirrors them. Synology rates the DS225+ for up to 40 TB raw, and ComputerBase ran the DXP2800 with two 20 TB IronWolf Pro drives. On the 2025 Synology Plus models, building a storage pool from non-Synology drives (WD Purple, Seagate SkyHawk) needs DSM 7.3 or later, so update DSM before you set up the pool. M.2 pools and cache still need drives from Synology's compatibility list.

Three setup points apply to every NAS route. A NAS has no PoE ports, so add an 802.3af switch: each RLC-810A draws under 12 W, so four need about 48 W of PoE budget. Reolink turns off the RTSP and ONVIF ports by default on some models; switch them on under Network > Advanced > Server Settings. Synology's Surveillance Station accepts H.265 over ONVIF Profile S. Frigate plays back H.265 recordings in Chrome 108+, Edge and Safari. UGREEN publishes no codec list for its own Surveillance Center app, so Frigate is the route documented here. QNAP lists no eligible models for QVR Surveillance's 6 bonus channels, so check that the TS-264 offers QVR Surveillance before you count on 8 free channels.

Not recommended

Avoid running four 4K cameras 24/7 on the RLN8-410's included 2 TB drive. 2,000 GB ÷ 265.4 GB = 7.5 days, so a break-in found after a week away will already be overwritten. If you buy the NVR, budget for the largest drive your hardware version accepts.
