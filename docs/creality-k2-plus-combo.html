Who it's for

Makers who want multi-color prints bigger than a 300 mm bed allows, such as helmets, props and batches of multicolor parts, and who also print ABS or ASA. The QIDI Max4 ($1,049) does better for single-color engineering work, with 390 × 390 × 340 mm, a 65 °C chamber and a 370 °C nozzle, though its QIDI Box color unit is a separate $228 purchase. Buyers who can live with 300 mm get the K2 Pro Combo for $350 less.

Caveats

  • HowToMechatronics measured 350 g of purge waste for 83 g of multicolor parts in a print that took about 14 hours.
  • On the same three-color model with 206 color swaps, Tom's Hardware logged 121.7 g of purge on the K2 Plus against 99 g on a Bambu Lab P1P.
  • The CFS does not feed flexible TPU, damp PVA or BVOH, or warped cardboard spools, per Creality's spec sheet; Tom's Hardware had TPU jam in the CFS tubing and printed it from the side spool holder instead.
  • Tom's Hardware found the CFS cables too short to stand the unit beside the printer, so it sits on the glass lid (916 mm tall in total) and has to be powered down and unplugged before the lid comes off.
  • 3DWithUs hit a CFS retraction error and repeated tube-detached errors during testing, and owners on Creality's forum report extruder jams with filament bulging between the drive gears.
