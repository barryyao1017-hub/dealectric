What the differences mean

To get hours per card, add up the three cameras' bitrates. Gigabytes per hour = total Mbps × 3,600 s ÷ 8 ÷ 1,000, which is Mbps × 0.45. The bitrates come from DashCamTalk's per-channel spec figures for each model. For the A329S, DashCamTalk forum tester rcg530 posted the same 65 + 27 + 27 Mbps (65.53 + 27.03 + 27.03) for 3-channel mode.

  • VIOFO A329S 3CH: 65 + 27 + 27 = 119 Mbps, so 53.6 GB/h. That gives 256 ÷ 53.6 = 4.8 h on a 256 GB card, 512 ÷ 53.6 = 9.6 h on its 512 GB microSD maximum and 4,000 ÷ 53.6 ≈ 75 h on a 4 TB external SSD.
  • Vantrue N4 Pro S: 32 + 9.8 + 14 = 55.8 Mbps, so 25.1 GB/h. That gives 256 ÷ 25.1 = 10.2 h on 256 GB and 1,000 ÷ 25.1 = 39.8 h on a 1 TB card.
  • 70mai 4K T800: 32 + 32 + 13 = 77 Mbps, so 34.7 GB/h. That gives 256 ÷ 34.7 = 7.4 h on 256 GB and 512 ÷ 34.7 = 14.8 h on its 512 GB maximum.
  • Vantrue N4S: 3 × 14.8 = 44.4 Mbps, so 20.0 GB/h. That gives 256 ÷ 20.0 = 12.8 h on 256 GB and 1,000 ÷ 20.0 = 50 h on 1 TB. DashCamTalk's spec table lists the same 14.8 Mbps for every camera, so treat this figure as rough.
  • Same 256 GB card: 10.2 ÷ 4.8 = 2.1, so the N4 Pro S keeps about twice as many hours as the A329S 3CH.
  • Rear cameras: VIOFO's "2K" rear (RC420) and Vantrue's "2.5K" rear are both 2560 × 1440 on Sony's IMX675. The different labels don't mean either rear view is sharper.
  • Front cameras: with all three cameras on, the A329S 3CH and the N4 Pro S both record the front at 3840 × 2160 and 30 fps. The A329S's 60 fps mode works only without the cabin camera.

A lower bitrate saves card space but doesn't make a sharper picture. In 3-channel mode the A329S uses 65 Mbps on its front camera, against 32 Mbps on the N4 Pro S and the 70mai T800. The cost is fewer hours before its oldest footage is overwritten.

How long three cameras last on one card, and what parking mode needs

A dash cam records in a loop. When the card is full, it writes over the oldest footage that isn't locked. With three cameras recording at once, the card fills at the combined rate of all three. That is why the A329S 3CH's 119 Mbps uses up a 256 GB card in under five hours of driving, while the N4 Pro S's 55.8 Mbps lasts about ten. Size the card to the longest stretch you might need to go back over. The maximums are 512 GB microSD for the A329S, which also records to an external SSD of up to 4 TB (Digital Camera World), 1 TB for both Vantrues and 512 GB for the T800.

All four cameras run on a supercapacitor rather than a battery, so they need a hardwire kit to record while the car is parked. VIOFO's own A329S FAQ says the HK4 or HK6 kit turns on automatic parking mode, but only the HK6 enables the camera's cut-off voltage, which protects the car battery. The HK6 was $25.99 at viofo.com on 2026-11-21. Vantrue's Voltage Display Hardwire Kit ($29.99 at vantrue.com, 2026-11-21) lists the N4 Pro S by name. 70mai's T800 listing says its parking guardian mode needs the 70mai hardwire kit, sold separately. The Pro Setup in the table includes one, and DashCamTalk lists the T800's parking recording as not buffered.

The Vantrue prices shown are store sale prices that need no code: $279.99 for the N4 Pro S and $199.99 for the N4S, read 2026-11-21. 70mai.store also lists the T800 on its own at $199.99 (2026-11-21), but that listing doesn't name the rear camera in the box.
