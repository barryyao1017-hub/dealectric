Why 40Gbps enclosures throttle

The ASM2464PD bridge converts PCIe Gen4 to USB4 and, with a Gen4 drive, the pair draws close to 10 W while a copy runs; in a palm-sized shell that heat has to leave through the case. A thermal pad and a fan keep the drive in the 40s °C (IT-Connect's 43 °C on the UGREEN CM642); a fanless shell relies on the drive's own thermal throttling, which shows up as the speed falling after the first tens of gigabytes. Drives matter too: the fast long-copy results used Gen4 x4 drives with DRAM (WD SN850X 8 TB, Samsung 990 Pro), and a larger drive keeps its write cache longer.

Cables and ports

40Gbps needs a USB4 or Thunderbolt 3/4 port and a 40Gbps cable: PCWorld saw an older USB-C cable cut the CM642 to roughly 10Gbps speeds. A USB 3.2 Gen 2 port gives about 1,000 MB/s whatever the enclosure. On a Thunderbolt 5 Mac an 80Gbps enclosure roughly doubles the ceiling (see the Thunderbolt 5 page); on everything else this class is the ceiling.
