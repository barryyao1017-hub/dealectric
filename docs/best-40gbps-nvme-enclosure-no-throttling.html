What the differences mean

  • 1 TB at the D1 SSD Plus's held rate: 1,000,000 MB ÷ 2,575 MB/s (the middle of NAS Compares' 2,550–2,600) = 388 s, about 6.5 minutes.
  • Mixed files copy slower than one big file: NAS Compares' 100 GB mixed set took 63 s. 100,000 MB ÷ 63 s = 1,587 MB/s, so 1 TB of mixed files takes about 10.5 minutes.
  • A Windows setting costs more than heat: CDRinfo measured the UGREEN CM642 at 2,523.54 MB/s single-queue write with Windows write caching on and 1,375.07 MB/s with it off (Quick Removal). Per TB that is 1,000,000 ÷ 2,523.54 = 6.6 min against 1,000,000 ÷ 1,375.07 = 12.1 min. Switch the drive to Better Performance in Device Manager before a long copy.
  • Thunderbolt 3 hosts: OWC's manual limits the Express 1M2 to USB 10Gb/s on Thunderbolt 3 ports, and OWC rates a 10Gb/s link at 990 MB/s. 1,000,000 ÷ 990 = 16.8 min per TB, about 2.6 times the D1's 6.5 min.
  • What the fan costs: the CM642 at $109.99 is $17.00 more than Unitek's fan model at $92.99 (109.99 − 92.99) and $10.00 more than the OWC Express 1M2 at $99.99.

Who has run a long copy, and what held

The speeds printed on the box come from short benchmarks. These are the named tests that ran long enough to heat the drive:

  • TerraMaster D1 SSD Plus. NAS Compares looped AJA with 1 GB files. Writes held at 2,550–2,600 MB/s and reads at 3,000–3,100 MB/s. After more than five minutes of large-file writes the SSD was at 44–45 °C and the case at 41–42 °C. Hardware Busters ran CrystalDiskMark for 15 minutes in a 30 °C room: the case levelled off at about 55 °C (54.8 °C max) with no thermal throttling. PCWorld's Jon L. Jacobi put the same Samsung 9100 Pro 4TB in the D1 and in UGREEN's CM850 and called the D1's 450 GB write 'a major win'. price on the Amazon page; buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FCS7YYY6?tag=dealectric-20
  • UGREEN CM642 (fan). PCWorld wrote 450 GB to an 8TB WD SN850X, and the enclosure 'never suffered any thermal related issues'. Its 48 GB copy was the fastest PCWorld had recorded for a USB4 SSD. In CDRinfo's test the Crucial T710 inside peaked at 50 °C. $109.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-40gbps-nvme-enclosure-cooling-fan, code UL15976 saves $34, pre-applied by the link
  • OWC Express 1M2. Macworld's Thomas Bergbold ran Blackmagic Disk Speed Test for about an hour on the 8TB configured unit. The rate 'did not drop' and the case reached about 40 °C.
  • Unitek SolidForce Reefer TurboCool. Back2Gaming wrote about 600 GB to a Kingston NV3 2TB in a 28 °C room, and the drive kept its peak write rate. The fan starts above 45 °C.

On long copies the SSD sets the speed, not the port. PCWorld found that the CM642's 450 GB write had no thermal trouble but was still slower than a couple of 20Gbps (about 2 GB/s) USB 3.2×2 SSDs. Once the SSD's fast cache fills, the SSD's own write speed takes over. For jobs of several hundred GB, choose an SSD that writes fast after its cache fills.

Watch the SSD's power draw. One owner (noise.paulos.cz) tried a Kingston KC3000 2TB and an Apacer AS2280Q4U, both rated at about 9 W, in a CM642. Both overheated and disconnected after a few minutes of writing. A WD Red SN700 4TB in the same enclosure fell from about 700 to 300 MB/s after a minute or two. The same owner's Lexar NM790 4TB (4.1 W) completed a full-disk rewrite at about 67 °C in a Satechi USB4 enclosure. In any bus-powered 40Gbps enclosure, a low-power Gen4 SSD is the safer choice.

Not recommended

UGREEN CM850 with its silicone sleeve on: PCWorld had to take the sleeve off before the CM850 would finish its 450 GB write. The CM642 finished the same test cleanly. OWC Express 1M2 on a Thunderbolt 3 host: OWC's own manual limits it to USB 10Gb/s there, and it does not accept SSDs with heatsinks.
