What the differences mean

  • Battery use per day, NAS left idle for 24 h. TS-233: 3.7 W × 24 h = 88.8 Wh, ÷ 12.8 V = 6.9 Ah. DH2300: 9–10 W × 24 h = 216–240 Wh, ÷ 12.8 V = 16.9–18.8 Ah. DXP2800: 7 W × 24 h = 168 Wh, ÷ 12.8 V = 13.1 Ah. DS223: 14.2 W × 24 h = 340.8 Wh, ÷ 12.8 V = 26.6 Ah. DS223j: 16.4 W × 24 h = 393.6 Wh, ÷ 12.8 V = 30.8 Ah. These figures leave out the DC-DC converter's own losses.
  • Synology DS223j against the TS-233 at idle: 16.4 W ÷ 3.7 W = 4.4 times the draw. The comparison isn't like for like, because LANOC tested the TS-233 with SSDs and PC-Welt doesn't name its drives. QNAP's own figure with both bays filled, 10.81 W, is still lower than Synology's 16.31 W for the DS223j: 16.31 ÷ 10.81 = 1.51, or 51% more.
  • Asleep, the gap closes. PC-Welt measured the DS223j at 3.1 W asleep: 3.1 W × 24 h = 74.4 Wh, or 5.8 Ah. QNAP rates the TS-233 at 3.43 W in disk standby. If your NAS spends most of the day with its drives spun down, the standby figure matters more than the active one.
  • Converter and fuse size at 12 V: TS-233, 48 W ÷ 12 V = 4 A. DH2300, 50 W ÷ 12 V = 4.2 A. DXP2800 and DS223j, 60 W ÷ 12 V = 5 A. Size for the adapter rating, not the idle figure. StorageReview's copy of Seagate's IronWolf spec gives 1.8–2.0 A at 12 V for each drive while it spins up, so two drives starting together draw about 4 A, or 48 W.
  • Don't wire it straight to the house battery. Seagate's IronWolf spec, as StorageReview lists it, allows 12 V ±10%, so 13.2 V at most. A 12 V LiFePO4 charger such as Redodo's 14.6 V model pushes the bus to 14.6 V, which is 1.4 V above that limit. Feed the NAS from a regulated DC-DC converter set to 12 V. Victron's Orion-Tr 12/12 takes 8–17 V in and gives an adjustable 10–15 V out. Its no-load draw is under 80 mA, about 1 W at 12.8 V.

Measured watts and how each reviewer took them

  • QNAP TS-233, LANOC (Wes Compton): 3.7 W idle, 5.7 W streaming to Plex, 7.4 W copying files, with two 500 GB SATA SSDs installed. With SSDs there is no drive spin-down, so these are lower than a hard-drive build would read. The review mentions a newly installed Sense whole-home energy monitor and names no plug-in meter. QNAP's own 10.81 W operating and 3.43 W disk standby were 'tested with drives fully populated', meaning two drives, but QNAP doesn't name the drive model.
  • UGREEN NASync DH2300, Mighty Gadget (James Smythe): 9–10 W idle and 12–13 W under load, with two 4 TB Seagate IronWolf drives in RAID 1. OCinside read 9–11 W idle and 12–13 W while copying on a Shelly Plug S, which it calls not very accurate. UGREEN's own 13.72 W and 3.304 W figures were taken with a single 2 TB drive.
  • UGREEN NASync DXP2800, OCinside: about 7 W idle and 34.7 W peak under a combined CPU, RAM and disk stress test. The unit was set up in RAID 1. The review names two WD40EFPX 4 TB drives as its test drives, and it ran NVMe tests first with a Samsung 980 Pro and then with a Crucial T705. UGREEN's 16.38 W figure is with one 2 TB drive, so a second spinning drive adds to it.
  • Synology DS223j and DS223, PC-Welt (Ines Walke-Chomjakov): DS223j at 16.4 W idle, 3.1 W asleep, 22.3 W under load and 0.1 W off. DS223 at 14.2 W idle, 3.3 W asleep and 18.2 W under load. PC-Welt names neither the drives nor the meter. Synology lists only a 60 W adapter with 100–240 V AC input. The 12 V output comes from retailer listings of the Adapter 60W_1, which name the DS223 and DS223j among its applied models.
  • Where the meter sat: none of these reviewers measured on a 12 V DC feed. A reading at the wall includes the AC adapter's own loss, which a DC-DC feed replaces with the converter's loss.

Not recommended

Synology DS223j or DS223 on battery alone. PC-Welt measured 16.4 W and 14.2 W idle, which is 30.8 Ah and 26.6 Ah a day from a 12.8 V bank if left awake. Synology also publishes no output voltage for the adapter, so the 12 V input rests on retailer listings. They make sense only if you need DSM and will set the drives to hibernate, where PC-Welt read 3.1–3.3 W.
