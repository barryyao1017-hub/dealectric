What the differences mean

A well pump's motor draws its locked-rotor current (LRA, on the pump nameplate) for a fraction of a second at start. Start VA = LRA × 230 V, with LRA from the Franklin motor tables in Pentair's 4-inch submersible pump manual:

  • 1/2 HP 3-wire: 23.0 A × 230 V = 5,290 VA; 2-wire: 32.2 A × 230 V = 7,406 VA
  • 3/4 HP 3-wire: 34.2 A × 230 V = 7,866 VA; 2-wire: 40.7 A × 230 V = 9,361 VA
  • 1 HP 3-wire: 41.8 A × 230 V = 9,614 VA; 2-wire: 48.7 A × 230 V = 11,201 VA
  • 1 1/2 HP 3-wire: 52.0 A × 230 V = 11,960 VA

Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus: 9,300 W measured − 7,866 VA = 1,434 of headroom over a 3/4 HP 3-wire start; 9,614 − 9,300 = 314 VA short of a 1 HP 3-wire start. DELTA Pro Ultra: 9,200 − 7,866 = 1,334 of headroom. F3800: 8,000 − 7,866 = 134, for 2-3 s.

Idle draw eats outage time too: the Jackery's 58 W measured idle (The Solar Lab) × 24 h = 1,392 Wh a day, and the DELTA Pro Ultra's 52 W (HOBOTECH) × 24 h = 1,248 Wh, before the pump runs. A 1 HP pump at 9.8 A × 230 V = 2,254 W for 30 minutes a day uses about 1,127 Wh, so the inverter left on uses more than the pump.

Measured surge is lower than the rated surge

Dealectric does not test units; every figure here is a named tester's or the maker's. The surge a station actually held under load is the number to set against your pump's start VA, and it sits below the advertised figure on every unit tested:

  • Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus: 9,300 W at 240 V and 4,800 W at 120 V, each for under 5 s (HOBOTECH, as summarized on EcoFlow's blog, a competitor) against a 14,400 W listed surge. The Solar Lab held 8,000 W and 9,500 W loads for 20-30 s, yet the unit struggled to start a 10,000 W-surge air conditioner. buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DFGLG35L?tag=dealectric-20
  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra: 9,200 W for about 10 s, peak 9,300 W (The Solar Lab), against EcoFlow's 10.8 kW for 10 s and LRA 120 A, the only motor-start rating any maker here publishes. It is the one 240 V unit orderable for delivery now.
  • Anker SOLIX F3800: 8,000 W for 2-3 s and 7,200 W for about 10 s (The Solar Lab); Anker lists no surge figure for the F3800 Plus. $2,999.99: https://dealectric.com/go/anker-solix-f3800-plus
  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3: shut down after 5,100 W for nearly a minute (The Solar Lab), yet Robb Sutton started a 1 HP 240 V submersible pump on it with no soft starter and read 2,800 W. A pump on an inverter can start below LRA × V because the inverter sags the voltage, so your pump may start on less than the table says; the nameplate LRA is still the safe number to plan on.
  • BLUETTI Apex 300 and EcoFlow TITAN 5000 Plus: no named surge test. BLUETTI does not call the Apex 300's 7,680 W Lifting Power a surge or motor-start rating, so it covers a 1/2 HP 3-wire pump on paper only. $1,699 on bluettipower.com, $1,614.05 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-apex-300

The Solar Lab does not state the voltage of its surge tests; only HOBOTECH's Jackery figure is stated at 240 V. Whatever the station, a hardwired pump connects through a transfer switch or interlocked inlet fitted by an electrician. For runtime and solar recharge on a pump, see solar generators that can run a well pump.

Not recommended

Anker SOLIX S5000 for a well pump. Anker's own FAQ says loads over 4,500 W trip its overload protection and its 8,000 W peak lasts a fraction of a second, so even a 1/2 HP 3-wire start (5,290 VA) is over its limit. It suits 240 V loads without a motor start, such as a 4,000 W resistive heater.
