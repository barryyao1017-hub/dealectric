Why the 15-inch Air is its own question: Apple gives every 15-inch MacBook Air (M2, M3, M4 and the current M5) the same 15.3-inch, 2880×1864 display at 224 ppi and 500 nits. The 13.40 × 9.35 in body, 3.3 lb weight and 66.5 Wh battery are also the same. What changes with the chip is how many external screens it drives: one on the M2, one with the lid open on the M3, two on the M4 and M5. So one-screen extenders rank first here, because they run on every 15-inch Air. They are then ordered by how close they come to the Air's own sharpness, then by weight and battery cost. Left out as just short of the size: the 13.3-inch Duex Plus DS, the smaller of Mobile Pixels' two Duex DS sizes.

What the differences mean

  • Sharpness. Pixel density is the diagonal pixel count divided by the diagonal in inches. Duex Float 2 Pro: √(2560² + 1600²) ÷ 16 = 3,019 ÷ 16 = 189 ppi, 84% of the Air's 224. Duex Max DS: √(1920² + 1080²) ÷ 14.1 = 2,203 ÷ 14.1 = 156 ppi, 70%. Any 15.6-inch 1080p screen, the Fold included: 2,203 ÷ 15.6 = 141 ppi, 63%. Trio 3 Pro: 2,642 ÷ 14 = about 189 ppi, 84%. espresso Pro 15: 4,406 ÷ 15.6 = 282 ppi, 126%, the only screen here denser than the Air's own.
  • Battery. Apple's 'up to 15 hours wireless web' on a 66.5 Wh battery implies 66.5 ÷ 15 = 4.43 W on average. Mobile Pixels' Duex Max sheet lists 4.5 W: 66.5 ÷ (4.43 + 4.5) = about 7.4 hours. That sheet is headed Duex Max and does not name the DS revision. Macworld's Simon Jary gives the Float 2 Pro 7.5 W from the MacBook in Limited Power Mode, with brightness capped at 80%: 66.5 ÷ (4.43 + 7.5) = about 5.6 hours. These are estimates from the makers' and Apple's figures, not tests, and they leave out the extra graphics work of a second screen, so expect less. Dealectric does not test units.
  • Weight. Macworld lists the Float 2 Pro at 2.9 lb: 2.9 ÷ 3.3 = 88% of the Air's weight, 6.2 lb carried together. The Trio 3 Pro is 4.13 lb (Macworld): 4.13 ÷ 3.3 = 125%, heavier than the laptop it hangs on. FutureFive's Owen McCarthy put the Duex Max DS at about 0.8 kg added: 0.8 ÷ 1.51 = 53% of the Air.

On the Trio 3 Pro, Macworld's Simon Jary notes its screens 'can't draw enough power from the connected laptop to allow for maximum screen brightness', so its 300 nits needs the power adapter.

Not recommended: a two-screen extender on an M2 or M3 Air without a driver

Apple lists 'One external display' for the 15-inch M2 Air. For the M3 it lists one, plus: 'Close the MacBook Air lid to use a second external display'. Apple's instructions for that mode require macOS Sonoma 14.3 or later, an external keyboard and mouse or trackpad, and the Mac on power. With both displays connected, opening the lid means 'the secondary external display will go dark or disconnect'. An extender mounted on the lid needs the lid open, so on an M3 Air it gets one screen natively. That last step is our reading of Apple's document, not a maker's statement. On either chip, the second screen of a two-screen set needs the maker's display driver.

On an M4 or M5 Air, Apple says the laptop supports 'up to two external displays simultaneously in addition to the built-in display', and that closing the lid does not raise the number. Each Trio 3 Pro screen then takes one of the Air's two Thunderbolt ports and charging moves to the MagSafe 3 port.
