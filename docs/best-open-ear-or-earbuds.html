Open ear or earbuds: which one should you buy?

Updated 2026-11-06

Short answer. The best open ear or earbuds pick for most shoppers is the soundcore AeroFit 2 ($129.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/soundcore-aerofit-2, code AFSC20 saves $25.99): 10 hours per charge, 42 with the case, an IP55 sweat and rain rating, and a 4-position ear hook that holds the speaker close enough to the ear canal for usable bass. Buy open-ear only if you mostly listen outdoors or at a desk. Open-ear buds block no outside noise, so on a subway, a plane or in a loud gym the music vanishes and you turn it up; for those places the honest answer is a sealed noise-cancelling bud such as the soundcore Liberty 4 Pro ($149.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/soundcore-liberty-4-pro, code AFSC20 saves $29.99), 10 hours per charge and 40 with the case. If you wear earbuds all day for calls and hate ear-canal pressure, the clip-on soundcore AeroClip 2 ($169.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/soundcore-aeroclip-2) has the clearest call pickup reviewers have tested in this class, at 6.2 g per bud. Leakage is real but modest: in a quiet office a person a few feet away hears open-ear buds once volume passes roughly 50 to 70 percent, in reviewer tests of the Bose Ultra Open and Shokz OpenFit 2.

Open-ear earbuds sit outside the ear canal on a hook or a clip. You hear traffic, coworkers and your own footsteps, and nothing presses inside the ear. The trade is physics: no seal means weaker bass, no noise blocking, and some sound escaping. In-ear earbuds seal the canal, which gives deep bass and lets noise cancelling work, but many people find them fatiguing after two or three hours. The right choice depends on where you listen, not on which type is better.

What owners and reviewers complain about

We read 12 hands-on reviews and retailer comment threads. Reddit threads were blocked. The same seven issues came up again and again.

  • Useless in loud places (7 mentions). No open-ear bud blocks noise. Reviewers call them "rotten" for city transit, and one gym-goer returned a pair because the gym's music drowned them out.
  • Weak bass (6). SoundGuys measured the AeroFit 2 at about 8 dB down at 100 Hz against its target. The AeroFit 2's hook set to its most open position helps; the Nothing Ear (Open) had almost no bass because it hangs loose.
  • Leakage at higher volume (6). Minimal at a quarter volume, audible to a neighbor past about 50 to 70 percent. Nobody has measured the AeroFit 2 independently.
  • Wind on calls (5). Open-ear mics sit exposed; the AeroFit 2's mics pick up wind, and one owner says they are poor in windy conditions.
  • Fit and stability (5). Clip-ons can loosen during hard exercise (the AeroClip 2's right bud slipped for one reviewer). Hooks stay put; the Shokz OpenFit 2 is rated the most secure, the AeroFit 2 a step behind.
  • Touch controls too sensitive (3) and a bulky case (2) on the AeroFit 2.

  • You wear earbuds all day at a desk and take calls: soundcore AeroClip 2 ($169.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/soundcore-aeroclip-2). Lightest of the group at 6.2 g per bud and the clearest voice pickup reviewers found; default sound is bright, so run the app's HearID tuning. On a budget, the soundcore C50i ($69.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/soundcore-c50i) is the same clip-on idea with 12 mm drivers for less than half the price.
  • You want the best-sounding open-ear bud and price is no object: the Bose Ultra Open Earbuds. Rich bass for an open design and limited leakage, but a 7.5-hour battery, IPX4 only, and a list price that launched at $299.
  • You commute by subway or fly, or you want to feel bass: soundcore Liberty 4 Pro ($149.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/soundcore-liberty-4-pro, code AFSC20 saves $29.99). Sealed tips, adaptive noise cancelling from 7 sensors, 10 hours per charge. Use its transparency mode when you need to hear a station announcement.

Not recommended

  • soundcore AeroFit 2 Pro as your only noise-cancelling bud. It rotates from open-ear to a sealed ANC mode, which is clever, but reviewers found the cancelling weak and the battery drains fast with it on. Buy it only if you value one pair for both jobs over doing either job well.
  • Nothing Ear (Open) for music. Comfortable and long-lasting, but it hangs loose, leaks, and reviewers found the bass nearly absent.
  • Any open-ear bud for a loud gym, subway or plane. You will turn it up to hear it, which defeats the hearing-safety reason many people buy open-ear in the first place.
