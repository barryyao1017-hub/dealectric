Who it's for

Makers who want the largest build volume in Creality's K2 line for single-color ABS, ASA, nylon-CF or PPA-CF parts up to 350 mm (13.8 inches) a side. Buyers who already know they want multi-color should take the Combo: it adds one CFS for $200, less than the $299 the CFS costs alone. The QIDI Max4 ($1,049) does better on size and heat, with 390 × 390 × 340 mm, a 65 °C chamber and a 370 °C nozzle, and the QIDI Plus5 ($699) has the same 65 °C chamber in a smaller 320 × 320 × 300 mm volume.

Caveats

  • Creality's chamber guide says the heater runs only for setpoints above 40 °C and that the chamber may not reach its target on 110 V power in a room below 15 °C.
  • Tom's Hardware could not get the closed chamber down to 30 °C and had a clog printing PLA with the door shut; Creality's own guide says to keep the chamber at 30 to 35 °C for PLA, PETG and TPU.
  • Owners on Creality's forum report extruder jams where filament bulges between the drive gears: one June 2025 buyer had two jams in the first three prints, and a December 2025 post describes a printer out of service for three months with an FR2836 feed error despite replacement parts.
  • It weighs 35 kg and measures 495 × 515 × 640 mm, so it needs a sturdy bench and two people to lift; 3DWithUs put the Combo's shipping weight at 54 kg.
  • Adding color later means buying a CFS, which per Creality's spec sheet does not feed flexible TPU, and HowToMechatronics measured 350 g of purge waste for 83 g of multicolor parts on this printer.
