What the differences mean

Every runtime on this page uses the same fridge. Bodega measured its BD60 12 V compressor fridge, empty, in a 70 °F room: 170 Wh per 24 hours set to 37 °F, and 356 Wh set to 0 °F. Days per charge is usable energy divided by that daily draw:

  • BLUETTI Elite 100 V2: 890 Wh ÷ 170 = 5.2 days; 890 ÷ 356 = 2.5 days as a freezer.
  • Jackery Explorer 1000 v2: 910 ÷ 170 = 5.4 days, the longest here and 0.2 days more than the Elite 100 V2.
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: 890 ÷ 170 = 5.2 days.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3: 870 ÷ 170 = 5.1 days.
  • Jackery Explorer 300: 292 Wh rated ÷ 170 = 1.7 days at most.
  • BLUETTI Elite 30 V2: 288 Wh rated ÷ 170 = 1.7 days. Rated capacity is a ceiling, so expect less.
  • Cost per fridge-day at the maker's price today: Elite 100 V2, $499 ÷ 5.2 = about $95; Elite 30 V2, $289.99 ÷ 1.7 = about $171; Explorer 300, $289 ÷ 1.7 = about $170. A 1 kWh unit costs about half as much per day of cooling.

How many days a power station runs a camping fridge

Use the measured figure, not the rated one. A power station loses some of its rated capacity in conversion. OutdoorGearLab measured 85% to 87% of rated capacity from the 1 kWh units above. ADAC tested eight units of 768 to 1,440 Wh and found usable capacity averaged about 10% to 15% below the maker's rating. A compressor fridge cycles on and off, so its average draw is far below its peak: Bodega's fridge peaked at 53.5 W but averaged about 7 W across the day (170 Wh ÷ 24 h). Bodega notes that a full fridge uses less than its empty test, and a hotter campsite pushes the draw up.

Plug the fridge into the 12 V car outlet, not an AC socket. BLUETTI rates the Elite 100 V2's car port at 12 V, 100 W, and Anker rates the C1000 Gen 2's at 12 V, 10 A, 120 W. Both are well above the fridge's 53.5 W peak. Running the fridge through the AC inverter costs more. The Solar Lab measured the C1000 Gen 2 drawing 13.8 W with the inverter on and nothing plugged in. Over 24 h that is 13.8 × 24 = 331 Wh, about twice what the fridge itself uses in a day.

Solar decides whether you can go past five days. BLUETTI lists the Elite 100 V2's solar input as 1,000 W max at 12 to 60 V. Anker's 600 W rating for the C1000 Gen 2 applies only from 29 to 60 V. Between 11 and 28 V the input is capped at 8.2 A, so a 12 V-class panel array delivers about 28 × 8.2 ≈ 230 W at most. The Solar Lab recharged the Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 from solar in about 4.5 h at its 400 W maximum. A 37 °F fridge-day is 170 Wh, under one hour at a steady 200 W of panel output.

Two Jackery notes. Jackery rates the Explorer 1000 v2 at 4,000 cycles to 70% capacity. BLUETTI rates the Elite 100 V2 at 4,000 cycles to 80%, so Jackery's rating allows 10 more points of capacity loss over the same number of cycles. In June 2025, Germany's ADAC auto club graded the European Explorer 1000 v2 'mangelhaft' (5.0) because its car and solar inputs were not decoupled. Charging from both at once could push voltage into the vehicle's 12 V system. Whichever unit you buy, charge it from one DC source at a time.

Not recommended

A 300 Wh unit for a fridge weekend. EcoFlow lists the RIVER 3 Plus at 286 Wh and 10.4 lb. That gives 286 ÷ 170 = 1.7 days at most, and two days needs 2 × 170 = 340 Wh. The Jackery Explorer 300, rated 292 Wh, lasts 1.7 days at most. Both work for phones, lights and a laptop, not for a fridge across a weekend. If you already own a RIVER 3 Plus, EcoFlow sells extra batteries that take it to 858 Wh, about 5.0 days of fridge at most.
