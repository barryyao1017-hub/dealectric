What the differences mean

  • Charging the K3 HE. Notebookcheck measured 3.6% drain per hour at maximum backlight: 100 ÷ 3.6 ≈ 27.8 h. At 3 hours of play a day that is about 9 days per charge (27 ÷ 3). With the backlight off, Keychron's 55 h works out to about 18 days (55 ÷ 3).
  • The price of magnetic sensing. Keychron gives the K3 HE and the mechanical K3 Ultra 8K the same 2,600 mAh battery. It rates them at 55 h and 550 h: 550 ÷ 55 = 10 times the runtime for the board without Rapid Trigger.
  • Polling over Bluetooth. Notebookcheck measured 1,000 Hz on the K3 HE over 2.4 GHz and cable, but 125 Hz over Bluetooth. That is one report every 1 ms (1 ÷ 1,000 s) against one every 8 ms (1 ÷ 125 s). Use the dongle or cable for games.
  • The G915 X premium. Logitech's G915 X LIGHTSPEED TKL costs $209.99 − $149.99 = $60 more than the G515 RAPID TKL. For that it has fixed 1.3 mm switches instead of adjustable magnetic ones. What the extra money buys is wireless: 42 ÷ 3 = 14 days per charge at full RGB, by Logitech's rating.
  • Usable actuation range. Logitech lists 0.1–2.5 mm for the G515 RAPID TKL, a 2.4 mm span (2.5 − 0.1). On the K3 HE, Notebookcheck's clean floor of 0.3 mm leaves 0.3–2.9 mm usable, a 2.6 mm span (2.9 − 0.3).

What Notebookcheck measured on the Keychron K3 HE

The K3 HE is the only board here with both magnetic switches and wireless, and Keychron sells it for $119.99. Notebookcheck's review (Julian van der Merwe, published 2026-06-05, updated 2026-10-05) tested it and found three things the spec sheet doesn't show:

  • Battery: 3.6% drain per hour with the backlight at maximum over 2.4 GHz, or about 27 h per charge. Keychron's 55 h figure assumes the backlight is off, and Notebookcheck found it accurate under that condition.
  • Double inputs at the lowest settings: at 0.2 mm actuation with 0.1 mm Rapid Trigger sensitivity, some keys registered twice. Notebookcheck traced this to the key bouncing as it returned to the top. Raising both settings to 0.3 mm resolved it completely, and Notebookcheck saw no missed inputs.
  • Size: 20.5 mm tall at the front with keycaps (16.1 mm without), and 560 g in Notebookcheck's spec table.

For a 75% layout instead of tenkeyless, the NuPhy Air75 HE is the wired magnetic alternative. Geek Gaming Tricks lists it at 8,000 Hz over the cable, 0.1–3.3 mm actuation and 12.8 mm at the front case edge, with no wireless mode. That height excludes the keycaps, so it can't be compared directly with the K3 HE's 20.5 mm. The G515 RAPID TKL link goes to Logitech's own store, and we don't earn from it.

Not recommended

Logitech G915 X LIGHTSPEED TKL for competitive play. At $209.99 it is the most expensive board here. Its GL mechanical switches fire at a fixed 1.3 mm, and Logitech lists no adjustable actuation or Rapid Trigger for it. A shopper who wants Logitech for FPS gets magnetic switches and Rapid Trigger in the G515 RAPID TKL for $60 less. The G915 X makes sense only if wireless runtime matters more than strafing response. The Keychron K3 Ultra 8K also deserves caution: MKB Guide notes early reports of missed or duplicated characters.
