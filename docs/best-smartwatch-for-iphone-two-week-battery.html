Two weeks with the screen on or off: what reviewers counted

Amazfit's "up to" figures assume light use. The Bip Max page footnote defines typical use this way: a static watch face, heart rate every 5 minutes, three 30-minute GPS workouts a week, 150 notifications a day and 5 minutes of screen time a day. The always-on display is not part of that test. Amazfit's own Active Max support guide says turning it on "greatly reduces the battery life".

  • Active Max: Philipp Briel (Basic Tutorials, February 2026) wore it with the always-on display, continuous blood-oxygen measurement and sleep tracking on, and it "lasted a good 10 days". He adds that switching the always-on display off gives "another three to four days". the5krunner estimated continuous GPS at about 40 hours, against Amazfit's claimed 64 hours, at minimum brightness with the always-on display off.
  • Bip 6: Luiza Mosneagu (Droid.tools) got "eight days, to be precise" with several GPS workouts, heart-rate monitoring and the always-on display. Carlsen Martin (Gizbot) got up to eight days with similar settings and says it reaches 14 days only on more conservative settings.
  • Active 2 (Round): Carlsen Martin (Gizbot) got about six days per charge with the always-on display, raise-to-wake and auto-brightness on.
  • Bip Max: There is no reviewer day count yet. Amazfit rates it 20 days typical, 10 days heavy and 7 days with the always-on display.

On an iPhone: all four watches use the Zepp app, which needs iOS 15.0 or later in the US App Store. All four are on the Zepp OS 5 list, so they can reply to WhatsApp, Telegram, Signal, Messenger, Slack and Discord chats through Zepp Messenger, which needs a free Beeper account. Zepp describes sending SMS from the watch only for Android users, and iMessage replies are not possible because Beeper connects iMessage only on a Mac. So replies don't separate these watches; battery does. $79.99: https://dealectric.com/go/amazfit-bip-6

Not recommended

Amazfit Active 2 (Round). It costs $99.99, the same as the Bip Max, but Amazfit rates it at half the battery (10 days against 20). Gizbot's reviewer got about six days with the always-on display on. Even its best-case rating falls short of two weeks.
