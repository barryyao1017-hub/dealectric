AVS (Adjustable Voltage Supply, part of USB PD 3.2) lets an iPhone 18 Pro or Pro Max ask the charger for an in-between voltage. The Pro Max asks for about 17.4 V at 3 A, per Charge-Test. Apple says 50% in around 15 minutes needs its 40W Dynamic adapter or a third-party adapter that is 60 W-capable, uses AVS and supports USB-PD 3.2. Without AVS the phone still fast charges at the fixed 15 V level. The AVS label on the box is not proof: Charge-Test lists several chargers that advertise SPR AVS yet fell back to fixed PD at about 45 W. Each row above therefore names who confirmed AVS, and claims that come only from the maker are marked. Dealectric does not test units; every figure here comes from the lab or maker named next to it.

What the differences mean

  • 60 W is the charger's ceiling, not the phone's draw. On Apple's adapter the iPhone 18 Pro drew 17.45 V × 2.42 A = 42.3 W (充电头网, ChargerLAB's Chinese site). The Pro Max drew 17.35 V × 2.95 A = 51.23 W with a 52.9 W peak (ChargerLAB): 52.9 ÷ 60 = 88% of the label.
  • Without AVS the phone takes fixed 15 V. Apple's own 140W MacBook adapter gave the Pro Max 14.44 V × 2.98 A = 42.97 W (ChargerLAB). On the smaller Pro it gave 14.46 V × 2.85 A = 41.2 W (充电头网).
  • Time AVS saves to 50%. Smaller Pro: 16 − 15 = 1 minute (充电头网). Pro Max: 17 − 15 = 2 minutes (ChargerLAB) or 18 − 15 = 3 minutes (Charge-Test). AVS does not finish a full charge sooner: on the smaller Pro the AVS adapter took 1 h 24 min against 1 h 17 min without it, 84 − 77 = 7 minutes slower (充电头网).
  • A second device can take the phone off AVS. The AH22's 45 W per port is less than the Pro Max's 17.4 V × 3 A = 52.2 W request.

Why the phone's port needs more than 45 W to itself

Under USB PD 3.2, a charger's rated power sets how much current its AVS mode can give above 15 V. GW Instek's summary of the spec: a source rated above 45 W and up to 60 W offers AVS from 9 to 20 V at 3 A, while a 27–45 W source offers it only from 9 to 15 V. TinkerVault's meter read the AVS of one 45 W charger, the Baseus EnerFill FN22, as 9–20 V with limits of 3 A and 2.25 A: 15 V × 3 A and 20 V × 2.25 A both come to 45 W. Under either reading, a 45 W port cannot meet the Pro Max's request: 17.4 V × 2.25 A = 39 W, which is less than the 45 W fixed 15 V level. The smaller Pro's 2.42 A is also above 2.25 A.

That is what a second device does to a multi-port charger. On the Baseus PicoGo Pro AH22, techtest.org found SPR AVS at 9–20 V and 5 A and confirmed AVS fast charging with an iPhone 18 Pro Max. With two devices plugged in, though, each port drops to 45 W. ESR states a 45 W maximum total for its Xtend 45W when both ports are in use, and Belkin gives 45 W + 20 W for its UltraCharge 67W. By the rule above, the phone on any of these falls back to fixed 15 V while the second device charges. No lab has tested that case, so this is a reading of the spec, not a measurement. Apple's adapter and the Nexode Air have a single port, so they never split. If one charger has to serve a laptop too, the AH22 still gives the phone AVS whenever it charges alone. $59.99: https://dealectric.com/go/baseus-picogo-pro-ah22-100w-3-port-display-charger

The Nexode Air 65W has its own catch. Michael Barton at techtest.org found three hardware versions sold under one model number, with the same print and the same housing. Version 1 has no AVS. Version 2 has AVS but does not hold 65 W. Version 3 has both. The only measurable clue is weight: 76 g for version 3 against about 82 g for the older two. Charge-Test confirmed AVS only on its own tested unit. Weigh yours when it arrives, and return it if it weighs about 82 g. $24.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nexode-air-65w-gan-charger

Does Apple's MacBook charger have AVS? No. ChargerLAB measured Apple's 140W at 42.97 W and its 70W at 43.31 W over fixed PD on the Pro Max.

Not recommended

UGREEN Nexode Pro 160W 5-Port, bought for iPhone AVS. UGREEN lists AVS on its USB-C 1 and 2 ports, but the labs disagree on whether the phone uses it. techtest.org found the iPhone 18 Pro Max did not use AVS on this charger, and Charge-Test found the iPhone did not use the X774's AVS profile. ChargerLAB's unit did negotiate 18 V / 3 A at nearly 52 W, but ChargerLAB gave no model number. It is a strong laptop charger, but don't buy it for the iPhone's AVS tier.
