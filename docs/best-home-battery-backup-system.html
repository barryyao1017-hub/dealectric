Best home battery backup system

Short answer. The best home battery backup system for most houses is the BLUETTI Apex 300 ($1,499 on bluettipower.com, $1,424.05 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-apex-300): 2,764.8 Wh, 3,840 W, 120 V and 240 V from one unit, and a 20 W idle draw, the lowest independently measured in this class (Anker rates its new S5000 at 8.8 W, not yet tested by a reviewer). Idle drain is the concern that decides a backup battery, because the battery sits waiting for months. The Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus measured 112 W idle before its June 2025 firmware update, enough to empty its own 3,840 Wh in about 34 hours with nothing plugged in; the Apex 300 at 20 W holds a charge for about six days. Add a B300K battery for 5,529.6 Wh at $2,599 (2026-11-07), and the Home Integration Kit ($699 single, $799 parallel) wires it to a sub-panel.

If you need a true whole-house battery in a box, the honest overall winner is the EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra: 6,144 Wh and 7,200 W, enough for a 3-ton central air unit, but it was $4,199 and sold out at EcoFlow on 2026-11-07. Among brands we link, the Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus ($2,599.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/anker-solix-f3800-plus) wins that segment: 6,000 W at 120/240 V, a maker-sold sub-panel kit ($1,799.99) and an optional $2,000 electrician install. For a permanent wall-mounted system, the Tesla Powerwall 3 (13.5 kWh, 11.5 kW, 10-year warranty) is the benchmark; it is sold only through installers and needs no solar.

Whole house battery backup without solar is fine: every unit here recharges from a wall outlet or a gas generator. What you lose without solar is the ability to recharge during a multi-day outage, so size for the longest outage you expect or keep a small generator to recharge from.

What owners complain about

Idle drain comes up more than anything else. A home backup battery spends 99% of its life waiting, so a unit that burns 100 W just being switched on is half empty when the lights go out. The F3800 Plus measured 112 W idle; Anker's June 2025 firmware says it cuts that by half, and an owner reported the unit still ran empty in just over two days left idle. The Apex 300 measured 18 to 20 W in three separate tests.

Random AC shutoff is the Apex 300's own forum thread. Several owners saw AC output switch off when the stack hit 100% charge, mostly with an older B300 battery attached, and some logged a battery overvoltage error. Later firmware (DSP V2174.12 and newer BMS builds) stopped it for most posters, but not all. Update the firmware the day it arrives and test a full charge before you rely on it.

Support and add-ons. BLUETTI has no phone line; tickets take 24 to 48 hours. The Apex 300 has no USB or 12 V ports, so the Hub D1 ($299) is a near-mandatory extra. Anker's weak spots are the 25 A outlet labelled 30 A, proprietary solar plugs and 240 V output that drops when you charge from a 120 V outlet.

Weight. The Apex 300 is 83.8 lb with no wheels; the F3800 Plus is 136.7 lb on wheels; the DELTA Pro Ultra is about 187 lb and splits into two 93 lb halves. Plan where it lives before it arrives.

Which one by need

  • Fridge, furnace blower, Wi-Fi, sump pump, no electrician: Apex 300. Plug the furnace in through a $100 to $200 furnace transfer switch if you want it on battery; the 20 ms or 0 ms UPS mode keeps a sump pump and CPAP running through the switchover.
  • 240 V well pump, central air or a dryer through a sub-panel: F3800 Plus with the Smart Home Power Kit, or the DELTA Pro Ultra when EcoFlow has stock. Both need a licensed electrician and a permit for the sub-panel.
  • Most watt-hours per dollar for 120 V loads: Growatt HELIOS 3600 ($1,198.98; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/growatt-helios-3600) gives 3,686 Wh for $90 more than the Apex 300, but a single unit is 120 V only and it has few independent tests.
  • One unit with the most storage and UL 9540 listing: Anker SOLIX S5000 ($2,599.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/anker-solix-s5000), 5,024 Wh and 4,500 W, if a fire-code listing matters to your insurer or inspector.
  • Permanent, hands-off, runs the whole panel: Tesla Powerwall 3. Installer quotes in 2026 guides run roughly $12,000 to $16,000 for one unit, and the 30% federal battery credit ended for systems placed in service after 2025, so without solar or a time-of-use rate it is backup insurance, not a bill-cutter.

Not recommended

Any 120 V-only station for a 240 V well pump. It cannot start the motor, no matter the watt rating. Check the pump's nameplate first.

OSCAL PowerMax 6000 is the cheapest 6,000 W, 120/240 V unit we track ($1,659.99 on 2026-11-07), but the only retail review found calls it less than perfect as a UPS and no independent idle or noise test exists. Wait for data.

Powerwall without solar to save money. At average US rates the payback runs 15 to 20 years or more. Buy it for outages, not for the bill.
