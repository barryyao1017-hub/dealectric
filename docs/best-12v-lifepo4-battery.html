What the differences mean

Every battery here stores the same 1,280 Wh (12.8 V × 100 Ah; Epoch's 105 Ah gives 1,344 Wh). What separates them is how fast the BMS will let that energy out.

  • Continuous watts = BMS amps × 12.8 V. Vatrer: 150 × 12.8 = 1,920 W. LiTime, Redodo, Battle Born: 100 × 12.8 = 1,280 W. Epoch: 105 × 12.8 = 1,344 W.
  • Inverter draw = AC load ÷ 0.9 efficiency ÷ 12.8 V. A 1,500 W inverter at full load pulls about 130 A, inside Vatrer's 150 A limit and over everyone else's 100 A. A 1,000 W inverter pulls about 87 A, which every battery here carries.
  • Price per continuous watt: Vatrer 1,920 W ÷ $259.99 = 7.4 W per dollar; LiTime Group 24 Bluetooth 1,280 W ÷ $311.09 = 4.1 W per dollar.
  • Run time at a 1,000 W AC load: 1,280 Wh × 0.9 ÷ 1,000 W = about 1.15 hours for any single 100 Ah pack. More output does not mean more energy.

Why the BMS amp rating decides your inverter

The battery management system opens the circuit when current exceeds its continuous rating for more than its short overload window. LiTime's Group 24 Bluetooth allows 500 A, but only for one second; Epoch allows 200 A for ten seconds. Neither window covers a microwave or coffee maker running for minutes, so the continuous figure is the one that matters. Vatrer's page puts the 150 A rating in exactly that context, saying it gives the headroom an RV needs when running an inverter. $259.99 on vatrerpower.com, $247 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/vatrer-12v-100ah-group-24-bluetooth-150a

Redodo makes the opposite point on its Group 24 Bluetooth page: the battery is not for water pumps, electric jacks, elevator lifts, pool vacuums or mowers, all loads with high start-up current. That is a 100 A BMS speaking. For lights, a 12 V fridge, electronics and a 1,000 W inverter it is the cheapest Bluetooth battery with a low-temperature cutoff in the group. $259.99 on redodopower.com, $234 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/redodo-12v-100ah-group-24-bluetooth-low-temp

Epoch publishes something nobody else does: a recommended sustained discharge of 52.5 A (0.5C) alongside the 105 A maximum. Read literally, that means the maker prefers you keep continuous inverter loads near 600 W, even though the BMS allows twice that.

All four brands in the table stop charging at 32°F. The Redodo Self-Heating and the Epoch 105Ah add heating pads that warm the pack below 32°F so charging can resume above 41°F. Epoch's heater runs from charger current, and Epoch states the charger must deliver at least 0.5 A for it to work. $251.99 on redodopower.com, $226.80 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/redodo-12v-100ah-self-heating-lifepo4
