What the differences mean

Each figure below is the maker's total with three ports in use, minus the 2 × 100 W the two laptops take.

  • Anker Prime 200W: 200 W − 2 × 100 W = 0 W for a phone. Anker lists a 200 W total with 2 to 4 ports in use.
  • UGREEN Nexode 200W 4-port: 200 W − 2 × 100 W = 0 W. Its third USB-C port tops out at 30 W in any case.
  • Satechi 200W 6-port: 200 W − 2 × 100 W = 0 W. Satechi's table cuts the second laptop to 45 W once a third device is plugged in.
  • Satechi ChargeView 240W: 240 W − 2 × 100 W = 40 W. This matches Satechi's C1 + C2 + C3 row of 100 + 100 + 40 W.
  • Anker Prime 250W: 250 W − 2 × 100 W = 50 W at most. Anker publishes the 250 W total but no per-port split.
  • Cost per 100 W laptop port: UGREEN $61.99 ÷ 2 = $31.00; Anker Prime 200W $69.99 ÷ 2 = $35.00; ChargeView 240W and Prime 250W $149.99 ÷ 2 = $75.00.

Adding a phone: why the third port decides

A multiport charger splits a fixed total. A third device charges at full speed only if wattage is left after both laptops take 100 W. On the 200 W chargers nothing is left, so the charger has to take power from a laptop.

  • Satechi ChargeView 240W: Satechi's power table keeps two ports at 100 W on 14 of its 20 three-port combinations, for example C1 + C2 + C5 = 100 + 100 + 30 W. Put the laptops on two of C1–C4 and the phone on C5 or C6. C1 + C3 + C4 and C2 + C3 + C4 leave only one port at 100 W, and no four-device combination keeps two ports at 100 W.
  • Satechi 200W 6-port: With two devices, any two of PD1–PD4 give 100 W + 100 W. Every three-device row is 100/45/45, 100/45/20 or 100/20/20 W. Satechi requires e-marked cables above 60 W. On 2026-11-13 it was listed at $114.99 but sold out on satechi.com.
  • Anker Prime 200W: Anker says that when two USB-C ports are in use, “each can deliver up to 100W max output”, and it asks for e-marker cables for 100 W. With three ports in use it lists only a 200 W total, with no per-port split.
  • UGREEN Nexode 200W 4-port: Notebookcheck says the first two USB-C ports (140 W and 100 W) can charge two laptops at 100 W each; C3 is 30 W and USB-A 22.5 W. Notebookcheck (Polly Allcock, 2026-05-11) passed on customer reports of high temperature and erratic power output on the 2025 unit (Amazon ASIN B0DG91Q6YD) and reported a revised US unit (B0G7HZT7DN). It names no marking that tells the two apart. UGREEN's US store lists it at $61.99, in stock: $61.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nexode-200w-4-port-charger
  • Anker Prime 250W: Anker gives up to 240 W with two ports in use and 250 W with three, but says its distribution is “for illustrative purposes only” and may change with firmware.

Long sessions: techtest.org (Michael Barton, 2024-07-25) held the Anker Prime 200W at a constant 2 × 100 W on two electronic loads. After about 1.5 hours it throttled to 70 W per port, 30% below its rating. Barton judged 100–150 W sustainable indefinitely and called the throttling no drama in practice.

Not recommended

The Anker Prime 250W, for this job. At $149.99 it costs $80.00 more than the Prime 200W, yet Anker never lists 100 W + 100 W for it, and for three devices it gives only a 250 W total with no per-port split. The Satechi ChargeView 240W costs the same and publishes its split.
