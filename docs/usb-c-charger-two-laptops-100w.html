How the sharing works

A multi-port charger has one power budget and a table that splits it by which ports are in use. Two laptops only get 100 W each if the total is at least 200 W and the table assigns 100 + 100 to the two ports you use: on the UGREEN 200W those are the two 100 W USB-C ports; plug a phone into the third port and a review reports the two laptops drop to 65 W each. UGREEN's Nexode Pro 160W gives 100 W + 60 W with two laptops and 65 + 65 with four devices; the Nexode 140W gives 65 + 65. On the 500W, port C1 can deliver 240 W (48 V) and C2–C5 100 W each, so two laptops get 240 + 100; UGREEN's UK and EU pages are reported to limit C2–C5 to 60–65 W while C1 draws its full 240 W, so a 240 W gaming laptop and a 100 W laptop may not both get full power.

Cables and the 140 W question

USB-C stops at 100 W (20 V, 5 A) unless the charger, the laptop and the cable all support PD 3.1 Extended Power Range; an EPR cable is e-marked and rated 240 W (50 V, 5 A). Two 100 W laptops need two 100 W (5 A) cables, which most laptops ship with. 140 W to one port needs the 240 W cable. Apple lists USB-C fast charging at 140 W only for the 16-inch MacBook Pro from November 2023 on (M3 Pro/Max and later) with its 240 W cable; earlier 16-inch models take 140 W only through MagSafe 3 and 100 W over USB-C.
