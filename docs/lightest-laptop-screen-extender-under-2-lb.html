Under 2 lb is the line because that is where makers themselves stop calling a screen travel weight. Mobile Pixels' travel weight table (Oct 18, 2026) rates every screen from 1.3 to 1.72 lb ultralight or carry-on friendly, calls 1.85 lb borderline and files its own 2.9 lb stacked screen under hotel-desk setups. Beside a 2.7 lb 13-inch MacBook Air (Apple's figure), a 2 lb screen adds 74% to the carried weight (2.0 ÷ 2.7). Every row above is under 2 lb by its maker's own figure. What the differences mean

  • Headroom under the line. Duex Plus DS: 2.0 − 1.3 = 0.7 lb (11.2 oz). Duex Max DS: 2.0 − 1.8 = 0.2 lb (3.2 oz). Fold 16 QHD: 2.0 − 1.89 = 0.11 lb (1.8 oz). No maker page read for this table gives a weight for magnets or cables, so the headroom is what is left for them.
  • Screen per pound on the lid. A 16:9 screen's area is diagonal² × 0.4273. Plus DS: 13.3² × 0.4273 = 75.6 sq in ÷ 1.3 lb = 58 sq in per lb. Max DS: 14.1² × 0.4273 = 85.0 sq in ÷ 1.8 lb = 47. The Max DS gives 12% more screen (9.4 ÷ 75.6) for 38% more weight (0.5 ÷ 1.3).
  • Screen per pound on the desk. Fold 15.6: 15.6² × 0.4273 = 104.0 sq in ÷ 1.65 lb = 63. Fold 16 QHD (16:10, diagonal² × 0.4494): 115.1 sq in ÷ 1.89 lb = 61. Both beat the lid screens per pound, and neither puts weight on the hinge.
  • In the bag with a 13-inch MacBook Air (2.7 lb, Apple): 2.7 + 1.3 = 4.0 lb with the Plus DS, 2.7 + 1.8 = 4.5 lb with the Max DS. This is a weight illustration, not a fit claim.

Video path decides whether any of them is a one-cable screen. All four take video natively over USB-C with no driver, but only from a USB-C port that outputs video (DisplayPort Alt Mode). On a laptop without one, Mobile Pixels' Duex Max DS guide adds the HDMI cable, so plan on a second cable in the kit. Each row adds one screen and uses one of the laptop's external-display outputs.

What each maker's weight covers

None of the maker pages read for this table says its figure includes the mount, the magnets or a cable. Mobile Pixels' table lists the Duex Plus DS at 1.3 lb and the same article says to weigh the whole kit, case, cables and charger included. Its comparison tool gives the Duex Max DS as 1.8 lb with no inclusion note. The Fold page lists the magnetic cover as a separate box item beside the 1.65 lb and 1.89 lb figures. Treat every weight in the table as the screen as its maker states it.

The older Duex Lite 12.5-inch is listed at the same 1.3 lb on PCWorld Shop's listing, with the USB-C cable, HDMI cable, 8 adhesive tapes and 4 magnetic adhesives listed apart from the weight. The smaller screen saves no weight: 12.5² × 0.4273 = 66.8 sq in ÷ 1.3 lb = 51 sq in per lb against the Plus DS's 58. That listing was marked sold out on Nov 26, 2026, and it notes "Driver required" on the USB-A plus HDMI path, where Mobile Pixels' Duex DS listing says no driver or software is needed.

On hinge load, Mobile Pixels' installation guide gives no figure. MikeShouts' review describes the Plus DS as "weighing down on an open hinge of a laptop". Howard Sneider at The Gadgeteer wrote of the SideTrak Swivel 14 that its kickstand "can help on lighter-weight laptops which may fall backwards with the weight of the external monitor". That SideTrak is 1.5 lb in the site specs Sneider reprinted in January 2024, with his warning that the manual differs. SideTrak's store did not load on Nov 26, 2026, so it has no priced row here.

Left out for weight: Xebec Tri-Screen 2 at exactly 2 lb (Xebec's help center, two 10.1-inch screens), which is on the line and not under it; Mobile Pixels Duex Float 2 Pro at 2.9 lb and Trio 3 Pro at 4.13 lb (Mobile Pixels).

Not recommended for a 2 lb limit: Xebec Snap

Xebec's help center lists the 13.3-inch Snap Screen at "1lb per screen", the only maker figure below 1.3 lb we read for a lid-attached screen. The screen cannot be used alone: Xebec's store says the Snap Bracket is "Required for use with the Xebec Snap Screen". Xebec's Snap spec list gives 1.5 lb for the part with a 225° range of motion made of a TPE and ABS/PC blend, which its help center names as the bracket's material. One screen mounted: 1 + 1.5 = about 2.5 lb. Two screens: 2 × 1 + 1.5 = about 3.5 lb. Cables are extra.

The Snap also uses a DisplayLink 6000-series chip, and Xebec says connection over USB-A needs the free DisplayLink driver. It starts at $529 on Xebec's Snap page and $609 on its store listing (both read Nov 26, 2026), against $183.99 for the Duex Plus DS. It is a modular two-screen system, not a sub-2 lb travel screen.
