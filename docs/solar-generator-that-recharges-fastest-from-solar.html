What the differences mean

Hours of full sun to refill is the battery's watt-hours divided by the solar watts the station actually takes in. Each line below is that sum; the measured lines use the energy a named reviewer metered going in to fill the battery from empty, which is more than the rated capacity because charging loses some of it. Dealectric does not test units.

  • DELTA 3 Plus: rated 1,024 Wh ÷ 1,000 W = 1.02 h; measured 1,180 Wh ÷ 1,000 W = 1.18 h. EcoFlow's 70-minute claim (1.17 h) sits on the measured line.
  • Elite 100 V2: 1,024 ÷ 1,000 = 1.02 h; BLUETTI claims 70 min. 1,000 W ÷ 20 A = 50 V, so only a single string working at 50 V or more, and at or under 60 V open-circuit, reaches the full input.
  • Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus: 5,040 ÷ 5,200 = 0.97 h with both inputs full; 5,040 ÷ 4,000 = 1.26 h on the high-voltage input alone.
  • Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus: 3,840 ÷ 3,200 = 1.2 h; Anker claims 1.5 h.
  • Apex 300 + B300K + SolarX 4K: 5,529.6 ÷ 4,000 = 1.38 h; BLUETTI claims 1.9 h.
  • C1000 Gen 2: rated 1,024 ÷ 600 = 1.71 h; measured 1,110 ÷ 600 = 1.85 h.
  • AC180: rated 1,152 ÷ 500 = 2.3 h; measured 1,360 ÷ 500 = 2.72 h.

The big stations refill in about an hour only with an array sized to their input. With the same 800 W of panels, wired inside each input's limits, the DELTA 3 Plus refills in 1,024 ÷ 800 = 1.28 h, the F3800 Plus in 3,840 ÷ 800 = 4.8 h and the Jackery 5000 Plus in 5,040 ÷ 800 = 6.3 h, before losses. So in one day the limit is usually the panel watts you add, not the station. Where the big stations win is energy stored per sunny day: five full-sun hours at 5,200 W offer 26 kWh, more than the Jackery's 5,040 Wh battery holds, while the DELTA 3 Plus is full after about 1.2 h and the rest of the day's sun goes to whatever it is running. For the largest single-unit input, the EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra takes 5,600 W (4,000 W at 80-450 V plus 1,600 W at 30-150 V) into 6,144 Wh, 6,144 ÷ 5,600 = 1.10 h (Solar Waypoint's spec table, read 2026-11-17). Measured energy to fill each station

A maker's refill time assumes the station takes its full solar input from empty to full. The battery takes in more energy than its rating, because the charger and cells lose some as heat. Three named reviewers metered that energy filling a station from empty: StorageReview measured 1,180 Wh into the DELTA 3 Plus (86.8% recharge efficiency, charge source not stated), Notebookcheck measured 1,110 Wh into the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 on an AC charge and StorageReview measured 1,360 Wh from the wall into the BLUETTI AC180. Solar runs through the station's DC MPPT input rather than its AC charger, so solar losses may differ, but those figures give an honest floor: 1.18 h, 1.85 h and 2.72 h at each station's maximum solar input. On solar itself, The Solar Lab reported the DELTA 3 Plus reaching 'really close to that 1,000W combined input' through its two inputs with 415 W bifacial panels, without logging a time to full. No named reviewer has published a measured energy-to-fill for the Elite 100 V2, the Jackery 5000 Plus, the F3800 Plus or the Apex 300, so their hours in the table are rated arithmetic only. buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DCC2BVFW?tag=dealectric-20

Which power station takes the fewest hours of sun to refill with two panels

The EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus, because each panel gets its own input. EcoFlow's worked example uses its 400 W rigid panel at 37.1 V open-circuit and 12.9 A. On the DELTA 3 Plus each panel sits on its own 11-60 V, 500 W input (37.1 V and 400 W both pass), so two give 800 W and a refill in 1,024 ÷ 800 = 1.28 h before losses. The same pair does not fit the single input of the other 1 kWh stations: in series it reaches 74.2 V (2 × 37.1), over the 60 V limit of the Elite 100 V2 and the C1000 Gen 2; in parallel it draws 25.8 A (2 × 12.9), over the Elite 100 V2's 20 A and the C1000 Gen 2's 14.5 A. EcoFlow's own FAQ rules the same pair out on the single-input DELTA 3. To reach 1,000 W on the $499 on bluettipower.com, $474.05 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-100-v2, pick panels whose series string works at 50 V or more and stays at or under 60 V open-circuit.

Not recommended

The Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 with two small panels wired in parallel. Below 29 V its input accepts only 8.2 A, so two 18-20 V panels in parallel top out at 28 V × 8.2 A = about 230 W, and the refill stretches from Anker's 1.8 h to 1,024 ÷ 230 = 4.5 h before losses. Anker's own two-panel route to 600 W is a 400 W plus a 200 W panel on its single input, working at 41.4 V or more.
