What the differences mean

  • One tank against one charge. Honda rates the EU2200i at 8.1 h on a 0.95-gal tank at ¼ load: 1,800 W × 0.25 = 450 W, and 450 W × 8.1 h = 3,645 Wh. The Elite 100 V2 delivered 946 Wh in TechReviewer's test, so 3,645 ÷ 946 = 3.9 charges per tank.
  • Headline watts. The '2200' in the Honda's name is its maximum. Its continuous rating is 1,800 W, the same as the Elite 100 V2's 1,800 W, so continuous power doesn't decide between them.
  • Energy cost. A tank costs 0.95 gal × $3.8724 (AAA national average today) = $3.68. Divided by 3.645 kWh, that's $1.01 per kWh at ¼ load, before oil and upkeep. A full 1,024 Wh recharge from the grid costs 1.024 kWh × 18.16¢ (EIA US residential average) = $0.19. StorageReview measured 1,180 Wh drawn to refill the DELTA 3 Plus, which still comes to only $0.21.
  • Light loads. TechReviewer found the Elite 100 V2's usable energy drops to about 80% at very low loads, because the unit's own consumption takes a bigger share. That leaves about 820 Wh for a router and phones.
  • Price per stored Wh. Elite 100 V2: $499 ÷ 1,024 Wh = $0.49. Elite 200 V2: $899 ÷ 2,080 Wh = $0.43.

Energy per fill: the figures behind the 4-to-1

Generators (our arithmetic from the makers' runtime ratings; no maker publishes a Wh-per-tank figure):

  • Honda EU2200i: 3,645 Wh per 0.95 gal at ¼ load, 5,760 Wh at rated load. That's about 3.8 kWh per gallon at ¼ load.
  • Champion 201530 2500W Dual Fuel Inverter: up to 11.5 h at 25% load on its 1.05-gal tank. 0.25 × 1,850 W running = 462.5 W, and 462.5 W × 11.5 h = 5,319 Wh. On a 20-lb propane tank Champion lists up to 25 h at 25% load: 0.25 × 1,665 W = 416.25 W, and 416.25 W × 25 h = 10,406 Wh, about 11 Elite 100 V2 charges. Its continuous output on propane is 10% lower (1,665 ÷ 1,850).
  • EcoFlow Smart Generator 3000 (Dual Fuel), $689 on EcoFlow's US store today: EcoFlow claims 20 kWh from a standard LPG tank, about 21 charges of a 946 Wh station. That's EcoFlow's claim and the page gives no load. EcoFlow says its automatic start-stop works only with the DELTA 3, DELTA 3 Plus and DELTA 3 Ultra Plus, and that it is no longer sold in California. EcoFlow rates it at 69 dB at 23 ft.

Power stations (named reviewers' measurements, 1,024–1,070 Wh rated):

  • BLUETTI Elite 100 V2: 946 Wh at a constant 500 W (TechReviewer)
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus: 910 Wh, full to empty (StorageReview)
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: 907 Wh at a 1 kW heater load (The Technology Man); 850 Wh at about 250 W on the EU 230 V version (Notebookcheck)
  • Jackery Explorer 1000 v2: 902 Wh (nextpit)

These tests used different loads, so treat the 902–946 Wh spread as close. Every 1 kWh unit here delivers about a quarter of one Honda tank. The two can also work together. A station paired with a generator gives silent battery power most of the time and the generator only to refill it. The EcoFlow pairing is the one listed here where the generator starts and stops itself, using the buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DCC2BVFW?tag=dealectric-20. Both generators shut themselves off if carbon monoxide builds up (Honda CO-MINDER, Champion CO Shield). Champion says its system is no substitute for an indoor CO alarm. Honda lists this EU2200i as not for sale in California.
