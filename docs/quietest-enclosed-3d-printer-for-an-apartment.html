What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test printers. Every reading below comes from a named tester or from the maker, and each derived figure shows its arithmetic. The testers used different rooms, meters and, mostly, unstated distances, so treat gaps of a few dB between testers as indicative; gaps of 10 dB or more hold up.

  • Kobra S1, claim vs measurement: 59.6 − 44 = 15.6 dB above Anycubic's Quiet-mode figure, and 59.6 − 46 = 13.6 dB above its Standard-mode figure.
  • Could distance explain it? Free-field sound falls about 6 dB per doubling of distance, so 40 cm → 1 m is 20 × log10(100 ÷ 40) = 8.0 dB. Even if igor'sLAB measured the Kobra S1 at 40 cm, as it did the P1S, that is 59.6 − 8.0 ≈ 51.6 dB at 1 m, still 51.6 − 44 = 7.6 dB over Anycubic's 1 m figure.
  • Same tester, same site: igor'sLAB read the P1S at 64.7 dB(A) and the Kobra S1 at 59.6 dB(A): 64.7 − 59.6 = 5.1 dB apart.
  • Centauri Carbon 2 vs Kobra S1: 59.6 − 47 = 12.6 dB between the Kobra S1's reading and the top of Notebookcheck's CC2 average (different testers).
  • P2S vs P1S: 72 − 65 = 7 dB quieter in the same 3D Printed Decor test.
  • Add-on boxes add noise: QIDI Q2 with QIDI Box 65 − 56 = 9 dB louder (androidpctv); Anycubic's own ACE 2 Pro rating is 48 − 44 = 4 dB above the printer's Quiet-mode figure, and 55 − 44 = 11 dB above it while drying.

Who measured what, and how

Notebookcheck (Vineet Washington) measured the Elegoo Centauri Carbon 2 at an average of about 44–47 dB while printing, with peaks to 54 dB, and noted a mild, constant beep at idle. The review does not give the distance or print mode. Elegoo's own 45 dB comes from its lab with Elegoo PLA in Silent Mode and the auxiliary part-cooling fan off, so expect more noise on prints that need that fan.

igor'sLAB (Tim Kutzner) is the only lab reading of the Anycubic Kobra S1: 59.6 dB(A) in operation, with no fans audible at idle. The same tester read the Bambu P1S at 64.7 dB(A) from about 40 cm in front of the closed door, and the open-frame Flashforge Adventurer 5M at 62.3 dB(A), mostly from its 3010 hotend fan. Anycubic's ≤44 dB is a 1 m figure in Quiet mode, and it carries the caveat that individual units may deviate.

3D Printed Decor, in a 600-hour three-printer test reported by StarterBench, measured the P1S at 72 dB and the P2S at 65 dB, with no distance given. Bambu's only published figure is for the P2S: below 50 dB in Silent Mode at 1 m. androidpctv measured the QIDI Q2 at 56 dB in normal use and 65 dB with the QIDI Box attached; QIDI publishes no noise figure for the Q2.

Noise is only one apartment factor. If you print ABS or ASA, chamber heat matters too; see which enclosed 3D printer heats up the fastest.

Not recommended

Buying the Anycubic Kobra S1 for its 44 dB figure. It is a printer-only, Quiet-mode, 1 m maker figure. The combo Anycubic sells in the US includes an ACE 2 Pro that Anycubic itself rates at 48 dB, or 55 dB while drying, and igor'sLAB measured the combo at 59.6 dB(A) while printing. The Bambu Lab P1S is the loudest printer here in both tests that measured it (64.7 dB(A) at igor'sLAB, 72 dB at 3D Printed Decor).
