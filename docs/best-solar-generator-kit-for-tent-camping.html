What the differences mean

  • Overnight idle cost. With the AC outlet on, Eco mode off and nothing plugged in, the Elite 30 V2 drew about 19 W (The Technology Man: 100% to 19% in 12 h). 19 W × 8 h = 152 Wh, about two-thirds of the 231 Wh it delivered from AC. With only the DC outputs on it drew about 11 W: 11 W × 8 h = 88 Wh.
  • A 30 W device all night. 30 W × 8 h = 240 Wh. That is more than the 231 Wh measured from AC but fits within the 244 Wh measured from DC, so anything that can run on 12 V should run from the 12 V port.
  • Solar refill, best case. 288 Wh ÷ 100 W = 2.9 h of full sun with the 100 W kit; 288 Wh ÷ 60 W = 4.8 h with the 60 W kit. The 489.99 − 399.99 = $90 step saves 1.9 h of sun per refill.
  • Solar refill, real sun. OutdoorGearLab measured 150 W from BLUETTI's 200 W panel, 75% of its rating. If the 100 W panel gives the same share, that is 75 W: 288 ÷ 75 = 3.8 h.
  • Price per measured watt-hour. Elite 30 V2 + 100 W: 489.99 ÷ 231 = $2.12 per AC watt-hour, panel included. C300X DC + 60 W: 349.99 ÷ 273 = $1.28, but with no AC outlet. Anker C300: 429.99 ÷ 230 = $1.87, with no panel.
  • Adding a second panel. The Elite 30 V2 takes up to 200 W of solar at 12–28 V, and BLUETTI's manual says not to use panels above 28 V open-circuit. Wiring panels in series adds their voltages, so a second panel goes in parallel.

Measured usable energy and idle draw

A power station's watt-hour rating describes its battery, not what reaches your devices. Converting the battery's DC to 120 V AC loses energy, and the inverter draws power even when nothing is plugged in. These are the measured figures, each from a named tester:

For AC power the Elite 30 V2 and the Anker C300 deliver the same energy (231 vs 230 Wh), but the BLUETTI gives twice the AC output (600 W vs 300 W). The DC-only C300X DC delivers 42 Wh more because it has no inverter. Those 42 Wh only help if every device you bring charges over USB or 12 V.

Idle draw separates a kit that lasts the night from one that doesn't. BLUETTI's store page claims 'only 4.5W standby'. The runtime appendix of its own manual assumes about 9 W of self-consumption. The Technology Man measured about 19 W with the AC outlet on and Eco mode off, on a UK 230 V unit, so a US 120 V unit may differ. In a tent, turn Eco mode on or switch the AC outlet off when nothing is charging, and charge phones from the USB-C ports. The same reviewer found that the 1,500 W 'Lifting Power' works by lowering the output voltage, so it suits only simple resistive loads such as hair dryers, not motors. Treat 600 W as the limit.

BLUETTI's manual lists a charging range of 32–104°F (0–40°C). Keep the station in shade on the panel's cable while the panel sits in the sun. On a frosty morning it won't take a charge until it has warmed above freezing.
