What the differences mean

Each runtime is the reviewer's measured usable AC energy divided by the blanket's draw plus the station's own draw with the AC outlet on. 60 W and 100 W are the U.S. DOE single and double blanket figures that Tested Watts quotes.

Why measured watt-hours and idle draw set the length of the night

A heated blanket is a small load, so the power the station spends just keeping its inverter running is a large share of what it delivers. Gidon at The Technology Man left the Elite 30 V2's AC outlet on with nothing plugged in and ECO mode off. The battery went from 100% to 19% in 12 hours, about 19 W (81% × 288 Wh = 233 Wh ÷ 12 h). On a 60 W blanket that is 19 ÷ 79 = 24% of everything the battery puts out. The Solar Lab measured 13.8 W idle on the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 and calculated about 16 W on the Elite 100 V2. BLUETTI lists 15 W for the AC180, and Jackery's help centre lists 18 W standby for the Explorer 1000 v2 (230 V model).

The second number is how much of the rated battery reaches the outlet. Kenji Mowrey at OutdoorGearLab measured 950 Wh from the AC180, which is 82% of its 1,152 Wh (light-bar jig at 80% load). He measured 910 Wh from the Jackery 1000 v2 (85%), and 890 Wh each from the C1000 Gen 2 and the Elite 100 V2. Those tests ran at heavier loads than a blanket. Subtracting the idle draw on top of them counts some inverter loss twice, so the hours in the table are more likely to be a little low than high.

Two settings can end the night early. The AC180 manual says ECO mode turns the AC output off when the load stays at a set 15–30 W or less for 1, 2, 3 or 4 hours. A blanket on low whose thermostat has clicked off can stay under that for long enough, so turn ECO off before bed. Jackery says the Explorer 1000 v2's Energy Saving Mode can be switched off. On the blanket side, WattFort reports that Sunbeam's manual says not to use its heated bedding with inverters. It also reports that Sunbeam's timer resets after a power cut, so the blanket stays off until someone presses its power button. Check your blanket's manual first.

Not recommended

The BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 should not run a 120 V blanket overnight. With 231 Wh measured and about 19 W of inverter overhead, it lasts about 2.9 h at 60 W. It does make sense for a 12 V blanket on its DC car port, where The Technology Man measured 244 Wh and there is no inverter to run: $289.99 on bluettipower.com, $275.50 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-30-v2.
