What the differences mean

  • Parking mode in the box: the Rexing R4-RD costs $20 more than the R4 ($279.99 − $259.99) and comes with the Smart Hardwire Kit and a built-in 360° motion parking monitor. The R4 needs Rexing's Type-C Smart Hardwire Kit, sold separately.
  • Sharper front from Rexing: the R4 Pro costs $60 more than the R4 ($319.99 − $259.99) for a 2K front with a Sony STARVIS 2 sensor. It still needs a hardwire kit for parking mode.
  • Sensors: Vantrue puts STARVIS 2 on all four N5S channels (4 of 4). REDTIGER names one STARVIS 2 sensor on the F17 Plus, on the front (1 of 4), and Rexing names one on the R4 Pro.
  • What the N5S costs extra: $293.99 − $259.99 = $34 more than the R4 buys those four sensors, but its two extra cameras face the seats.
  • Recording time on the F17 Plus: DashCamTalk lists 16 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 46 Mbps across the four channels. 46 × 3,600 ÷ 8 = 20.7 GB an hour, so the 128 GB card in the box holds about 6.2 hours before loop recording overwrites the oldest footage.
  • Cheapest: the Rexing C4 costs $60 less than the R4 ($259.99 − $199.99) and comes with a 64 GB card. GPS needs an adapter and parking mode needs a hardwire kit, both sold separately.

Where the side cameras actually sit

None of these dash cams puts a camera at the doors. Rexing says the R4 has “only two units to install”: the windshield body and the rear camera. That puts the left and right lenses on the windshield body. Each “can be adjusted 180-degrees”, and you “can even choose to turn one or both side cameras in to face your cabin”, with 4 IR lights on each. Rexing's R4-RD listing says its side cameras “can also rotate inward for optional cabin monitoring”, and the C4 listing calls them “cabin and side cameras”. REDTIGER says the F17 Plus monitors “all four sides”, but its own listing calls the extra pair a “360° rotatable dual cabin lens”, and DashCamTalk lists the channels as Front, Rear, Interior and Interior Rear.

So on every camera here, the left and right views are filmed from the windshield through the front door glass, by a lens that doubles as a cabin camera. What differs is where it points. The Rexing lenses can turn to face straight out each side window; the Vantrue N5S cabin cameras face the seats. Ben Andrews of Digital Camera World found that on the N5S, “exterior coverage from the cabin cameras isn't great”, and that at night “very little will be visible through the car's side windows”.

One caveat on image quality: no named reviewer has published a test of the R4 itself. In The Smart Home Hookup's 13-camera test, the R4's sibling R4-RD (also 1080p on all four channels, with side lenses that turn inward) got the tester's verdict of “my least favorite image”. Its parking mode used 0.4 Wh in 30 minutes (0.4 ÷ 0.5 h = 0.8 W). If footage quality matters more to you than aiming at the sides, the Vantrue N5S $293.99: https://dealectric.com/go/vantrue-nexus-5s is the safer buy, and it also covers rideshare passengers.

Not recommended

Botslab G980H. Botslab lists a 3K front and three 1080p cameras, with 120° side cameras on “a detachable magnetic mount”, a 128 GB card, and no hardwire kit for parking mode. DashCamTalk forum reviewer Vortex Radar describes “a pair of side cams slapped onto the side”. The same review says the front has “No HDR, so really struggles at night” and the rear has “subpar video quality”, and calls the whole camera a “crummy half-baked dashcam”.
