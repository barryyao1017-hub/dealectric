Best 4 Channel Dash Cam

Updated 2026-11-14

Short answer. The best 4 channel dash cam is the Vantrue Nexus 5S (N5S) ($293.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/vantrue-nexus-5s): four Sony STARVIS 2 sensors, a 2560 × 1440 rear camera and 4 infrared LEDs on each of its two cabin cameras, so the front seats and back seat both show up on video in a dark car. In a two-month DashCamTalk test, faces were visible in pitch black, with no crashes and no lost files. Parking mode needs a hardwire kit sold separately ($29.99 at Vantrue on 2026-11-14). Its weak spot is the 2592 × 1944 front camera, which has no HDR: night license plates are hard to read.

On a budget, the REDTIGER F17 Plus ($199.99 at REDTIGER on 2026-11-14, 128 GB card included) also records front, both cabin rows and rear. Reviewers rate its low-light video and parking mode below the N5S. If reading plates ahead matters more than a back-row camera, Vantrue's 3-channel Nexus 4 Pro S ($279.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/vantrue-nexus-4-pro-s) has a 4K STARVIS 2 front camera with HDR.

What owners and reviewers complain about

  • Installation takes hours (6 of 10 sources). Routing three or four cables and hardwiring for parking mode took 1.5 hours in New Atlas's test and about 4 hours for one Vantrue store reviewer. Notebookcheck needed a second fuse tap. On the N5S, the rear module carries two video feeds on one cable, so swapping the rear cables kills the cabin feed.
  • Weak night plates (5 sources). DashCamTalk found night plate capture nearly impossible on the N5S front camera, rated the F17 Plus low-light video weaker, and called the original N5 rear camera grainy at night.
  • Parking mode costs extra (5 sources). All three Vantrue models and the F17 Plus need a hardwire kit for parking mode. The F17 Plus parking mode is not buffered, so it can miss the seconds before an event.
  • The back-row view has limits (3 sources). In an SUV with a third row or child seats, the N5S rear cabin camera sees less, and its rear module can't aim down from a headliner.
  • Firmware glitches (3 sources). One N5 test unit stopped recording twice, and an N5S update reset New Atlas's settings.

Which one by need

  • Rideshare, taxi or delivery at night: Vantrue Nexus 5S ($293.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/vantrue-nexus-5s). It is the only one here with STARVIS 2 sensors and IR on both cabin cameras, and DashCamTalk names it its premium 4-channel pick.
  • Tightest budget: REDTIGER F17 Plus, $199.99 at REDTIGER on 2026-11-14 with a 128 GB card, about $94 less than the N5S. Expect weaker night video and audio distortion.
  • Plates ahead matter most: Vantrue Nexus 4 Pro S ($279.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/vantrue-nexus-4-pro-s), with a 4K STARVIS 2 front camera and HDR, at the cost of the second cabin camera.
  • Trucks, RVs and trailers: the N5S takes a 49.2 ft rear cable ($19.99 at Vantrue).

Not recommended

  • Vantrue Nexus 5 (original N5): $265.99 on 2026-11-14, only $28 less than the N5S, with an older 1080p rear camera that DashCamTalk calls grainy at night.
  • Botslab G980H 4-Camera: the only pick here with left and right cameras, but DashCamTalk's April 2026 review found that motion detection uses only the front camera, the impact sensor barely triggers, and the front has no HDR.
