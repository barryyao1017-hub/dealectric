AM or FM: which antenna does the work

On these boomboxes the telescopic aerial is for FM. AM comes in through an antenna built into the case. Sony's CFD-S70 manual lists 'AM: Built-in ferrite bar antenna' and says to 'reorient the unit itself for optimum reception'. Jensen's CD-490 manual tells you to extend and rotate the telescopic antenna for FM, and for AM to hold the unit upright and move it around. GPX's BC112 manual labels its rod 'Telescopic FM Antenna'. So if AM stations are faint, turn the whole radio rather than pulling out the rod. Sony's manual also names mobile phones near the unit as a source of noise.

One Target reviewer of the Jensen CD-560 wrote that it 'doesn't pick many AM stations that my tiny portable radio can do just fine'. If you mostly listen to AM talk or sports, place it near a window and turn it for the clearest signal.

Some CD radios have no AM band at all. Panasonic's own North American spec for the SC-PM270 lists only FM (87.5–108 MHz, 30 presets) and 120 V AC power. Target's listing for the Philips TAZ4300 describes FM with 30 presets and doesn't mention AM. Philips' TAZ6000/10 leaflet lists DAB+/FM radio. If you want AM, buy one of the GPX, Jensen or Sony models. If FM at home is all you need, the buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CZ9Q8332?tag=dealectric-20 has the highest rated output we found (20 W against 3.4 W for the Sony).
