What the differences mean

Every kit in the table is one listing at one price with 600 W of panels in it (640 W for EcoFlow). The panels are the same size, so the batteries decide the outcome: a panel only produces energy while the battery has room for it or something is plugged in. The day figure uses the formula StormWatts publishes, panel watts × 4.5 sun-hours × 0.85. It is an assumption about a middling day, not a measurement.

  • One day from 600 W: 600 × 4.5 × 0.85 = 2,295 Wh. From 640 W: 640 × 4.5 × 0.85 = 2,448 Wh.
  • Share of that day each battery holds: P1800 1,024 ÷ 2,295 = 45%. R2500 V2 1,920 ÷ 2,295 = 84%. The R3500 holds all of it and ends 2,295 ÷ 3,168 = 72% full. The Apex 300 holds all of it and ends 2,295 ÷ 2,764.8 = 83% full. The DELTA 3800 Gen4 ends 2,448 ÷ 3,763 = 65% full.
  • What the smaller batteries leave unused: 2,295 − 1,024 = 1,271 Wh a day on the P1800 kit and 2,295 − 1,920 = 375 Wh on the R2500 V2 kit. That energy exists only if a load runs while the sun is up.
  • A short winter day: at 3 sun-hours the panel gives 600 × 3 × 0.85 = 1,530 Wh. The 1,920 Wh and larger batteries hold all of it; the 1,024 Wh battery holds 1,024 ÷ 1,530 = 67%.
  • Full-sun hours to refill: battery Wh ÷ panel W at rated output, for example 3,168 ÷ 600 = 5.3 h. Real panel output is lower, so real refills take longer.

ALLPOWERS's claim that the R3500 solar-charges in 2.5 hours assumes 2,000 W of panels. With the 600 W in this kit, the arithmetic is the 5.3 hours in the table.

One panel or several. The ALLPOWERS SP039 carries all 600 W in one folding panel of six sections: 44 V, MC-4 output, 27.5 lb, folded 23.6 × 35.7 × 1.9 in. ALLPOWERS lists an MC-4 to XT60 cable and a 1.5 m MC4 extension in the panel's box, so there is no series or parallel wiring to plan. BLUETTI's kit is three 200 W panels into an input rated 12-60 V, and BLUETTI's page says each unit is delivered separately; the station's box list on the kit page shows an AC cable, a car cable, a grounding screw and the manual. StormWatts lists BLUETTI's SP200L 200 W panel at 24.6 V open-circuit. If the kit ships that panel, three in one series string would be 3 × 24.6 = 73.8 V, above the 60 V ceiling, while two in series is 49.2 V, so plan the wiring before the panels arrive. EcoFlow's bundle is four 160 W panels.

Cycle life as each maker states it. ALLPOWERS: over 3,500 charging cycles for the R3500 and over 3,000 for the P1800, with no capacity basis given. BLUETTI: up to 6,000 cycles with 80% capacity retention for the Apex 300. The ALLPOWERS figures cannot be ranked against BLUETTI's without a basis.
