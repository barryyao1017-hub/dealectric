What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure here is either a maker's rating or a measurement credited to a named lab. Each line of arithmetic uses our link prices, cart-tested 2026-11-17, and store pages read 2026-11-17 and 2026-11-18.

  • Price per Wh: the R2500 V2 kit costs $913.49 ÷ 1,920 Wh = $0.476 per Wh. The Elite 200 V2 kit costs $1,424.05 ÷ 2,073.6 Wh = $0.687, which is 0.687 ÷ 0.476 = 1.44, or 44% more per Wh, for 153.6 Wh more battery.
  • Refill from the kit's own panels: 1,920 Wh ÷ 400 W = 4.8 h of full sun at rated output. The R3500 needs 3,168 ÷ 400 = 7.9 h and the Elite 400 needs 3,840 ÷ 400 = 9.6 h, so one day of sun won't refill either one from the panels in its box.
  • Room to add panels: the R2500 V2 accepts up to 1,000 W of solar, leaving 1,000 − 400 = 600 W for panels added later. The Elite 200 V2 and Elite 400 also accept 1,000 W (12–60 V, 20 A).
  • Kit vs parts: at ALLPOWERS' store prices on 2026-11-18, the R2500 V2 station ($649.00) plus the SP037 panel ($379.05) comes to $1,028.05. That is $10.00 less than the $1,038.05 kit price. The code on our link brings the kit to $913.49. BLUETTI's Elite 300 kit is $298 less than buying its parts separately, and the Elite 400 kit is $398 less.
  • Peak loads: ALLPOWERS rates the R2500 V2 at 5,000 W peak. BLUETTI's 3,900 W figure for the Elite 200 V2 is 'Power Lifting' and covers resistive loads only, such as kettles and hair dryers. It is not a surge rating, and BLUETTI's page gives no surge figure.

Price per Wh among 400 W kits, and what it leaves out

Among the 400 W kits on this page that ship today, the R2500 V2 + SP037 has the lowest price per Wh and the lowest price. The S2000 Pro + SP037 costs $4.44 more for 469 Wh less. The R3500 kit is bigger but costs $673.24 more. Only one BLUETTI 400 W kit, the Elite 300, ships before December, and BLUETTI's pages on 2026-11-18 say orders placed on or after Nov 18 for the Elite 200 V2 ship in early January. The Elite 400's page says mid-January. On 2026-11-17, EcoFlow's store marked its DELTA 3 Max 2× 220 W kit as unavailable.

Price per Wh uses rated capacity, so here are the measured figures we have. Notebookcheck got about 2,650 Wh from the Elite 300, which works out to $1,614.05 ÷ 2,650 = $0.609 per measured Wh. StorageReview got 3.07 kWh from the Elite 400 ($1,804.05 ÷ 3,070 = $0.588) and 1.91 kWh from the Elite 200 V2 ($1,424.05 ÷ 1,910 = $0.746). We have no named lab's capacity figure for the R2500 V2, so its $0.476 is based on the rated 1,920 Wh. One caution applies to every ALLPOWERS SP037 kit. ALLPOWERS' own S2000 Pro + SP037 listing calls the bundled panel polycrystalline and says a 100 W panel gives 'about 55W-65W'. At 60%, the R2500 V2 refill becomes 1,920 ÷ (400 × 0.60) = 8.0 h. StorageReview's 2023 test of an S2000 Pro got 920 Wh before the unit shut off with 22% still showing, and it described older NCM cells. That unit was sold as 1,500 Wh, so today's 1,451 Wh S2000 Pro may be different hardware. Even so, it is a reason to choose the LiFePO4 R2500 V2 over the S2000 Pro.

Not recommended

BLUETTI AC180 + 2× 200 W. BLUETTI lists the AC180's solar input as 500 W max, 12–60 V and 10 A. Two 200 W panels wired in parallel run at about 20 V, so 10 A × about 20 V ≈ 200 W, which is half the panel wattage you pay for. Only series wiring (about 40 V × 10 A ≈ 400 W) uses both panels fully. The kit also costs $1,170.40 ÷ 1,152 Wh = $1.016 per Wh, which is 2.1 times the R2500 V2 kit's figure, and it is a pre-order with a Dec 14 dispatch date.
