Short answer. What owners complain about

Across eleven reviews and forum threads on scanners in this class, the same problems repeat.

  • Shiny, black or metallic parts (7 sources). Blue and infrared light both fail on gloss and deep black. Owners of the Seal, Otter Lite, POP 2 and MINI 2 all ended up buying scanning spray or using dry shampoo. The Seal's reviewer at 3D Printing Industry had to wash spray off afterward; the sublimating AESUB-type sprays cost more but vanish on their own.
  • Lost tracking on smooth or symmetrical parts (6 sources). Markerless visual tracking needs surface features. A Seal lost tracking at the edge of a flat tile and duplicated the arms of pliers; a Creality forum owner could not scan a flat black part until stickers went on. Fix: slow down, scan from a feature-rich area, or add LEGO bricks or markers around the object.
  • Software learning curve and crashes (5 sources). JMStudio crashed on a low-spec PC in one test, its advanced tools are unintuitive, and auto-alignment can fuse scans wrongly on symmetrical objects. Revo Scan is more polished but the MINI 2 had alignment and color problems out of the box.
  • PC demands (4 sources). 16 GB RAM is the practical floor for mesh fusion; the MINI 2 wants a recent Core i7. Older laptops stall mid-scan.
  • Dense meshes (3 sources). Seal scans produced STL files heavy enough to slow SolidWorks; expect to decimate and patch holes in Meshmixer or Blender before printing.
  • Buying direct. Nine of the ten Trustpilot reviews of 3dmakerpro.com from 2024 to October 2026 complain about late or missing shipments and slow support. The scanner itself gets good marks from reviewers; the store does not. MatterHackers and Best Buy also stock the Seal if you would rather buy from a US retailer.

Which one, by what you scan

  • Small parts, figurines, knobs, brackets (10 to 300 mm): 3DMakerpro Seal. Measured 0.02 mm on a reference block, color texture, Mac and Windows, and the optional multi-axis turntable automates objects up to 100 mm.
  • 28 mm miniatures, rings, tiny mechanical parts: Revopoint MINI 2. Its 0.02 mm point spacing is the only consumer spec fine enough for faces on a 28 mm figure. Budget for spray and a strong Windows PC.
  • Under $250, shape only: Seal Lite. Same optics and 10 mm minimum as the Seal with 0.07 mm resolution and no color. Good enough for printable geometry of a sculpt or a replacement part.
  • Small objects plus occasional large ones: Creality Otter Lite. It scans from 20 mm up to 2 m, so it covers helmets and car parts the Seal cannot. The wired Basic kit matches the Seal's price; the wireless kit does not.

Not recommended for small objects

  • Creality Ferret SE and Ferret Pro. Minimum object size is 150 mm. Fine for car parts and torsos, useless for anything that fits in your hand.
  • 3DMakerpro Moose and Moose Lite. Medium-format scanners with a 15 mm floor on paper, but 0.07 to 0.10 mm resolution and a wide capture window; 3DMakerpro itself points small-object buyers to the Seal pair.
  • 3DMakerpro Mole for anything under about 30 mm. Cheapest on the list, but its 0.1 mm resolution and 200 × 100 mm window drop detail on thin or tiny parts, and reviewers found alignment struggled on them.
