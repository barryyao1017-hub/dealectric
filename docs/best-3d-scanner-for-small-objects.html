What the differences mean

  • Frame size: the MINI 2's closest frame covers 52 × 64 = 3,328 mm², and the Seal's covers 100 × 75 = 7,500 mm². That is 2.25× more area, so a small part fills less of each Seal frame.
  • Share of the frame: a 15 mm part covers 15 × 15 = 225 mm². That is 225 ÷ 3,328 = 6.8% of the MINI 2's near frame and 225 ÷ 7,500 = 3.0% of the Seal's. Shape tracking has less to work with on the Seal.
  • Detail per millimeter: the MINI 2 has 1 ÷ 0.02 = 50 points per mm, the Seal 1 ÷ 0.05 = 20 and the Seal Lite 1 ÷ 0.07 = 14.3. Fine engraving shows up best on the MINI 2.

Why small parts lose tracking, and how each scanner copes

A handheld scanner builds a model by lining up each new frame with the last one. It does that either by matching the object's shape or by following reflective markers. 3D Printer World's Owen Marsh notes that 'a plain cylinder or repeating pattern gives it fewer clues'. A small, smooth or symmetric part is exactly that case, and it fills only a few percent of the frame.

  • Revopoint MINI 2: it offers both feature and marker tracking. Revopoint's $29.90 Portable Turntable comes with a topper that has markers already on it 'for easier featureless object scanning', so the dots sit around the part instead of on it. Its 3 mm and 6 mm markers are too large to stick on a 10–20 mm part anyway.
  • 3DMakerpro Seal and Seal Lite: both track visually, with no markers. In 3D Printing Industry's review (Alex Tyrer-Jones, 2024), the Seal 'faced challenges when scanning symmetrical models', and 'reference markers had to be added'. Turntable mode 'achieved very good results'. Black and metallic surfaces needed scanning spray.
  • Creality CR-Scan Raptor: CNC Kitchen (Stefan Hermann, 2024) found that its blue-laser mode 'always needs retroreflective markers', with 'at least 4 overlapping points always in frame'. For smaller parts he uses 'a scan table which already has a ton of points attached'. NIR mode needs no markers but resolves less detail.

Measured results: CNC Kitchen scanned a 100.25 mm ball bar with the Raptor and got 100.24, 100.25 and 100.22 mm. 3D Printing Industry got 0.02 mm (X and Z) and -0.16 mm (Y) from the Seal on its reference block. 3DTechValley reports the MINI 2 within 0.05 mm of CAD on overall form, but gives no figures for individual objects.

Not recommended

Don't buy the Creality CR-Scan Raptor as a marker-free scanner for tiny parts. The 0.02 mm detail comes from blue-laser mode, and that mode cannot track without at least 4 markers in view. For a coin or a small bracket, that means building a marker-covered scan table. Its marker-free NIR mode gives up that detail.
