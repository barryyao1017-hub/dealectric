What the differences mean

  • 72 V carts: a 24-cell LiFePO4 pack rests at about 81.6 V when full. 120 − 81.6 = 38.4 V of headroom on the Renogy and Redodo meters; 81.6 − 70 = 11.6 V over the Victron SmartShunt's 70 V ceiling.
  • 48 V carts: a full 16-cell pack rests at about 54.4 V, inside every range in the table. The Vatrer touchscreen has the least headroom: 90 − 54.4 = 35.6 V.
  • Same-spec shunt meters: Redodo 500A at $92.99 − Renogy at $87.99 = $5 more for the same 10–120 V range, 500 A shunt and 20 ft cable. LiTime's screen version is $89.99 − $87.99 = $2 more and starts lower, at 8 V. Redodo's 500A monitor makes sense if you would rather buy from Redodo.
  • BMS display saving: $87.99 − $56.99 = $31 less for LiTime's RS485 display, but it only works with three LiTime packs.
  • Parked drain: Renogy rates its monitor at 1 mA standby. 0.001 A × 720 h = 0.72 Ah a month, and 0.72 ÷ 100 Ah = 0.7% of a 100 Ah pack.

Which meter can read which battery

A shunt meter is wired into the main negative cable, so every amp going in or out of the pack passes through it. It counts amp-hours itself, which is why it works with any brand and chemistry; its voltage range is the only limit. A BMS display does no counting of its own. It shows the state of charge the battery's own management system reports, over RS485 or Bluetooth, so it only works with the batteries it was built for.

LiTime says its RS485 display is "Designed for LiTime Golf Cart Series Batteries with RS485 Communication: 51.2V 30Ah GC2 Comm, 51.2V 60Ah GC Comm or 51.2V 100Ah GC Comm" and adds "Not compatible with any other brand battery". It must be fitted on the first or last battery in a series to show the whole system. Vatrer's 10–90 V touchscreen is likewise sold only for Vatrer Power Bluetooth batteries, though it can show up to 10 of them in series or parallel.

Shunt meters have two catches of their own. Footprint Hero's Alex Beale found that the Renogy 500A has no port for Renogy's BT-1 or BT-2 module, so Bluetooth cannot be added later. If you want phone readings on a 72 V cart, buy LiTime's Bluetooth shunt instead. Also, a shunt's count wanders unless the pack gets full charges. Victron's SmartShunt manual says the state of charge "will start to drift over time" without regular full charges. It recommends a 55.6 V charged-voltage setting for 48 V lithium, which sits below the 58.4 V that golf cart chargers such as the one in Redodo's kit finish at.

Not recommended

Victron SmartShunt on a 72 V cart. Victron lists its supply range as 6.5–70 VDC, which is 11.6 V short of a full 72 V LiFePO4 pack. On 36 and 48 V carts it draws under 1 mA (Victron) and works with any battery type, but it has no screen, so you read it on a phone. LiTime's RS485 display on any battery other than the three LiTime GC Comm packs will not work, by LiTime's own statement.
