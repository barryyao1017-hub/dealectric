Will both screens work on a 16-inch laptop without a driver?

A driver-free extender shows up as two ordinary external monitors, so the laptop has to drive two external displays itself. Each screen needs its own video path: a USB-C port that carries video (DisplayPort Alt Mode or Thunderbolt) or HDMI. FOPO's S9G listing warns that a charging-only USB-C port cannot carry video.

16-inch MacBook Pro: Apple has never sold a 16-inch MacBook Pro with a base chip. The first Apple-silicon 16-inch from 2021 drives up to two external displays with the M1 Pro and three at 6K plus one at 4K with the M1 Max. Today's 16-inch comes only with the M5 Pro, which drives three external displays over any mix of Thunderbolt and HDMI, or the M5 Max, which drives four. Its ports are three Thunderbolt 5 USB-C ports plus HDMI. The one-display limit applies to base M1 and M2 Macs, and FOPO's S11 listing excludes only base M1, M2 and M3 chips. Every 16-inch MacBook Pro can therefore run both S11 screens natively.

What a driver costs you: FOPO's S788 setup asks you to install its driver and allow UsbDisplay under Screen Recording on a Mac. Plugable documents what USB-graphics drivers do on macOS. Once DisplayLink software has Screen Recording permission, macOS turns off HDCP system-wide, including on displays the Mac drives itself. With a Silicon Motion USB-graphics device connected, protected video such as Netflix, Prime Video and Blu-ray will not play on any screen, the laptop's own included. Plugable also notes that macOS 15 and later may ask you to renew the Screen Recording permission from time to time. FOPO does not say which chip UsbDisplay uses, but both families Plugable documents block protected video. Expect to unplug the S788 before you stream.

A 16-inch MacBook Pro already drives two native screens, so for laptop + 2 the S11 does the job without those costs. Choose the S788 only if you want four displays, or if your laptop lacks two video-capable outputs.
