Why 1920 × 1200 matters on a 16-inch laptop

A 16-inch laptop's own panel is 16:10. Side screens at 1920 × 1200 line up with it and give the same vertical room for documents; the 1080p 16:9 panels on most extenders sit shorter next to it. The S788's three panels are IPS, 100% sRGB, 60 Hz and 25 ms, with brightness buttons and an OSD; FOPO's spec sheet gives a 388 × 244 × 41.3 mm folded size and a 344.7 × 215.4 mm active area per screen.

Driver, not native

FOPO's S788 page says it plainly: a driver-based setup, not a driver-free one. Windows installs UsbDisplay on its own when online; a Mac needs the driver installed by hand and Screen Recording permission granted, and company-managed laptops may block the install. The upside is that a base M1, M2 or M3 Mac, which Apple limits to one native external display, drives all three through the driver. Expect some latency and no HDR; it is a workspace, not a gaming rig.

Weight and fit

2.64 kg clamps onto the laptop, so a thin 16-inch machine will want the rear support FOPO builds in; Notebookcheck's review of FOPO's 12-inch model called the format clunky and more fragile than two standalone portable monitors, which is the honest alternative if you never move the setup. FOPO's fit range is 12–18.5 inches, subject to the laptop's physical dimensions and clearance.
