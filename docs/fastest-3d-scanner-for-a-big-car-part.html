What the differences mean

On a big car part, total scan time follows how much surface each frame covers more than the points-per-second figure. These are the frame areas from the table, as arithmetic on the makers' stated maximum field of view:

  • FreeScan Trak Nova: 2.6 × 2.2 = 5.72 m². SCANOLOGY KSCAN-X: 2.6 × 1.8 = 4.68 m².
  • EINSTAR 2 and EINSTAR Rockit in IR Rapid: 1.17 × 1.385 = 1.62 m². FreeScan Omni: 1.205 × 1.104 = 1.33 m².
  • Revopoint MetroX: 0.32 × 0.215 = 0.069 m².
  • The Trak Nova frame covers 5.72 / 1.62 = 3.5 times the EINSTAR IR frame, and 5.72 / 1.33 = 4.3 times the Omni's, although the Omni lists more points per second (7.62 million vs 6.14 million).
  • The EINSTAR IR frame is 1.62 / 1.33 = 1.2 times the Omni's and 1.62 / 0.069 = 23 times the MetroX's.
  • At SKYLAB3D on 2026-11-17 the Trak Nova cost $49,999 − $38,999 = $11,000 more than the Omni.

EINSTAR publishes no accuracy figure for IR Rapid, and its finest IR point spacing is 0.2 mm (laser modes go to 0.05 mm), so the EINSTAR scanners are for shape capture, not inspection. Measured scan times on car parts

The one timed test of a car part by a named lab comes from King Saud University's Advanced Manufacturing Institute (Applied Sciences, January 2018). It scanned the same free-form car door with two handheld Creaform scanners at optimized settings:

  • White-light area capture (Go!SCAN): 2.6585 min, RMS deviation 0.1995 mm from the reference.
  • Laser lines (VIUscan): 10.3607 min, RMS deviation 0.1285 mm.
  • Arithmetic: 10.3607 / 2.6585 = 3.9 times longer with laser lines; 0.1995 / 0.1285 = 1.55 times more deviation with area capture.

Aniwaa's review of the SHINING 3D FreeScan Combo (Pierre-Antoine Arrighi, December 2023) points the same way: an object over 1 m took about 10 min in laser mode and about 5 min in the infrared large-object mode. Preparation can outweigh capture, though. In a vendor-run benchmark at an automotive mold maker, Creaform timed its HandySCAN BLACK Elite at 45 min in total against 294 min for an unnamed blue-light scanner that needed spray and more setup (84 min of preparation vs 5). Because Creaform ran that benchmark itself, it is not independent.

Neither of these tests used today's scanners, and no named lab or reviewer has published a timed car-part scan for the Trak Nova, KSCAN-X, Omni or EINSTAR models. EINSTAR's own claim is a full-body capture in about 60 seconds in IR mode. Treat that as a maker's figure. So the rule from the measurements is: area capture (infrared or structured light) finishes a big panel fastest when about 0.2 mm is enough, and a tracked laser system with a large frame is fastest when you need laser accuracy and want to skip markers.

Not recommended

The Revopoint MetroX is not the tool for a big car part. Its largest frame is 320 × 215 mm at 400 mm, 0.069 m², so a 1 m² door takes at least 1 / 0.069 = 15 frames before any overlap. Its fast 7,000,000 points/s full-field mode covers that same small frame. Its laser modes also need markers. It fits brackets and small trim, which is what Revopoint rates it for.
