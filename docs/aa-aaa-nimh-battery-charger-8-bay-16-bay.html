For a household or a camera bag with dozens of AA and AAA NiMH cells, the charger that matters is one with 8 or 16 bays, each on its own channel, so a tired cell in slot 3 does not stop slot 4 from filling. The table compares every such charger sold in the US today, including the brands Dealectric does not list, on the figures the makers publish: current per bay, time for a full AA, how the charger decides a cell is full, and what else it does (refresh, per-slot display, USB-C power).

Short answer. Sixteen bays at once: the Tenergy TN438 ($57.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/tenergy-tn438-16-bay-nimh-charger, 500 mA per AA, Delta-V cut-off plus a safety timer, refresh mode) or the cheaper BONAI 16-bay (LEDs only, 250–500 mA). Eight bays, fast: the Powerex MH-C800S (1 A per bay, conditioning cycle) or the 1-hour MH-C801D (2 A). Eight bays, cheap and USB-C powered: the Tenergy TN480U ($23.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/tenergy-tn480u-8-bay-nimh-charger, 500 mA per AA, per-slot LCD) or the EBL C9008. Eight bays for eneloop owners who want Panasonic's own charger: the BQ-CC63, which Panasonic does not sell in the US. Anything with 4 bays (La Crosse BC1000, SkyRC MC3000, eneloop BQ-CC65) is here for reference only: better per-cell data, far fewer cells per session.

How to read the current column: NiMH cells charge well at 0.3–1 C (300–1,000 mA for a 2,000 mAh AA; 100–300 mA for a 800 mAh AAA). Below 0.3 C the end-of-charge signal is too weak for the charger to detect and a timer has to stop it; above 1 C only cells rated for it (most 2,000 mAh+ AAs) should be used. Chargers that end on −ΔV (the small voltage dip when a NiMH cell is full) stop on the cell's own signal; those with a timer alone stop on the clock.

Which one, for which pile of cells

  • Sixteen or more cells in one go (a houseful of remotes, flashlights and toys): the Tenergy TN438, if you want a display, a refresh mode and a stated cut-off; the BONAI if LEDs are enough. Both run at 500 mA or less per AA, so a full set takes an evening.
  • Eight cells fast, for cameras or flashes: the Powerex MH-C800S or MH-C801D. They charge at 1–2 A, which is only for AAs of 2,000 mAh and up; the manual says to use soft mode for smaller cells.
  • Eight cells from a USB-C port: the Tenergy TN480U or the EBL C9008. Both need a 5 V 2 A supply, which with eight bays limits them to 500 mA per AA; plan on several hours.
  • Cell health over speed: the La Crosse BC1000 or the SkyRC MC3000 measure capacity and internal resistance per cell, which is how you find the one bad cell that keeps draining the rest. Four bays, so they complement an 8- or 16-bay charger rather than replace it.
