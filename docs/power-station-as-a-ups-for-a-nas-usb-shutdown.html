Which portable power station can act as a UPS for a NAS and signal it to shut down over USB? (2026)

Short answer. One portable power station sends a NAS a USB UPS shutdown signal: the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus (price on the Amazon page; buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DCC2BVFW?tag=dealectric-20). It shows up as a USB HID UPS, NUT 2.8.3 added basic support and StorageReview measured an 8 ms switchover, but no owner has yet reported a NAS shutting down cleanly from it: most NAS systems ship an older NUT, and a Synology owner says DSM does not see it.

What to know: if the NAS must shut itself down on a USB signal today, a small UPS does it (APC Back-UPS BE600M1, $99.99 at Markertek on 2026-11-03). The DELTA 3 Plus is the pick for keeping a 2-bay NAS running about a day (910 Wh delivered in StorageReview's test, about 28 hours at 32 W including the station's own draw), with the shutdown handled by a NUT server you run yourself.

How the DELTA 3 Plus talks to a NAS

EcoFlow's manual describes the link in an "Advanced Setup (DELTA 3 Plus only)" section: in UPS mode the station talks over one of its USB-C ports to a computer or NAS through a USB 2.0 data cable, which is not in the box, and EcoFlow's Power Manager software reads it. Plugged in, it enumerates as a standard USB HID power device ("EF-UPS-DELTA 3 Plus", ID 3746:ffff), and Network UPS Tools added basic support for it and the RIVER 3 Plus in release 2.8.3. Owner dumps in NUT's issue tracker show what it reports: on-line or on-battery, charge and runtime, with the low-battery point following the discharge limit set in EcoFlow's app (30 % in one dump). Two gaps showed up in that thread: the driver cannot tell the station to cut its outlets after the shutdown (one owner's host halted cleanly and the AC stayed on for over a minute), and the plain DELTA 3 has no data link at all.

Why a NAS may not see it yet

A NAS uses whatever NUT version its maker bundles, and the reports so far are older than 2.8.3: Debian 13 and Proxmox VE 9 ship 2.8.1 and TrueNAS owners report 2.8.0, while a pfSense owner on NUT 2.8.2 got "failed to match a subdriver". On the RIVER 3 Plus, which uses the same driver, a QNAP TS-269L showed nothing on its UPS page and a Synology owner says DSM does not list it; EcoFlow's Power Manager supported Windows and four Synology models at launch (ifun.de, March 2025) and EcoFlow's US download page still lists it as "coming soon". We found no report from Synology, QNAP, UGREEN UGOS, Unraid or TrueNAS of the DELTA 3 Plus shutting a NAS down cleanly, so treat it as a project, not a feature.

The two setups that work today

For a guaranteed shutdown, put a small USB UPS between the wall and the NAS and let the NAS read it natively; our small UPS for a home NAS page compares those. For runtime measured in hours, run NUT 2.8.3 or newer on a Linux box or Raspberry Pi connected to the DELTA 3 Plus by USB and point the NAS at it as a network UPS client in its UPS settings, after testing the shutdown with a real pull of the plug. The RIVER 3 Plus is smaller and comes with its cable, but its open NUT bug means the low-battery state never arrives; one owner let it drain to 0 % with the status unchanged.

Runtime, and the station's own draw

Makers' figures for drive access: Synology DS224+ 14.69 W, UGREEN DXP2800 16.38 W, Synology DS923+ about 35.5 W, UGREEN DXP4800 Plus 42.36 W. A power station adds its own inverter draw of roughly 15–17 W (BLUETTI AC180 manual, a DELTA 3 Classic measurement), about as much as a 2-bay NAS, which is why the table uses 32 W and 59 W. Runtimes are our arithmetic from measured delivered energy where it exists (910 Wh for the DELTA 3 Plus, 907 Wh for the C1000 Gen 2, about 1,910 Wh for the Elite 200 V2) and from rated capacity otherwise.

Switchover and battery care

All of these are standby UPS designs: the outlet switches to the inverter when the grid drops. StorageReview's measurements put older units outside their own claims (Anker SOLIX F2000 about 27 ms, EcoFlow DELTA 2 about 38 ms), while the DELTA 3 Plus measured just over 8 ms. A NAS's external power brick has no published hold-up time, so a unit at 10 ms or less is the safe choice. EcoFlow's manual asks for a discharge to 0 % and a recharge to 60 % every three months in storage and voids the warranty after six months unused; it gives no guidance for permanent UPS duty at 100 %, so set a charge limit in the app if you leave it on the wall.
