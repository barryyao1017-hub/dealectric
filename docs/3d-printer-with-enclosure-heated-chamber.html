Heated chamber vs passive enclosure: where the warmth comes from

An enclosure only traps heat. In most enclosed printers the only heat source is the bed. In its P2S review, CNC Kitchen states plainly: 'It's not a heated chamber.' With the bed at 100–110 °C, its two in-chamber thermocouples read 50.3 °C and 54.1 °C. The P2S's flap decides where the air goes. In PLA mode a side fan pulls in cool outside air. For ABS, ASA or PC the flap closes and the printer recirculates air through a rear carbon filter.

That passive figure depends on the fan setting. Bambu forum owner Druaka logged 37–42 °C while printing ABS with the right auxiliary fan at 100%, and 50–52 °C with it off. Smaller passive boxes run cooler still. Clever Creations timed the Creality K1C's chamber rising from 25 °C to its 38 °C maximum in 30 minutes, with the lid on and all fans off. The K1C has no dedicated chamber heater.

The QIDI Q2 adds a separate 280 W PTC heater that warms the chamber independently of the bed, up to 65 °C. That figure is QIDI's rating, not a lab measurement. QIDI's own guidance says PLA usually needs no heated chamber, ABS benefits from one, PC strongly needs one, and nylon needs one in many cases. It also warns that too much chamber heat causes heat creep and sagging overhangs. So for PLA, leave the heater off.

Not recommended

QIDI Q2C if you are buying the enclosure for ABS or ASA. It uses the Q2's 270 × 270 × 256 mm frame and costs $100 less, but Botland's spec sheet lists its active chamber heating as 'NO' and its rated power as 350 W with no heater line. The air filter is an optional extra. For PLA and PETG it is a fair saving. For warp-prone filaments, the extra $100 for the Q2 buys the 280 W heater.
