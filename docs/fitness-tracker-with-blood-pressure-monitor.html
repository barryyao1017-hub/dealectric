One yardstick: which blood pressure watches passed ISO 81060-2

ISO 81060-2 is the clinical test for blood pressure monitors. The device is compared with a reference measurement across a study group. To pass, the mean difference must stay within 5 mmHg and the standard deviation at 8 mmHg or less (Kuwabara et al., 2019). Here is where each candidate stands:

  • Huawei Watch D2 (LCA-B11): pass for the general population under ISO 81060-2:2018 with the 2020 and 2024 amendments. Studies: an 85-person primary study, a 35-person stress study and a 35-person ambulatory study (Zhou et al., Hypertension Research 2025, as listed by Medaval, which rates it three stars). Medaval also lists a 2026 Revista Clínica Española comparison with a SunTech Oscar 2 ambulatory monitor and notes that it was not a formal validation.
  • Huawei Watch D3: Sofian Nouira of L'Éclaireur Fnac (6 October 2026) writes that the D3 'n'a pas encore fait l'objet d'une telle validation' (has not yet been through such a validation). His D3 readings averaged 7 mmHg lower systolic and 6 mmHg lower diastolic than an Omron M3 Comfort upper-arm cuff.
  • Omron HeartGuide BP8000-M, code HEM-6410T-ZM: passed ISO 81060-2:2013 at −0.9 ± 7.6 / −1.1 ± 6.1 mmHg (Kuwabara et al., J Clin Hypertens 2019). Medaval lists this code as discontinued but still available through some outlets.
  • Omron HeartGuide BP8000-M, code HEM-6411T-ZM: Medaval finds no peer-reviewed clinical validation and rates it 'Not recommended'.
  • Samsung Galaxy Watch (Watch4 and later): estimates only, which Samsung labels 'for wellness only'. Each 28-day calibration needs 3 readings from your own upper-arm cuff.
  • Hilo Core: Hilo says 'the clearance attaches to the cuff only'. Hilo says the band itself is not FDA-cleared.
  • Apple Watch: hypertension notifications analyse heart-sensor data and alert you when they detect a pattern of hypertension. Apple states that 'not all people with hypertension receive a notification'.

When the D2's cuff can't read you. Huawei says to sit and rest for at least 5 minutes and hold the watch level with your heart, elbow bent with your palm on your chest. It also warns that arrhythmia, frequent premature beats, a heart rate below 40 or above 180 bpm, or severe arteriosclerosis can make a measurement fail or come out wrong. If any of these applies to you, use an upper-arm cuff. The D2 also has no FDA 510(k) clearance. The FDA's September 2025 safety communication tells shoppers not to rely on unauthorized blood pressure devices and to check the 510(k) database. Treat the D2 as a validated tracker, and confirm any reading you would act on with an authorized upper-arm cuff.

Not recommended

Omron HeartGuide BP8000-M. The version with the published validation (HEM-6410T-ZM) is listed by Medaval as discontinued, though still available through some outlets. Medaval lists the HEM-6411T-ZM, sold under the same BP8000-M name, as currently available in the US, with no clinical validation and a 'Not recommended' rating. A HeartGuide bought today may well be the unvalidated code, so check the HEM code on the box before buying. Apple Watch SE models get no blood pressure feature at all: Apple excludes SE from hypertension notifications.
