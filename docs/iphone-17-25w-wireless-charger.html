What the differences mean

The column to read is the adapter. Each charger needs more watts going in than the 25 W it sends to the phone, and how much more varies by brand:

  • Apple MagSafe Charger: 30 W ÷ 25 W = 1.2× the output. A 30 W adapter you already own is enough.
  • ESR Cyclone: 35 W ÷ 25 W = 1.4×. A 30 W adapter is below ESR's minimum. Pad plus a 45 W adapter: $43.99 + $24.99 (UGREEN Nexode Air 45W) = $68.98.
  • UGREEN MagFlow Pro pad: 45 W ÷ 25 W = 1.8×, because the fan also draws power. Pad plus the same 45 W adapter: $49.99 + $24.99 = $74.98.
  • Belkin WIZ040 and Anker A25X7: both come with their adapter (45 W and 65 W). The A25X7 runs three devices and a fan from 65 W, which is 65 ÷ 25 = 2.6× the phone's share. The sticker price is the whole cost.

Why the adapter, not the charger label, decides whether you get 25 W

Apple's support note for the MagSafe Charger puts the iPhone 17, 17 Pro and 17 Pro Max in its 25 W tier. That tier needs a charger marked A3502, A3503, A2580 or A3250 (printed on the charger or its cable) and an adapter rated 30 W or more, or one that offers 15 V/2.0 A. The original MagSafe Charger, A2140, is in the 15 W tier. If yours is an A2580 or A3250, you already have a 25 W charger.

Third-party Qi2.2 chargers set their own minimums, and they are higher than Apple's. ESR lists 25 W for iPhone 17 'with ≥35W input'. UGREEN marks the MagFlow Pro '45W Adapter Required'. Anker says its Prime foldable 3-in-1 (A25N1) 'requires a 45W or higher adapter to achieve full 25W output', and it tells A25X7 owners to use 65 W or more. ESR and Anker also need iOS 26 or later, and they limit case thickness to 2 mm (ESR) and 2.5 mm (Anker).

Even with the right adapter, Apple gives three cases where you get less than 25 W. Plug the charger into power before you place the phone. The iPhone 17 drops to 16 W while a USB-C accessory such as wired headphones is connected. And the iPhone Air peaks at 20 W, which Belkin's 'Qi2-enabled iPhones (Series 16 and later) charge at 25W' line leaves out. To check that a third-party charger really is 25 W, look it up in the Wireless Power Consortium's Qi database. A 25 W model shows version 2.2.1 and power profile MPP25. A record showing 2.0.0 or MPP is the 15 W tier.

Measured results: CNET's lab, as reported by 9to5Mac, got an iPhone 17 from 10% or less to 49% more charge in 30 minutes on wireless, against Apple's claim of 50%. Macworld timed an iPhone 17 on Apple's MagSafe puck at about 1 h 15 min to 80% and 2 h 06 min to full.

Not recommended

For an iPhone 17, don't buy, and don't count on, an original A2140 MagSafe Charger or any charger certified only to the 15 W Qi2 tier. Apple and the WPC both place these at 15 W, so they deliver 60% of the 25 W the phone can take (15 ÷ 25 = 0.6), whatever adapter you use.
