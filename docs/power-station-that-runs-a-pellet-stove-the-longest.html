What the differences mean

Every hours figure is arithmetic, not a test: rated Wh × 0.90 ÷ (stove running watts + idle watts). The 0.90 is the inverter efficiency BLUETTI uses in its own runtime formula, which also adds the station's self-consumption to the load. It is applied to every station here, so the gaps between rows mean more than the exact hours. Dealectric does not test units; the idle figures come from the named reviewers and the stove figures from the makers' manuals.

  • Apex 300 + 2× B500K: 13,004.8 × 0.90 ÷ (180 + 19.5) = 58.7 h; ÷ (115 + 19.5) = 87.0 h.
  • DELTA Pro Ultra X: 12,288 × 0.90 ÷ (180 + 36) = 51.2 h; ÷ (115 + 36) = 73.2 h. It holds 716.8 Wh less than the Apex bundle and loses 16.5 W more to idle, so it ends about 7.5 hours behind on the 180 W stove.
  • Anker S5000: 5,024 × 0.90 ÷ (180 + 10) = 23.8 h. The 6,144 Wh DELTA Pro Ultra gets 6,144 × 0.90 ÷ (180 + 45 to 60) = 23.0–24.6 h, so 22% more battery buys it no extra hours.
  • Jackery 5000 Plus: 5,040 × 0.90 ÷ (180 + 58) = 19.1 h, almost 5 hours less than the S5000 from about the same battery.
  • F3800 Plus: 3,840 × 0.90 ÷ (180 + 80) = 13.3 h. Rated Wh × 0.85 ÷ 150 W would have shown 21.8 h.

Why idle draw decides the order at pellet-stove loads

A pellet stove keeps the inverter switched on for the whole outage, so the inverter's own no-load draw runs every hour on top of the stove. At 180 W, a 20 W idle adds 11% to the load and a 58 W idle adds 32%. That fixed draw is why a smaller battery behind a frugal inverter can match a bigger one. On the 180 W stove, each 1,000 Wh of battery gives about 4.7 h behind the S5000 (900 ÷ 190), 4.5 h behind the Apex 300 (900 ÷ 199.5), 4.2 h behind the DELTA Pro Ultra X (900 ÷ 216), 3.8 h behind the Jackery 5000 Plus (900 ÷ 238) and 3.5 h behind the F3800 (900 ÷ 260).

Every system in the table takes more batteries, so any of them runs longer with more packs, and the per-1,000 Wh figure says how much each pack is worth. Anker's S5000 with one added battery (10 kWh, Anker) works out to about 10,000 × 0.90 ÷ 190 = 47 h. That is still behind the Apex 300 + 2× B500K, but it is the lowest-idle way to grow.

The stove figures come from two manuals. The US Stove 5660 lists 180 W operational and 346 W with the igniter running. The Lennox Bella lists 115 W maximum in burn mode (90 W minimum) and 360 W while the igniter is on for the first 8 minutes. One Bella start therefore costs (360 − 115) × 8 ÷ 60 = 33 Wh above running, about 15 minutes of runtime on the Apex bundle. Every station here supplies 360 W with a wide margin. Your own stove's rating plate gives the running watts to put into the formula.

The Apex 300's 19.5 W was measured with the inverters on and nothing plugged in, on the unit alone. No one has published the idle draw with B300K or B500K packs attached. Not recommended

Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus, for a pellet stove. Its 5,040 Wh nearly matches the S5000's 5,024 Wh, but The Solar Lab measured about 58 W with the inverter on and nothing connected. That leaves about 19 hours on a 180 W stove against about 24 for the S5000. The longer the outage, the more of the battery the idle draw takes.
