What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test printers. Every figure here is a named reviewer's measurement or the maker's own figure, labelled, read 2026-11-17.

  • ABS melt-rate ceiling on a big part (arithmetic). Time to extrude 100 cm³ of ABS at each maker's ABS flow figure: X2D profile 40 mm³/s: 100,000 ÷ 40 = 2,500 s, about 42 min. H2S profile 35 mm³/s: 100,000 ÷ 35 ≈ 2,857 s, about 48 min. QIDI's ABS figure for the Plus5, about 30 mm³/s: 100,000 ÷ 30 ≈ 3,333 s, about 56 min. These are floors set by flow alone; walls, cooling and travel add time.
  • Top speed you can use with ABS (arithmetic). A 0.4 mm-wide, 0.2 mm-tall line has a 0.4 × 0.2 = 0.08 mm² cross-section. At 40 mm³/s: 40 ÷ 0.08 = 500 mm/s; at 35 mm³/s: about 438 mm/s; at 30 mm³/s: 375 mm/s. Each is below the 600 to 1,000 mm/s claims, so on long ABS lines the melt rate, not the motion system, sets the pace.
  • The profile moves a Benchy more than the printer (arithmetic). The same K2 Plus took 37 − 14 = 23 min longer on Creality's 0.2 mm Standard preset than on its pre-sliced speed file (HowToMechatronics). On one reviewer's bench (AndroidPCTV) the Plus5, Max4 and Q2 spread only 20 − 17 = 3 min.
  • Claimed top speed is not the measured order (arithmetic). The 800 mm/s Max4's 18 min Benchy (AndroidPCTV) beat the 1,000 mm/s X2D's 24 min (The Technology Man) by 24 − 18 = 6 min, but with different testers, profiles and PLA, so it does not rank them for ABS.
  • ABS running cost on the X2D (arithmetic). 330 W × 3 h = 0.99 kWh, about 1 kWh per three hours of ABS (The Technology Man).
  • Same claimed speed, different price (arithmetic). H2S $1,049 − X2D $589 = $460 at Bambu's US store on 2026-11-17. Both claim 1,000 mm/s, 20,000 mm/s² and a 65 °C chamber, so the extra buys build volume: 340 × 320 × 340 mm against 256 × 256 × 260 mm.

What makes ABS print fast, and what was measured

ABS needs a hot nozzle (Bambu's stock ABS profiles run 270 °C; HowToMechatronics printed ABS at 260 °C on a 100 °C bed) and a warm chamber so the part does not warp as it cools. With the chamber at 60 to 65 °C the printer can run its normal ABS profile instead of slowing down to save the part, and from there the hotend's melt rate decides how fast a large part finishes. For how quickly each chamber reaches temperature, see which enclosed 3D printer heats up fastest.

Measured so far: the QIDI Q2 printed an ABS Buddha in 18 min (AndroidPCTV), the only timed ABS print among these printers. The Technology Man reported zero warping and excellent layer adhesion on Bambu ABS with the X2D's 0.20 mm Optimal profile and measured 330 W average over an overnight ABS print. Bambu Studio's stock ABS profile for the X2D 0.4 mm nozzle lists flow caps from 16 to 40 mm³/s across nozzle variants with a 65 °C chamber setting; the H2S profile lists 20 to 35 mm³/s with 60 °C. QIDI states that high-speed PLA and ABS typically print around 30 mm³/s on the Plus5. Every 3DBenchy time above is PLA, from different testers and profiles, so none of them ranks the printers for ABS.

Not recommended

FLSUN S1 Pro for ABS. It carries the highest claimed figures (1,200 mm/s, 40,000 mm/s², 110 mm³/s), but its enclosure is passive, rated for 50 °C, with no active chamber heating (maker claims listed by desktop3dprinters.us, read 2026-11-17). Every printer in the table heats its chamber to 60 to 65 °C, which is what keeps ABS from warping at speed.
