What each filament changer can't feed or dry

An enclosed printer handles multiple filaments through a 4-spool changer that sits beside it. The enclosure is what lets you print ABS, ASA, nylon and fiber-filled filaments, but each changer has its own list of filaments it refuses and its own way of keeping spools dry. Here is what the three common changers accept and how they dry.

Fiber-filled filament from other brands. MatterHackers' copy of Bambu's AMS 2 Pro table rejects 'other filaments that contain carbon fiber or glass fiber', so a third-party PETG-CF or PA-CF has to bypass the AMS 2 Pro. The QIDI Box takes ABS-, PLA-, PETG-, PET-CF/GF and PPS-CF from any brand, and Creality's CFS takes PLA-CF and PA-CF.

Damp engineering filament. Creality's store lists the K2 Pro Combo's CFS with 'Drying Method: Desiccant'. It keeps dry spools dry but cannot dry a damp one, so ABS, ASA and nylon need a separate dryer before they go in. The QIDI Box heats to 65 °C during the print. That is the same ceiling at which Bambu's table says the AMS 2 Pro cannot fully dry ABS, ASA, PA or PC, so plan on a standalone dryer for nylon and PC on any of these.

Flexible filament. None of the three changers feeds standard TPU 95A. Creality's K2 Plus FAQ says to load TPU on the printer's own spool holder, and the same applies to the QIDI and Bambu machines. A multi-filament job can still mix one flexible spool with the boxed ones.

If you already own a Bambu P1S. MatterHackers' AMS compatibility guide lists the P1S as taking the AMS 2 Pro and AMS HT (up to 4 units in any mix, through an AMS Hub for more than one), with a separate power adapter for each AMS 2 Pro. Bambu's forum announcement says that adapter is needed for drying.
