What the differences mean

  • Eco keeps about a third of the light. MoGo 4: 151 ÷ 400 = 38% of its Performance-mode output (ProjectorCentral). Mars 3 Air: 106 ÷ 290 = 37% of its Standard battery brightness (Nebula's table).
  • The Mars 3 holds twice the light on battery, but it costs more and weighs more. 300 ÷ 151 = 2.0× the MoGo 4's Eco output, for 5 h ÷ 2.5 h = 2× the rated runtime. It also weighs 9.9 lb ÷ 2.9 lb = 3.4× as much (ProjectorCentral, XGIMI), and at Nebula's store it is $1,049.99 − $1,000 = $49.99 over budget.
  • Five hours for about half the price. The MoGo 4 + PowerBase Stand bundle at $519 costs $1,049.99 − $519 = $530.99 less than the Mars 3 for the same 5-hour rating, with about half the Eco brightness.
  • The Mars 3 Air costs more than that bundle. $599.99 − $519 = $80.99 more, for 2.5 to 3 rated hours against 5 (Nebula's product page says 2.5 h, its support table 3 h).
  • The laser upgrade buys light, not hours. Laurent Willen measured the MoGo 4 Laser at 370–390 lm in Standard mode. ProjectorCentral measured the MoGo 4 at 258 lm in the same mode: (380 − 258) ÷ 258 ≈ 47% more, though the two reviewers used different methods. Battery life on the built-in battery stays under 2 hours in both. Adding the stand to the Laser costs $669 + $129 = $798.
  • Lumen units differ. XGIMI rates in ISO lumens and Nebula in ANSI lumens. ProjectorCentral's MoGo 4 figures are ISO 21118 and its Nebula figures are ANSI, so treat cross-brand gaps of a few percent as noise.

Why the battery hours and the lumens don't come together

Each maker rates brightness at full power and battery life in Eco mode, and the projector switches itself to Eco when you unplug it. XGIMI rates the MoGo 4's 450 ISO lumens as "tested under ideal laboratory conditions in Performance Mode/High Power Mode". The 2.5 hours are rated in Eco Mode, and the projector "will automatically enter Eco Mode upon startup if it's not plugged in". Nebula's support page says that with the auto brightness switch on, unplugging the adapter moves the projector from Standard to Eco brightness. Its own table puts the Mars 3's 5 hours at 300 lm, not the rated 1,000. BenQ says the GV50's 2.5-hour maximum "is measured under Eco mode, specifically while streaming content from YouTube". It also says the GV50 runs at 70% general brightness on its battery.

Running past the built-in battery. XGIMI says a third-party bank for the MoGo 4 series must deliver 65–150 W over USB-C PD: 65 W at 20 V/3.25 A, or 100 W at 20 V/5 A. A common 45 W bank is below that range. UGREEN lists its 145W 25,000 mAh bank's main USB-C port at 20 V-5A from a 90 Wh cell, which matches XGIMI's 100 W profile $79.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-145w-25000mah-3-port-power-bank. For the BenQ GV50, BenQ says a 45–65 W bank caps peak brightness at 20%, and only a bank above 65 W (20 V 3.25 A) gives 100%. Nebula lists 45 W PD 2.0 banks for the Capsule 3 but not for the Mars 3 or Mars 3 Air. ProjectorCentral reports that the Mars 3 Air uses a proprietary power port.

Not recommended

Nebula Mars 3 Air. It is sold as a battery projector, but Nebula's own table gives it 106 lm in Eco for its 3 hours and 290 lm for 1 h 40 min at Standard brightness. It has no USB-C power input to extend that. At $599.99 from Nebula it costs $80.99 more than the MoGo 4 + PowerBase Stand, which is rated for 5 hours and gave more light in Eco when measured (151 vs 145 lm, ProjectorCentral).
