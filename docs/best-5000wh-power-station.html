What the differences mean

Why 5,000 Wh is the line: EcoFlow, whose TITAN range is built on a 5 kWh block, tells buyers to plan on 4 kWh reaching the loads after a 20% allowance for reserve and conversion losses. A 150 W essentials load (the figure OffGrid Current uses for a fridge and small loads) needs 150 × 24 = 3,600 Wh a day, which fits inside 4 kWh. A 3,840 Wh station under the same allowance gives 3,840 × 0.8 = 3,072 Wh and falls short of a day. Every row above is 5,000 Wh or more in one unit by its maker's rated figure. Left out for being short of it: the Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus (3,840 Wh).

Inside the line, the ranking uses output (watts and voltage) and what a named reviewer measured. Dealectric does not test units; every measurement here is credited to the reviewer who made it.

  • Idle loss per day, inverter on, nothing plugged in. Anker: 8.7 W × 24 h = 209 Wh, 4% of 5,024 Wh (Wes Davis, Gizmodo). Jackery: 58 W × 24 h = 1,392 Wh, 28% of 5,040 Wh (The Solar Lab); HOBOTECH metered 515 Wh in 6 hours, which is 2,060 Wh a day, 41%. Pecron F5000LFP: 52 W × 24 h = 1,248 Wh, 24% of 5,120 Wh (The Solar Lab).
  • Share of the label that comes out as AC. Anker: 4.87 ÷ 5.024 = 97%, read from the app (Andrew Riha, pv magazine USA). Jackery: 4,450 ÷ 5,040 = 88% (HOBOTECH). Pecron: 5,120 × 0.84 = about 4,300 Wh (The Solar Lab). EcoFlow's planning figure for the TITAN is 80%, or 4 kWh.
  • A fridge-only outage. Riha ran a refrigerator and phones for 61.5 hours from 100% to 18% on the Anker: 0.82 × 5,024 = 4,120 Wh ÷ 61.5 h = 67 W drawn, about 58 W of it load. On a full charge that is 5,024 ÷ 67 = 75 hours. The same 58 W load on a Jackery idling at 58 W drains 116 W: 5,040 ÷ 116 = 43 hours at most. At HOBOTECH's 85 W idle it is 5,040 ÷ 143 = 35 hours. That is our arithmetic, not a test, and it leaves out conversion loss.
  • Moving it. 134.5 − 97 = 37.5 lb between the Jackery and the Anker; the TITAN 5000 Classic sits between at 102.5 lb.

Why the TITAN 5000 Classic leads the units on sale now: all 4,000 W is on 120 V, it is the lightest EcoFlow in the class, and EcoFlow's US store listed it at $2,199 against $3,199 for the TITAN 5000 on 2026-11-27 (Backup Power Hub's tracker). The $1,000 difference buys 240 V and 3,200 W more. Its place rests on EcoFlow's specs: Station Arena's November 4 update says nobody has published a measured capacity for any TITAN. If measured efficiency matters more to you than the delivery date, the Anker SOLIX S5000 at $2,499.99 (pre-order, ships within 20–21 weeks of ordering) is the one to wait for.

What reviewers measured on 5,000Wh power stations

The capacity tests used different methods, so read them as each unit's own result, not as a head-to-head gap. The idle tests are comparable: inverter on, nothing connected.

  • Anker SOLIX S5000. Andrew Riha (pv magazine USA, 2026-10-28): the app reported 4.87 kWh delivered on a full discharge from the 120 V outlets, and the unit lost 5% of its charge in 24 hours idle, about 10 W. A full recharge at the 1,800 W setting took 6.24 kWh from the wall in 3 h 39 min, a round trip of 4.87 ÷ 6.24 = 78%. Wes Davis (Gizmodo, 2026-11-22): 1,256 Wh used over 144 hours idle, about 8.7 W; a chest freezer ran about 114 hours; 6% to 100% took 3.5 hours on a wall outlet against Anker's claimed 3.1. Anker's own idle figure is 8.8 W.
  • Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus. HOBOTECH (2024-11-25): 4,450 Wh at 120 V and 4,440 Wh at 240 V; 85 W with the inverter on; 44 dB fan noise; 9,300 W at 240 V and 4,800 W at 120 V for under 5 seconds; UPS switchover 20 ms, or 0 ms in online mode. The Solar Lab: about 58 W idle; it ran 8,000 W and 9,500 W loads for 20-30 seconds. OffGrid Current lists Jackery's own standby figure as 50 W and its cycle life as 4,000 cycles to 70%; Anker rates the S5000 at 6,000 cycles to 80%.
  • Pecron F5000LFP. The Solar Lab: 84% efficiency and roughly 52 W idle. Output is 3,600 W at 120 V or 7,200 W at 240 V. A 120 V wall outlet filled it in a little over 3.5 hours and the 30 A port in about 1 h 40 min; it cannot take a 240 V charging input. Weight 124 lb.
  • EcoFlow TITAN 5000 Classic, 5000 and 5000 Plus. On sale since November 3 with no reviewer discharge or idle test published as of Station Arena's November 4 update. EcoFlow's planning figure is 4 kWh from the 5,017.6 Wh pack, and EcoFlow calls it an illustrative assumption, not a measured efficiency.

One Anker limit to know before wiring it to a house: Anker states that its 120 V and 240 V outputs cannot be used simultaneously, and that the S5000 is not compatible with Anker's Home Power Panel. It connects through a transfer switch or power inlet box.

Not recommended

The Pecron F5000LFP bought on its 7,200 W label for 120 V loads. The 7,200 W is a 240 V figure. At 120 V it gives 3,600 W (The Solar Lab), less than the TITAN 5000 Classic's 4,000 W, and its 52 W idle draw costs 24% of the battery a day. Pecron's store did not load for us on 2026-11-27, so we have no current price or ship date to print and it is not in the table.

If you own or buy the Jackery, switch its AC output off when nothing is running: left on, it uses 1,392 to 2,060 Wh a day by the two reviewers' measurements.
