What the differences mean

  • Speed stability: the new decks are rated 9 to 13 times shakier. 0.25 ÷ 0.019 = 13.2× the Dragon's rating; 0.25 ÷ 0.022 = 11.4× the TC-KA7ES's; 0.25 ÷ 0.027 = 9.3× the CR-7A figure SkyFi Audio quotes. All three are WRMS figures. The Revox's 0.1% rating uses a different standard (DIN peak maximum), so the table shows its measured RMS range instead.
  • Measured against rated: John E. Johnson Jr. of HomeTheaterHifi measured 0.52% on the W-1200, a figure that combines the deck's recording and its playback, measured without a lab-grade calibration tape. 0.52 ÷ 0.25 = 2.1× TEAC's rating.
  • Hiss when recording: Dolby C cuts noise by about 20 dB above roughly 1 kHz (Nakamichi's CR-7A description). The W-1200 and 202MKVII apply 0 dB of encoding when they record, because their noise reduction works only on playback.
  • Treble: the new decks are rated to 15 kHz at ±4 dB, at a recording level they don't state. The Dragon and TC-KA7ES figures are ±3 dB at −20 dB. 22 − 15 = 7 kHz on paper, but the test conditions differ, so this is a direction rather than an exact gap.
  • Digitizing a C90 over USB: 48,000 samples/s × 2 bytes × 2 channels × 5,400 s = 1.04 GB of WAV. It takes 90 minutes in real time, plus a flip by hand, because both W-1200 decks are one-way.
  • Size: the 202MKVII weighs 5.4 kg (11.9 lb) and the US W-1200 weighs 4.2 kg (9 3/8 lb), so the Tascam is 5.4 − 4.2 = 1.2 kg (2.6 lb) heavier. It is a 3U rack unit, and it comes with the same RC-1331 remote as the W-1200.

Why the new decks can't make Dolby recordings

Each well in the W-1200 and 202MKVII has one recording/playback head and one erase head. TEAC's manual says the NOISE REDUCTION switch reduces hiss when you play tapes recorded with Dolby B, and that it "cannot be used when recording to achieve a noise reduction effect like Dolby B". Tascam describes the 202MKVII's circuit as "equivalent to Dolby B NR" and lists it for playback only. Both makers also say Type IV metal tape is playback-only: "This unit does not support recording to metal (type IV) tapes" (TEAC manual).

The vintage decks are built the other way round. Because a three-head deck records and plays back at the same moment, the Dragon "requires a Double Dolby system, one for recording and one for playback" (Nakamichi feature text on naks.com). The Revox B215 uses four Hitachi Dolby B/C ICs in a double-Dolby layout and calibrates bias and sensitivity per channel. The Sony TC-KA7ES records with Dolby S and has a three-point record calibration. So the question to ask is whether you are playing tapes or making them. For playing and digitizing, a new deck is enough. For making tapes, the vintage decks can do things the new ones cannot.

USB compatibility: the W-1200 manual lists Windows 7 to 10 and Mac OS X 10.7 to macOS 10.13, as of November 2017. Tascam's 202MKVII page lists Windows 11 up to 25H2 and macOS up to Golden Gate.

Not recommended

An unserviced Dragon or CR-7A. SkyFi Audio, a US restorer, says the rubber parts in these decks last about 12 to 15 years before they need service, and warns against buying an unrestored one. It sold one CR-7A as playback-only because its auto-bias part "is no longer available". On 2026-11-08, SkyFi's restored CR-7A listings were sold out, with "usually a 3 month lead-time". Budget for a restoration and the wait, or buy new.
