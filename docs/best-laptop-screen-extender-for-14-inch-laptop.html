What the differences mean

  • Fit on a 14-inch lid. The Duex Max DS body is 12.6 × 8.9 in (Mobile Pixels). Apple gives the 14-inch MacBook Pro as 12.31 × 8.71 in. 12.6 − 12.31 = 0.29 in (about 7 mm) wider, and 8.9 − 8.71 = 0.19 in taller, so the screen sits flush with a small overhang. Help Desk Geek calls the Duex Max suitable for 14-inch-and-larger laptops and found it too big for a 13-inch MacBook.
  • Added pixels. Duex Max DS: 1920 × 1080 = 2.07 MP. FOPO S7: 1920 × 1200 × 2 = 4.61 MP. Aura Dual Boost 14 and Dual Air 14: 1920 × 1080 × 2 = 4.15 MP. Aura Dual Go 14 Ultra: 2560 × 1600 × 2 = 8.19 MP, the sharpest pair here.
  • Battery. Mobile Pixels rates the Duex Max at 4.5 W. Over an 8-hour day that is 4.5 × 8 = 36 Wh, or 36 ÷ 72.4 = 50% of a 14-inch MacBook Pro's 72.4 Wh battery (Apple). This is the screen's rated draw only.
  • Weight against the laptop. Help Desk Geek gives the Duex Max as 1.8 lb. Against the 3.4 lb 14-inch MacBook Pro (Apple), 1.8 ÷ 3.4 = 53% of the laptop's weight. FOPO lists the S7 at 1.61 kg (read 2026-11-18), and 1.61 ÷ 1.55 kg = 104% of that laptop.

Which 14-inch laptops light both screens of a two-screen extender

A two-screen extender gets its picture one of two ways. Native video sends each screen down its own USB-C port with DisplayPort Alt Mode, or HDMI. This needs one video-capable port per screen, and the laptop must be able to drive two external displays. The FOPO S7, the Aura Dual Go 14 Ultra and the Aura Dual Air 14 work this way. A display driver carries both screens over one cable through software the maker supplies. The Aura Dual Boost 14 works this way. It needs only one port, but it depends on the driver.

On a 14-inch MacBook Pro, the chip decides how many external screens can run, not the screen size:

Macworld (Simon Jary) adds that M1, M2 and M3 Pro and Max MacBooks run both screens of Mobile Pixels' two-screen unit without software, while base M1 and M2 Macs need Mobile Pixels' free driver for the second screen. Closing the lid frees a second display on the base M3, but a lid-mounted extender needs the lid open. Plugable notes that macOS does not use DisplayPort MST for separate desktops, so two screens fed from one port without a driver show the same image on a Mac. On a 14-inch Windows laptop, the test is the same: count the USB-C ports that carry video (or HDMI) and allow one per added screen.

Where the line was drawn: a row qualifies when its fit range includes a 14-inch laptop. The Duex Max DS range comes from Help Desk Geek's review; every other range is the maker's. Mobile Pixels' Trio 3 Pro is left out of the table: Macworld gives it as compatible with 13-inch to 16-inch MacBooks, but it is wider than a 14-inch lid and heavier than the laptop it hangs on (see below). Three-screen units such as FOPO's 14-inch S780C add three screens, not two, so they are outside this page's two-added-screen limit.

Not recommended for a 14-inch laptop: the Trio 3 Pro

Macworld ran the Trio 3 Pro on a 14-inch MacBook Pro and gives it as compatible with 13-inch to 16-inch MacBooks. Folded, it measures 34 × 22.4 cm (Macworld). Against Apple's 31.26 cm lid, 34 − 31.26 = 2.74 cm wider. It weighs 4.13 lb (Macworld), and 4.13 ÷ 3.4 = 121% of the laptop it hangs on. Macworld also found its screens cannot reach full brightness on laptop power alone without an adapter plugged into the Trio.
