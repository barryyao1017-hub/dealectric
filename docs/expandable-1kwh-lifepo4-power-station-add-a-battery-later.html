What the differences mean

Every station here starts at about 1 kWh. They differ in the size of the first battery you can add, and so in where one add-on gets you:

  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 and DELTA 3 Plus: the 1,024 Wh pack doubles either one to 2,048 Wh (1,024 + 1,024). That is the smallest step in this group. On the DELTA 3 Plus, one DELTA 3 Series pack is the documented limit (BatteryRank). EcoFlow's listing says that going past 2,048 Wh toward 5 kWh needs the larger DELTA Pro 3 Extra Battery on an Alternator Charger XT150 Output Cable.
  • OUPES Mega 1 and Jackery HomePower 1000 Plus v2: the smallest pack for each is 2,048 Wh, so one pack gives 3,072 Wh (1,024 + 2,048). Neither can go from 1 kWh to just 2 kWh.
  • DELTA 3 vs DELTA 3 Plus: both take the same pack and reach the same 2,048 Wh. At EcoFlow today the Plus costs $140 more ($719 − $579).

Waiting costs a little. A DELTA 3 plus its pack comes to $967 for 2,048 Wh. EcoFlow lists its 2,048 Wh DELTA 3 Max series from $879, so expanding later costs $88 more ($967 − $879). The add-on route makes sense only if you might never need the second kWh.

Bundles cost more than buying separately. EcoFlow's DELTA 3 Plus + DELTA 3 Series Extra Battery bundle is $1,119, $12 more than the two bought separately ($1,119 − $1,107). OUPES's Mega 1 + B2 bundle is $1,379, $102 more ($1,379 − $1,277).

Cheapest per Wh isn't cheapest to start. OUPES charges the least per added Wh ($0.28), but only in 2,048 Wh steps, so its first add-on costs $578. EcoFlow's first add-on costs $388.

Not recommended

  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Classic: $100 less than the DELTA 3 at EcoFlow ($579 − $479), but EcoFlow's listing names no extra battery for it. To grow, you would need a second station.
  • Jackery Explorer 1000 Plus: Jackery still advertises 5 kWh with three packs, but its own page says the Battery Pack 1000 Plus has been discontinued.
  • BLUETTI AC180: the B230 and B300 connect by cable (P090D) to the station's 500 W, 12–60 V solar input, with the pack in Power Bank Mode (BLUETTI). The pack recharges the AC180 rather than joining its battery. At its full 1,800 W output, the AC180's own battery still drains at about 1,300 W (1,800 − 500). On 8 November 2026, BLUETTI's US store listed both packs as unavailable.
