What the differences mean

  • EcoFlow reach without the wrong version: the 2.5 m XT60i cable plus the 3 m extension pair gives 2.5 + 3 = 5.5 m (about 18 ft), for $24.99 + $29.99 = $54.98.
  • Long 10 AWG run: the VEVOR pair makes a 60 ft round trip. 60 ft × 0.9989 Ω per 1,000 ft = 0.060 Ω. That loses 10² × 0.060 = 6.0 W at 10 A and 20² × 0.060 = 24 W at 20 A. Cost per foot of distance: $43.90 ÷ 30 = $1.46.
  • Jackery 10 m at 14 AWG: 65.6 ft round trip × 2.525 Ω per 1,000 ft = 0.166 Ω. That loses 10² × 0.166 = 16.6 W at 10 A, almost three times the VEVOR pair's 6.0 W over a similar distance. At 5 A it is 5² × 0.166 = 4.1 W. Cost per foot: $59 ÷ 32.8 = $1.80.

Which cable fits which station: the plug decides

The panel end is almost always MC4 (Jackery's DC8020 panels are the exception). The station end is what changes, and each maker sells a separate lead for each port. EcoFlow sells one listing in two versions. The XT60i version is 2.5 m (8.2 ft), and EcoFlow says it works best with RIVER 2 / RIVER 2 Max / RIVER 2 Pro / DELTA 2 / DELTA Max (2000) / DELTA Pro. The 3.5 m (11.48 ft) version is plain XT60, which EcoFlow lists for the earlier RIVER and DELTA models. There is no 3.5 m XT60i on EcoFlow's US store. DELTA Gen4 stations use an LP60 solar input. EcoFlow lists a separate XT60i-to-LP60 cable for them at $39, marked 'Coming Soon' on 2026-11-13.

BLUETTI sells its MC4-to-XT60 lead (rated 20 A) separately from its MC4-to-DC7909 lead, which BLUETTI says is for the B80 or EB3A. BLUETTI names the Elite 200 V2, Apex 300, Elite 300, Elite 320 and Elite 400 as stations with an XT60 solar input.

Jackery's 5 m and 10 m extensions are DC8020 at both ends. They plug straight into DC8020 Explorers. Jackery's FAQ says the original E240 / E300 / E500 / E1000 need a DC8020-to-DC7909 adapter, and the E100 Plus, E240 V2 and E300 Plus need a DC8020-to-USB-C adapter. Jackery rates that USB-C adapter at 5 A and 100 V maximum, so it limits current on those smaller stations.

At the panel end, mixing MC4 plugs from different makers carries its own risk. In a 2021–2023 project with connector maker Stäubli as partner, Bern University of Applied Sciences (BFH) found contact resistance above the 5 mΩ limit for new connectors only where plugs from different makers were mated. One pair read above 45 mΩ. At 10 A that pair turns 10² × 0.045 = 4.5 W into heat at one joint, against 0.5 W at 5 mΩ. The safest panel-end joint is two plugs from the same maker, so where possible use the cable sold by your panel's maker.

Not recommended

The 3.5 m XT60 version of EcoFlow's cable for a RIVER 2, DELTA 2, DELTA Max (2000) or DELTA Pro. EcoFlow lists that version for the older models and the 2.5 m XT60i version for these. Solar Waypoint writes that in 'rare cases' a station limits a plain XT60 lead to 8 A or 10 A, though it publishes no test. If you need reach, buy the XT60i version plus EcoFlow's 3 m extension pair.
