What the differences mean

All the figures below assume your laptop has the usual 15.6-inch 16:9 screen at 1920×1080. A 16:10 or QHD laptop screen changes the match. Each figure is worked out from the screen size and resolution the maker states.

  • Your laptop's screen: 15.6 × 16 ÷ 18.358 = 13.60 in wide; 15.6 × 9 ÷ 18.358 = 7.65 in tall; √(1920² + 1080²) ÷ 15.6 = 2202.9 ÷ 15.6 = 141.2 PPI.
  • 15.6-inch 1080p extender (both Aura models, UPERFECT): the same sum gives 7.65 in and 141.2 PPI, so a 0.00 in height difference. Text and windows are the same physical size on all three screens.
  • Trio 3 Pro, 14.1-inch 16:10 at 2240×1400: 14.1 × 10 ÷ 18.868 = 7.47 in tall (0.18 in shorter); √(2240² + 1400²) ÷ 14.1 = 2641.5 ÷ 14.1 = 187.3 PPI, so it is sharper but draws text smaller unless you raise its scaling.
  • Duex Float 2 Pro, 16-inch 16:10 at 2560×1600: 16 × 16 ÷ 18.868 = 13.57 in wide (0.03 in narrower). It sits directly above the laptop, so width is the edge that lines up. 16 × 10 ÷ 18.868 = 8.48 in tall; 3018.9 ÷ 16 = 188.7 PPI.
  • A 14.1-inch 1080p screen: 14.1 × 9 ÷ 18.358 = 6.91 in tall (0.74 in shorter); 2202.9 ÷ 14.1 = 156.2 PPI; 141.2 ÷ 156.2 = 0.904, so everything draws 9.6% smaller.

Why panel size and sharpness decide a 15.6-inch match

An extender with the same diagonal, shape and resolution as your laptop screen has the same pixels per inch. A window or a spreadsheet row is then the same physical size on every screen, and the screens' edges line up. That is why the 15.6-inch 1080p sets lead this table. A sharper 14.1-inch or 16-inch panel (187–189 PPI) shows more detail, but it draws text smaller until you raise that screen's scaling. A 14.1-inch 1080p screen draws everything 9.6% smaller and stands 0.74 in shorter than the laptop beside it.

The second check is whether the screens run on your laptop at all, and the makers' own wording settles it. Screens that need no software need one video output per added screen. Aura says the Dual Go Max needs "two video outputs". FOPO says its S10 needs either two video-capable USB-C ports or "USB-C + HDMI + USB-A power". Running two screens from one cable means installing a driver: Aura says "One quick driver install on first use" for the Dual Boost Pro Max Slim. Makers also count screens differently. Mobile Pixels' "Dual Screen" Duex adds one screen and its "Tri-Screen" Trio adds two, while Aura's "Dual" adds two. FOPO states that "Base M1, M2 and M3 MacBooks are not supported" for the S10's two-screen setup. A base M1, M2 or M3 MacBook is better served by one added screen.

Measurements also show that power matters. Notebookcheck measured the dual 15.6-inch UPERFECT Delta at 226 cd/m² on its adapter and 160 cd/m² on one USB-C cable. Macworld found the Duex Float 2 Pro capped at 80% brightness on 7.5 W laptop power and recommends a 15 W or larger charger. Macworld also says the Trio 3 Pro cannot reach full brightness on laptop power alone. Budget a wall charger for any of these.
