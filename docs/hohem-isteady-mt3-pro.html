Who it's for

Solo shooters filming themselves, a pet or a car with a mirrorless camera, who also want one gimbal for a phone and an action camera. Anyone who only needs steady footage from a camera under 2 kg can spend less on a DJI RS 3 Mini ($199) or RS 4 Mini ($309).

Caveats

  • Digital Camera World's George Cairns, with a Sony A7 and 24mm f/1.4 lens (about 1.05 kg), saw occasional pan-motor twitches, and the camera flopped over in POV roll mode despite being well under the 2.5 kg rating.
  • Fstoppers' Zhen Siang Yang found the remote's live view frames differently from the camera, most of all beyond 35mm, with no way to calibrate it; ProVideo Coalition's Iain Anderson says it shows only roughly what is in shot.
  • Fstoppers also notes the battery cannot be swapped, the axis locks are manual, and there is no weather sealing.
  • Camera Jabber's Ali Jennings rates the finish and grip comfort a step below DJI and Zhiyun at this price.
  • Maker-stated limits: the gimbal must be rebalanced after switching between horizontal and vertical, the 20-hour figure is with tracking off and the gimbal still, and the Spot Mini focus motor and twist handle come only with the $384.99 Pro Kit. At 1,070 g it is 180 g heavier than the DJI RS 4 Mini (890 g).
