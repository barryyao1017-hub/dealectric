What the differences mean

Headroom is the rated output minus what the plate draws. Elite 200 V2: 2,600 − 1,800 = 800 W. DELTA 3 Max and AC200L: 2,400 − 1,800 = 600 W. C1000 Gen 2: 2,000 − 1,800 = 200 W. AC180, P1800 and DELTA 3 Plus: 1,800 − 1,800 = 0 W. EcoFlow's own advice is to compare the cooker's input wattage with the station's rated output, and to remember that anything else plugged in shares that same output.

Minutes per charge at a full 1,800 W = measured usable Wh ÷ 1,800 × 60. Elite 200 V2: 2,028 Wh (APH Networks), about 68 min. AC180: 950 Wh (OutdoorGearLab), about 32 min. DELTA 3 Plus: 910 Wh (StorageReview), about 30 min. C1000 Gen 2: 907 Wh (The Technology Man, UK model), about 30 min. APH Networks and The Technology Man measured at about 1 kW, so these are estimates for a full 1,800 W draw.

Watt-hours per meal: 20 minutes at full power uses 1,800 × 20 ÷ 60 = 600 Wh. That gives the Elite 200 V2 about 3 such sessions per charge (2,028 ÷ 600 = 3.4) and a 1 kWh station about 1.5 (907 to 950 ÷ 600). Lower settings draw less, so simmering uses far fewer watt-hours.

Cost per usable Wh: Elite 200 V2, $899 ÷ 2,028 Wh = $0.44. AC180, $549 ÷ 950 Wh = $0.58.

Why boost watts don't count for an induction cooktop

Most stations print a bigger number next to the rated output. BLUETTI calls it Power Lifting and EcoFlow calls it X-Boost. EcoFlow says X-Boost "works by lowering voltage, so results vary by device" and tells buyers: "Do not rely on X-Boost as a substitute for the standard rating." BLUETTI says the Elite 200 V2's 3,900 W Power Lifting is for "pure resistive loads" such as kettles, electric blankets and hair dryers. An induction plate is not a resistive load. Its electronics watch the supply voltage, and the Duxtop 9620LS manual lists error E3: "Voltage input is too high or too low and the unit shuts down." No maker has published a test of an induction plate running on a boost mode. The shutdown risk is our reading of the two makers' wording. Peak figures (3,000 W on the C1000 Gen 2, 3,600 W on the P1800) are short surge limits, not running power.

Outlet ratings matter too. Anker lists the C1000 Gen 2's AC output as 120 V 15 A and 120 V 16.66 A, 2,000 W max (120 × 16.66 = 2,000 W). An 1,800 W plate draws 120 V × 15 A, which leaves 200 W for anything else plugged in. The Elite 200 V2 runs the same plate on its rated 2,600 W, with no boost mode involved.

Not recommended

Avoid pairing an 1,800 W plate at full power with an 1,800 W-rated station: the BLUETTI AC180, ALLPOWERS P1800 or EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus. They have 0 W of rated headroom, and the 2,700 W Power Lifting and 2,200 W X-Boost figures that would carry them higher are the boost modes described above. They suit a plate you keep at 1,500 W or below. The C1000 Gen 2 has only 200 W spare, and The Technology Man's UK model overheated after 26 minutes on a 2 kW heater. Plan on short meals with it, not long braises.
