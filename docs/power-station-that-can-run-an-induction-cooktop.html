Updated 2026-11-08

Why 1,800 W stations are tight

A plate set to maximum draws its full 1,800 W, which is the AC180's, P1800's and DELTA 3 Plus's continuous limit, leaving no margin; one owner's 1,800 W induction stove shut down an 1,800 W inverter after about a minute on high. Cook at 1,200–1,500 W on those stations, or pick an inverter rated 2,000 W or more. A 1,000–1,500 W kettle is easier: one owner measured 87 Wh to boil a litre with a 1,450 W kettle.

How many meals a charge gives

Boiling a litre takes about 90–110 Wh (87 Wh measured with a kettle; about 112 Wh quoted for induction). A simple one-pot meal at 1,200 W for 15 minutes is about 300 Wh. So 1 kWh is three or four meals or eight boils; 2 kWh about twice that (arithmetic, before inverter losses of roughly 10%).

Low settings that aren't low

Many cheap plates reach low heat by switching 1,200 W on and off rather than drawing less (Permies). On a station that cycling is fine for energy but the inverter still sees 1,200 W peaks, so size for them.
