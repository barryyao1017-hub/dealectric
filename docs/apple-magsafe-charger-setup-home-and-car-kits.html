What the differences mean

  • Home stand: 25 W to the phone + 5 W to the Watch + 5 W to the AirPods = 35 W. That is more than the 30 W adapter Apple names for its single puck. ESR's 50 W charger leaves 50 − 35 = 15 W of headroom before conversion losses.
  • Car: the OmniLock needs at least 35 W in before it tries Qi2.2 speed. The included adapter's USB-C port gives 40 W, so 40 − 35 = 5 W is spare. Its 18 W USB-A port is 35 − 18 = 17 W short.
  • Single puck: UGREEN's MagFlow Pro asks for 45 W. The Nexode 65W's USB-C ports give 65 W, so 65 − 45 = 20 W is spare. Its USB-A port gives 22.5 W, half of what the puck needs, so use a USB-C port.

How much power a MagSafe setup really needs

Apple's rule covers one charger and one phone: a 30W (15V/2.0A) USB-C adapter for up to 25 W, a 20W adapter for up to 15 W, and 12 W at minimum, which charges more slowly. A setup adds loads on top of that, and each end of it has its own power source:

In the car, Bradley Chambers of 9to5Mac wrote that a USB-A port built into the car, "or even a USB-C port, is probably not going to provide enough power" for Qi2.2 speed. The charger still works from those ports, but slower. Plug it into the 40 W USB-C port on the included 12 V adapter. At home, Macworld's Simon Jary measured an iPhone 16 Pro going from 0% to 50% in just over half an hour on the ESR 3-in-1 with its own charger. Both ESR pieces need a magnetic case 2 mm or thinner.

Also avoid powering any 25 W car mount from the car's own USB port, and running Apple's puck on a 20W adapter, which caps it at 15 W.
