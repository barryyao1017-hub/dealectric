One dongle for both: when it works and what it costs

A shared receiver works only inside one maker's system, and only for the models that maker lists. It is not a general USB feature. Even Redragon's own sets differ: the S136 and BS8773 share one 2.4 GHz dongle, while Redragon notes that the HORUS K618 combo's keyboard and mouse "use separate dongles" in 2.4 GHz mode.

  • Logitech LIGHTSPEED: at most 2 devices (one keyboard and one mouse) on the keyboard's receiver, set up in G HUB 2022.7 or later. Headsets are excluded. The supported keyboards are the G715, G915, G915 TKL, G915 X, PRO X TKL, PRO X 60 and G515 LIGHTSPEED TKL. The supported mice include the G705, G309, G303 Shroud Edition, the PRO X SUPERLIGHT models, the PRO 2 LIGHTSPEED and the G502 X line. Logitech's own advice: "For maximum performance, we recommend using your keyboard and mouse with their individual receivers."
  • Polling above 1 kHz: Logitech says the PRO 2 LIGHTSPEED mouse can report above 1 kHz when it is connected to the PRO LIGHTSPEED receiver, which is sold separately. On a shared keyboard receiver, plan on 1 kHz.
  • Razer: keyboard-and-mouse sharing works on "select" HyperSpeed models paired in Synapse. Razer's 8,000 Hz HyperPolling mouse dongle and 4,000 Hz keyboard dongle have no multi-device support.
  • Software: Redragon offers the S136 driver as a Windows download only. Typing and pointing work without it, but you need it for macros, lighting and custom DPI steps. Keychron's bundle lists macOS, Windows and Linux support.

For office work or casual gaming, one shared dongle saves a port and is enough. For competitive play at 4,000 to 8,000 Hz, give the mouse its own receiver and plan on two ports.

Not recommended

If your goal is a mechanical keyboard and mouse on one receiver, skip the Redragon HORUS K618 + M693 combo. It costs more than the S136 at Redragon's store, and Redragon itself says its keyboard and mouse use separate dongles in 2.4 GHz mode.
