What the differences mean

The pick, the Pecron F3000LFP, is not a product Dealectric links, and we earn nothing from it. Dealectric does not test units: the efficiency and idle figures are The Solar Lab's, the specs are the makers', and the arithmetic below is ours.

  • Usable watt-hours = rated Wh × measured efficiency. Pecron: 3,072 × 0.83 = 2,550 Wh. Elite 200 V2: 2,073.6 × 0.94 = 1,949 Wh. Jackery: 2,042 × 0.90 = 1,838 Wh. S2000: 2,010 × 0.91 = 1,829 Wh. DELTA 3 Max: 2,048 × 0.79 = 1,618 Wh.
  • Cost per usable Wh = price ÷ usable Wh. Pecron: $899 ÷ 2,550 = 35.3¢. S2000: $649.99 ÷ 1,829 = 35.5¢. Elite 200 V2: $854.05 ÷ 1,949 = 43.8¢. DELTA 3 Max: $879 ÷ 1,618 = 54.3¢. Jackery: $999 ÷ 1,838 = 54.4¢.
  • The rated order flips. Per rated Wh the DELTA 3 Max looks cheaper than the Elite 200 V2 at store prices: $879 ÷ 2,048 = 42.9¢ against $899 (pre-order, ships early January 2027) ÷ 2,073.6 = 43.4¢. Per usable Wh it is 54.3¢ against $899 (pre-order, ships early January 2027) ÷ 1,949 = 46.1¢.
  • Continuous watts per dollar. Pecron: 3,600 ÷ 899 = 4.00. Elite 200 V2: 2,600 W ÷ $854.05 = 3.04. DELTA 3 Max: 2,400 ÷ 879 = 2.73. S2000: 1,500 W ÷ $649.99 = 2.31. Jackery: 2,200 ÷ 999 = 2.20.
  • Idle loss per day = idle watts × 24 h, with the unit left on and nothing plugged in. The Solar Lab says the AC inverter was on for the Pecron, DELTA 3 Max and S2000 figures; its Elite 200 V2 and Jackery reviews do not say. Pecron: 37 × 24 = 888 Wh (29% of its battery). DELTA 3 Max: 27 × 24 = 648 Wh (32%). Jackery: 17 × 24 = 408 Wh (20%). Elite 200 V2: 9.5 × 24 = 228 Wh (11%). S2000: 6 × 24 = 144 Wh (7%).
  • Estimated hours at a steady 100 W = usable Wh ÷ (100 W + idle W). Pecron: 2,550 ÷ 137 = 18.6 h. Elite 200 V2: 1,949 ÷ 109.5 = 17.8 h. S2000: 1,829 ÷ 106 = 17.3 h. Jackery: 1,838 ÷ 117 = 15.7 h. DELTA 3 Max: 1,618 ÷ 127 = 12.7 h. This is our estimate, not a test: it adds the reviewer's idle draw on top of the load. On the same sum the Elite 200 V2 outlasts the Pecron below an average load of about 80 W (both give about 21.8 h at 80 W).

Measured efficiency and idle draw, collected

A power station never delivers its full rated watt-hours through the AC outlets: the inverter loses some in conversion, and it draws power for as long as it is switched on, even with nothing plugged in. The Solar Lab publishes both figures for each unit, which is why this page uses one reviewer throughout. Limits: its reviews carry no author or date, it states the test load only for the Pecron (3,600 W, full to empty), and it calls the Pecron and DELTA 3 Max idle figures calculated. As a cross-check, Dylan Dougherty at StorageReview drew 1.91 kWh from the Elite 200 V2, which is 1.91 ÷ 2.0736 = 92% of rated.
