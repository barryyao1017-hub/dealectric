What the differences mean

The inverter's idle loss comes off the top every day, before the battery gets anything: 20 W × 24 h = 480 Wh. That is 17.4% of the kit's 2,764.8 Wh (480 ÷ 2,764.8).

Kit refill: 1,000 W × 4 h = 4,000 Wh, minus 480 Wh, leaves 3,520 Wh a day, and 2,764.8 ÷ 3,520 = 0.8 day from empty.

The 400 W bundle: 400 W × 4 h = 1,600 Wh, minus 480 Wh, leaves 1,120 Wh, and 5,529.6 ÷ 1,120 = 4.9 days. Its panels have to cover the same 480 Wh idle loss out of less than half the output.

With a duller sun: Impulse Gamer averaged closer to 400 W per SORA 500 in Australian winter. 2 × 400 W × 4 h = 3,200 Wh, minus 480 Wh, leaves 2,720 Wh, and 2,764.8 ÷ 2,720 = 1.0 day.

Price per stored Wh: the kit is $3,197 for 2,764.8 Wh. The 2×B300K bundle is $5,499 for 8,294.4 Wh on the same panels, so the extra 5,529.6 Wh costs $2,302.

Why panel watts decide whether the battery keeps up

A battery sized for several days of power only helps if the panels can refill it between cloudy spells. BLUETTI says the Apex 300 cuts idle loss to 20 W. That loss runs around the clock, so a small array spends most of its output keeping the inverter on.

The inputs limit how many panels you can use. Notebookcheck lists the Apex 300's built-in solar inputs as 2× XT60 at 12–60 V and 20 A each. Impulse Gamer lists the SORA 500 at 49.1 V open-circuit and 13.31 A short-circuit, so one panel goes on each input. That makes 1,000 W the most the built-in inputs take from these panels. BLUETTI's 4,000 W solar figure carries the footnote 'Requires a SolarX 4K'.

Buying the items separately costs less than the store's bundle. The Apex 300 on its own ($1,499) and two SORA 500 panels ($849 each) come to $3,197. The store's Apex 300 + 2×500W bundle is $3,299 for the same set, $102 more.
