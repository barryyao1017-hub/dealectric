What the differences mean

  • Cable sets the ceiling. A USB-C cable without an e-marker chip carries 3 A: 20 V × 3 A = 60 W. A 5 A e-marked cable carries 20 V × 5 A = 100 W. Going above that needs PD 3.1's 28 V mode and an EPR (240 W) cable: 28 V × 5 A = 140 W.
  • A 65 W charger on a plain cable gives 60 of its 65 W: 60 ÷ 65 = 92%.
  • Price per watt from one port (maker stores, 2026-11-17): AOHi The Youth 140W $59.99 ÷ 140 W = $0.43; Anker Nano 100W $35.99 ÷ 100 W = $0.36; UGREEN Nexode 100W $54.99 ÷ 100 W = $0.55; UGREEN Nexode Pro 160W $89.99 ÷ 140 W = $0.64.

Which MacBook needs which charger and cable

Apple's fast-charge page (published 2026-11-05) defines fast charging as about 50% in around 30 minutes. For the 16-inch MacBook Pro, Apple lists only its 140 W USB-C adapter: either with a USB-C to MagSafe 3 cable, or with the 240 W USB-C Charge Cable on the 16-inch from Nov 2023 or later. No 96 W or 70 W adapter appears for the 16-inch. For the 14-inch Pro, Apple lists the 140 W and 96 W adapters. For the MacBook Air (2022 or later), it lists 140 W, 96 W and 70 W, plus 67 W for 13.6-inch models only. Apple's list names only its own adapters, so we read those wattages as the floor a third-party charger has to meet.

Anker's support page for its Prime 160W gives the cable rule by chip: on a 16-inch MacBook Pro with M1 or M2, 140 W comes only over a USB-C to MagSafe 3 cable; on M3 and M4 models, an EPR USB-C cable also works. USB-IF explains why: USB PD stopped at 100 W (20 V on a 5 A cable) until PD 3.1 added a fixed 28 V level for 140 W. A cable labelled 100 W therefore stops at 100 W, even when the charger is rated 140 W.

The AOHi The Youth 140W reaches 140 W from one USB-C port, and only while that port charges alone. Notebookcheck reports that with two devices plugged in, each USB-C port drops to 65 W. AOHi says it takes a 16-inch MacBook Pro from 0% to 56% in 30 minutes. If you charge a laptop and a phone together every day, the UGREEN Nexode Pro 160W is the better fit: Notebookcheck lists 140 W on both USB-C1 and USB-C2. In Notebookcheck's one-hour test with four other devices charging, USB-C1 still delivered about 56–58 W. UGREEN's store also lists a Nexode Pro 160W + 240W USB-C cable bundle at $71.60 (2026-11-17).

Not recommended

Anker Prime 100W 3-Port (A2688). Anker's US store listed it as sold out on 2026-11-17. Anker's own spec sheet also shows how much the laptop loses once a second device is plugged in. With both USB-C ports in use, the split is 65 W + 35 W, so the laptop on the top port gets 65 W. With the laptop on the lower USB-C port and a phone on USB-A, each gets 12 W, which is below what most laptops need to charge under load. At its best it is a 100 W charger, so it also can't fast-charge a 16-inch MacBook Pro on Apple's list.
