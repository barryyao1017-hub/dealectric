What the differences mean

Willrun reports ResMed’s power table for the AirSense 11 at 7 W with the humidifier off, 19 W with the HumidAir humidifier, and 55 W with the humidifier plus the ClimateLineAir heated tube. ResMed’s user guide gives 56.1 W typical for the whole system. Here is what that comes to over an 8-hour night:

  • Humidifier off, on ResMed’s 12 V DC converter: 7 W ÷ 0.75 × 8 h = 75 Wh a night. The 0.75 is the converter’s full-load ratio: 90 W out for 120 W in. So the AC180’s measured 985 Wh lasts 985 ÷ 75 = 13 nights.
  • Humidifier off, on the AC outlet: OffGridVolt measured the AC180’s inverter at 15 W idle, so a night takes (7 + 15) × 8 = 176 Wh. That gives 985 ÷ 176 = 5.6 nights, short of a week. The DC converter is what makes one charge last.
  • Humidifier on, heated tube off: On DC a night takes 19 ÷ 0.75 × 8 = 203 Wh, so an AC180 lasts 985 ÷ 203 = 4.9 nights. On AC the Elite 200 V2 takes (19 + 10) × 8 = 232 Wh a night, so its 1,910 Wh lasts 8.2 nights with no sun at all.
  • Humidifier and heated tube on: A night takes 56.1 × 8 = 449 Wh. That gives 985 ÷ 449 = 2.2 nights on an AC180, or 1.7 counting its 15 W idle. The Elite 200 V2 gives 1,910 ÷ 449 = 4.3 nights, or 3.6 counting its 10 W idle. None of these stations stores a full heated week, so the panel has to make up the rest.

Turn ECO off on the AC180 before the trip. Its manual sets the auto-off thresholds at 15–30 W on AC and 5–10 W on DC, after 1–4 hours. A dry CPAP draws 7 W, or about 9.3 W at the car outlet through ResMed’s converter (7 ÷ 0.75), which is under the whole AC range and under the 10 W DC setting. Test one full night at home on the exact setup you’ll take.

How many hours of sun finish a humidified week

Seven heated nights take 7 × 449 = 3,142 Wh at the outlet. Counting the AC180’s inverter idle, that becomes 7 × (56.1 + 15) × 8 = 3,982 Wh. If you start with a full battery, the panel has six days to cover the rest. OffGridVolt measured BLUETTI’s 200 W panel putting 165–180 W into an AC180 (June, Arizona, 75°F). On the third day of its camping test the panel took the station from 23% to 74%, about 588 Wh. The figures below use the low 165 W and each station’s measured outlet-to-battery ratio: 985 ÷ 1,152 = 0.855 for the AC180, and 1,910 ÷ 2,073.6 = 0.921 for the Elite 200 V2.

  • AC180 + 200 W: (3,142 − 985) ÷ 6 ÷ 0.855 ÷ 165 = 2.5 h a day, or 3.5 h counting the 15 W idle. Even then the battery needs 584 Wh a day, under the 588 Wh OffGridVolt saw.
  • AC180 + 2× 200 W: half the hours, 1.3–1.8 h. 400 W of panels is under the AC180’s 500 W solar limit. $1,232 on bluettipower.com, $1,170.40 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-ac180-2x200w-solar-kit
  • Elite 200 V2 + 200 W: (3,142 − 1,910) ÷ 6 ÷ 0.921 ÷ 165 = 1.4 h. With its 10 W idle the week needs 3,702 Wh, which works out to 2.0 h.
  • Elite 200 V2 + 2× 200 W: 0.7–1.0 h.

Read “hours of sun” as hours at full panel output. A clear summer site gives you that by lunch. A tree-shaded site in November may not, and that’s where the bigger battery is worth the extra money. Prices at BLUETTI’s US store on 2026-11-13: AC180 + 200 W $769, AC180 + 2× 200 W $1,232, Elite 200 V2 + 200 W $1,199, Elite 200 V2 + 2× 200 W $1,499.

ResMed’s Air11 DC/DC converter (ref 39231) takes 12 V at 10 A, the same rating as the AC180’s regulated 12 V/10 A car outlet. ResMed says it powers the AirSense 11 with the HumidAir humidifier and ClimateLineAir heated tube. It shuts off when its supply drops too low to run the machine, and its light turns amber first. CPAPsupplies.com sells it in the US for $95 with no prescription. The lighter BLUETTI Elite 100 V2 weighs 25 lb against the AC180’s 35.27 lb and costs $729 with a 200 W panel. Its product page gives no 12 V car outlet rating, though, so you can’t check it for the DC-converter setup above. $499 on bluettipower.com, $474.05 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-100-v2

Not recommended

BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 + 100 W ($489.99): Gidon at The Technology Man measured 231 Wh on AC and 244 Wh on DC. That’s about half of one 449 Wh heated night. He also measured 11 W of drain with its DC outputs on and nothing plugged in, which empties 244 Wh in 22 hours. BLUETTI’s own page claims 4.5 W standby. With the humidifier off on DC it gives 244 ÷ 75 = 3.3 nights, and only if you switch the port off every morning. That covers half a week.
