What the differences mean

Why $300 is the line: it is the top of the small class. On 2026-11-27 the stores we checked sold 288 to 576 Wh units with 300 to 600 W outlets below $300. The next size up, EcoFlow's 768 Wh / 800 W RIVER 2 Pro, cost $349.00. Every row was $300 or less at the seller named, on the date we checked. Left out for being just over: EcoFlow's RIVER 520 Gen4 at $309.00 (EcoFlow US store, 2026-11-27). Also left out: the 512 Wh Segway Lumina 500, which Segway's US store listed at $399.99 and out of stock the same day. The Pecron is under the line only at its sale price (compare-at $528.00).

Within the $300 limit, we rank by capacity per dollar first, then by continuous AC watts.

  • Rated Wh per dollar. Pecron E500LFP: 576 ÷ 269 = 2.1. EcoFlow RIVER 2 Max 500: 499 ÷ 289 = 1.7. BLUETTI Elite 30 mini: 288 ÷ $189.05 = 1.5. BLUETTI Elite 30 V2: 288 ÷ $299.99 = about 1.0.
  • Rated lifetime energy (rated Wh × rated cycles, no fade or inverter loss applied). Pecron 576 × 3,500 = 2,016 kWh. Elite 30 mini 288 × 6,000 = 1,728 kWh. EcoFlow 499 × 3,000 = 1,497 kWh. Elite 30 V2 288 × 3,000 = 864 kWh.
  • Lifetime kWh per dollar (the line above divided by the same prices). Elite 30 mini 9.1, Pecron 7.5, EcoFlow 5.2, Elite 30 V2 about 3. Only Pecron and EcoFlow state the capacity left at the end (80%).
  • Calendar check. At one full cycle a day, 3,000 cycles is 8.2 years (3,000 ÷ 365) and 6,000 is 16.4 years. An occasional user will not use up the mini's extra cycles.
  • Energy refilled per minute from the wall. EcoFlow 499 Wh ÷ 60 min = 8.3. Pecron 576 ÷ 90 = 6.4. Elite 30 V2 to 80%: 230 Wh ÷ 45 = 5.1. Elite 30 mini to 80%: 230 ÷ 46 = 5.0.
  • What reaches the outlet. The Technology Man measured 231 Wh of AC from the 288 Wh Elite 30 V2 (80%). It also measured 439 Wh from the 512 Wh RIVER 2 Max at about 200 W (86%), and StorageReview got 410 Wh from the same EcoFlow model. Both reviewers tested the 512 Wh version, not the 499 Wh one on sale.

Continuous watts, with the boost modes stripped out

Every unit here is a 300 to 600 W machine. The larger numbers in the listings are boost modes for heating elements, not extra inverter power.

  • EcoFlow X-Boost (1,000 W on the RIVER 2 Max 500). EcoFlow describes it as "intelligent voltage control that marginally reduces voltage, allowing you to power devices with a lower-rated inverter", and says it "is not available to use when your EcoFlow portable power station is charging". The inverter stays at 500 W.
  • BLUETTI Power Lifting (1,500 W on the Elite 30 V2, 600 W on the mini). BLUETTI limits it to "pure resistive devices". The Technology Man measured no more than 600 W of real power from the Elite 30 V2 in this mode. A 450 W air duster with a start-up surge of about 2,300 W overloaded the unit with Power Lifting off and ran with it on.
  • Pecron E500LFP. Pecron's listing gives one figure, a 600 W pure sine inverter.

A kettle or heater on any of these gets about the rated watts and heats more slowly. A motor that needs more than the rated watts to start can trip the unit. None of the four suits a 1,500 W space heater.

Cycle claims need the same care. Pecron's "3,500+ Life Cycles to 80%" and EcoFlow's 3,000 cycles to 80%+ say how much capacity is left at the end. BLUETTI's "Over 3,000" and "6,000+" give no percentage, so their lifetime figures above are less certain. One more measured caution on the Elite 30 V2: The Technology Man saw about 19 W of drain with the inverter on and Eco mode off. That took the battery from 100% to 19% in 12 hours with nothing plugged in.

Not recommended

The Elite 30 V2 in Light Gray or Deep Gray, if you need it this month: BLUETTI's page says Light Gray ships Dec 28 and Deep Gray in early January. Blush Pink carried no ship notice on 2026-11-27. The Elite 30 V2 is also the wrong buy for runtime alone, at about 1 rated Wh per dollar against 2.1 for the Pecron. Its case is the UPS, the charging speed and the weight. Before paying for the EcoFlow, check its ship date. The store's feed listed it as available at $289.00 on 2026-11-27, but one reading of the product page that day showed a "Sold out" pre-order note.
