  • Outlet headroom. Goal Zero's manual rates the Sherpa 100AC's outlet at 110 V, up to 0.9 A, 100 W max, which leaves it 50 W short of ResMed's 150 W inverter minimum. The T320's 200 W continuous clears that line by 50 W, and the Elite 30 V2's 600 W clears it by 450 W.

What a 100 W AC outlet can and can't run

The outlet on a power bank is a small inverter, and its rating decides what you can plug in. Goal Zero's manual rates the Sherpa 100AC's US outlet at 110 V, up to 0.9 A, 100 W max, 60 Hz, modified sine wave, and lists no surge figure. Omnicharge also calls the Omni 20+ outlet a modified sine wave. On the Omni, you first double-press the power button and choose 120 V AC mode rather than 150 V HVDC mode before the socket works.

CPAP. ResMed's camping guidance (Australian site) says newer machines (AirMini, AirSense 10, AirCurve 10, Lumis, S9) can run from a modified sine wave inverter with a continuous rating of 150 W or higher, or from a ResMed DC converter. Older setups with H2i, H3i or H4i humidifiers need a pure sine inverter rated 300 W continuous and 500 W peak. Both flight-legal banks here are 100 W, so neither meets the 150 W line. The T320 (200 W continuous) and the Elite 30 V2 (600 W) meet it. For the older 300 W pure-sine case, the 200 W T320 is too small.

Laptops. A USB-C laptop doesn't need the inverter at all. Goal Zero's manual rates the Sherpa's USB-C PD port 2 at 100 W (5–20 V, up to 5 A) and port 1 at 60 W. Goal Zero's current store listing for the New Sherpa 100AC says 60 W for each port, so check the port label on the unit you receive before relying on 100 W. The Omni 20+ adds a 5–25 V DC barrel output, up to 100 W, and its USB-C tops out at 60 W (Omnicharge FAQ).

Flying. IATA's passenger guidance: up to 100 Wh is generally allowed in carry-on; 100–160 Wh may be allowed with airline approval; over 160 Wh is usually not permitted; spare batteries and power banks never go in checked baggage.

Not recommended

Omnicharge Omni 20+. It costs $249 on Omnicharge's store, within a dollar of the Sherpa 100AC, but holds 71 Wh, so it works out to $3.51/Wh, the highest here. PCWorld measured 56.6 Wh delivered. In techtest.org's test, Michael Barton ran an EU unit at a constant 85 W and it shut down with an over-temperature warning with about 40% of its charge still unused. It is the lightest carry-on AC bank here at 1.4 lb (PCWorld), but the Sherpa delivers more energy per dollar.
