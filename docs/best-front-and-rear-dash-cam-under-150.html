What parking mode adds to each price

A dash cam plugged into the 12 V socket switches off with the car. To record a bump or a break-in while parked, it needs a hardwire kit: a cable to the fuse box that keeps the camera powered and cuts it off before the car battery runs down. Vantrue's Voltage Display Hardwire Kit lets you set that cut-off at 11.6, 12 or 12.4 V. Each maker sells its own kit, and the price varies more than the cameras do:

  • Vantrue: Voltage Display Hardwire Kit, $29.99 (list $34.99), USB-C; its listing names the S1 Pro. The S1 Pro's listing gives up to 15 seconds of pre-event footage in parking mode.
  • 70mai: UP03, $27.99, for USB-C dash cams. 70mai's A810 page says UP03 is required for all parking functions and is sold separately. The A810 Lite listing marks parking protection "Hardwire Kit required" and sells a Front + Rear Cam + 64GB + UP03 set at $129.99.
  • Botslab: Type-C Hardwire Kit with Fuse Tap, $44.99 (list $54.99); its listing names the G980H. Botslab's spec page says "Hard Wire Kit: Not Included".

All kit prices were read on the makers' stores on 2026-11-21. None of these totals counts a memory card where one is not in the box: the Vantrue and the A810 ship without one.

Not recommended for a car parked in the sun: 70mai A810

The A810 looks like the bargain here: the same Sony STARVIS 2 IMX678 front sensor as dearer cameras for $129.99 with the rear camera. But 70mai's own spec table lists a 500 mAh battery, and DashCamTalk calls that "not ideal in hot climates". DashCamTalk also found the front camera drops from 30 to 25 fps once the rear is connected, and names the rear sensor as a SmartSens SC200AI, so the Sony claim covers the front channel only. In a mild climate it pairs 3840 × 2160 with the Sony STARVIS 2 IMX678 for $129.99; in a hot one, pick a supercapacitor model.
