What the differences mean

A gaming PC resets when its power supply drops the power-good (PWR_OK) signal. That happens a few milliseconds before the PSU's output rails actually fail. Cybenetics measured this window at 14.1 ms on an XPG KYBER 850 (hold-up 15.8 ms), so the table uses 14.1 ms as the deadline. A station has to finish switching over before then.

  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus: 14.1 − 8 ≈ 6 ms to spare, using StorageReview's measured 'just over 8 ms'.
  • BLUETTI Elite 300: 14.1 − 8 = 6.1 ms to spare (StorageReview measured 8 ms).
  • 10 ms and under-10 ms ratings (Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2, BLUETTI Elite 100 V2, EcoFlow DELTA 3 Classic): 14.1 − 10 = 4.1 ms to spare. Against the 11 ms ATX 3.1 minimum that every compliant PSU must meet, the margin is 11 − 10 = 1 ms.
  • BLUETTI Elite 200 V2: 14.1 − 15 = −0.9 ms. It only holds a PC up if the PSU's power-good window is longer than this one's.
  • 20 ms ratings (BLUETTI AC180, Jackery Explorer 2000 v2 'under 20 ms'): 14.1 − 20 = −5.9 ms.
  • Runtime at a 500 W wall draw: 910 Wh ÷ 500 W = 1.8 h on the DELTA 3 Plus; 890 Wh ÷ 500 W = 1.8 h on the C1000 Gen 2; 1,910 Wh ÷ 500 W = 3.8 h on the Elite 200 V2; 2,670 Wh ÷ 500 W = 5.3 h on the Elite 300.

Measured switchover against measured PSU hold-up

When the grid drops, the station has to move the load from wall power to its inverter. During that gap, the PSU's capacitors keep the PC running. Seasonic says ATX 3.1 requires 12 ms of hold-up at full load, down from 17 ms in ATX 3.0, and recommends 17 ms at 80% load. HWBusters summarizes the spec's power-good minimum as 11 ms from AC loss. Real gaming PSUs measure only a few milliseconds above those minimums: Cybenetics measured the XPG KYBER 850 at 15.8 ms hold-up and 14.1 ms to PWR_OK. HWBusters measured the Cooler Master Elite Gold 850W at 15.6 ms hold-up, 'below the 17ms I like to see', and the NZXT C1200 Platinum at 19.8 ms hold-up with PWR_OK dropping at 17.1 ms (230 V input).

The two current stations measured close to a dedicated UPS. Two older stations ran 7 to 9 ms slower than their ratings, so a 20 ms rating gives no margin. StorageReview tested with workstations: in its 2023 roundup the load was an idle EPYC machine, and its newer reviews don't state the wattage. None of these tests used a named gaming PSU under GPU load. StorageReview also quotes BLUETTI's Elite 300 manual, which says the UPS feature 'is not intended for data servers or workstations that require a high-performance UPS'.

Shutdown signal: EcoFlow says the DELTA 3 Plus 'sends a signal before the battery is fully depleted' over HID (set up through the EcoFlow PowerManager app and a NUT server). The twolven GitHub project reports that on September 2026 firmware, Windows 11 rejects the device's HID interface with Code 10. Its workaround is NUT's usbhid-ups driver after swapping the interface to WinUSB in Zadig, which shuts the PC down at a set charge level (15% by default). On the Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2, the UPS-mode limit is the 1,800 W bypass rather than the 2,000 W inverter, which is still far above a 500 W gaming PC.

Not recommended

The BLUETTI AC180 as a gaming-PC UPS. BLUETTI rates its switchover at 20 ms, which is 5.9 ms past the KYBER 850's 14.1 ms window. At $549 it also costs $50 more than BLUETTI's own Elite 100 V2 ($499), which is rated 10 ms with the same 1,800 W output. The AC180 is fine for a router, modem or NAS that can ride through a longer gap.
