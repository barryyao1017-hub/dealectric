Is plug-in solar legal where I live?

Laws passed (Bright Saver's tracker, 2026-11-05): Utah (in force May 2025), Maine, Maryland, Vermont (July 2026), Colorado (August 2026, 1,920 W cap), Connecticut (October 2026), California, New Hampshire, Virginia (January 1, 2027) and New Jersey (March 2027); New York's bill awaits signature. California's SB 868 caps a home at 1,200 W and requires a certified device and a free online registration in place of an interconnection application. Your lease or HOA can still say no, and a power station with a panel never touches the building's wiring.

Does it pay back?

EnergySage puts an 800 W plug-in kit at 70–110 kWh a month, $13–20 off a bill at the $0.18 US average and $40–50 in California, New York or Hawaii, for a 2–5 year payback. A battery adds about $1,000 per kWh and rarely pays back on its own; buy one for outages and evening use, not savings.

Mounting on a balcony

Railing kits use light panels zip-tied or hooked on without drilling; a vertical railing panel makes noticeably less than a tilted one, and wind load is the main safety question in apartment threads.
