Thirteen-inch laptops are the smallest common size, and an extender's "13.3-inch" is its own screen diagonal, not a statement about your lid. Apple's 13-inch MacBook Air (M2) is 11.97 in wide, 8.46 in deep and 2.7 lb, so every row above is a screen whose maker's fit range reaches down to 13 inches, and the figures below are set against that laptop. Left out because their ranges start just above the line: the 14.1-inch Duex Max DS (14–17-inch laptops, as The Dad Sneeze lists it) and the Duex Float 2 Pro (15–16-inch, Mobile Pixels). What the differences mean

  • Weight on the lid. Duex Plus DS: 1.3 lb ÷ 2.7 lb = 48% of a MacBook Air 13, 4.0 lb carried together (weight per Gigaparts' listing and John Hagensieker's review; laptop per Apple). Trio 3 Pro: 4.13 lb ÷ 2.7 lb = 153%, 6.83 lb together (Macworld).
  • Overhang. Gigaparts lists the DS body at 12.67 × 8.85 in. 12.67 × 25.4 = 321.8 mm against the Air's 304.1 mm lid, so 17.7 mm wider, about 9 mm a side when centred. 8.85 × 25.4 = 224.8 mm against 215 mm, so 9.8 mm deeper. Mobile Pixels' spec page for the earlier Duex Plus gives 12.25 × 8.46 in, which is 7.1 mm wider and level in depth.
  • Brightness. 300 nits rated ÷ the Air's 500 nits = 60%. Teoh Yi Chie (Parka Blogs) measured the earlier Duex Plus at 152 nits in 2022 and Mark B (MBReviews) read about 200 nits, so plan on a second screen visibly dimmer than the laptop's own.
  • Battery. An estimate, not a measurement: Apple's 52.6 Wh ÷ 15 h of wireless web = 3.5 W for the laptop. Add the DS's 4.5 W (Gigaparts' listing) and 52.6 ÷ 8.0 = about 6.6 h. Heavier work shortens both figures.

How an attached screen fits a 13-inch lid

Mounting. Mobile Pixels' install guide has you stick magnetic plates to the back of the lid by their adhesive, centre the Duex Plus DS on them and slide the screen out to the side. The whole 1.3 lb hangs on the lid and hinge. Teoh Yi Chie, with the earlier Duex Plus on a 13.3-inch M1 MacBook Air, reported that the weight can pull the screens down when the lid is tilted too low. John Hagensieker ran the 13.3-inch DS on a 13-inch MacBook Air in February 2026, on a unit Mobile Pixels supplied.

Cable order, per Mobile Pixels. 1) The USB-C to USB-C cable alone, if the laptop has a USB-C port. 2) If that shows no picture, also connect the HDMI cable. 3) If neither works, use the USB-C cable on its USB-A adapter together with the HDMI cable. Step 2 is what covers a 13-inch laptop whose USB-C port carries no video.

How many screens the laptop drives. Apple's specs give the M1 and M2 MacBook Air one external display. The 13-inch M3 Air drives one with the lid open and a second only with the lid closed. M4 and M5 Airs drive two with the lid open. An attached extender needs the lid open, so on a base M1–M3 Air one added screen is the ceiling without a display driver. That is why a single-screen extender is the pick at this size.

Not recommended for a 13-inch laptop

The FOPO S980 and S788 sit inside FOPO's 12–18.5-inch range, but each adds three panels of 15.3 or 16 inches, runs only through a display driver and needs a separate power supply. They suit a larger laptop at a desk. The Trio 3 Pro is not one to carry daily with a 13-inch laptop: it is 153% of the laptop's weight, and on a base M1 or M2 Air its second screen needs a driver download.
