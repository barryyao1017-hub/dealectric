A built-in retractable cable is a USB-C lead that reels into the charger body, so you travel with the plug alone. It suits travellers and anyone who keeps losing cables. The cable often carries fewer watts than the number on the box, though, so go by the maker's per-output figures or a named tester's readout. ChargerLAB, for example, reads the voltage and current steps off the built-in cable itself. Dealectric does not test units; every figure below names who stated or measured it.

What the differences mean

  • Share of the headline through the cable. Nexode Pro 160W: 140 ÷ 160 = 87.5%. Nexode 100W: 100 ÷ 100 = 100%. Nexode 65W: 60 ÷ 65 = 92%. Belkin 67W: 67 ÷ 67 = 100%.
  • What the cable keeps when you add devices. Nexode Pro 160W: 65 ÷ 140 = 46% of its solo rating with every port in use, still 65 W (PCWorld). Belkin: 45 ÷ 67 = 67% to the larger of two devices. Nexode 65W: 45 ÷ 60 = 75% with three devices. Nexode 100W: 45 ÷ 100 = 45% at most with four devices (Macwelt).
  • Volts × amps. 20 V × 5 A = 100 W on the 100W cable (ChargerLAB). 20 V × 3 A = 60 W, which is the ceiling of ESR's stated 5–20 V, up-to-3 A range and matches the 60 W AVS figure 9to5Mac gives. A 140 W cable has to run USB PD 3.1, which Notebookcheck lists for the 160W.
  • Length. Belkin's 1 m ÷ 0.3048 = 3.3 ft is the longest. ChargerLAB's 72.5 cm ÷ 30.48 = 2.38 ft for the 100W.

How many watts each built-in cable carries, alone and shared

The rated figure is a peak for one device. What a laptop actually gets depends on heat, and on whether anything else is plugged in. These are the measurements we found from named testers:

  • UGREEN Nexode 100W: Smartzone held a constant 100 W load for an average of 35 minutes before the charger settled at 65 W. Smartzone does not say whether the load was on the cable or on USB-C1, which share the same 20 V/5 A profile. ChargerLAB's readout shows the cable lacks the FCP, SCP and AFC modes the ports have, so a phone that fast-charges only over those protocols should use a port.
  • UGREEN Nexode 65W: Andreas Gohr (splitbrain.org) measured the cable holding a steady 55 W for about an hour into a Framework Laptop 13 under full CPU load, against the 60 W Cubed3 gives for the cable. techtest.org found the cable's PPS stops at 3 A, which it says holds some Samsung phones to about 25–30 W.
  • Baseus EnerCore CJ11 67W: Android Authority's Rita El Khoury measured about 65 W through its retractable cable into a MacBook Air M3, against 69.1 W from its USB-C ports. With a Pixel 10 Pro XL also plugged in, the MacBook took 45 W and the Pixel 20–23 W. The unit she tested has one retractable cable and two USB-C ports. We could not read a price on Baseus's US store today, so it is not in the table.
  • UGREEN Nexode Pro 160W: no reviewer we read metered the cable; its 140 W is UGREEN's rating. PCWorld reports the cable and the top USB-C port drop to 65 W each when all ports are in use. That is the same 65 W the 100W model's best output keeps with two devices.

Not recommended

The UGREEN Nexode 65W with Retractable Cable: its 65 W is the USB-C port's figure, not the cable's. Cubed3 gives the cable 60 W, and splitbrain measured it holding 55 W. At $41.99, it costs about the same as the Nexode 100W ($42.49), whose cable carries 100 W.
