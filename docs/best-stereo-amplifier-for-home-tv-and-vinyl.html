What the differences mean

  • Yamaha A-S301 vs Denon PMA-600NE, both rated 20 Hz–20 kHz into 8 Ω: 10·log10(60 ÷ 45) = 1.25 dB more headroom for the Yamaha. That is close to the smallest step most listeners notice, so power shouldn't decide between them.
  • Marantz Model 50 vs the 60 W amps: 10·log10(70 ÷ 60) = 0.67 dB louder on paper, for $2,000 − $379 = $1,621 more than the WiiM Amp Pro. Whatever the extra money buys, it isn't loudness.
  • Denon over Yamaha: $599 − $429.95 = $169.05 more for 60 − 45 = 15 W less full-band power per channel.
  • WiiM Amp Pro over WiiM Amp: $379 − $329 = $50 more for the same 60 W rating. Part of what the $50 buys is Dolby Digital over HDMI ARC.
  • Price per rated 8 Ω watt: WiiM Amp Pro $379 ÷ 60 = $6.32; Yamaha $429.95 ÷ 60 = $7.17; Denon $599 ÷ 45 = $13.31; Marantz $2,000 ÷ 70 = $28.57. WiiM states no test terms for its watts and Marantz attaches no THD to its rating, so treat those two figures as approximate.
  • The 4 Ω column is not a ladder. Yamaha's 140 W is a short-burst dynamic figure, Denon's 70 W is measured at one frequency at 0.7% THD, and WiiM's 120 W and Marantz's 100 W carry no stated THD. They are four different measurements.

What each amp accepts from your TV

A TV's digital audio output can send plain stereo PCM or a compressed bitstream such as Dolby Digital. Which one an amp accepts decides whether TV sound works without changing settings.

  • WiiM Amp Pro. WiiM lists its HDMI ARC and optical inputs as taking 'Stereo PCM, Dolby Digital (DTS is not supported)'. WiiM's support article adds that neither the Amp nor the Amp Pro supports Dolby Atmos or DTS. Plug it into the TV's ARC/eARC-labeled port and set the TV's output to stereo PCM or Dolby Digital. WiiM rates the Amp Pro for 8, 6 and 4 Ω speakers with a 2.67 Ω minimum, and it has a subwoofer output with a 30–250 Hz crossover.
  • WiiM Amp (non-Pro). The same WiiM article says the base Amp accepts PCM only over HDMI ARC and optical. Set the TV to PCM.
  • Denon PMA-600NE. Denon's owner's manual allows only Linear PCM at 32–192 kHz on the optical and coaxial inputs and warns: 'Do not input non-PCM signals, such as Dolby Digital, DTS and AAC. This causes noise and could damage the speakers.' Set the TV's digital output to PCM before connecting it. It has no HDMI input.
  • Yamaha A-S301. TV sound comes in over optical or coaxial; it has no HDMI ARC.
  • Marantz Model 50. The Marantz manual's specifications list no optical, coaxial, USB or HDMI input. A TV connects only through an external DAC or the TV's analog output. Its power-amp input takes 1.5 V at 15 kΩ.

Measured on the bench: Audio Science Review's Amir measured the WiiM Amp Pro at 117 W, called its noise performance 'excellent', and found its frequency response showed 'essentially no difference' between the two load impedances he tested.

Not recommended

Denon PMA-600NE. At $599 it costs $169.05 more than the Yamaha A-S301 and has 15 W less full-band power per channel. Its digital inputs need the TV set to PCM. Audio Science Review's Amir measured it and wrote that its distortion 'can't even clear the noise floor of CD (-93 to -96 dB)' and that he 'can't recommend Denon PMA-600NE'. For vinyl, the Yamaha A-S301 also has an MM phono input; for Bluetooth and TV, the WiiM Amp Pro costs less.
