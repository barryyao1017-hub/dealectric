Electronics data, structured for the assistant doing the shopping.

Dealectric normalizes the facts that decide an electronics purchase — the specifications that matter for the category, compatibility, warranty, price, return window and any active discount — into one consistent schema, so an AI assistant can compare products accurately and hand the person it's shopping for a link to the right product at the best verified price.

What's here

Normalized spec sheets

Every product in a category described with the same fields and units — watts, Wh, ports and protocols for power gear; drivers, codecs and ANC for audio; capacity, wattage and footprint for appliances. No marketing prose to parse.

Verified offers

Current price, the merchant it's sold by, shipping and return terms, and any active discount code — each with a "last verified" timestamp, so an assistant knows how fresh the data is.

Plain-language buying guides

Human-readable explainers on the specs that decide a purchase — starting with USB-C Power Delivery, PPS, GaN, power-bank airline limits and power-station chemistry — written to be cited.

Open to agents

An llms.txt, a .well-known descriptor, JSON-LD on every page and a robots.txt that welcomes AI crawlers.

Coverage

Dealectric covers consumer electronics and the small appliances that plug in next to them. We're building the catalog category by category, starting where a spec mismatch most often causes a return: charging and portable power (USB-C wall chargers, power banks with airline-limit checks, 60/100/240 W cables, Qi2 wireless chargers, portable power stations), then smart home (cameras, plugs, lighting, networking), audio (earbuds, headphones, speakers) and small appliances (robot vacuums, air purifiers, kitchen electrics). Each category gets its own field set in the schema.
