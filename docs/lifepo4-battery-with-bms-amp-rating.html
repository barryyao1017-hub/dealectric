What the differences mean

  • Vatrer Group 24: 150 A × 12.8 V = 1,920 W. That is 640 W (50%) more than the 1,280 W a 100 A BMS allows (100 A × 12.8 V).
  • Redodo 120Ah Group 24: 120 A × 12.8 V = 1,536 W. Its 12.8 V × 120 Ah = 1,536 Wh is 20% more energy than a 100Ah pack (1,536 ÷ 1,280 = 1.2).
  • Inverter sizing on 12 V: a 1,500 W load pulls 1,500 ÷ 12.8 ≈ 117 A from the battery before inverter losses. That is over a 100 A BMS and inside Vatrer's 150 A.
  • Charge time: Redodo recommends a 20 A (0.2C) LiFePO4 charger, so 100 Ah ÷ 20 A = 5 hours from empty.

How the BMS sets the limits

The BMS inside a LiFePO4 battery switches the pack off when current goes past its rating. The BMS amp figure times 12.8 V nominal is therefore the most power the battery will hold for more than a moment. Vatrer uses this arithmetic itself: its 12V 300Ah page says the 200 A BMS 'supports up to 2,560W of continuous output' (200 × 12.8). Redodo's 120Ah Group 24 BMS 'supports max 120A continuous' and turns back on by itself 30 seconds after an overload trip. Short surges are rated separately. Redodo gives its 100Ah trolling battery 300 A for 5 s and 500 A for 1 s, and LiTime gives its Group 24 Bluetooth 500 A for 1 s.

The BMS also prevents cold charging. Victron's Lithium Battery Smart manual says a lithium cell 'will sustain permanent damage when charged at temperatures below 5°C'. Redodo's low-temperature models stop charging below 32 °F (0 °C) and resume above 41 °F (5 °C). Vatrer's Group 24 BMS 'stops charging below 32°F'. A self-heating pack sends the charger's power to its heater first. Vatrer's heated Group 24 heats from 32 °F and starts charging above 41 °F, and Redodo's self-heating 100Ah uses 100 W pads that switch off at 41 °F. $251.99 on redodopower.com, $226.80 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/redodo-12v-100ah-self-heating-lifepo4

Bench measurements: Michael Barton (charge-test.com, February 2026) measured an average of 104.2 Ah / 1,350 Wh over four runs from a Vatrer 12V 100Ah self-heating Bluetooth pack. Its BMS cut off at about 9.6 V. On techtest.org in August 2022, Barton measured 107.7 Ah / 1,378 Wh from a Redodo 12V 100Ah. Neither tested unit is the exact Group 24 model in the table, but both delivered more than 100 Ah. Capacity therefore doesn't separate the two brands. The BMS rating does.

The same arithmetic holds at 48 V: 100 A × 51.2 V = 5,120 W. That is the figure Vatrer states for its 48V 100Ah server-rack battery.

Not recommended

Skip the Redodo 12V 100Ah Group 24 Deep Cycle if you will charge in freezing weather. Its listed BMS protections are overcharge, over-discharge, over-current, short circuit and high temperature, with no low-temperature charge cutoff. For $10 more, the Redodo Group 24 Bluetooth adds the cutoff. $259.99 on redodopower.com, $234 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/redodo-12v-100ah-group-24-bluetooth-low-temp Redodo also says the Deep Cycle is not for use as a starter or golf-cart battery, or with water pumps, electric jacks, lifts or mowers.
