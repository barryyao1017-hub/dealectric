What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure here is the maker's stated figure or a named reviewer's result, and the derived figures are arithmetic on those numbers:

  • Quiet band: Anker states 0-20 dB up to 200 W of output; GridSpecLab measured the DJI Power 1000 V2 fans off only below 100 W. 200 W ÷ 100 W = 2, so Anker's stated quiet band covers twice the fridge draw.
  • Claim gap: BLUETTI's Elite 100 V2 ceiling is 30 dB against Anker's 20 dB. 30 − 20 = 10 dB, which is 10× the sound energy (10^(10/10) = 10), though BLUETTI's band runs to 600 W, three times Anker's (600 ÷ 200 = 3).
  • Overnight idle cost: The Solar Lab measured idle draw at 13.8 W on the Anker (inverter on, no load) and about 18 W on the DJI (outputs on not stated). Over a 10-hour night that is 13.8 × 10 = 138 Wh and 18 × 10 = 180 Wh, or 13.5% and 17.6% of each 1,024 Wh battery before the fridge draws anything.

To see where your fridge sits, plug it in and read the output watts on the station's display while the compressor runs; the brief start-up spike settles within seconds.

What reviewers heard at a fridge-sized load

A fridge draws power in cycles: the compressor runs, stops, and starts again through the night. A station's fan answers to inverter heat, so at a few hundred watts or less a well-tuned unit should stay still. The difference between units is firmware: some keep the fan off until a set wattage, others run it whenever an output is on.

T3's Matt Kollat found the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus in the second group: after more than a month of use, its fans ran as soon as he switched an output port on, with nothing plugged in, and came on about every minute at low loads, against EcoFlow's claim that they stay off until 600 W. Michael Klements of The DIY Life wrote the opposite, that its fan only comes on under higher loads, so the evidence on that unit is split. On the Anker, Notebookcheck heard it get much quieter to silent at lower load, and MacRumors called it near silent with small devices; both found it loud near full output or fast charging. GridSpecLab's 0 dB(A) below 100 W for the DJI is the only per-load discharge reading at fridge level we could open, but it names no tester or meter.

No reviewer we could open put a meter beside any of these stations while a household fridge cycled on AC output through a night. The table gives the closest evidence for each unit, with the load each figure covers.

Not recommended

The EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus for a fridge in or next to a bedroom: EcoFlow says its fans stay off up to 600 W, four times a 150 W fridge draw (600 ÷ 150 = 4), yet T3 heard its fans cycle about every minute at low load. If you already own one, check whether its fans run with only the fridge plugged in before relying on it overnight. The BLUETTI AC180 publishes no dB figure at any load and is a pre-order shipping Dec 24 (BLUETTI, 2026-11-26).
