What the differences mean

  • Headroom over 10 people. DS925+: 40 rated users ÷ 10 staff = 4.0×. DS425+: 10 ÷ 10 = 1.0×, and only once it has a 6 GB RAM upgrade. DS225+ and DS423: 5 ÷ 10 = 0.5×.
  • The all-in bill with two drives. A mirrored pair of Seagate IronWolf 4 TB drives costs 2 × $219 = $438 on QNAP's US store (2026-11-13). With that pair, the TS-464-8G comes to $639.00 + $438 = $1,077.00, the UGREEN DXP4800 Plus to $619.99 + $438 = $1,057.99, the UGREEN DXP4800 GT to $559.99 + $438 = $997.99 and the TerraMaster F4-425 Plus to $539.99 + $438 = $977.99. The DS925+ enclosure costs more than any of these (see the price column), so it also goes over $1,000 once drives are in.
  • Usable space. Two 4 TB drives in RAID 1 give 4 TB. Four 4 TB drives in RAID 5 give (4 − 1) × 4 = 12 TB before filesystem overhead. Filling the TS-464-8G that way costs $639.00 + 4 × $219 = $1,515.00.
  • 10GbE on hard drives. In KitGuru's backup copy on the DXP4800 Plus, 10GbE wrote 297 ÷ 230 = 1.29× faster and read 347 ÷ 257 = 1.35× faster than 2.5GbE, not the 4× the link speeds suggest. KitGuru also saw the CPU reach 100 °C and the fan get very loud during large transfers.
  • Running cost. DS925+: 37.91 W × 8,760 h = 332 kWh × 14.47¢ (EIA US commercial average, Aug 2026) ≈ $48 a year. TS-464: 40.536 W × 8,760 h = 355 kWh ≈ $51 a year. Both are maker figures with drives fitted, so power doesn't decide the pick.

What Synology's user ratings measure

Synology lists a "Maximum Number of SMB Connections (FSCT-Based)" for each DiskStation. This is a load test, not a lockout. Microsoft's File Server Capacity Tool 1.3 runs its HomeFolder workload for 600 seconds against a Windows Server 2022 domain from 8 Windows 10 clients. FSCT simulates user actions such as opening a file in Word, copying one in Windows Explorer or deleting one from the command line, and Microsoft built the HomeFolder workload from traces of real, heavily accessed file servers. During the test every bay holds a Synology SATA hard drive and every network port is in use. The result is the largest number of simulated users the unit keeps up with. Synology tests each model that takes extra memory with the maximum installed, and it won't give complete warranty or support if the memory isn't Synology's.

The DS423 is also rated for 5 users and has only two 1GbE ports. The DS925neo+, launched in the US on 2026-10-21, carries the same 40 rating. It ships with non-ECC memory, though, and Synology doesn't support mixing that with its ECC upgrades. The DS925+ has no PCIe slot, so it stays at 2.5GbE. QNAP's figure of 1,500 for the TS-464 counts something else: the most concurrent CIFS connections with 16 GB installed. That is a connection cap rather than a workload test, so it can't be set against Synology's 40. At 10 people, neither limit comes close.

Not recommended: the Synology DS425+ for a 10-person office

The DS425+ runs the same DSM software for less money. But Synology rates it for exactly 10 users, and it earned that score with 6 GB installed (the 2 GB it ships with plus a 4 GB D4NS01-4G module), so a stock unit has no headroom at all. It also has no expansion port, tops out at 6 GB, and one of its two network ports is 1GbE.
