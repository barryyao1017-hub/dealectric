Short answer. For the most hardware under $1,000, buy the UGREEN NASync DXP4800 Plus ($619.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nasync-dxp4800-plus), $619.99 diskless on 2026-11-03: a 4-bay Intel NAS with 10GbE and 2.5GbE built in (no card to buy), 8 GB DDR5 to 64 GB, two M.2 slots, Btrfs snapshots, per-user quotas and two-factor admin login in UGOS Pro. What UGOS Pro lacks today, so the DXP4800 Plus is the pick only if these do not matter: no native S3 or Backblaze B2 backup (Dropbox yes; Duplicati in Docker is the workaround), no equivalent of Active Backup for office PCs, and owner reports of failed Active Directory joins, so test the domain join before buying. A sample bill: NAS $619.99 + four 8 TB NAS drives (about $1,200 in late 2026, 24 TB usable in RAID 5) + a UniFi Flex 2.5G switch ($159) = about $1,980.

What ten people actually need

  • Permissions and sign-in: per-user and per-group folder rights over SMB on every OS here; joining a Windows domain is documented and routine on Synology and QNAP, and offered on UGOS with owner reports of joins that failed where Synology's worked.
  • Versions and ransomware: Btrfs snapshots on UGOS (per folder, incremental), Synology's Snapshot Replication with immutable snapshots, QNAP snapshots plus Security Center, TerraMaster's lockable snapshots and WORM.
  • Backup: the NAS is the office's file store, not its backup. Synology's Active Backup for Business backs up the PCs and Macs to the NAS at no licence cost; Hyper Backup and QNAP's HBS 3 send the NAS to S3-class cloud storage; UGOS backs up to another NASync or an rsync target and to Dropbox, with S3 and B2 on UGREEN's roadmap.
  • Quotas and 2FA: both on UGOS (quotas from 1.18, authenticator-app 2FA) and on DSM and QTS.

Network: 2.5GbE or 10GbE for ten users

A 2.5GbE NAS port moves about 280 MB/s in total; ten people copying at once get about 28 MB/s each, and most office PCs sit on 1GbE (about 110 MB/s) anyway. A 10GbE port (about 1,100 MB/s) lets ten 1GbE clients all run at full speed, or one video workstation on 10GbE take the lot; four hard drives in RAID 5 will cap sustained transfers around 400–500 MB/s regardless. The switch: UniFi Flex 2.5G (eight 2.5GbE ports plus one 10G port for the NAS, $159 on 2026-11-03) for a small office; TP-Link's Omada 2.5G PoE switches with SFP+ uplinks (about $230–420) for more ports.

The bill

NAS $619.99 (DXP4800 Plus) or $639.99 (DS925+ list) + four 8 TB NAS drives (IronWolf or WD Red Plus ran about $300–350 each in 2026 guides, with lows near $170) + a $159 switch: about $1,460 to $2,200 all in, 24 TB usable. File sharing carries no per-user licence on any of them; Synology's Drive and Active Backup are free, with paid ActiveProtect packs only beyond three backup servers.
