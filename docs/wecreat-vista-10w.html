Who it's for

First-time laser buyers and crafters who mostly engrave wood, leather, slate and coated tumblers and cut plywood up to about 3 mm, and who want an enclosed machine with a camera for under $500 (on 2026-11-18 the xTool M2 10W was $499 and the Creality Falcon A1 10W $549 at their makers' stores); at 166 mm tall it also fits on a bench beside a 3D printer. Shoppers who cut 6 mm and thicker stock often should step up to the Vision 20W or Vision Pro, and those who want two cameras and add-on color inkjet printing should look at the xTool M2.

Caveats

  • Cutting is slow at 10 W: Tom's Hardware cut 3 mm basswood at 2 mm/s, and a 59-piece plywood job took about 45 minutes; The Gadgeteer said the 10 W head may be too slow for a production shop.
  • MakeIt!'s time estimates can be far off: Tom's Hardware was quoted 10 minutes 43 seconds for a line engraving that took 41 minutes 36 seconds.
  • Clear acrylic and bare metal are out: WECREAT lists only "some dark opaque acrylic" as cuttable, and Creative Bloq marked the Vista down for no metal work without the $499 infrared module and for trouble with clear blue materials.
  • The Gadgeteer found no way to reorder layers front-to-back in MakeIt!, called the crumb tray tricky to fit, and noted the external air-assist pump and the exhaust hose need space beside and behind the machine; a honeycomb panel is a $99 extra.
  • It must be vented outdoors or filtered: Tom's Hardware reported that smoke and the smell of burning wood filled a garage at once without the optional purifier.
