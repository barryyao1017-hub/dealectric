Measured capacity: what a 12V 100Ah battery really holds

The label figure is 12.8 V × 100 Ah = 1,280 Wh. Techtest.org (Michael Barton) fully charged each battery and then discharged it at the fixed currents below until it was empty.

Battery (unit tested)Measuredvs 1,280 Wh
Redodo 12V 100Ah, standard (2022)1,379 Wh at 5 A, 1,377 Wh at 10 A, 1,378 Wh at 15 A; 107.7 Ah; 11 V cutoff+7.7%
Power Queen 12.8V 100Ah, self-heating version (2023)1,368 Wh at 5 A, 1,351 Wh at 10 A, 1,321 Wh at 20 A; about 105.5 Ah+3% to +7%
ECO-Worthy 12V 100Ah, 2021 version1,315.5 Wh at 10 A, 1,289.3 Wh at 20 A; about 101.5 Ah; 10 V cutoff+1% to +3%

After about a year of daily solar cycling, Techtest.org re-measured two of them. The Power Queen had gone from about 105 Ah to about 102 Ah (about 3% lost) and the ECO-Worthy from 102 Ah to 97 Ah (about 5% lost). Each brand was tested on one sample, and each tested unit is a sibling of the current listing rather than the exact model sold today.

The Redodo unit Techtest.org measured has no low-temperature charge cutoff, and the review counts that as a minus. Charge it above 32 °F, or choose the Low-Temp version. Redodo says that version stops charging below 32 °F and resumes above 41 °F. Redodo's 14.6 V 20 A charger uses a pre-charge, constant-current, constant-voltage cycle and can reactivate a battery whose BMS has shut it off (Redodo). LiTime explains why that matters: after an over-discharge cutoff the battery shows no output, and a charger with an activation function is what usually brings it back.

Not recommended

Dakota Lithium Powerbox 10. TackleDirect sells it for $199.00 with 10 Ah and a 3 A charger. That is $199 ÷ 120 Wh = $1.66 per Wh, 2.5× Bixpower's $0.66, for half the capacity, and both take about 3.3 h to charge (10 Ah ÷ 3 A). Its '2,000+ recharge cycles' figure comes with no depth of discharge (Inverters R Us), so it can't be compared with Redodo's 4,000 cycles at 100% DoD. Buy it only if you need its built-in USB ports, 12 V socket and lights.
