Best mini projector (2026)

Updated 2026-11-05

Short answer. The best mini projector for most people is the Nebula Capsule 3 (price on the Amazon page; buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CHW11C8H?tag=dealectric-20): 1080p, licensed Netflix on Google TV, and about 2.5 hours on battery, in a soda-can size. It is dim (123 lumens measured by RTINGS), so for a brighter picture on battery step up to the XGIMI MoGo 4 (price on the Amazon page; buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F3CBV5HP?tag=dealectric-20), rated 450 ISO lumens.

Will Netflix play? Only on projectors with licensed Netflix (Capsule 3, MoGo 4, Aurzen BOOM mini, Dangbei N2). Mirroring from a phone, and most cheap Android minis, give a black screen. Aurzen's own FAQ says its ZIP needs a $99.99 dongle for Netflix. On a budget, the plug-in Aurzen BOOM mini (price on the Amazon page; buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0DR2L1L3G?tag=dealectric-20) has licensed Netflix and a 500 ANSI claim that no reviewer has measured yet.

What owners complain about

Brightness far below the box number (the most common theme: "lux" and "LED lumens" are not ANSI lumens; a "6,800 lux" mini measures about 420 ANSI at best); Netflix that won't play; fans loud at full brightness (XGIMI's MoGo 3 Pro measured 53 dBA in its brightest mode); batteries shorter than a film; and "4K supported" on 720p chips.

Are $50–100 mini projectors worth it?

Only with licensed Netflix and an ANSI figure on the spec sheet. Most "8,000 lumen" minis are 720p, measure around 100–260 lumens, run Netflix only through side-loaded apps and draw early-failure reviews. Dangbei's N2 Mini, often $149–229, is the step up that has licensed Netflix.

When the Aurzen ZIP makes sense

It folds to pocket size and mirrors a phone or Switch with no Wi-Fi, which suits camping and trips. As a Netflix projector it is the wrong buy: 720p, 100 ANSI and up to 1.5 hours.
