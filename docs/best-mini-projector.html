What the differences mean

  • Box lumens need the loud mode. The MoGo 4 reaches 90% of its rating only in Performance: 404 ÷ 450 = 0.90. That mode measured 48–52 dBA. In ECO it gives 153 ÷ 450 = 34% of the rating.
  • Plugged-in gap vs battery gap. Plugged in, the MoGo 4 is 400 ÷ 161 = 2.5× brighter than the Capsule 3. On battery the gap shrinks to 153 ÷ 112 = 1.4×.
  • Quiet brightness. In Standard, the MoGo 4 measured about 264 lm at 31–32 dBA. The Mars 3 Air measured 383 lm in Standard picture mode: 383 ÷ 264 = 1.45× the light. Its fan read 40.0–40.3 dBA in the modes ProjectorCentral measured, but that was in a 38.9 dBA room (the MoGo 4's room was 30.5 dBA), so the two reviews' dBA figures can't be subtracted.
  • A full film on battery. The Mars 3 Air ran 3 h 10 min = 190 min at 145 lm, 40 minutes past its 2.5-hour rating. The MoGo 4 has the same 2.5-hour ECO rating (151–154 lm in ECO), but its measured runs are in Standard: a 90-min film (ProjectorCentral) and 1 h 52 min (Expert Reviews).
  • Weight. Capsule 3: 0.85 kg (soundcore). MoGo 4: 2.9 lb = 1.3 kg. Mars 3 Air: 3.7 lb = 1.7 kg (ProjectorCentral for both).

Rated lumens vs the mode you will actually use

Each of these projectors has power modes that trade brightness for fan noise and runtime, and the box number comes from the brightest one. ProjectorCentral (Nikki Kahl) measured the MoGo 4 in all three modes across four picture modes: Performance 400–404 lm, Standard 258–270 lm, ECO 151–154 lm. The reviewer also noted that Performance boosts brightness at the expense of color. XGIMI rates the 2.5-hour battery figure in ECO mode. Expert Reviews (Stuart Andrews) got 1 h 52 min of streaming in Standard. XGIMI's PowerBase Stand is rated for up to 5 hours with the MoGo 4 series.

The Mars 3 Air behaves differently. ProjectorCentral (Brian Nadel) measured its fan at about 40 dBA in each mode it tested, in a 38.9 dBA room, and its Auto setting puts it into Eco as soon as you unplug, at about 145 lm. For the Capsule 3, The Hook Up measured 161 lm plugged in and 112 lm in Eco on its 200-lumen LED unit. ProjectorReviews (Philip Boyle) measured 201 lm in Bright mode and 125–145 lm in the other picture modes on the Google TV model, and reports output about 40% lower on battery.

Treat small differences with care. ProjectorCentral uses ISO 21118 and The Hook Up uses a nine-point ANSI average, so a few percent between products from different labs means little. Power is the other split. The Capsule 3 charges over USB-C from a 45 W PD charger. The MoGo 4 and MoGo 4 Laser need 65–150 W at 20 V. The Mars 3 Air charges only through its 19 V/4.74 A adapter on a proprietary power port.

Not recommended

XGIMI MoGo 4 Laser, if you are buying it for brightness. It costs $669 at XGIMI and is rated 550 ISO lumens. Laurent Willen measured 370–390 lm in standard configurations, which is no more than ProjectorCentral's 400 lm for the regular MoGo 4. He found Eco under 200 lm and got 1.5–2 hours at standard brightness, and XGIMI gives it the same 2.5-hour battery rating as the LED model. Willen measured 975:1 contrast before calibration, so buy it for its laser color and contrast, not for extra light.
