Battery hours are Eco-mode hours

XGIMI's MoGo 4 page says it plainly: "In Eco Mode, the built-in battery provides up to 2.5 hours of video playback." The same page says the 450 ISO lumen rating was measured in Performance (High Power) mode, so the brightness figure and the battery figure come from different settings. ProjectorCentral measured the MoGo 4 at 400–404 lumens in Performance, 258–270 in Standard and 151–154 in Eco. Its Standard-mode battery lasted through a 90-minute film. Expert Reviews measured 1 h 52 min of streaming in Standard. So a full film at the brighter setting fits on one charge, but only just.

Nebula's Capsule 3 spec line reads "Battery Life (Eco Mode) 2.5 Hrs". The Hook Up measured 161 lumens plugged in and 112 on battery in Eco. ProjectorCentral found that the Vibe One drops into Eco on its own when unplugged (you can turn that off) and measured 58–64 lumens there, against 229 plugged in. XGIMI rates it for 1.2 hours of video.

Power banks extend some of these but not others. The MoGo 4 takes USB-C PD at 65–150 W (XGIMI), so a 45 W bank falls short. UGREEN's 145W 25,000 mAh bank (90 Wh, 140 W from its first USB-C port) falls inside that range $79.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-145w-25000mah-3-port-power-bank. Nebula lets the Capsule 3 charge from a bank of 45 W or more at 5–20 V. The Vibe One has a DC barrel input and no USB-C charging, according to Android Authority. The Elfin Flip Plus also uses a DC jack: The Hook Up ran it for 90 minutes from a 20,000 mAh bank through a 20 V USB-C-to-barrel converter.

Not recommended

XGIMI Vibe One as a battery projector. Its 1.2-hour rating is shorter than most films. Android Authority got 96 minutes "on a good run", and ProjectorCentral measured 64 lumens on battery and called the runtime "too short for most feature-length films". Plugged in it measures 229 lumens, which makes it a reasonable low-cost bedroom projector, but not a portable one.
