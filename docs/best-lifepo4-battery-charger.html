What owners complain about

Five themes come up again and again in reviews and forum threads for 14.6 V chargers.

  • The charger won't start on a dead battery. The most common thread. A LiFePO4 battery's BMS disconnects the terminals at its low-voltage limit, usually around 10 V, and the battery then reads near 0 V. A lead-acid charger or a lithium charger without an activation stage refuses to charge it. Epoch Batteries warns against forcing voltage in from an uncontrolled source; a charger with a pre-charge or activation function is the safe fix, and every charger in the table except the Victron has one.
  • Fan noise. Five separate owners of Redodo, LiTime and ECO-WORTHY 20 A units call the fan loud or noticeable. It runs the whole time the charger is at full current. Charge in a garage or compartment, not next to a bed.
  • Current below the rating. Three owners measured 10 A to 16 A rather than 20 A. Some of that is the normal taper in the constant-voltage stage, and some is cable loss: one owner measured 14.6 V at the charger and 13.8 V at the battery through thin leads. Keep the output lead short and tight.
  • The charger says full but the monitor shows 95%. Usually the battery monitor has drifted, not the charger. A LiFePO4 battery rests at 13.4 V to 13.6 V after a full 14.6 V charge, and a shunt monitor needs that full cycle to re-sync.
  • No charge below freezing. The battery's BMS blocks charging at or below 32°F and the charger shows a fault or zero current. That is the battery protecting itself; warm it indoors or buy a self-heating battery.

Owners also ask whether the charger can stay connected. A dedicated LiFePO4 charger stops at the end of the constant-voltage stage, so leaving it on is fine for days; for storage of a month or more, unplug it and leave the battery at roughly 50% to 70%.

Not recommended

  • Any lead-acid charger without a lithium mode. Most never reach 14.6 V, so the battery stops at 90% or so, and desulfation pulses can trip the BMS.
  • The NOCO Genius10 as the only charger for a 100 Ah battery. 10 A is too slow for daily use, and its Force mode disables protections while it runs.
  • Vatrer's 14.6V 20A. It was $74.99 but out of stock on 2026-11-06; its 70 A model at $269.99 only makes sense for 300 Ah and larger banks.
