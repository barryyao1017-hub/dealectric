What the differences mean

Cost per amp is the price divided by the rated charge current. Refill time is the battery's amp-hours divided by that current, which covers the constant-current stage only. The final top-off adds a little: Victron's own worked example is 9.5 h of bulk charging plus about 0.5 h of absorption for 100 Ah on a 10 A charger.

Cost per amp: what a LiFePO4 charger's price buys

Most of a LiFePO4 refill happens at constant current: in Victron's worked example, 9.5 of the 10 hours are bulk charging. So the amp rating sets your wait, and price per amp shows what that speed costs. Among the 10–20 A chargers that suit a single 100 Ah battery, the Redodo 20A is the cheapest per amp at $4.15. The lowest figure found for a new charger is LiTime's 80 A charger, at $259.99 ÷ 80 A = $3.25 per amp. It runs on 100–120 V AC input only and uses a 120 A Anderson connector, so it suits a large bank. Use it only if your battery's own spec sheet allows 80 A of charge current.

Charge voltage. The Redodo and LiTime chargers charge to 14.6 V. The Redodo then shuts off instead of floating. In Li-ion mode the Victron stops at 14.2 V, has no float stage and holds 13.5 V for storage. It also drops temperature compensation in that mode. On a 20 Ah LiFePO4 battery in lithium mode, Auto Median (Chris Perling) measured the NOCO GENIUS10 topping off at about 14.2 V and holding about 13.6 V. The two premium chargers therefore fill a little more gently, while the 14.6 V units fill to the top.

Waking a battery whose BMS has shut off. Redodo says its 20 A and 10 A chargers 'can activate lithium battery protected by BMS', and its 40 A has a pre-charge mode that starts from 0 V. LiTime lists 0 V activation on its 20 A. Victron's manual says the Blue Smart IP65 'will automatically reset UVP and start charging'. The NOCO has a manual Force Mode instead, which Auto Median notes 'bypasses battery detection'.

Heat and cold. Victron rates the 12/15 for −20 to 50 °C, but gives full rated output only up to 30 °C (86 °F). Its manual says not to charge Li-ion below 0 °C, yet the charger keeps running below freezing, so a cold boat or RV needs a battery that blocks cold charging itself. Redodo's low-temperature 100 Ah battery stops charging below 32 °F and resumes above 41 °F. US buyers need the 120 V Victron model (100–130 VAC input).

Not recommended

The NOCO GENIUS10 as the only charger for a 100 Ah or larger LiFePO4 battery. At $9.99 per amp it costs 2.4× the Redodo 20A's $4.15 and takes about 10 h to the Redodo's 5 h on 100 Ah. Auto Median measured its lithium top-off at about 14.2 V. It earns its place only if lead-acid batteries share the garage.
