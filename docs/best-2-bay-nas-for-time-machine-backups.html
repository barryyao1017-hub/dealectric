What the differences mean

Running cost. Each figure assumes 8 hours of drive access and 16 hours with drives asleep per day, at the EIA's August 2026 US residential average of 18.16¢/kWh. Formula: (access W × 8 + asleep W × 16) × 365 ÷ 1000.

  • DH2300: (13.72×8 + 3.30×16) × 365 ÷ 1000 = 59.3 kWh a year, about $10.78.
  • DS223j: (16.31×8 + 4×16) × 365 ÷ 1000 = 71.0 kWh, about $12.89.
  • DXP2800: (16.38×8 + 5.24×16) × 365 ÷ 1000 = 78.4 kWh, about $14.24.
  • DS225+: (16.98×8 + 6.08×16) × 365 ÷ 1000 = 85.1 kWh, about $15.45.
  • AS5402T: (22.9×8 + 12.7×16) × 365 ÷ 1000 = 141.0 kWh, about $25.61.

Each maker tests with its own drives, so treat gaps of a few dollars a year as ties. The one real gap is the AS5402T: its 12.7 W with drives asleep is about twice the next-highest.

First full backup. Blackvoid timed a Mac Studio writing to the DS225+ over its 2.5GbE port at 263 MB/s. That test used one large file on two drives in RAID 0. For a 1 TB Mac that works out to 1,000,000 MB ÷ 263 MB/s ≈ 3,800 s, or about 63 minutes. A 1GbE port tops out near 125 MB/s, so the same backup takes at least 1,000,000 ÷ 125 = 8,000 s, about 2.2 hours. Time Machine writes many small files, and Blackvoid's mixed-file job on the same box averaged about 108 MB/s. After the first run, hourly backups are small enough that link speed matters little, and a Mac on Wi-Fi rarely fills even 1GbE.

Sizing the quotas. QNAP advises a Time Machine quota of at least twice the Mac's storage. For a 1 TB Mac and a 512 GB Mac that means 2 TB + 1 TB = 3 TB of quotas. Two 4 TB drives in RAID 1 give 4 TB usable, which leaves room for both.

Why each Mac needs its own cap, and where each brand puts it

Apple's Mac User Guide says that as the backup disk fills, Time Machine deletes older backups to make room for new ones. It doesn't stop at a fair share. Over the network, the backup disk is whatever size the NAS share reports. The Samba manual describes its Time Machine size option as limiting the reported disk size, "thus preventing Time Machine from using the whole real disk". With two Macs on one uncapped share, the Mac that grows faster takes the other Mac's space. Apple supports a NAS only over SMB: AFP isn't supported in macOS 27 or later. So each brand's SMB cap is the setting that matters.

  • Synology DSM. DSM 7.3 lists shared-folder quotas among the features that need a Btrfs volume, and caps quotas on an ext4 volume at 4 TB. Format the pool as Btrfs and give each Mac its own Time Machine share with a quota. Don't use an admin account for backups: users in the administrators group have unlimited quota. On the DS223j, Btrfs is available from DSM 7.2-64570.
  • UGREEN UGOS. The quota belongs to the shared folder. UGREEN's own guide says that if several Macs share one Time Machine folder, you have to plan the quota around their combined size, and one fast-growing Mac leaves less room for the others. Make one folder per Mac. UGREEN requires UGOS 1.9.0.0035 or later and macOS Monterey 12 or later.
  • Asustor ADM. The SMB settings page has a Time Machine usage limit, introduced in ADM 4.3: "Each Apple device will not store more than the value set." The same help page says Time Machine settings are synchronized with AFP settings. Keep that in mind now that macOS 27 drops AFP for Time Machine.
  • QNAP QTS. QNAP's guide sets Highest SMB version to SMB 3 and lets you give each Mac its own optional Time Machine user with a quota, recommended at at least twice the Mac's storage. With the shared HBS 3 account, a backup larger than the quota fails.
  • Ubiquiti UniFi Drive. You turn on Time Machine under Settings > Services > File Services, and turning off SMB also turns it off. Ubiquiti's help article doesn't mention a quota or size limit.

The DS225+ wins because it runs the same Synology quota setup as the cheaper models, adds a 2.5GbE port for the first full backup, and comes with a 3-year warranty that Synology's Extended Warranty Plus extends to 5. Synology's product page ties full functionality to drives on its compatibility list, so check that list before you buy drives.

Not recommended

Ubiquiti UniFi UNAS 2 for a couple of Macs. At $199.00 on Ubiquiti's US store ($219.00 with the surcharge the store adds, 2026-11-17), it is a low-cost 2-bay box with 2.5GbE. But its Time Machine help article documents no per-user or per-share quota. With nothing to cap them, two Macs back up into one shared pool until it is full, which is the problem this page is about. The store page also doesn't state the CPU, memory or standard warranty.
