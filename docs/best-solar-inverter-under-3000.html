Battery voltage decides what you can pair

A hybrid inverter's battery port accepts one voltage family. The figure that sets your total cost is that range, not the inverter price. There are three families on this list:

  • 48 V (51.2 V LiFePO4): SPH6548P, EG4 6000XP and both Sol-Ark 5K versions. These units take common server-rack packs. Sun Gold Power lists its SG48100P (51.2 V, 100 Ah, UL 1973 / UL 9540A) as compatible with SunGoldPower, Deye, Growatt, Victron and other inverters over CAN/RS485. The two-pack costs $1,953 at the maker $1,953: https://dealectric.com/go/sungoldpower-sg48100p-2-pack.
  • High voltage (120–500 V): Solis S6-EH1P5K-H-US. A 48 V pack runs at 51.2 V nominal, which is 51.2 ÷ 120 = 43% of the Solis minimum, so it will not work at all. Solis's US support site also says that its high-voltage hybrids do not support open-loop batteries and that an unverified battery can void the inverter warranty. You need a closed-loop HV stack from Solis's matching list. In return, the Solis is the only inverter here that lists UL 1741 SB and California Rule 21 for grid export, and it makes native 120/240 V backup power.
  • 24 V: Rich Solar NOVA MAX 3K. It suits RV and cabin banks, gives 120 V from one unit, and needs paralleled units for split phase.

There are two Sol-Ark 5K listings at the same store. The $2,998 split-phase unit is, in RENVU's words, 'not CEC listed (CA) nor HECO compliant (HI). Offgrid only in these markets.' The $1,486 single-phase 5K is 'Perfect for 120V only setups', so it won't run a 240 V well pump or dryer. The SPH6548P's UL 1741 listing is worded for off-grid systems. If your utility must approve grid export, the Solis is the one on this list that lists Rule 21 and IEEE 1547.

Not recommended

SMA Sunny Boy 5.0 (SB5.0-1SP-US-41). It is a grid-tie string inverter with no battery input, and Solar-Electric.com marks it 'discontinued' and 'at End of life'. It cannot store solar for night use or run a battery-backed system. If you want backup, pick one of the hybrids above.
