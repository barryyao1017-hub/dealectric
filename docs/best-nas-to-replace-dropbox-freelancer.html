What a freelancer actually needs, brand by brand

  • Large-file links for clients: UGREEN's Files app makes links with a validity period, password, access limit and download toggle; Synology the same, including from its phone app. Upload speed to the client is your home connection's upload rate, or UGREEN's relay if you leave DDNS off.
  • Version history: Synology Drive keeps up to 32 versions per file with Intelliversioning; UGREEN keeps versions on the NAS per shared folder (limits not published) and conflict copies in the client (the newer file wins, the older is renamed), plus Btrfs snapshots for ransomware rollback.
  • A laptop with a small SSD: Dropbox's online-only placeholders exist in Synology Drive and Nextcloud's client; UGREEN syncs whole folder pairs, so sync only the active projects.
  • Phone: UGREEN's app browses and uploads, backs up the camera roll in the background; Synology Drive adds offline files. Both use a relay with no router setup; both relays are slower than a direct connection.
  • Moving off Dropbox: UGREEN's Cloud Drives app mounts Dropbox, Google Drive and OneDrive for the copy; afterwards the NAS is the only copy unless you add a backup (Sync & Backup to another NAS or a USB drive, or a cloud bucket).

The money

Dropbox's own plans page showed Plus at $9.99 a month (2 TB, 30-day history) on 2026-11-03, $11.99 billed monthly per third parties, and 3 TB tiers at about $15–20 a month; that is $120–240 a year. The DXP2800 at $389.99 plus two 4 TB drives (about $200–260) costs roughly $600–650 once and runs on about 16 W, so it pays back in three to five years against Plus, sooner against a 3 TB plan, and holds 4 TB in RAID 1 with room to grow. The honest caveat is time: UGOS's client is less polished than Dropbox's and Synology's, and reviewers describe the setup as more hands-on.
