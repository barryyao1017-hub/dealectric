What the differences mean

  • The 2-bay Synology is rated for a fifth of the files: 500,000 ÷ 100,000 = 5. The DS225+ and DS425+ have the same Intel Celeron J4125 and the same 2 GB of RAM (6 GB max). The difference is the Drive rating Synology publishes for each model, and Synology does not say why.
  • The ratings assume more RAM than the box ships with: Synology tested with the maximum memory installed, which is 6 GB, while the DS225+ ships with 2 GB. 6 − 2 = 4 GB, which is Synology's D4NS01-4G module, to reach the tested setup.
  • Versions are not days: Dropbox Plus keeps 30 days of history. Synology keeps up to 32 versions per file, rotated over as long as 120 days (120 ÷ 30 = 4× the Dropbox window). On a file saved every 15 minutes, though, 32 versions cover 32 × 15 = 480 minutes, or 8 hours, unless Intelliversioning keeps the important ones.
  • Running cost: DS225+ at 16.98 W × 8,760 h = 148.7 kWh × 18.16¢ (US average, August 2026) = about $27 a year. The DS425+ comes to 28.25 W → about $45 a year. Dropbox Plus billed yearly at $9.99 a month is $119.88 a year ($11.99 a month if billed monthly).

How many files your Dropbox holds decides the Synology model

The Synology Drive spec sheet has two different limits. Each Drive Client syncs up to 500,000 files, and it says the recommended number of connections 'varies by Synology model'. The per-model number is on each NAS's own spec page, under Synology Drive: 100,000 hosted files and 10 users for the DS225+, 500,000 and 20 for the DS425+, and 500,000 and 80 for the DS925+. The file count covers files indexed or hosted by Synology Drive. Files you reach over SMB fall under the separate File Services limits.

Synology's footnotes explain the test. It used the maximum supported memory, Btrfs, non-encrypted shared folders, and 20% of users active with 80% idle. Synology says going over the rating 'will not block application operations, but may result in longer response time.' A DS225+ with 150,000 files will still sync, just more slowly. Count the files in your Dropbox folder before you buy. Under 100,000, the DS225+ is enough. Over it, the DS425+ is rated for 500,000 files. It also has two M.2 NVMe slots, but storage pools there need Synology-verified SSDs.

Version history works differently on each brand. Synology keeps 32 versions per file and can rotate them over 7 to 120 days. QNAP Qsync allows up to 64, with a default of 32 set on up to five subfolders. UGREEN keeps 8 by default and allows up to 32, so raise it after setup. UGREEN's two-way sync also puts deleted files in a #SyncVersion recycle bin. Synology and QNAP both offer on-demand files the way Dropbox does: Synology On-demand Sync and QNAP Space-Saving Mode. UGREEN's sync guide doesn't describe an on-demand mode. On 2025 Synology Plus models, DSM 7.3 lets you build the hard-drive pool from third-party drives. For drives, use two NAS-rated HDDs in a mirror, and keep an offsite backup, because a mirror doesn't protect against deleted files or ransomware.
