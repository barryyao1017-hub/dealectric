Only two kinds of magnetic charger go past 15 W on an iPhone: chargers certified for Qi2 25W (sold as "Qi2.2") and Apple's current MagSafe Charger, which Apple says is itself certified for Qi2 25W. Even then the phone sets the ceiling. Apple's figures: 25 W on the iPhone 17, 17 Pro, 17 Pro Max, 18 Pro, 18 Pro Max, iPhone Duo, 16 Plus and 16 Pro Max; about 22 W on the iPhone 16 and 16 Pro; 20 W on the iPhone Air; 15 W on the iPhone 17e and on every iPhone from the 12 to the 15 (12 W on the 12 mini and 13 mini). An original Qi2 or older MagSafe charger stops at 15 W on every iPhone, and a plain, non-magnetic Qi pad at 7.5 W. The iPhone 16e has no magnets and charges as a plain Qi phone. What else limits the watts

  • The adapter. Apple's MagSafe Charger needs a 30 W adapter or higher (15 V / 2 A) for 25 W and 20 W for 15 W; with a 12 W adapter it still works, slowly. The WPC says a 25 W station needs a 30–40 W adapter or larger, and that on an old 20 W brick it charges below 25 W without saying so. Anker asks for 65 W on its Prime station.
  • Heat. The WPC says active cooling makes a measurable difference at 25 W under sustained load and far less at 15 W. Apple says that if the battery gets too warm, software might limit charging above 80 percent.
  • Anything plugged into the phone. With USB-C or Lightning accessories connected, Apple caps wireless charging at 7.5 W on the iPhone 16 models and the iPhone 15 and earlier, 10 W on the 17 Pro and 17 Pro Max, and 16 W on the iPhone 17, Air, 18 Pro and 18 Pro Max.
  • Order of plugging in. Apple: plug the MagSafe Charger into power before placing the iPhone on it; otherwise take the phone off, wait three seconds and put it back to get full power.
  • Cases. Case limits differ by maker: ESR supports magnetic cases under 2 mm, Anker says cases over 2.5 mm may reduce efficiency, UGREEN charges through cases up to 3 mm.
  • Checking a "Qi2.2" claim. Search the maker's part number in the WPC's certified-product database and read three fields: Version 2.2.1, Power Profile MPP25, Potential Load Power 25.0. Version 2.0.0, MPP and 15.0 is the 15 W tier.
