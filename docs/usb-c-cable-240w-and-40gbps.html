The rules behind the ratings

  • 240 W is USB PD 3.1 Extended Power Range: 48 V at 5 A. The cable must carry an e-marker chip that declares 50 V 5 A; without it, a charger negotiates 100 W (20 V 5 A) at most. The laptop and the charger must both support EPR too; most laptops take 100 W or 140 W.
  • 40Gbps passive USB4 and Thunderbolt 4 cables are 1 m or shorter (vendors quote 40Gbps at 1 m, 20Gbps at 2 m); longer runs need active cables, which cost $60–80 and are not all rated for 240 W.
  • Thunderbolt 5 uses passive cables up to 1 m for 80Gbps (120 Gb/s one way for displays), per Intel's 2023 announcement, and is backward compatible with Thunderbolt 4, 3 and USB4 at their own speeds. A Thunderbolt 5 cable is therefore the one cable that covers every USB-C need below it.
  • Certified versus rated. Intel certifies Thunderbolt cables and USB-IF certifies USB4 cables; both keep public product lists. A "rated" cable may work the same, but only a certified one has been tested against the spec by a third party.
