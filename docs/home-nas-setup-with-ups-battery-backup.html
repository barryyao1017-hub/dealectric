What the differences mean

Each kit's UPS shuts the NAS down when the battery reaches 15%, so 85% of its rated energy is available before shutdown. The runtimes below divide that energy by the NAS's measured draw. They ignore conversion losses, so real runtimes will be shorter. UGREEN's UPS guide lists about 10 minutes of runtime for the US3000 but doesn't say at what load.

  • Usable battery: US3000 43.2 Wh × 0.85 = 36.7 Wh; US2000 22.2 Wh × 0.85 = 18.9 Wh.
  • DXP4800 Plus on the US3000: 36.7 Wh ÷ 49 W = 0.75 h ≈ 45 min; 36.7 Wh ÷ 32 W = 1.15 h ≈ 69 min. Headroom: 120 W − 64 W = 56 W. Mighty Gadget thinks the 64 W peak came while two M.2 drives switched to read/write SSD cache.
  • DXP2800 on the US2000: 18.9 Wh ÷ 34.7 W = 0.54 h ≈ 33 min at ocinside.de's stress-test maximum. Headroom: 72 W − 34.7 W = 37.3 W.
  • DH4300 Plus on the US3000: IT-Connect measured 79 min with 4 HDDs. That implies an average draw of 36.7 Wh ÷ 1.32 h ≈ 28 W.
  • DH2300 on the US2000: 18.9 Wh ÷ 17 W = 1.11 h ≈ 67 min; 18.9 Wh ÷ 11 W = 1.72 h ≈ 103 min. Headroom: 72 W − 17 W = 55 W.

Sizing the UPS to the NAS

UGREEN's NAS UPS units are DC units that run off the NAS's own power brick; UGREEN says the US3000 doesn't include a power adapter of its own. The NAS's own power brick plugs into the UPS's DC IN, and the UPS's DC OUT feeds the NAS. A USB data cable from the UPS to a rear USB port on the NAS lets UGOS detect the outage. Without that cable the NAS runs on battery until the battery is empty and never gets a shutdown signal. In UGOS, go to Control Panel → Hardware & Power → UPS and choose when to shut down: right away, after a set wait, or below 15% battery. IT-Connect found that UGOS detects the UPS on its own, with no driver to install. UGREEN warns that the unit may arrive in Energy-Saving Mode; switch it to Normal Mode before you rely on it.

Match the UPS to the NAS's measured draw, not to its power brick. The DXP4800 Plus ships with a 150 W brick, but Mighty Gadget measured 32–49 W in daily use and 64 W at peak, well under the US3000's 120 W cap. The 72 W US2000 would leave only 8 W above that peak, and UGREEN's guide doesn't list it for the DXP4800 Plus. The US2000 is listed for the DH2300, DH4300 Plus, DXP2800 and DXP2800 GT. UGREEN's compatibility page menu, however, says the US2000 supports only the DH series, so check the product page before buying it for a DXP2800.

Each UPS has one DC output, and it feeds the NAS only. Your router and switch go dark in an outage, so remote access drops even while the NAS keeps running. Buy hard drives separately, because UGREEN's NAS store doesn't sell them. UGREEN advises against SMR drives and against mixing old and new drives in one RAID array, so start with two matching NAS-rated CMR drives in a mirror.

Not recommended

Don't pair the US3000 with a DXP6800 Pro or DXP8800 Plus. UGREEN states that the US3000 is not compatible with the DXP6800 or DXP8800 series, and IT-Connect describes those models as more power-hungry. Also don't count on automatic shutdown if you replace UGOS with TrueNAS SCALE. In a TrueNAS Forums thread, users on TrueNAS 25.10.4 report that its bundled NUT 2.8.0 does not detect the US3000. The workaround they describe is running a newer NUT in a Docker container. Stay on UGOS if you want the clean shutdown to work out of the box.
