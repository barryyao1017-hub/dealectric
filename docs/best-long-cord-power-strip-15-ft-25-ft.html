What the differences mean

  • Amps decide what a strip can run. 1,875 W ÷ 125 V = 15 A, the full capacity of an ordinary household circuit. 1,250 W ÷ 125 V = 10 A, which is 5 A less, or a third (5 ÷ 15 = 33%).
  • 1,800 W and 1,875 W labels are the same strip size. Tripp Lite's 1,800 W is 15 A × 120 V, and CRST's, GoGreen's and TESSAN 1026's 1,875 W is 15 A × 125 V. Both are 15 A.
  • A 25 ft 14 AWG cord loses little. The PS725B's cord has 50 ft of copper in the loop. 15.24 m × 8.2 Ω/km = 0.125 Ω, and 0.125 Ω × 15 A = 1.9 V, or 1.6% of 120 V, with 15² × 0.125 = 28 W of heat in the cord at full load.
  • A 15 ft 14 AWG cord loses less. 9.14 m × 8.2 Ω/km = 0.075 Ω, and × 15 A = 1.1 V (0.9%), with about 17 W of heat at full load.
  • A thinner 16 AWG cord at 25 ft would lose 15.24 m × 13 Ω/km × 15 A = 3.0 V (2.5%) and give off about 45 W. That is about 60% more than 14 AWG at the same length and current.
  • Surge rating: 2,800 J ÷ 900 J = 3.1× more surge energy absorbed by the CRST 10-Outlet than by the TESSAN 1026.

Read the rating label before the cord length

The cord length tells you how far a strip reaches. The rating tells you how much it can carry when it gets there. TESSAN's own range shows how far these differ within one brand. The 1026 is rated 1,875 W across 8 outlets with cords from 5 to 25 ft. The PS07 (5, 10 or 15 ft) and the 10 ft PS09 are rated 1,250 W, about 10 A. Those two have the higher joule figures (2,100 J and 1,050 J against 900 J), so joules and watts have to be read separately. CRST rates its 15 ft metal strips at 15 A / 125 V / 1,875 W, and its 12-outlet model names its cord: 14 AWG SJT.

Gauge is the second figure to check. Markertek and Target list the PS725B's 25 ft cord as 14 gauge, and Markertek gives the type as SJT. TechGearLab (Chris McNamara with Ian Nicholson, updated 2023-12-19) tested 10 strips, including the 15 ft CRST 10-Outlet Heavy Duty. It reported that every cord was at least 14 AWG and that all of them held around 120 V with several higher-draw devices plugged in. The CRST got the top cord-length score in that test, 9.0 out of 10.

Buy the length you need rather than extending a shorter strip. Tripp Lite's Power It! manual says not to use the strip with extension cords or adapters that eliminate the connection to ground, and not to connect it to an ungrounded outlet. It calls for a standard 3-wire grounded 120 V outlet, indoors only.

Not recommended

Tripp Lite PS725B as a surge protector. Its 25 ft 14 AWG cord and 15 A breaker are solid. But Target's spec sheet lists only the overload breaker and no joule rating, so it is a plain power strip. If the strip feeds a TV, computer or router, pick one with a joule rating, such as the CRST strips above, or the TESSAN 1026 if you need 25 ft.
