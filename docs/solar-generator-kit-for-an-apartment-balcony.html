What the differences mean

Usable energy is what actually reaches your plugs after inverter losses. Refill hours divide that energy by the watts the kit's panel actually produced in clear sun, not by its label.

  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Classic + 220 W: 870 Wh ÷ 193 W = 4.5 h of full sun. 669 ÷ 870 = $0.77 per usable Wh at EcoFlow's $669.
  • BLUETTI AC180 + 200 W: 950 Wh ÷ 150 W = 6.3 h. 769 ÷ 950 = $0.81 per usable Wh at BLUETTI's $769 (2026-11-14). It stores 80 Wh more than the DELTA 3 (950 − 870) but needs 1.8 h more sun to refill (6.3 − 4.5), because its panel measured weakest.
  • Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 + SolarSaga 200: 910 Wh ÷ 171 W = 5.3 h.
  • BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 + 100 W: 231 Wh ÷ 100 W = 2.3 h at the panel's rating. 489.99 ÷ 231 = $2.12 per usable Wh, so you pay for a small battery that refills quickly, not for stored energy.
  • Panel length: the three 200 W-class panels unfold to 82.5–89.8 in long (OutdoorGearLab), about 7 ft. Measure your balcony rail or floor before you buy.

Measured, not rated: what the batteries and panels delivered

OutdoorGearLab's Sam Schild connected each panel to an identical Jackery 1000 for two hours, logged input every 30 minutes, and ran the test on a clear 55–65°F day at 6,300 ft. The 220 W EcoFlow delivered 193 W (88%), the Jackery SolarSaga 200 delivered 171 W (86%) and the BLUETTI 200 W portable delivered 150 W (75%). In a separate full-discharge test, Kenji Mowrey got 870 Wh from the DELTA 3, 910 Wh from the Explorer 1000 v2, 950 Wh from the AC180 and 230 Wh from the SOLIX C300, each 80–85% of its rating. The DELTA 3 he tested shares the DELTA 3 Classic's 1,024 Wh rating, but it isn't the Classic itself.

All of these numbers come from panels laid flat in open ground. A panel hung on a railing, standing upright or partly shaded will produce less, so treat the refill hours as a best case.

The DELTA 3 Classic can't take an extra battery: EcoFlow's US spec page lists 'Expandable Battery: NO'. That makes its 1,024 Wh rating the most it will ever hold.

For the Elite 30 V2, The Technology Man measured about 19 W of idle drain with AC on and Eco mode off (100% to 19% in 12 h with no load). Over a night, that's 19 × 12 = 228 Wh, almost its whole 231 Wh. With only DC on, idle drain was about 11 W. Run an overnight router from the DC ports or in Eco mode. BLUETTI's 1,500 W 'lifting' figure covers only purely resistive devices such as kettles and heaters, not motor start-up.

Not recommended

The Anker SOLIX C300 as a balcony kit. Anker's US store sells it as a station only for $429.99 (2026-11-14). The BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 + 100 W costs $489.99, so $60 more gets you the 100 W panel and twice the AC output (600 W against 300 W), and the measured usable energy is nearly the same (231 Wh against 230 Wh). On top of that, the C300 takes solar only at 11–28 V through XT60, its solar cable is sold separately, and Anker says its PS30 USB-C panels don't work with it.
