What the differences mean

  • USB-A power for the Duex Max DS: a USB 3 Type-A port must supply at least 0.9 A at 5 V, and 0.9 × 5 = 4.5 W. That equals Mobile Pixels' 4.5 W figure for the Max DS, so it runs with no spare power. A USB 2 port supplies 0.5 × 5 = 2.5 W, which is not enough, so use a USB 3 port (ChromiumOS EC documentation).
  • Laptop power for the Duex Float 2 Pro: Macworld's 7.5 W Limited Power Mode works out to 1.5 A × 5 V, the typical USB-C output the ChromiumOS document gives. Full brightness needs 15 W = 3 A × 5 V, and Microsoft sets that as the minimum only for USB 40Gbps ports on certified PCs.
  • Brightness on laptop power: 350 nits × 80% = about 280 nits on the Float 2 Pro, if output follows the brightness setting. The Max DS is rated at 300 nits.
  • Pixels and weight: 2560 × 1600 = 4,096,000 pixels against 1920 × 1080 = 2,073,600, so the Float 2 Pro has 4,096,000 ÷ 2,073,600 = 1.98× the pixels. It also weighs 2.9 lb − 1.8 lb = 1.1 lb more (both weights from Legit Product Reviews).

Will it run on your laptop? The video path and the power budget

Mobile Pixels' installation guide for the Duex Max DS lists three ways to connect it, in this order: the USB-C to C cable alone; that cable plus HDMI; and the USB-C cable on its USB-A adapter plus HDMI. None of the three needs a driver, and none sends video over USB-A. In the third method HDMI carries the picture and the USB-A port only powers the screen. A laptop with neither a USB-C port that carries video nor an HDMI port can't run it.

First check whether your USB-C port carries video. Microsoft's Windows hardware requirements, released alongside Windows 11 version 24H2, require DisplayPort Alt Mode on every USB-C port of a compliant laptop, and every USB 40Gbps port must supply at least 15 W. Microsoft also says makers are on track to reach compliance across most systems in the coming years, so older laptops can still have USB-C ports that carry data only. On a Mac, one added screen stays within the one-external-display limit of base M1 and M2 MacBooks (Legit Product Reviews).

On one cable, the Max DS takes its power from the same port. Mobile Pixels says USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode carries video, data and up to 15 W of power on one cable, and that both Duex models support it. The Float 2 Pro needs more power: on laptop power alone, Macworld found it drops to a 7.5 W Limited Power Mode with brightness capped at 80%. Legit Product Reviews, citing Macworld, says full brightness needs a USB-C charger of 15 W or more.

To stand a Duex DS on the desk instead of on the lid, Mobile Pixels' store lists the Levstand ($15.99 on 2026-11-21) as working with all Duex series monitors. Its Kickstand lists only the DUEX Pro, TRIO and TRIO Max.
