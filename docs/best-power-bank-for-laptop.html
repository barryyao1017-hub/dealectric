What the differences mean

  • On older 16-inch Pros, the cable sets the limit, not the bank. ChargerLab tested a 16-inch M1 Max MacBook Pro on a 140 W power bank. It drew up to 130 W through Apple's USB-C to MagSafe 3 cable and 90 W through a USB-C to USB-C cable. 90 ÷ 130 = 0.69, so the USB-C cable cost it about 31% of its charging power.
  • 90 Wh is less than one 16-inch battery. Techtest measured 74.3–80.4 Wh coming out of a 2023 UGREEN 145W unit. A 16-inch MacBook Pro battery is 99.6 Wh, so 74.3 ÷ 99.6 = 75% and 80.4 ÷ 99.6 = 81% of a full charge from empty, before counting the laptop's own draw. The unit Techtest tested had a 100 W top port; the current version adds 140 W on USB-C1 and keeps the same 25,000 mAh / 90 Wh rating.
  • The Anker Prime mostly buys a second 140 W port, not much more energy. Techtest measured 79.6–89.8 Wh from it. That is 79.6 − 74.3 = 5.3 Wh to 89.8 − 80.4 = 9.4 Wh more than the UGREEN result, for 229.99 − 79.99 = $150 more.
  • Refill time. The UGREEN recharges at up to 65 W, so 90 Wh ÷ 65 W ≈ 1.4 h at best; Techtest timed about 2 h. EcoFlow's RAPID 25K takes 140 W, which is 140 ÷ 65 = 2.2× the input rate, for 85.99 − 79.99 = $6 more.
  • A 14-inch Pro or an Air doesn't need 140 W. For fast charging, Apple lists a 96 W adapter for the 14-inch Pro and 70 W (67 W on the 13.6-inch) for the Air. Since 100 W ≥ 96 W, a 100 W port such as the Anker A1695's already covers them.

Why the cable decides whether a 16-inch MacBook Pro gets 140 W

The bank has to offer the 28 V⎓5 A step that gives 140 W. UGREEN lists 28V-5A on USB-C1 of the 145W, and Anker lists 28 V⎓5 A on the Prime 26K. The laptop also has to accept 140 W on the input you plug into. Apple's fast-charge list gives the 16-inch MacBook Pro (2021 or later) 140 W through the USB-C to MagSafe 3 Cable. It lists the 240W USB-C Charge Cable only for the 16-inch from Nov 2023 or later. MacRumors reports that the earlier M-series 16-inch models take at most 100 W on their USB-C ports, which matches ChargerLab's 90 W reading.

For most owners this costs nothing. Apple's tech specs list a 2 m USB-C to MagSafe 3 cable in the box of the 16-inch MacBook Pro (2021); plug that into the bank's 140 W port. A bank whose ports and built-in cables stop at 100 W, like the Anker A1695 (20 V⎓5 A on every cable and port, per Anker), can't reach 140 W on any 16-inch with any cable. To charge a Nov 2023 or later 16-inch over USB-C, use a 240 W USB-C cable. Not recommended

Goal Zero Sherpa 100AC, for USB-C laptops. Its USB-C ports give 60 W each (Goal Zero). That is below the 70 W Apple lists for MacBook Air fast charging and the 96 W it lists for the 14-inch Pro. At Goal Zero's own store it costs about three times as much as the UGREEN. It only makes sense for a laptop that charges from an AC brick, using its 100 W AC outlet.
