Why Wi-Fi alone doesn't get Netflix onto a mini projector

Wi-Fi on a mini projector does two jobs: it runs streaming apps on the projector, and it receives a cast or mirror from your phone. Netflix blocks the second route. Its help center lists the only devices a phone can cast Netflix to: Chromecast 3rd gen or older, Google Nest Hub, and select cast-enabled Vizio and Compal TVs. On anything else, Netflix says you won't be able to cast from your phone or tablet. It also limits casting and mirroring to Standard and Premium plans, so the ad-supported plan can't cast or mirror at all.

Makers' own support pages say the same. Nebula's Capsule 3 FAQ says AirPlay comes through its Nebula Cast app and that DRM-protected content can't be mirrored or cast over AirPlay. It says DRM video plays only over an HDMI cable. The only wireless route that always works is a Netflix app licensed for the projector itself, which the MoGo 4, Capsule 3, MoGo 2 Pro (New), EAZZE D1 and BOOM mini all have. The original 2023 MoGo 2 Pro shows why the label matters: SlashGear wrote that Netflix wouldn't certify any XGIMI projector at the time. XGIMI's listing for the (New) model now names Netflix through Google TV, so check you're buying the (New) version.

Bluetooth handles sound, not picture. Expert Reviews notes the MoGo 4 can double as a Bluetooth speaker. On the Capsule 3, Nebula says the built-in speaker turns off when a Bluetooth speaker or headphones connect, and it has a Bluetooth Speaker Mode in Settings.

Not recommended for this question

Aurzen ZIP Tri-Fold. It is small and has a 5,000 mAh battery, but Aurzen's help center says it has no Google TV or built-in streaming apps. It also says Netflix, Hulu and Disney+ can't be mirrored or wirelessly cast to it. Its mirroring doesn't use Wi-Fi at all, and Aurzen sends Netflix viewers to a separate CastPlay dongle. Its battery is rated at up to 1.5 hours. If you want Wi-Fi streaming, any row in the table above is a better buy.
