What the differences mean

On battery alone, at night, the three large units are the same size. The FlexBOSS21 reaches 16 kW and the Sol-Ark 15K reaches 15 kW only while solar is producing. From the battery, each of them gives 12 kW, the same as the 12000XP.

  • EG4 FlexBOSS21: $3,599.99 ÷ 12 kW on battery = $300 per kW.
  • Sol-Ark 15K-2P-N: $5,750 ÷ 12 kW = $479 per kW. The $2,150 extra ($5,750 − $3,599.99) buys 200 A grid pass-through (the FlexBOSS21 has 90 A) and a published 24,000 VA surge for 10 s.
  • EG4 12000XP: $2,099.99 ÷ 12 kW = $175 per kW. It has no grid sellback, and Current Connected lists it as out of stock.
  • EG4 6000XP: $1,499.99 ÷ 6 kW = $250 per kW. Each 120 V leg tops out at 3,000 W (Current Connected), so a panel heavy on 120 V loads can overload one leg well below 6 kW.
  • Sun Gold Power SPH6548P: $1,035 ÷ 6.5 kW = $159 per kW, which is $465 less than the 6000XP ($1,499.99 − $1,035).
  • Standing loss: the Sol-Ark 15K draws 90 W idle × 24 h = 2.16 kWh a day. The 6000XP draws about 50 W on battery × 24 h = 1.2 kWh a day (both figures from Current Connected).

Which ones can grow: GridBOSS, parallel stacks and battery lists

EG4's GridBOSS is a whole-home interconnect. It accepts up to three hybrid inverters at up to 90 A each and adds four smart-load or AC-coupling ports (125, 80, 60 and 60 A). It also has a generator input of up to 125 A (30 kW) and is service-entrance rated to 200 A. EG4 lists it as pairing with the FlexBOSS21, FlexBOSS18 and 18kPV. On EG4's forum on 2024-11-18, admin Jared wrote that "our hybrid inverters are designed to connect to the GridBoss Hybrid Ports using the AC IN port" and said EG4 would announce any future compatibility. A user asked again in January 2026 about the 6000XP and 12000XP, and no staff reply had appeared on the page as of 2026-11-13. In practice, the 12000XP and 6000XP expand only by stacking more of the same unit, up to 16 of each. The FlexBOSS21 can stack and can also take the GridBOSS.

The battery rules differ as well. In October 2025, EG4 Online Support said a battery missing from the 6000XP's compatibility list, "which is the same for the 12000XP", "will still function" with the inverter. In May 2025 the same support account had said that troubleshooting support is typically not provided for batteries that aren't on the list. Current Connected says the 6000XP runs any 48 V lithium bank or lead-acid in open-loop mode, set by voltage. Current Connected lists the Sol-Ark 15K as working with "any 48V lithium battery that communicates over CAN bus or RS485 MODBUS", and Briggs & Stratton's catalog lists lithium and lead-acid. Sun Gold Power lists AGM, gel, flooded and lithium batteries for the SPH6548P, plus a user mode for other types. It can also run battery-free and up to six units in parallel for 39 kW. That makes it the cheapest way into an off-grid all-in-one if you already own a lead-acid bank: $1,035: https://dealectric.com/go/sungoldpower-sph6548p-6-5kw-48v-hybrid-inverter. Its listing certifies it as UL 1741 by ETL for off-grid use, so it cannot sell power back to the grid. Size the array against its 550 V open-circuit limit before you buy.

Not recommended for this question

The Sun Gold Power 6000W 48V Split Phase Pure Sine Wave Inverter With Charger is not an all-in-one solar unit. Its listing describes it as "a combination of an inverter, battery charger and AC auto-transfer switch" and names no solar charge controller, so it needs a separate MPPT. The Victron MultiPlus-II 48/5000 120V also has no built-in solar input, and it outputs 120 V only. WildJoule notes that 240 V loads need two units, plus a separate MPPT.
