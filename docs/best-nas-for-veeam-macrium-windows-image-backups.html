What the differences mean

Energy for this job assumes one hour of access a night and the drives asleep for the other 23 hours. The figures are each maker's access and hibernation/standby watts:

  • Synology DS225+: 16.98 W × 1 h + 6.08 W × 23 h = 156.8 Wh a day, or 57.2 kWh a year.
  • Synology DS425+: 28.25 × 1 + 6.10 × 23 = 168.6 Wh a day, or 61.5 kWh a year.
  • UGREEN DXP2800 (spec list in The Gadgeteer's review): 16.38 × 1 + 5.24 × 23 = 136.9 Wh a day, or 50.0 kWh a year.
  • Asustor AS5402T: 22.9 × 1 + 12.7 × 23 = 315.0 Wh a day, or 115.0 kWh a year.
  • QNAP TS-264: 29.086 × 1 + 18.288 × 23 = 449.7 Wh a day, or 164.1 kWh a year. That is 2.9 times the DS225+ (164.1 ÷ 57.2).

Bays: two drives in a mirror give one drive's capacity (2 × 8 TB mirrored = 8 TB). Four bays let you add drives later without replacing the pair.

Immutable or only locked: what a ransomware-hit PC can delete

Veeam Agent and Macrium Reflect write the images to an SMB network share on the NAS. Veeam's guide says the account the job uses needs Full Control of that share. If you enter no credentials, Veeam connects as NT AUTHORITY\SYSTEM, so on a home network you give it a NAS user. That login is saved on each PC, and Full Control lets anything using it delete the image files. What survives is the snapshot of the share underneath, but only if the NAS refuses to delete that snapshot too.

  • Synology DS225+ and DS425+: both product pages list immutable snapshots. iFeeltech's guide (Nandor Katai, updated 2026-10-27) quotes Synology: an immutable snapshot "cannot be removed by any means until its protection period ends", and the shared folder, volume or storage pool that holds it can't be deleted before then either. The guide adds that Synology recommends a 7–14-day period and that the feature needs DSM 7.2 or later.
  • QNAP TS-264: "uses the QTS operating system by default". QNAP documents immutable snapshots for QuTS hero h6.0, whose protection policy "prohibits automatic deletion by the system and/or manual deletion by a user" until an expiry you set. Since QuTS hero h6.0.2.3591, which lists the TS-264, deleting a shared folder that holds unexpired immutable snapshots needs a USB key file. QNAP also says QuTS hero needs 16 GB of RAM for inline deduplication, and the TS-264 has 8 GB.
  • Asustor AS5402T: in ADM's Snapshot Center, a locked snapshot "will not be removed automatically". When all 256 snapshot slots fill with locked ones, "users need to manually remove" them. There is no protection period.
  • TerraMaster F2-425 Plus: TOS 6 lets you lock snapshots "to ensure permanent preservation". Its help page describes no protection period and no immutability.
  • UGREEN DXP2800: the author of the open-source ugos-client library tested the UGOS API against a live NAS on 2026-08-19 (the code doesn't name the model) and wrote that "a locked snapshot is deleted just the same" through the API, calling the lock "a hint the web UI honours". This is that author's test, not a statement from UGREEN.

To make the Synology protection count, give each PC its own NAS user and folder. Run Veeam's daily job (it keeps 7 days of restore points by default), and schedule a daily immutable snapshot of the backup share with a 7–14-day period. On PCs that sleep in Modern Standby, Veeam "does not start scheduled backups", so turn on "Backup once powered on" or keep the PCs awake at backup time.

Not recommended

The QNAP TS-264 as it ships, holding your only copy. On its default QTS, QNAP documents no immutable snapshots; it documents them for QuTS hero. Its 18.288 W disk-standby draw is three times the DS225+'s 6.08 W hibernation figure (18.288 ÷ 6.08 = 3.0). Switched to QuTS hero h6.0.2, it matches Synology's protection, but it still uses 164.1 kWh a year against the DS225+'s 57.2.
