Veeam Agent Free on a NAS share

Create a backup folder and a dedicated NAS user with write access; in the job pick "Shared folder", enter the UNC path and that user's credentials (the same account must exist on the NAS and be typed in the job, the usual cause of "cannot create job" errors). Free runs incrementals after the first full and keeps the number of restore points you set; synthetic fulls, GFS, the CBT driver and backup windows are paid features. Rebuild the recovery media after each agent upgrade or major Windows update. Veeam compresses and can dedup at the source, so the on-disk total is below the raw sizing.

Ransomware and the share

A PC that saves the share's credentials can encrypt or delete the backups it wrote, so the NAS side matters: Synology's Snapshot Replication can lock snapshots for a protection period during which they cannot be deleted; Asustor can lock snapshots; UGOS's Snapshot app takes read-only Btrfs snapshots but publishes nothing about immutability, and a compromised admin login could remove them. On any NAS, use a backup-only user, no admin, and keep a second copy off the NAS. Macrium's Image Guardian hardens local backup volumes on the PC that runs it (shareable to other MIG PCs), not a NAS target.

Sizing and the wire

Fulls: 3 × 300 GB = 900 GB. Incrementals at 5–10 % a day: 15–30 GB per PC, 1.35–2.7 TB over 30 days. Plan 2.3–3.6 TB raw, less after compression or dedup. A 300 GB full takes about 45 minutes at a gigabit wire speed of 110 MB/s and 18–35 minutes at 2.5GbE (drive-limited); a 15–30 GB incremental takes 1–5 minutes on either, which is why the port only matters the first night and on restore day.
