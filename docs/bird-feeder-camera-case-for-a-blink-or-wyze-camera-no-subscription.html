Why a security camera blurs the birds at its own feeder

Blink and Wyze cameras are built to watch a yard, not a perch a few inches away. Wyze says its cameras are mostly used for "security and monitoring, not for close-up observation of wildlife." That is why the Wyze Bird Feeder comes with a Lens Bracket whose diopter "helps the camera focus within a range of 4-8 inches." Blink makes no close-up lens. Its support page says Blink cameras "detect motion best from about 7 to 19 feet." The Outdoor 4, Outdoor (3rd Gen), XT and XT2 detect by body heat (PIR), and Blink warns that objects at the same apparent temperature as the background may not be detected. The Mini and Mini 2 detect changes in the picture instead.

Here is what named testers saw. Both case reviews used a Ring Stick Up Cam, not a Blink or Wyze:

  • Jim Vorel, Paste (Jan 2023): blurry video of birds "only a few inches away", but "very clear, HD images" of things a foot or two away. He suggests putting the camera "a foot away or so from the feeders." The battery lasted close to a week at a hundred or more bird visits a day.
  • Adam Juniper, Digital Camera World (Apr 2023): lists "Security cameras can't focus closely enough" as a con. The birds were "closer than the Ring's nearest focusing distance," and squirrels showed up "a lot more often than birds."
  • TechGearLab, Blink Outdoor 4 (2025): in a walk test, it detected a person at 2, 5 and 10 ft and missed at 15 and 20 ft.

Not recommended

A Blink Outdoor 4 kit whose only hub is a Sync Module Core. Blink says the Core does not support local storage, so a case on that kit saves no clips without a Blink Subscription Plan. Add a Sync Module 2 or XR first. The older Blink XT, XT2 and Indoor (1st Gen) can't use local storage either. Blink gives them 7,200 seconds of cloud storage per Sync Module instead.
