What the differences mean

Cost per Wh is the kit price divided by battery size. Elite 30 V2 + 100 W: $489.99 ÷ 288 Wh = $1.70. Elite 30 V2 + 60 W: $399.99 ÷ 288 = $1.39. Anker C300X DC + 60 W: $349.99 ÷ 288 = $1.22. Jackery Solar Generator 300 v2: $299 ÷ 288 = $1.04. ALLPOWERS P1800 + 200 W: $531 ÷ 1,024 = $0.52. For the extra cost over the Jackery, the Elite 30 V2 + 100 W kit has an inverter twice the size (600 W vs 300 W) and a panel 2.5 times larger (100 W vs 40 W).

Solar refill hours use PowerSized's published method: battery Wh ÷ (panel W × 0.75), where 0.75 allows for real-world panel losses. Elite 30 V2 + 100 W: 288 ÷ 75 = 3.8 h. Elite 30 V2 + 60 W and C300X DC: 288 ÷ 45 = 6.4 h. Jackery 300 v2 with 40 W: 288 ÷ 30 = 9.6 h. P1800 with 200 W: 1,024 ÷ 150 = 6.8 h. Of the 288 Wh kits, only the 100 W kit is likely to refill from empty in a single sunny day.

Measured usable energy. The Technology Man measured 231 Wh from the Elite 30 V2's AC outlets (231 ÷ 288 = 80%) and 244 Wh from DC (85%). techtest.org averaged 272.9 Wh from the C300X DC's DC outputs over five tests (95%). If you only power USB-C and 12 V gear, the DC-only Anker delivers more of its battery.

What the Elite 30 V2's 1,500 W "Power Lifting" actually covers

BLUETTI's US store advertises "600W Rated Power, 1500W Lifting Power" that "handles essential appliances and power surges with ease." Its station page is narrower: "When it comes to pure resistive devices, it can momentarily reach up to 1,500W." The Technology Man (Gidon, December 2025) explains how it works: the mode runs devices rated up to 1,500 W "by lowering the output voltage" and is "only suitable for simple resistive loads, such as hair dryers." CGMagazine's Matt Keith describes it the same way, giving an electric kettle or hair dryer as examples.

Motors are different. The Technology Man overloaded the Elite 30 V2 with a 450 W mains air duster that has no soft start. He measured a startup current of over 10 A, which he puts at roughly 2,300 W at switch-on. BLUETTI's smaller 300 W AC2A ran the same duster without trouble. So for fridges, pumps, fans and power tools, plan to the 600 W continuous figure, not 1,500 W. BLUETTI's US pages give no separate surge rating for this station. Jackery quotes 600 W surge for the Explorer 300 v2, and ALLPOWERS quotes 3,600 W peak for the P1800.

Solar input limits. The Technology Man lists the XT60 solar input as 12–28 V at up to 10 A and 200 W. Wiring panels in series adds their voltages, so two panels in series exceed 28 V unless each is under 14 V open-circuit. To reach the full 200 W, add a second panel in parallel or use a single 200 W panel rated 28 V or less. On a bench supply simulating ideal sun, he reached the full 200 W.

Standby drain. BLUETTI claims 4.5 W standby. The Technology Man left the AC output on with Eco mode off and no load connected, and the battery fell from 100% to 19% in 12 hours. Turn on Eco mode, or switch AC off, when nothing is plugged in.

The obvious upgrade doesn't work either: Jackery lists the SolarSaga 100 Air as "Not compatible with Explorer 300 v2, Explorer 300, 300D, or 240D for recharging."
