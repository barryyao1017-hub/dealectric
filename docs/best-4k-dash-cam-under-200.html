What the differences mean

  • Card space. Mb/s ÷ 8 × 3,600 gives MB per hour. A119M Pro: 62.0 ÷ 8 × 3,600 = 27.9 GB per hour, so 128 ÷ 27.9 = 4.6 hours of 4K before the loop overwrites. S1 Pro Max front camera: 32.0 ÷ 8 × 3,600 = 14.4 GB per hour, 128 ÷ 14.4 = 8.9 hours. A810S front: 29.4 ÷ 8 × 3,600 = 13.2 GB per hour, 9.7 hours; with its 1080p rear camera recording too, 29.4 + 10.1 = 39.5 Mb/s = 17.8 GB per hour, 128 ÷ 17.8 = 7.2 hours. These use the nominal card size; locked event clips shorten them.
  • Largest card. The A119M Pro tops out at 512 GB: 512 ÷ 27.9 = 18.4 hours. The S1 Pro Max takes 1 TB: 1,000 ÷ 14.4 = about 69 hours of front video.
  • One scale. Doubling the two H.265 figures (see below) gives 29.4 × 2 = 58.8 and 32.0 × 2 = 64 Mb/s against the VIOFO's 62 Mb/s of H.264. The spread is (64 − 58.8) ÷ 58.8 = 9%.
  • Budget after the camera. Parking recording needs a hardwire kit on all three. VIOFO's HK6, whose listing names the A119M Pro series, is $25.99 (viofo.com, 2026-11-26); a CPL filter is already in the A119M Pro box (Chuck McCoy). Vantrue's Voltage Display Hardwire Kit, listed “for All Type C Dash Cams”, is $29.99 (vantrue.com, 2026-11-26); its text does not name the S1 Pro Max, so ask Vantrue before ordering. 70mai says the A810S's parking modes need its hardwire kit. The S1 Pro Max 4K + 2.5K is $199.99, under $200 only at Vantrue's sale price (the list price is $299.99), so a card or a kit takes the total past $200.

Why 62 Mb/s against 32 Mb/s is not twice the detail

Bitrates compare only within one codec. DashCamTalk's spec table lists the A119M Pro's compression as H.264, and Chuck McCoy's review on the DashCamTalk forum lists “Video Codec: H.264”. The same database lists the 70mai A810S and the Vantrue S1 Pro Max as H.265. When ITU approved H.265 in January 2013 it said the codec “will need only half the bit rate of its predecessor”, H.264. On that figure the three front streams sit within 9% of each other, and the VIOFO's larger number buys a card that fills about twice as fast: 4.6 hours per 128 GB against 8.9 and 9.7.

Two limits on that reading. ITU's figure is the standard's design target, not a measurement of these cameras, and a dash cam encoding in real time may not reach all of it. DashCamTalk's scores agree by day (9 of 10 for all three) and separate at night (A119M Pro 8, the other two 7), which is why the VIOFO stays the pick rather than a tie.

Firmware moves these numbers. Chuck McCoy lists the A119M Pro's maximum bitrate as 37 Mb/s (267 MB per minute) at launch and 62 Mb/s (445 MB per minute) with firmware V1.2_20260409; on the older firmware it writes less than either H.265 camera's equivalent (37 against 58.8 and 64), so update it. EricSan measured the S1 Pro Max front camera at 23.8 Mb/s, then 31.9 Mb/s after a late-2025 firmware. Power draw is close: Chuck McCoy measured the A119M Pro at 4.0 to 4.2 W (Wi-Fi and screen off, on the 37 Mb/s firmware), and EricSan measured the S1 Pro Max front camera at 4.9 W with GPS.

Not recommended for night plates

The Botslab G980H 4K + 2K ($109.88, front + rear, 64 GB card in the box) meets the price, but it is not in the table. Android Authority's Bogdan Petrovan (review dated 2024-12-24, of a unit with a 1080p rear camera) lists a Sony IMX415 front sensor rather than the IMX678 in the four rows above. He found the 4K front camera “excellent for capturing finer details like license plates” in daylight, but wrote that at highway speeds or in low light “reading license plates can be difficult” and that “The camera lacks HDR or a dedicated night mode”. It suits daytime 4K with a card included, not night plate capture.
