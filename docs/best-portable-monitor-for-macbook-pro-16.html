What the differences mean

Apple lists the 16-inch MacBook Pro's own display as 16.2 inches, 3456×2234 at 254 pixels per inch, with refresh up to 120 Hz; 3456 ÷ 2234 = 1.55, close to 16:10. The portable panel that matches it in size, shape and motion is therefore a 16-inch 16:10 screen at 2560×1600 and 120 Hz, and every row above states at least that (the maker's or the listing's figure, as marked; VisionOwl's title gives it as 2.5K QHD at 16:10 without the pixel count). Dealectric does not test units: each figure here is a maker's, a listing's or a named reviewer's. Rows a named reviewer ran on a Mac come first, because the one instrument reading on this line came in far under the rating.

  • Sharpness beside the MacBook. √(2560² + 1600²) ÷ 16 in = 188.7 ppi, and 188.7 ÷ 254 = 74% of the MacBook's density. This is the same for every row at 2560×1600, so it does not rank them. Parkablogs' Teoh Yi Chie writes that Mac users need the BetterDisplay app for sharp interface scaling at this size and resolution.
  • Brightness claims. On UPERFECT's 16-inch 120 Hz screen Parkablogs measured 238 nits against 400 cd/m² rated: 238 ÷ 400 = 59.5%. The Duex Float 2 Pro's 350 nits and SOTSU's 400 nits are ratings; no instrument reading is behind them here.
  • Charging through the screen. The Duex Float 2 Pro passes up to 65 W; Apple ships a 140 W adapter with the 16-inch MacBook Pro: 65 ÷ 140 = 46%.
  • Battery, as an estimate. Apple rates the M5 Pro model at 17 hours of wireless web on a 99.6 Wh battery: 99.6 ÷ 17 = 5.86 W average. Add the Float 2 Pro's 7.5 W: 99.6 ÷ 13.36 = about 7.5 hours. Add the 12 W rated power of UPERFECT's screen: 99.6 ÷ 17.86 = about 5.6 hours. These are sums on stated draws, not tests.
  • Bag weight. MacBook Pro 16 at 4.7 lb + Duex Float 2 Pro at 2.9 lb = 7.6 lb; + UPERFECT at 1.59 lb = 6.29 lb.

One cable, the MacBook's ports and the 140 W charger

Video. The current 16-inch MacBook Pro is sold only with M5 Pro or M5 Max. Apple says the M5 Pro drives up to three external displays (three at up to 4K 144 Hz) and the M5 Max four, over its Thunderbolt 5 (USB-C) and HDMI ports. One 2560×1600 screen at 120 Hz on a USB-C cable is inside that, with no display driver. The 2021 model (M1 Pro or M1 Max) also has native DisplayPort over USB-C, but Apple rates its HDMI port at 4K 60 Hz, so use the USB-C cable for 120 Hz. For a variable-refresh (Adaptive Sync) screen, Apple says to connect by Thunderbolt, USB-C or DisplayPort cable and not by HDMI.

Power. These screens have no battery; on one cable the MacBook feeds them, and both reviewed screens hold brightness back when it does. Macworld's Simon Jary found the Duex Float 2 Pro takes 7.5 W from the MacBook in a limited power mode that caps brightness at 80%; a USB-C PD charger of 15 W or more plugged into the screen lifts the cap and prevents flickering. The Camping Nerd found UPERFECT's 16-inch 120 Hz screen on a 16-inch MacBook "only goes to 75 before resetting" without its own power, and it did not charge the MacBook. For scale, ServeTheHome measured a 60 Hz panel of the same size and resolution (Arzopa Z1RC) at 6.7 W at half brightness and 11.0 W at full.

What to plug in where. The 16-inch MacBook Pro has a MagSafe 3 port as well as three USB-C ports. Keep Apple's 140 W adapter on MagSafe and give the screen its own small USB-C charger: 65 W through the Float 2 Pro is under half what the Mac's adapter supplies, and under 10 W through the UPERFECT (Parkablogs) will not charge it.

Fit. The Float 2 Pro's base holds to the back of the lid with four included magnets; Macworld says it also works without them, less steadily. Mobile Pixels gives its fit range as 15 to 16-inch laptops.

Not recommended

UPERFECT's 16-inch 120 Hz screen as the main second display in a bright room. It is the lightest reviewed screen here, but the unit Parkablogs measured reached 238 nits, 59.5% of its 400 cd/m² rating, and The Camping Nerd's stopped at 75 of 100 brightness on a MacBook's cable alone. Both reviews are of 2023 units and the Amazon listing's title does not name a model, so current stock may differ. The same caution applies to any row whose brightness rests on a listing alone.
