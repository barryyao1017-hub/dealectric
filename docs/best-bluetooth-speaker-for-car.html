What the differences mean

Rated hours only compare fairly when the volume behind them is the same. Tribit and soundcore rate at 50% volume, Avantree at 60%, and JBL names no level. Only soundcore publishes a full-volume figure.

  • Boom Go 3i, lights on: 12 h ÷ 24 h = 50% of the headline rating. At 100% volume: 6 h ÷ 24 h = 25%. SoundGuys measured 5 h 55 min at 100% volume with the lights and BassUp on.
  • JBL Clip 5: TechGearLab measured 8.8 h at 75% volume against a 12 h rating: 8.8 ÷ 12 = 73%.
  • soundcore Select 4 Go: TechGearLab measured 25.4 h at 75% volume against a 20 h rating: 25.4 ÷ 20 = 127%.
  • Tribit vs Avantree Roadtrip: 24 h ÷ 8 h = 3 times the rated music time, at 50% volume against 60%. On each maker's store the two cost the same today.
  • Loudness: TechGearLab measured 87 dB at maximum from the Clip 5 and 84 dB from the Select 4 Go: 87 − 84 = 3 dB.

Whichever you buy, take it with you when the car is parked in the sun. JBL rates the Clip 5 to 45 °C, and Jabra says to keep its speaker batteries between 0 and 45 °C. UC San Diego and Arizona State measured cabin air averaging 116 °F (46.7 °C) and dashboards averaging 157 °F (69.4 °C) after one hour parked in the sun.

What each battery rating assumes

Tribit StormBox Micro 3. Tribit's footnote says the 24 h comes from 'TRIBIT lab at 50% volume, normal temperature', and the battery callout on the same page adds 'default EQ'. Tribit calls the 13 W 'sustained working power', not a peak figure. A 15-minute charge gives about 3 h of play, and the 4,800 mAh battery can top up a phone over USB-C. The 24 h is Tribit's own figure: the Micro 3 was not in TechGearLab's bench test.

soundcore Boom Go 3i. soundcore publishes four ratings: 24 h (50% volume, lights off, BassUp), 40 h (50% volume, lights off, Eco mode), 12 h (50% volume, lights on, BassUp) and 6 h (100% volume, lights on, BassUp). The 24 h on the store page is the lights-off case.

JBL Clip 5. JBL rates 'up to 12 hours plus 3 hours with Playtime Boost (dependent on volume level and audio content)' and names no level. TechGearLab looped music at 75% volume until each speaker died. The Clip 5 lasted 8.8 h, the shortest run on that bench.

Avantree Roadtrip and CK11. itechguides lists the Roadtrip at 8 h of music and 12 h of talk, both at 60% volume, from two 3 W speakers. Avantree's own listing title says '6W Speakers' and '22hr Playtime'. The CK11's 18 h is a single figure with no split between talk and music.

Jabra Drive. The manual rates 20 h of talk and 30 days of standby, with no separate music figure. It holds two phones at once. If a call comes in on the second phone, the first phone's call goes on hold.

Not recommended

Avantree Roadtrip, for a car with no radio. Its headline feature is an FM transmitter, which only works with a radio tuned to the same unused frequency. Without a radio you are left with two 3 W speakers rated for 8 h of music at 60% volume, even though the listing title claims 22 h. The Tribit StormBox Micro 3 costs the same on its maker's store, is rated 24 h at 50% volume and also has a mic for calls.
