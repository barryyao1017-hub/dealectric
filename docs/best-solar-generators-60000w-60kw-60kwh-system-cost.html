What a 60 kWh solar generator costs, part by part

Both home systems store energy in 6,144 Wh battery modules, so 10 modules make 61.44 kWh in each case. The difference is how many inverters you need to hold them. One DELTA Pro Ultra X inverter takes up to 10 batteries, so a single 12 kW inverter carries the whole 61.44 kWh. An E10 power module takes up to 5 B6000 batteries, so 60 kWh needs two modules and Anker's Power Dock to join them. That extra module costs more, but it also adds output: 15.36 kW rated, or 20 kW turbo for 90 minutes.

The EcoFlow price advantage depends on how you connect it to the house. Battery-only, the DELTA Pro Ultra X is $2,401 cheaper than the E10. Add the Smart Home Panel 3 kit and it costs $599 more than the E10, whose bundle already includes the Power Dock (200 A grid and subpanel connection, ≤20 ms switchover). EcoFlow says Smart Home Panel 3 requires installation, with a fee set after assessment. None of the prices here include installation. On battery life, Anker states 80% capacity after 4,000 cycles. EcoFlow's DELTA Pro Ultra X spec page gives no cycle figure.

We don't link to the DELTA Pro Ultra X; EcoFlow sells it from its US store. The E10 build is available here: $14,098: https://dealectric.com/go/anker-solix-e10-2-power-module-4-battery-power-dock. Add six B6000 add-on batteries to reach 61.44 kWh. The original DELTA Pro Ultra (7.2–21.6 kW) shows as unavailable on EcoFlow's US store as of 2026-11-10.

If you truly need 60,000 W of continuous output, you need commercial equipment. The Sol-Ark 60K-3P-480V delivers 60,000 W, but only at 277/480 V three-phase, and the seller says it is designed for commercial and industrial customers. Both home systems above output 120/240 V split phase. The Sol-Ark also ships without batteries and needs a 300 V+ bank, so neither EcoFlow nor Anker batteries will work with it.

Not recommended

Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus for a 60 kWh goal. Anker lists it as expandable to 53.8 kWh at 12 kW, so it can't reach 60 kWh at all. The largest build is the two-unit Smart Home Power Kit plus 12 BP3800 batteries: $7,799.99 + 12 × $1,999.99 = $31,799.87. That works out to $31,799.87 ÷ 53,760 Wh = $0.59 per Wh, which costs more than either 61.44 kWh build above and stores less.
