How solar works on an Outdoor 4

Blink's Outdoor 4 ships with two AA lithium batteries and a USB-C port behind a rubber cover. Blink's own support page says the camera can be powered through that port by a weather-resistant USB-C adapter and that, once the cover is removed, the camera is no longer weather-resistant until it goes back on. A solar panel for the Outdoor 4 is therefore a USB-C power supply with a battery: the panel charges its internal cell by day, the cell runs the camera at night, and the AAs are removed. Blink's Solaris is the official solar option: it attaches to the camera itself (IP65 when connected), so it only helps where the camera gets direct sun, and Blink's FAQ says the app shows no battery or charging status for an Outdoor 4 with Solaris attached. Blink's Battery Extension Pack is compatible with the Outdoor 4 too, with the rule that USB power cannot be connected while it is on.

What Blink's own mount and Solaris do

The Blink Solar Panel Mount is a micro-USB design for the Outdoor (3rd Gen). Blink's FAQ names the Outdoor 4, XT and XT2 as unsupported, and in the US it is sold with a camera ($74.98 bundle), standalone only abroad (£34.99 at John Lewis). It is not an option for an Outdoor 4 owner at any price; the Solaris ($39.99 at Best Buy and Amazon on 2026-11-03) is, with the mounting limit above.

Internal battery and cable

Wasserstein's panel stores daytime charge in an internal battery and runs the camera through the night and through cloudy days; the 13.1 ft weatherproof cable lets the panel sit in sun while the camera sits in shade, and the 360° wall mount comes with screws and anchors. Owner reviews at Best Buy and Lowe's praise fewer battery swaps and report charge levels rising over weeks; a minority report no charging, and Wasserstein's own reply is that its panels trickle-charge rather than fill a flat battery.

Warranty and returns

Wasserstein's Blink accessories carry a 3-month warranty per its listings and the store gives 30 days to return. Tom's Guide's piece on Wasserstein's panel says it mounts more flexibly than Blink's own but is not as seamless.

What we could not verify

Long-term owner reports on r/blinkcameras, how long the Solaris keeps an Outdoor 4 running in winter (early Amazon-forum reports include units that never showed charging), and whether the Outdoor 4 keeps its weather rating with a third-party plug in the port.
