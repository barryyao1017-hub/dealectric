What the differences mean

  • Solaris stores 5.12 Wh. 3.2 V × 1.6 Ah = 5.12 Wh, per Blink's spec page, and all 156 g of it hangs on the camera.
  • Blink's 'month without sun' claim implies a very small daily draw. 5.12 Wh ÷ 30 days ≈ 0.17 Wh a day, about 7 mW on average. That is Blink's best case, stated for the Outdoor 2K+.
  • The older Solar Panel Mount holds more than twice as much. 3.7 V × 3.2 Ah = 11.84 Wh, so Solaris holds 5.12 ÷ 11.84 = 43% of that. The mount fits only the Outdoor (3rd Gen).
  • A busy Outdoor 4 drains much faster than Blink's two-year rating. TechGearLab measured a 12% drop over four days of constant motion. At 3% a day, 100% lasts about 33 days, which TechGearLab put at a little over a month on the two-battery setup. Blink rates the camera at up to 2 years on default settings.

Wasserstein publishes no wattage or battery capacity for its Blink panel, so its reserve can't be compared with these figures.

How a solar panel connects to the Outdoor 4

The Outdoor 4 takes outside power through its USB-C socket. Blink says it “can be powered through its USB-C port using a weather resistant USB-C adapter; however, once the USB port cover is removed, the camera is no longer weather-resistant until it's reinserted.” The camera runs on AA 1.5 V lithium non-rechargeable cells. Blink describes USB power as an alternative to those batteries, not as a way to charge them.

Wasserstein's panel comes with a 13.1 ft (4 m) weatherproof micro-USB cable and a USB-C converter. The maker's instruction for the Outdoor 4 is to “use the included USB C converter to connect to micro USB cable.” The listing doesn't say the converter joint is sealed, so mount the camera where that joint stays out of direct rain. $36.99: https://dealectric.com/go/wasserstein-solar-panel-blink-outdoor-4

Two limits apply to any cabled panel. First, Blink says “Third party power solutions are not supported” and “We cannot warranty devices connected to unreliable power sources, or when weather protection is removed.” Second, when the Outdoor 4 Battery Extension Pack is fitted, “the Outdoor 4's USB-C power socket is blocked,” so you can use the pack or a panel, but not both.

Solaris avoids the cable, but Blink's FAQ says “Blink Solaris is compatible with the Blink Outdoor 2K+.” Its setup guide asks for “a compatible Blink Outdoor 2K+ camera,” and neither page names the Outdoor 4. If a store lists Solaris for an Outdoor 4, buy it only where returns are easy.

Not recommended

Blink Solar Panel Mount on an Outdoor 4. It is Blink's own panel and the only one with published watts, but Blink states that the Outdoor 4, XT and XT2 are “NOT compatible with its Micro-USB connector to provide a weather resistant seal.” It is made for the Outdoor (3rd Gen) only.
