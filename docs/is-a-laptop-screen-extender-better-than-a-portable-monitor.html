Is a laptop screen extender better than a portable monitor?

Short answer. Usually no: for one added screen, a portable monitor wins on compatibility and puts no weight on the hinge. Buy the ViewSonic VA1655. A dual laptop screen extender needs a laptop that drives two external displays; Apple lists one for M1–M3 MacBook Air (lid open) and MacBook Neo.

What the differences mean

  • Screen area per kilo, one added screen. The VA1655's 15.6-inch panel gives about 104.0 sq in against 85.0 sq in for the 14.1-inch Duex Max DS (15.6² × 144/337 = 104.0; 14.1² × 144/337 = 85.0). That is 22% more screen (104.0 ÷ 85.0 = 1.22) at 0.7 kg against 0.8 kg, and none of the VA1655's weight rests on the lid.
  • Weight per added screen, two screens. The Trio 3 Pro weighs 1.9 kg ÷ 2 = 0.95 kg per screen (Macworld). The VG1457 weighs 1.18 kg ÷ 2 = 0.59 kg per screen and sits on the table (ViewSonic).
  • Two screens as two monitors. At ViewSonic on 2026-11-21, two VA1655s cost 2 × $109.99 = $219.98 and one VG1457 dual cost $349.99. On a one-display MacBook, only one of either pair extends (see below).
  • Screen quality follows the panel, not the mount. ServeTheHome measured the ASUS ZenScreen MB169CK portable monitor at 140.2 nits peak, against ASUS's 250 cd/m² rating. Notebookcheck measured a FOPO 12-inch triple extender at 185 cd/m² centre but only 247:1 contrast. Macworld says the Trio 3 Pro's screens reach full brightness only with a power adapter plugged into the Trio.

How many screens your laptop can drive decides it

Each screen on a dual or triple extender counts as a separate external display. A single portable monitor, or a one-screen attachable, needs one. Apple's spec pages list:

MacBookExternal displays (Apple)Two-screen extender
MacBook Air M1 (2020)1, up to 6K 60 HzSecond screen only through display software
MacBook Air M2 (2022)1, up to 6K 60 HzSecond screen only through display software
MacBook Air 13-inch M3 (2024)1 with the lid open; a second (up to 5K 60 Hz) only with the lid closedAn extender is used with the lid open, so one native screen
MacBook Air 13-inch M4 (2025)Up to 2, up to 6K 60 HzBoth screens natively
MacBook Air M5 (current)Up to 2Both screens natively
MacBook Neo1, up to 4K 60 HzSecond screen only through display software

Macworld's Simon Jary quotes the Trio 3 Pro manual: with "two DP enabled USB-C ports", no driver is needed. He writes that plain M1 and M2 Macs and the MacBook Neo need Mobile Pixels' free software, "rather like DisplayLink". He also says M1, M2 or M3 Pro or Max chips and any M4 or M5 run both screens without it. Apple's M3 MacBook Air spec allows one display with the lid open, so treat a base M3 Air like an M1 or M2. On Windows, count the laptop's USB-C ports that carry DisplayPort video: you need one per added screen for a driver-free setup.

ViewSonic's VG1457 runs both its screens over one cable using MST. ViewSonic says this is unavailable on macOS, so only the lower screen activates when extending. Hinge load is the other cost of a lid-mounted unit. Notebookcheck's Allen Ngo found a FOPO triple extender's hinges "tend to teeter" and advised using its rear kickstand to avoid straining the laptop's hinges. He concluded, "we would likely go with the individual portable monitors".

Not recommended

The ViewSonic VG1457 for MacBook owners. ViewSonic states that its two-screen extend mode doesn't work on macOS, so a Mac gets one extended 14-inch screen for $349.99. A VA1655 gives one 15.6-inch screen for $109.99 (both at ViewSonic, read 2026-11-21).
