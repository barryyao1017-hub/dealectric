EDID emulation: why matching specs aren't enough

A monitor describes itself to a computer with a block of data called EDID. When a plain KVM switches away, the computer you left loses that data. It then treats the monitor as unplugged and can shuffle your windows. A switch with EDID emulation keeps feeding the data to both computers. TESmart describes this as 'Prevents window changes during switching', and CKL as 'Supports EDID emulation to maintain correct display'.

You can't tell from the spec line. CKL's 922HUA-4 and 922HUA-5 share the same title specs (HDMI 2.1, 8K 60 Hz, 4K 144 Hz, 2 computers, 2 monitors). Only the -4's listing adds 'Note: this model does not support EDID emulation.' CKL's HDMI 2.0 matrix model, the 922HUA-M, lists EDID 'under dual monitor mode (not in matrix mode)'. UGREEN's CM664 manual lists 4K at 60 Hz, RGB and YCbCr 4:4:4, and USB 3.0. It states the switch 'does not support Hotkey Switching' and makes no claim about EDID emulation. So search the listing or manual for the word EDID before you buy.

On a 4K 60 Hz switch, full colour means 8-bit. EveZone's HDMI bandwidth breakdown puts 4K 60 Hz at 4:4:4 8-bit at about 17.82 Gb/s, inside HDMI 2.0's 18 Gb/s. At 10-bit the same signal needs 17.82 × 10 ÷ 8 ≈ 22.3 Gb/s, which doesn't fit, so 10-bit HDR at 4K 60 Hz drops to subsampled colour. BenQ puts 4K 120 Hz at about 16 Gb/s, and that holds only at 8-bit 4:2:0, so a 4K 120–144 Hz monitor belongs on an HDMI 2.1 switch. Dual-monitor HDMI switches such as the 922HUA-5 and HKS202-M24 also need two HDMI outputs on each computer.

Not recommended

CKL 922HUA-4. It is listed with the same HDMI 2.1 4K 144 Hz modes as the 922HUA-5. CKL's own listing says it does not support EDID emulation, so windows can rearrange each time you switch. The 922HUA-5 adds EDID for $20 more ($189.00 against $169.00).
