Best solar and wind generator

Updated 2026-11-06

Short answer. The best solar and wind generator is the Nature's Generator L3600 ($1,299.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/natures-generator-l3600-expandable-power-station): 3,072 Wh LiFePO4, 3,600 W, up to 2,400 W of solar and a 150 W wind port. The concern is the wind number: the matching turbine ($549.99 (sold out); buy: https://dealectric.com/go/natures-generator-150w-wind-turbine) makes 150 W only in a 27 mph wind, and power falls with the cube of wind speed, so a 10 mph site gets a few watts. Treat wind as an overnight top-up and size the solar for the real load.

If you do not have steady wind, the honest winner is a solar-only station: the BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 ($899; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-200-v2, Dealectric-exclusive code DEALECTRIC saves $44.95, pre-applied by the link) gives 2,073.6 Wh and 2,600 W with a 5-year warranty for less money. If you need 240 V for a well pump or central air, the Nature's Generator L6000 ($2,099.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/natures-generator-l6000-expandable-power-station) is the cheapest split-phase station with a wind port: 3,840 Wh, 6,000 W, 2,400 W solar, 150 W wind.

Nature's Generator is the only mainstream brand whose power stations accept a wind turbine directly. EcoFlow, Jackery, Anker and BLUETTI list no wind input, and BLUETTI's own forum says it does not recommend wind charging because the voltage is unstable. A generic 12 V turbine kit from Amazon does not plug into any of them.

What owners complain about

Three owner reports describe a Nature's Generator turbine that arrived damaged or never produced usable power: two Tractor Supply reviews of the wind bundles and one Better Business Bureau complaint from February 2024. Test the turbine as soon as it arrives, inside the 30-day return window, and keep the box. The Better Business Bureau lists four complaints in three years; two are about slow or missing support after a fault, and one about a shattered panel and wrong cables. Older reviews of the AGM-battery models call them heavy and slow to charge; the L3600 and L6000 are lithium and take up to 2,400 W of solar, so those complaints do not carry over. Expect to buy a pole: no Nature's Generator turbine includes one, and the Department of Energy's siting rule puts the rotor 30 ft above anything within 300 ft.

Which one by need

  • Cabin or essential-circuit backup with real wind. L3600 plus the 150 W turbine. 2,400 W of solar does the daytime work; the turbine adds a trickle at night. Add L3600 Power Pods for longer outages.
  • Well pump, dryer or central air (240 V). L6000. 6,000 W split-phase and the same wind port, 112 lb, five pods possible.
  • Whole house for days. MyGrid 10K Gen 2 at $5,599.99 (2026-11-06): 10,496 Wh, 10,000 W, and the only unit that takes the 350 W turbine. Curbside freight only.
  • No steady wind. BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 for 120 V loads, or BLUETTI Apex 300 for 240 V. Both have 5-year warranties against 1 year here.

Not recommended

The refurbished Elite 3600 is cheap, but it is an AGM unit with a 90-day warranty and a 10 to 15 hour charge; skip it as a primary system. Do not mount the turbine on a vehicle or a roof edge; turbulence kills output and the maker advises against it. Do not buy a generic 12 V turbine kit expecting to feed a BLUETTI, EcoFlow or Jackery; none has a wind input and BLUETTI says it does not support it.
