What the differences mean

On every unit here, wind is the smaller power source. On the L3600 the solar port is rated at 16 times the wind port (2,400 W ÷ 150 W). At its rated 27 mph, the 150 W turbine refills the 3,072 Wh battery in about 20.5 hours (3,072 ÷ 150). Full solar input refills it in about 1.3 hours (3,072 ÷ 2,400).

A small turbine's output falls with the cube of the wind speed (DOE Small Wind Guidebook). DOE's minimum average wind for an off-grid hybrid site is 9 mph. At that speed the 150 W turbine makes about 5.6 W (150 × (9/27)³), or 133 Wh a day. That is about 23 days to refill an L3600 (3,072 ÷ 133). At a steady 12 mph it makes about 316 Wh a day (150 × (12/27)³ × 24), or about 10 days per refill. These are steady-wind estimates worked from the maker's 27 mph rating. Nobody measured them.

One port, two sources: how the wind input connects

The L3600 manual shows one Solar/Wind port. It "connects up to 2400W solar panels or up to 150W wind turbine", and the spec sheet reads "Wind Charger: 150W (shared port)". The turbine plugs into that port through a Wind Turbine Cable Adapter. A shopper asked about this on the product page, and Nature's Generator replied that the L3600 "is not designed to charge simultaneously with solar panels and wind turbine." To use the turbine you unplug the panels. The 2,400 W solar rating and the 150 W wind rating are alternatives; they don't add up to 2,550 W.

There are three ways to get a second input:

  • L3600 Power Pod. It has its own Solar/Wind port (up to 1,000 W of solar or a 150 W turbine), and up to five pods connect to one L3600. The pod is 1,299.99 at the maker on 2026-11-14. Its manual does not say whether solar on the base and wind on the pod can charge at the same time.
  • L6000. The manual lists solar as "12 - 60Vdc 25A 1200W x2" and wind as "12Vdc 150W (shared port)". The maker's own documents disagree on which turbine it takes. The 150 W turbine's help page lists the Standard 1800, Lithium 1800, Elite and Lithium 3600 as compatible, but not the L6000. The 350 W turbine's help page does list the L6000. Ask Nature's Generator before buying an L6000 for wind.
  • MyGrid 10K. Its spec sheet lists "1x Wind Input Port" (350 W) separately from "2x Solar Input Ports" (12,000 W). It is the only unit here where the turbine does not take the place of a solar input.

The turbine and the site. The 150 W turbine is rated at 27 mph and survives winds up to 110 mph. The pole is not included (maker's reply on the L3600 page). The maker asks for 1.5-inch schedule 40 steel pipe that can take 160 lb of horizontal load, and says never to use plastic pipe. DOE advises mounting a small turbine so the bottom of the rotor sits at least 30 ft above anything within 300 ft. The 150 W turbine was listed at 549.99 on 2026-11-14 $549.99: https://dealectric.com/go/natures-generator-150w-wind-turbine. Every L3600 and L6000 solar-and-wind bundle showed sold out that day, and the maker says turbines ship separately in early December 2026.

Not recommended

The WindSoleil 600-Watt Off-Grid Hybrid Kit costs 1,950. That buys a 700 W inverter, two 100 W panels and a 12 V gel battery. For less money, the Elite 200 V2 (899) gives 2,600 W of AC output and the L3600 (1,299.99) gives 3,600 W. The kit's 600 W hybrid charge controller takes both sources, but a 700 W inverter won't start most of the household loads a generator is bought for.
