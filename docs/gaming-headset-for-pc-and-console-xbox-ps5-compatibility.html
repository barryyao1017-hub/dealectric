Why Xbox support has to be in the headset you buy

Microsoft's rule for Xbox Series X covers two routes. Over USB, Microsoft promises support for "officially licensed" accessories. Through the 3.5 mm port on the Xbox Wireless Controller, "any headsets" are supported. A generic wireless dongle does not make a headset an Xbox headset. That is why one SteelSeries model works on Xbox and its near-twins do not.

  • Only the 5X: SteelSeries Support says "The Arctis Nova 5X is the only Nova 5 model built for Xbox". It adds that the Nova 5 and Nova 5P "won't work with Xbox—even if you'd purchase the Nova 5X dongle separately." The X has to be on the box when you buy. You cannot add it later.
  • Set the switch: the Nova 5X dongle has two positions. Use Xbox for an Xbox Series X|S or Xbox One, and USB for a PC, a PlayStation or other platforms. If the headset will not connect on Xbox, check this switch first.
  • The same split at HyperX: HyperX lists the Cloud III Wired for Xbox Series X|S and Xbox One. Its own listing for the Cloud III Wireless names only PC, PS5 and Nintendo Switch.
  • Logitech PRO X 2: Logitech's US product page names no consoles. SoundGuys says the dongle works on PC, PS5 and a docked Switch but not on other consoles, so on Xbox it connects with the 3.5 mm cable. The reviewer measured 200–250 ms of delay over Bluetooth and called that too slow for competitive play.
  • Surround claims on consoles: Razer says the BlackShark V2 X's 7.1 surround is "Only available on Windows 11". Over 3.5 mm on a console you get stereo.

Not recommended

The Arctis Nova 5 or Nova 5P, if there is or may be an Xbox in the house. They look like the 5X and sit next to it in store listings, but SteelSeries says neither will ever work with Xbox, even with a 5X dongle. If you can't see "5X" or "Xbox" in the product name, pick another listing.
