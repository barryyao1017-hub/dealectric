What the differences mean

Every linked-output figure above is the maker's rating. No named reviewer has published a load test of two linked units, so the safest planning figure is two times what one unit held in a test. Dealectric does not test units; the measured figures come from The Solar Lab's single-unit reviews.

  • Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus pair: 2 × 5,100 W = 10,200 W sustained to plan on, against 12,000 W rated (arithmetic).
  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 pair: 2 × 3,650 W = 7,300 W held in testing, against 8,000 W rated (arithmetic).
  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro pair: 2 × 3,000 W = 6,000 W, the load one unit finished a full discharge at, against 7,200 W rated (arithmetic).

How close linked power stations come to their ratings

Linking only works through the maker's own hub, panel or parallel cable, which synchronizes the two inverters and splits the load. Joining the AC outlets of two power stations with a homemade cable is unsafe. Each hub here also adds a 240 V outlet or split-phase output, so the extra watts arrive at 240 V or across two 120 V legs, not at a single 120 V household outlet.

The Solar Lab's single-unit tests show how much headroom each rating has. The Anker F3800 held 5,100 W, 85% of its 6,000 W rating, for 37 minutes, and carried 7,200 W for about 10 seconds. The EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 held 3,650 W, 91% of its 4,000 W rating, for a full hour at about 89% efficiency. The original EcoFlow DELTA Pro shut down at about 3,650 W, then again at 3,500 W after a recharge and cooldown, and finished a full discharge only at 3,000 W, 83% of its 3,600 W rating. The Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus ran 8,000 W and 9,500 W for 20 to 30 seconds but struggled to start an air conditioner with a 10,000 W start surge. The Solar Lab gives the BLUETTI Apex 300 an idle draw of about 20 W and about 85% AC efficiency but no sustained-watts figure; its weak points are the Hub A1, which costs $699 with two cables, is out of stock on BLUETTI's store, and which The Solar Lab calls a steep price, and a two-unit rating of 7,680 W, below an F3800 Plus pair. Anker notes the F3800 Plus cannot use its 240 V output while it charges from 120 V AC.

Not recommended

Jackery Explorer 2000 Plus pair for 120 V loads. Jackery's Connector page says two 2000 Plus units deliver 4,000 W only to 220 V appliances and that for 120 V loads only one unit and its battery packs supply power, so linking adds no 120 V output.

EcoFlow DELTA Pro pair for a load near 7,200 W. It is the cheapest pair to buy, but one unit could not hold 3,500 W in The Solar Lab's test; size the loads for about 6,000 W.
