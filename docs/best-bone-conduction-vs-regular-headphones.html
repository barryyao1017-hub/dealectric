Why only some bone conduction headphones work in the pool

Shokz states it plainly: 'water disrupts Bluetooth signals underwater', and 'For underwater listening, MP3 Mode is required.' This is radio physics, not one brand's design. Any headset that only streams from your phone goes silent once your head is underwater. To swim with music you need two things together: an IP68 rating and music stored on the headset.

  • MOJAWA Run Plus: IP68 and 32 GB of MP3 storage. HeadphonesAddict cites MOJAWA's site for up to 2 h underwater and rates it for swimming down to 2 m.
  • MOJAWA Purra Swim: IP68 to 2 m, 32 GB (MOJAWA says 8,000+ songs) and 15 h. Earplugs come in the box 'for maximum underwater sound quality'.
  • Shokz OpenSwim Pro: IP68 and 32 GB. Shokz rates it at 9 h on Bluetooth but 6 h in MP3 mode, so plan swim time on the 6 h figure.
  • Shokz OpenRun Pro 2 and soundcore AeroFit 2: both IP55. Shokz says the OpenRun Pro 2 'does not have onboard storage' and 'is not intended for swimming'.
  • soundcore Space One: no water rating at all. soundcore advises against using it at the gym.

The Run Plus and Purra Swim charge through MOJAWA's Type-C magnetic cable, not a plain USB-C port, so pack it when you travel (a spare is $15.90). On the regular-headphone side, the Space One cannot enter multipoint pairing while in LDAC mode, and the AeroFit 2's LDAC works only with Android, so iPhone owners get AAC on both. Bone conduction's thinner bass has a mechanical cause. Shokz calls strong vibration 'one of the main criticisms of bone conduction headphones', and the OpenRun Pro 2 hands low frequencies to a separate air-conduction driver.

Not recommended

Shokz OpenRun Pro 2 for anyone who swims. It is a strong road-running set, but it is IP55, has no onboard storage, and Shokz itself says it is not for swimming. Swimmers should pick one of the IP68 sets above. soundcore AeroFit 2 for sound quality: SoundGuys gave it a 5.3 rating and wrote that 'if you're willing to shell out a bit more money, there are better-sounding options.' SoundGuys found its response 'severely compromised in all but a small section of the midrange' because the open design has no seal. Buy it for comfort and awareness, not for sound.
