What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure here is a maker's figure or a named tester's reading, with the pixel counts worked out from them.

  • Pixels per frame. Vantrue N4S: 2592 × 1944 = 5.04 MP front, and 2560 × 1440 = 3.69 MP each for cabin and rear, so 5.04 + 3.69 + 3.69 = 12.41 MP. Redtiger F17 and Rexing C3 as labelled: 3840 × 2160 = 8.29 MP front and 1920 × 1080 = 2.07 MP each for cabin and rear, so 8.29 + 2.07 + 2.07 = 12.44 MP. The totals are nearly equal; the split is not. Totals are added from the unrounded pixel counts, so the last digit can differ from the rounded figures shown.
  • Cabin and rear detail. 3.69 ÷ 2.07 = 1.78, so each N4S cabin or rear frame holds 1.78 times the pixels of a 1080p frame.
  • Card space. EricSan listed the N4S at 16.0 + 14.3 + 14.3 = 44.6 Mb/s with all three cameras recording. That is about 20.1 GB an hour, so 256 ÷ 20.1 = about 12.7 hours on a 256 GB card before it loops. Vantrue lists cards up to 1 TB. The F17 manual lists 32 to 256 GB, with a 64 GB card included.
  • Parking mode costs extra on every three-camera row we priced. The N4S box has no hardwire kit. Vantrue's Voltage Display Hardwire Kit, whose listing names the N4S, was $29.99 on vantrue.com on 2026-11-21, and on top of $199.99 that took the total past $200. Redtiger's kit was $35.99, so the F17 came to $129.99 + $35.99 = $165.98.

What “4K” and “2.7K” mean once all three cameras record

The sensor sets the ceiling. Sony's STARVIS 2 IMX675 has a 2608 × 1964 pixel array, 5.12 MP, in a 4:3 shape (FRAMOS distributor listing). A 4K frame is 3840 × 2160 = 8.29 MP. 5.12 ÷ 8.29 = 0.62, so the sensor has 62% of the pixels a 4K frame holds.

Redtiger F17. Redtiger's store listing names the “STARVIS 2 IMX675” as the F17's front sensor, so its 4K front file is built from a 5.12 MP sensor. The same listing says “Turn off the inside camera to enable 4K front and 1080P rear recording”, while the manual's spec table lists Front 4K + Inside 1080P + Rear 1080P as a mode. Either way the cabin and rear cameras record 1080p. The Redtiger whose listing names the 8 MP-class IMX678 behind its 4K front is the F17 Elite, which is over this page's budget.

Vantrue N4S. DashCamTalk reviewer Hack_man lists the same IMX675 in all three cameras. Vantrue labels the result 2.7K front and 2.5K inside and rear. The top front setting is a 4:3 frame of 2592 × 1944; Hack_man relays Vantrue's note that 4:3 “may not look as natural on widescreen displays”. His mode list shows the cabin camera at 1944p in front + cabin mode, and at 1440p with all three recording. EricSan's recorded files agree for the two cameras he measured: 2592 × 1944 front and 2560 × 1440 rear, each at 30 fps; his mode list puts the cabin camera at 1440p and 30 fps as well.

The N4S's weak point is bitrate. EricSan logged its front stream at 16.0 Mb/s, against 45.9 Mb/s on a VIOFO A139 Pro front camera in the same test. In low-bitrate parking mode he logged 1920 × 1080 at 15 fps per camera. His readings cover the N4S only; the other rows rest on their makers' labels.

Vantrue OnDash N4. Vantrue states 1440P + 1080P + 1080P at 30 fps with front, cabin and rear recording. Its 4K figure (2160P at 25 fps) is for the front camera recording alone.

Not recommended

Rexing C3, if you need GPS or parking mode. Rexing lists 4K front + 1080p cabin + 1080p rear recorded together, and its listing names no image sensor behind the 4K label. GPS is not in the box. Rexing states the C3 “requires this dedicated logger” and that its standard 3.5 mm and 2.5 mm loggers are not compatible. The logger was $29.99 on 2026-11-21, which makes $149.99 + $29.99 = $179.98 to match the GPS the N4S and F17 include. Rexing also says the parking monitor “requires hardwire kit for continuous power”, and the Rexing hardwire kit listing we read that day names the R4, L4 and RH2 series, not the C3.
