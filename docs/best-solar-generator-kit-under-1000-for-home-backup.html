What the differences mean

In an outage that lasts more than a night, the battery runs out on day one. From then on, the only thing that matters is how much energy the panel puts back each day. Here is the arithmetic behind the table:

  • November harvest per rated panel watt: 3.53 sun-hours (the NREL PVWatts average across 50 US cities) × 0.75 planning derate = 2.65 Wh per day.
  • 600 W kit: 600 × 2.65 = 1,589 Wh/day. Spread over 24 h, that is 66 W. The battery refills in 1,024 ÷ 1,589 = 0.64 day.
  • Any 200 W kit: 200 × 2.65 = 530 Wh/day, or 22 W around the clock. The AC180 refills in 1,152 ÷ 530 = 2.2 days.
  • Price per sustained watt: $798 ÷ 66 W = $12 for the 600 W kit, against $769 ÷ 22 W = $35 for the AC180 + 200 W.
  • The AC180's bigger battery: it has 1,152 − 1,024 = 128 Wh more. That equals about 17 minutes of strong sun on the 600 W panel (128 ÷ (600 × 0.75) = 0.28 h).
  • Your own load: take the kWh per year on your fridge's yellow EnergyGuide label and divide by 8.76 to get its average watts. Compare that number with the November solar column.

How much the panel puts back in a November outage

A panel's rated watts assume full sun at noon. A November day in the US averages 3.53 peak sun-hours (December 2.87), and folding panels deliver about 75% of their rating once angle, heat and wiring losses are counted. Averaged over the whole day, each kit's panel can only keep that much flowing. Every 200 W kit lands near 22 W, so the gap between a 1,024 Wh and a 1,152 Wh battery matters less than the gap between a 200 W and a 600 W panel. A panel only counts in full if the station accepts it. ALLPOWERS rates the P1800 for 650 W of solar, so its 600 W panel is not clipped. BLUETTI's AC180 accepts up to 500 W (12–60 V, 10 A), which means a 200 W panel uses less than half of its input.

Battery ratings also overstate what reaches the outlets. OutdoorGearLab drained the AC180 at 80% load and measured 950 Wh of its rated 1,152 Wh. Similar inverter losses apply to every kit in the table, so compare the kits by the panel figures above.

One pricing trap: BLUETTI sells the same AC180 + 200 W bundle at $769 on its /products/ac180 listing and at $999 on its solar-generator-ac180 listing (both read 2026-11-14). Check which page you are on before you pay.
