What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Store prices below were read on the makers' US stores on 2026-11-18. ALLPOWERS shipping states were read on 2026-11-17, and BLUETTI, Jackery and EcoFlow shipping states on 2026-11-18. Link prices include the code our link applies. Measurements come from the named reviewers.

  • Cost per Wh. R2500 V2 + SP037: $913.49 ÷ 1,920 Wh = $0.48. Elite 200 V2 + 2× 200 W: $1,424.05 ÷ 2,073.6 Wh = $0.69. The BLUETTI kit costs $510.56 more ($1,424.05 − $913.49) for 153.6 Wh more battery.
  • Refill from the panels in the box. 1,920 Wh ÷ 400 W = 4.8 h of full sun at rated output for the R2500 V2 kit, and 2,073.6 ÷ 400 = 5.2 h for the Elite 200 V2 kit. Panels deliver less than their rating in real sun, so real refills take longer.
  • Room to add panels. ALLPOWERS and BLUETTI both list a 1,000 W solar input limit for these stations. With 400 W of panels in the box, 400 ÷ 1,000 = 40% of the input is in use.
  • Starting motors. ALLPOWERS lists 5,000 W surge for the R2500 V2. BLUETTI lists no surge rating for the Elite 200 V2. Its 3,900 W Power Lifting mode runs resistive loads such as kettles and heaters only.
  • Expansion. Among the R2500 listings on the ALLPOWERS store, the B1000/B3000 expansion batteries and 4,000 W parallel output appear only on those for the older 2,016 Wh R2500 Plus, which the store shows as unavailable. The R2500 V2 listings name no expansion battery, so buy the battery size you need now.

Cost per Wh and refill time: what each kit gets you today

Two numbers sum up a panel bundle. Price ÷ battery Wh is what each stored watt-hour costs. Battery Wh ÷ panel watts is how many hours of full rated sun the panels in the box need to refill the battery. Shipping is the third check: on 2026-11-18 BLUETTI's store said new Elite 200 V2 orders are expected to ship in early January, so the R2500 V2 + SP037, which ALLPOWERS ships Dec 11 (2026-11-26), is the only kit near 2 kWh with a 400 W panel that ships before January.

What reviewers measured coming out of the batteries: Riccardo Conti at Your Life Updated drew 1.92 kWh from the R2500 V2 at about 900 W and 1.55 kWh at a steady 100 W (1.55 ÷ 1.92 = 81% of rated at the light load). The 900 W result equals the full rating, which is unusually high, so treat it with caution. Jonathan Kwan at APH Networks drew 2,028 Wh from the Elite 200 V2 with a 987 W heater. At roughly 1 kW, that works out to $913.49 ÷ 1,920 = $0.48 against $1,424.05 ÷ 2,028 = $0.70 per delivered Wh. The Solar Lab measured the Elite 200 V2 at 94% efficiency with 9.5 W idle draw (9.5 × 24 = 228 Wh a day). It measured EcoFlow's DELTA 3 Max at 79% and about 27 W idle (648 Wh a day), and Jackery's Explorer 2000 v2 at 90% and about 17 W.

Other brands' bundles on their own stores the same day: Jackery's Solar Generator 2000 v2 was listed at $1,239 (2,042 Wh, 2,200 W, 4,400 W surge), and the listing says its 200 W panel is a pre-order that ships by Nov 28. EcoFlow's DELTA 3 Max Solar Generator started at $1,229 (2,048 Wh, 2,400 W, 4,800 W surge) with no pre-order note. That starting price does not name the panel set, so check the panel count in EcoFlow's selector. Even at that price it is $1,229 ÷ 2,048 = $0.60 per Wh, $315.51 more than the R2500 V2 kit.
