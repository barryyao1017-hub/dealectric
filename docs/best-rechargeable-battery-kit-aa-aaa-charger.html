What the differences mean

  • Time to fill an AA on the TN480U: 2,800 mAh ÷ 500 mA = 5.6 h at least. The starter's 2,500 mAh cells take 2,500 ÷ 500 = 5 h, and a Premium Pro AAA takes 1,100 ÷ 250 = 4.4 h. Real charges run longer because NiMH charging loses some energy. Tenergy doesn't publish a charge time.
  • Power the TN480U needs: 5 V × 2 A = 10 W. The Amazon Basics charger in both Tenergy carts is rated 12 W at up to 2.4 A, which works out to 5 V (12 ÷ 2.4) with 2 W more than the TN480U is rated for. An old 5 V 1 A phone cube supplies 5 V × 1 A = 5 W, half the rated input.
  • Charge left after a year in a drawer: Tenergy rates Premium Pro cells at 85% after 1 year in storage, so an AA keeps 2,800 × 0.85 = 2,380 mAh.
  • Rated wear: Tenergy rates Premium Pro cells for up to 1,200 recharges and the starter cells for up to 1,000.
  • Warranty: Tenergy lists 12 months on the charger and the Premium Pro AA and AAA cells in the 16-cell set, and 12 months on the charger in the starter set. Woot's listing for the Amazon Basics charger gives a 1-year Amazon Basics warranty.

The Tenergy box has no wall plug: add a 5 V 2 A adapter

Tenergy's listings for both TN480U combos say the charger takes DC 5 V 2 A through its Micro-USB or USB-C port. The 8 AA + 8 AAA listing says "Micro-USB to AC power adapter is NOT included" and puts one Micro-USB cable in the box. The 4 AA + 4 AAA starter box lists only the charger and the cells.

Both Tenergy carts add the Amazon Basics 12W One-Port USB-A Wall Charger. Its Amazon listing rates it at 2.4 A, and Woot's listing describes one 12 W USB-A port, with only the charger in the box. In the 16-cell cart it uses the Micro-USB cable from Tenergy's box. The starter cart adds an Amazon Basics 3 ft USB-A to USB-C cable, which its Amazon listing says is USB-IF certified, for the TN480U's USB-C port.

The TN480U charges NiMH and NiCd cells only. Keep 1.5 V lithium AA cells out of it. GeekChamp: "Do not put regulated lithium cells in a charger that supports only NiMH."

IKEA's STENKOL needs no separate adapter. IKEA says it connects directly to a wall socket, takes NiMH cells of at least 1,000 mAh (AA) or 500 mAh (AAA), and fills them in 4–8 hours.

Not recommended

Don't buy a TN480U combo on its own, or pair it with Tenergy's USB-C PD 20W charger. Without an adapter, the charger has nothing to plug into a wall socket. The 20 W brick has a single USB-C port and comes with no cable, and Tenergy doesn't say whether the TN480U accepts power over a USB-C-to-USB-C cable. A USB-A charger with a USB-A cable, as in both carts here, avoids that question.
