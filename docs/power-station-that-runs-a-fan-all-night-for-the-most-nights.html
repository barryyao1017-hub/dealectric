What the differences mean

Each night count is the energy a station delivers per charge divided by what one night costs it: the fan's watts plus the inverter's own idle draw, for 8 hours. Dealectric does not test units; the energy and idle figures come from the reviewers and makers named in the table, read 2026-11-17, and the nights are arithmetic.

  • BLUETTI Apex 300 + 2× B500K: 13,004.8 Wh × 0.85 = 11,054 Wh; (50 + 20) W × 8 h = 560 Wh a night; 11,054 / 560 = 19.7 nights (11,054 / 320 = 34.5 with a 20 W fan).
  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra X: 12,288 Wh × 0.85 = 10,445 Wh; (50 + 36) × 8 = 688 Wh; 10,445 / 688 = 15.2 nights (17.5 if it delivers 98% like the original DELTA Pro Ultra).
  • BLUETTI Apex 300 + 2× B300K: 8,294.4 Wh × 0.85 = 7,050 Wh; 7,050 / 560 = 12.6 nights.
  • Anker SOLIX S5000: 4,870 Wh delivered; (50 + 10) × 8 = 480 Wh; 4,870 / 480 = 10.1 nights (4,870 / 240 = 20.3 with a 20 W fan).
  • EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra: 6,144 Wh × 0.98 = 6,021 Wh; (50 + 52) × 8 = 816 Wh; 6,021 / 816 = 7.4 nights.
  • Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus: 4,450 Wh delivered; (50 + 85) × 8 = 1,080 Wh; 4,450 / 1,080 = 4.1 nights (4,450 / 864 = 5.2 at The Solar Lab's 58 W).

Why idle draw decides fan nights

A fan draws little power, so the watts an inverter burns just by having its AC outlets on are a large share of every night. Measured no-load draw: Anker SOLIX S5000 about 10 W (pv magazine USA, 5% of charge lost in 24 h); BLUETTI Apex 300 19.5 W (ESS News) and 20 W (The Solar Lab); EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra X about 36 W (The Solar Lab); EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra 52 W (HOBOTECH) and about 45 W (The Solar Lab); Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus 85 W (HOBOTECH) and 58 W (The Solar Lab); original Anker SOLIX F3800 about 80 W (The Solar Lab).

With a 50 W fan, the DELTA Pro Ultra's 52 W idle takes half of each night's energy, so its 6,144 Wh gives fewer nights (7.4) than the S5000's 5,024 Wh (10.1). The Apex 300's low idle is why BLUETTI's 13 kWh stack outlasts EcoFlow's 12 kWh DELTA Pro Ultra X by about four nights.

Limits of these figures: no named reviewer has run a fan overnight on these units, so draw under a 40-50 W load is taken as fan watts plus the no-load idle, and inverter losses at light load may differ. The Apex 300 idle was measured on a single unit; any standby draw the B500K or B300K packs add is not counted. The S5000 also takes Anker expansion batteries (about 5 kWh each, up to 30 kWh in total, per pv magazine USA), which at 10 W idle would roughly double its nights; no reviewer has measured that setup. A 12 V DC fan on a station's DC port does not need the AC inverter switched on at all.

Not recommended

For running a fan, the Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus: HOBOTECH measured 85 W idle (The Solar Lab 58 W), more than the fan itself, so its 5,040 Wh gives 4 to 5 nights of a 50 W fan, about half of the S5000's or less from a battery the same size. The original Anker SOLIX F3800 does worse: The Solar Lab measured about 80 W idle and StorageReview measured 2,980 Wh delivered, so 2,980 / ((50 + 80) × 8 = 1,040) = 2.9 nights. No named reviewer has published an idle figure for the newer F3800 Plus.
