What the differences mean

  • Price per rated watt. MODEL M1: $1,000 ÷ 100 W = $10.00 per W/ch. NAD C 3050: $1,899 ÷ 100 W = $18.99. Marantz MODEL 60n: $1,600 ÷ 60 W = $26.67. Audiolab 7000A: $1,999 ÷ 70 W = $28.56.
  • The rated watts were measured to different standards. Marantz rates the M1 at 0.05% THD across 20 Hz–20 kHz. Audiolab rates the 7000A at THD below 1%, a limit 1 ÷ 0.05 = 20 times looser. The M1's lead on paper is real.
  • Measured power. Hi-Fi News measured 113 W into both 8 Ω and 4 Ω for the M1, 120 W / 200 W for the NAD C 3050 LE, and 81 W / 128 W for the MODEL 60n, which beats its 60 W rating.
  • Difficult speakers. Into 2 Ω, Hi-Fi News measured the C 3050 LE's dynamic power at 252 W and the M1's at 82 W: 252 ÷ 82 ≈ 3.1 times as much. The M1 suits easy 8 Ω speakers. Low-impedance speakers are the NAD's job.
  • Power drawn while idling. Hi-Fi News measured 12 W at idle for the M1 and 44 W for the MODEL 60n. Over four hours of TV a day, that is (44 − 12) W × 4 h × 365 ÷ 1,000 = 46.7 kWh a year more for the 60n.

Why the TV's audio setting decides whether you hear anything

HDMI ARC and eARC carry sound from the TV back to the amplifier. A stereo amp with no Dolby decoder can only play 2-channel PCM, so if the TV sends Dolby, its digital audio output has to be switched to PCM. Cambridge Audio explains the same failure for its Evo One: when ARC is silent, the TV is usually sending Dolby, and Netflix or Prime Video may need their own audio setting changed too.

  • Marantz MODEL 60n: "Only the PCM 2-channel setting can be used to play sound from a TV via the HDMI ARC function." The manual adds: "Do not input non-PCM signals, such as Dolby Digital, DTS and AAC. This causes noise and could damage the speakers."
  • NAD C 3050: the owner's manual says to set eARC-connected devices "to PCM only". The amp can turn on and off with the TV through CEC Power.
  • Audiolab 7000A: Audiolab's specification says it does not decode Dolby Digital, Dolby Atmos or DTS multichannel soundtracks. The TV has to output PCM Stereo / PCM 2-channel, with ARC and CEC both enabled.
  • Marantz MODEL M1: Marantz lists decoding of Dolby Digital+, Dolby Digital, LPCM and DSD over its eARC input, so the TV's Dolby stream plays as sent. Marantz does not list DTS, so set the TV to PCM for DTS sources.
  • WiiM Amp Ultra: WiiM lists PCM and Dolby Digital up to 24-bit/192 kHz over ARC, but not DTS or Atmos. Set the TV to Stereo PCM or Dolby Digital. buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F995W2NG?tag=dealectric-20

Not recommended

The Marantz MODEL 60n, for a TV-first room. It costs $600 more than the MODEL M1 ($1,600 against $1,000) and is rated at 60 W/ch instead of 100 W/ch. Its ARC accepts only PCM 2-channel, and Marantz warns that a TV left on Dolby or DTS can send noise that could damage the speakers. It makes sense only if you want its MM phono stage and Class AB design and will keep the TV set to PCM.
