What the differences mean

  • Aim. The Satco Starfish tilts 0–80° and rotates 360°. Lumary's 4-inch 9W lists 40° of tilt, so the Satco reaches twice as far off vertical (80 ÷ 40 = 2). That is what you need for wall washing, artwork and sloped ceilings.

Certified figures vs maker claims

The ENERGY STAR Certified Downlights dataset lists 35 gimbal records. Only two are marked connected-capable, and both are Satco Starfish: the S11293R1 4-inch (ID 4515574, certified 2025-11-19) and the S11295R1 6-inch (ID 4515575). ENERGY STAR lists the 4-inch at 750 lm from 9 W with CRI 96 and a 90,000-hour rated life. It lists the 6-inch at 1,250 lm from 15 W with CRI 97. On its own product page Satco claims only CRI 90+, so the certified figure is 6 points higher than the claim. A brand search for Lumary or CloudyBay in the same dataset returns no records. Their 810 lm, 850 lm and CRI 90 figures are therefore the makers' own numbers, not independent ones.

How each one aims and connects. All of these gimbals are aimed by hand. Voice and app control covers brightness, color and scenes, not the beam direction.

  • Satco Starfish: 0–80° tilt, 360° rotation and a 36° beam. It joins 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi and works with Alexa, Google Home, Siri and SmartThings. Satco lists dimming as Starfish app only, so plan for a plain on/off switch, not a wall dimmer.
  • Lumary 4-inch 9W: 40° tilt and a 45° beam. It uses 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi plus Bluetooth and works with Alexa, Google Assistant and Siri. It is IC rated and damp-location rated, with a 25,000-hour rated life.
  • Lumary Ultra: Lumary's 2024 launch release lists 90° tilt and 270° rotation. It also gives the Ultra an independent control box that Lumary says boosts signal strength.
  • All Lumary Wi-Fi lights: Lumary's install guide says to pair them on 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi.

Before you buy the Satco S11293R1: Lighting New York shows a discontinued banner for it but also lists 100+ in stock, and Electrical Gopher lists it as in stock. Order from a dealer that shows stock.
