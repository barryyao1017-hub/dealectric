What the differences mean

The figures below come from each maker's rated power and phono sensitivity. Sensitivity is the cartridge voltage that drives the amp to full rated power with the volume all the way up. The example cartridge here puts out 0.25 mV, which is low-output moving-coil (MC) territory.

  • Rated output voltage (8 Ω): Marantz and Luxman √(100 W × 8 Ω) = 28.3 V; Yamaha √(90 × 8) = 26.8 V; Musical Fidelity A1 √(25 × 8) = 14.1 V.
  • MC gain to the speaker terminals: Marantz 20·log10(28.3 V / 0.25 mV) = 101.1 dB; Luxman 20·log10(28.3 / 0.30 mV) = 99.5 dB; Yamaha 20·log10(26.8 / 0.15 mV) = 105.1 dB; A1 20·log10(14.1 / 0.45 mV) = 89.9 dB.
  • Power from a 0.25 mV cartridge: rated power × (0.25 / sensitivity)², capped at rated power. Luxman 100 × (0.25 / 0.30)² = 69 W, only 1.6 dB short of full output. A1 25 × (0.25 / 0.45)² = 7.7 W, which is 11 dB less gain than the Marantz (101.1 − 89.9). The Yamaha's 0.15 mV sensitivity is lower than 0.25 mV, so it reaches its full 90 W with 20·log10(0.25 / 0.15) = 4.4 dB of volume to spare.
  • Moving-magnet (MM) cartridges: all four MM/MC amps reach full power from a 5 mV MM cartridge. Rated MM sensitivities are Marantz 2.3 mV, Luxman 2.5 mV, Yamaha 3.5 mV and A1 5 mV.

With an MM cartridge, all four MM/MC amps play to full power, and the Rega's phono stage is MM-only, so the choice comes down to power and price. With a low-output MC cartridge, the A1 runs out of gain long before the others. Between the other three, the MC load is what separates them.

How much power a moving-coil cartridge gets from each phono stage

An MC cartridge puts out about a tenth of the voltage of an MM cartridge, so the phono stage has to add roughly 20 dB more gain. Musical Fidelity's own A1 figures show the step: 40 dB of gain on MM and 60 dB on MC. The MC stage also has to load the cartridge at the right resistance, and that is where these amps differ most.

  • Marantz MODEL 30: a front-panel cartridge selector switches between MM and three MC loads. Marantz's manual says to use MC LOW (33 Ω) for cartridges under 50 Ω, MC MID (100 Ω) for cartridges of about 50 Ω, and MC HIGH (390 Ω) for cartridges of 100 Ω or more. Sound returns about 4 seconds after a change. The manual rates maximum phono input at 8 mV on MC and 80 mV on MM, which is 20·log10(8 / 0.25) = 30.1 dB of headroom above MC sensitivity and 20·log10(80 / 2.3) = 30.8 dB above MM sensitivity.
  • Luxman L-505Z: one MC setting, 0.3 mV at 100 Ω. Luxman rates phono S/N at 87 dB or more on MM and 70 dB or more on MC (IHF-A). Marantz lists 88 dB and 75 dB, measured at 5 mV and 0.5 mV inputs. Because the two makers use different measurement conditions, these noise numbers can't be ranked directly.
  • Yamaha A-S1200: Ed Kobesky at Positive Feedback found the MC input fixed at 50 Ω. He wrote that cartridges calling for at least 100 Ω 'sounded thick, dull, rolled off, and even slightly muffled' through it. MM use is unaffected.
  • Musical Fidelity A1: MC input at 450 µV with a 1 kΩ nominal load, which Musical Fidelity calls automatic input impedance matching.
  • Rega Elex Mk4: Rega lists a moving-magnet phono stage only, and suggests its separate Fono MC stage for moving-coil cartridges.

Not recommended

Musical Fidelity A1 with a low-output MC cartridge. It is labeled MM/MC, but its 450 µV MC sensitivity and 25 W rating mean a 0.25 mV cartridge gets about 7.7 W at full volume, 11 dB less gain than the Marantz MODEL 30. It suits MM and higher-output MC cartridges. Ed Selley at hi-fi+ also reported his review sample running at around sixty degrees after an hour and wrote that it will need its own shelf.
