What the differences mean

Every runtime on this page uses one sum: days = 0.85 × rated Wh ÷ (freezer Wh per day + idle W × 24). The freezer is a typical chest freezer using 1 kWh a day, and the 0.85 allows for conversion losses. The idle term is what the inverter draws just by being switched on. It runs for all 72 hours, because the freezer needs AC power even when its compressor is off.

  • Apex 200 + B300K: 0.85 × 5,324.8 = 4,526 Wh ÷ (1,000 + 12.8 × 24 = 1,307 Wh/day) = 3.5 days.
  • Elite 300: 0.85 × 3,014 = 2,562 Wh ÷ (1,000 + 15 × 24 = 1,360) = 1.9 days.
  • DELTA 3 Ultra: 0.85 × 3,072 = 2,611 Wh ÷ (1,000 + 22 × 24 = 1,528) = 1.7 days.
  • Explorer 5000 Plus: 0.85 × 5,040 = 4,284 Wh ÷ (1,000 + 58 × 24 = 2,392) = 1.8 days.
  • DELTA Pro 3: 0.85 × 4,096 = 3,482 Wh ÷ (1,000 + 960 to 1,200) = 1.6–1.8 days.
  • F3800 Plus: 0.85 × 3,840 = 3,264 Wh ÷ (1,000 + 112 × 24 = 3,688) = 0.9 day.
  • Idle over 72 h = idle W × 72. For example, 12.8 × 72 = 922 Wh, while 58 × 72 = 4,176 Wh, more than the freezer's own 3,000 Wh.
  • Solar to cover a full day = daily need ÷ 3 Wh per panel watt (4 peak sun-hours × 75% delivered). For the Apex 200 + B300K: 1,307 ÷ 3 = 436 W.
  • Cost per Wh for the Apex 200 + B300K: 2,199 ÷ 5,324.8 = 0.41 dollars per Wh.

Why idle draw, not battery size, decides a 3-day freezer outage

A chest freezer's compressor cycles on and off, so over a day it averages only about 42 W (1,000 Wh ÷ 24). The power station's inverter does not cycle. It stays on and draws a fixed amount the whole time. On stations built around 6,000–7,200 W inverters, that fixed draw is as big as the freezer's own use or bigger. The Solar Lab measured the Jackery Explorer 5000 Plus at about 58 W with the inverter on and nothing plugged in. That is 1,392 Wh a day, more than the freezer itself. The Solar Lab puts the EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 at 40–50 W. Zelpio measured the Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus losing 1,350 Wh in 12 hours with nothing plugged in, about 112 W.

The Solar Lab measured the BLUETTI Apex 200 at 12.8 W with the inverter on and no load, and at 90% efficiency. With one B300K attached it holds 5,324.8 Wh, so its idle draw costs only 307 Wh a day. That is why it clears 3 days while larger-inverter stations with similar capacity stop short of 2. The 12.8 W was measured on the Apex 200 alone. Even if the expansion battery raised total idle draw to 20 W, the sum gives 4,526 ÷ 1,480 = 3.1 days.

Does solar help? Yes. It turns a close call into a margin. At 4 peak sun-hours with 75% delivered, 400 W of panels gives about 1,200 Wh a day. That covers 92% of the Apex 200 + B300K's 1,307 Wh daily need, so a sunny day uses only about 107 Wh of the battery. The same 400 W covers only half of the Explorer 5000 Plus's 2,392 Wh need. The Apex 200 has two solar inputs (BLUETTI): a 60–280 V MC4 port for up to 2,000 W and an XT60 port for up to 1,000 W. A string of panels below 60 V can't use the MC4 port, so a pair of portable panels goes into the XT60 port.

Before an outage, check the setup. Keep the AC outlet switched on. Look in the app for any energy-saving or auto-off setting that could cut power while the compressor is off. Anker's F3800 user guide says the unit powers off only when its output buttons are off and no load has been detected for 12 hours.

Not recommended

Anker SOLIX F3800 Plus, for a freezer-only outage. At Zelpio's measured 112 W idle, a full unit empties itself in about 34 hours with nothing plugged in. With a 1 kWh/day freezer it lasts 0.9 day. Zelpio also measured its usable AC energy at 83% of the rated 3,840 Wh. Anker says its June 27, 2025 firmware update cuts idle consumption by 50%. If that brings idle to about 56 W, the sum gives 3,264 ÷ 2,344 = 1.4 days, still well short of 3. Anker's US store lists it as sold out.
