The arithmetic

Daily freezer energy from EPA's ENERGY STAR data (kWh a year ÷ 365): Arctic King 5.0 cu ft 537 Wh, Danby 10 cu ft 578 Wh, GE 15.7 cu ft 759 Wh, GE 21.7 cu ft 948 Wh; ENERGY STAR's average certified chest freezer is about 215 kWh a year, 590 Wh a day. Real homes run higher: a plug meter on a GE 15.7 cu ft read 125 W running at a 40 % duty cycle, about 1.2 kWh a day. Over 72 hours that is 1.65–2.85 kWh for the freezer (3.6 for the warm-garage case), plus the inverter's idle draw of 0.7–1.4 kWh, divided by about 90 % inverter efficiency. The Elite 300 delivered 2.67 kWh at its AC outlets before empty in StorageReview's test, which is why it is a two-day unit at 0.6 kWh a day and a three-day unit only for a small freezer in a cool room. Start surge is 500–1,200 W by the usual 3–5× rule (no measured figure found); every station here is rated 1,800 W or more continuous.

Solar, honestly

In full sun a 400 W folding panel delivers 260–320 W (ALLPOWERS' SP037 owners' figures), roughly 1.3–1.6 kWh over a day, which replaces a freezer day; a 200 W panel about half that. Under storm cloud panels give 10–25 % of rated (a Voltaic test saw 15 % "behind clouds" and nothing in heavy rain), so 400 W yields 0.2–0.5 kWh, a fifth to a half of a freezer day. Check the input window: the AC180 caps at 500 W, the Elite 200 V2 at 1,000 W, the Elite 300 and AC200L at 1,200 W. Car charging at about 100 W adds a tenth of a freezer day per hour of driving. BLUETTI bundles the Elite 300 with a 350 W panel for $1,499 and with two 200 W panels for $1,699 (2026-11-03).

Set it up before the storm

Leave the freezer plugged into the station and the station into the wall: the UPS switchover (8–9 ms measured on the Elite 300; 15–20 ms specified on the others) is far faster than a compressor notices, so it takes over with nobody home. Disable ECO mode (default on for the Elite 300; an AC180P owner's fridge went dark after two hours of the compressor resting) and keep the lid shut: a full freezer holds about 48 hours closed, a half-full one about 24 (USDA guidance), which stretches any of these stations past their battery alone. A 3,500 W dual-fuel generator ($540–630 undated) needs fuel, 20 feet from windows and a carbon-monoxide alarm, and it is what covers a week.
