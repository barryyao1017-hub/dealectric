What the differences mean

  • Reach: AV Access rates its kit at 197 ft on Cat5e/6 at 1080p, and plain HDMI at 49 ft: 197 ÷ 49 = 4 times the run. On Cat6a the kit reaches 230 ft.
  • Where the projector hangs: a 150-inch 16:10 screen is 150 × 16 ÷ 18.87 = 127.2 in (10.6 ft) wide. The PT-VMZ62's 1.09–1.77:1 lens puts it 1.09 × 10.6 = 11.6 ft to 1.77 × 10.6 = 18.8 ft from the screen. The Epson L690U's 1.35–2.20:1 lens puts it 14.3 to 23.3 ft back.
  • Power at the mount: Panasonic rates the PT-VMZ62 at 320 W (3.3 A) maximum. The L690U's 415 W ÷ 120 V = 3.5 A. Either one fits on an ordinary 15 A outlet circuit.
  • Weight on the ceiling: 15.2 lb for the PT-VMZ62 (Panasonic) against 19.8 lb for the L690U (Epson), 4.6 lb less.

Getting the picture from the booth to the ceiling

Measure the cable route, not the straight line. That means up the booth wall, across the ceiling and down to the mount. AV Access's own table gives a passive HDMI cable 49 ft at 1080p, 33 ft at 4K30 and 10–16 ft at 4K60. A booth at the back of a sanctuary is usually past 49 ft of route, so the signal has to go over HDBaseT on a Cat cable. The 4KEX70-H2 reaches 197 ft on Cat5e/6 and 230 ft on Cat6a at 1080p@60. AV Access lists 1920×1200@60, the PT-VMZ62's native resolution, among its supported modes. Its distance table names 1080p only, so treat 197 ft as the limit at WUXGA too. If you feed it a 4K source, the reach falls to 115 ft on Cat5e/6.

Wire it the way AV Access specifies:

  • Run one straight-through T568B cable directly from transmitter to receiver. AV Access says the kit won't work through an Ethernet switch, so the church network can't carry it.
  • Keep each patch cord at 5 m or less.
  • One transmitter pairs with only one receiver.
  • Plug the receiver into the projector's HDMI input. AV Access doesn't claim its transmitter works with a projector's built-in HDBaseT or DIGITAL LINK port.

One 18 V adapter at either end powers both boxes (12.8 W for the pair). Put it at the booth, and the ceiling only needs the projector's own outlet.

If you choose the Epson L690U instead, its built-in HDBaseT port takes Epson's ELPHD02 transmitter directly, with no receiver box. Epson's guide calls for Category 5e STP or better and up to 100 m (328 ft). It also warns that turning on HDBaseT Control Communications switches off the projector's own LAN and RS-232C ports.
