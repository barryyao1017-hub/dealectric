Price per Wh: what the S2000 trades for its capacity

Anker's US store lists the S2000 at $699.99 (original price $999.99) and the C1000 Gen 2 at $699.99. That is the same money for 2,010 Wh against 1,024 Wh. Notebookcheck (Marcus Schwarten, EU 230 V unit) drew just over 1,900 Wh from the S2000 at about 250 W. That is roughly 95% of the label, so the extra capacity holds up under load. The S2000 is also rated for 6,000 cycles to 80%, against 4,000 to 80% for the C1000 Gen 2.

What the S2000 gives up for camping:

  • No 12 V car socket. Anker's own comparison table marks the S2000's 12 V DC port as "No". A 12 V compressor fridge needs its own AC adapter to run from the S2000. The C1000 Gen 2 has a 12 V 10 A, 120 W outlet.
  • Slow solar charging. Notebookcheck's datasheet lists 400 W solar input, so a full refill takes at least 5.0 h of full sun.
  • Less output. It is rated at 1,500 W against the C1000 Gen 2's 2,000 W.
  • No expansion. Anker lists the battery as not expandable.
  • Weight. At 35.7 lb it is 10.8 lb heavier than the C1000 Gen 2.

Idle draw matters if you leave the inverter on overnight. Notebookcheck measured the S2000 losing just over 10% of its charge per 24 h with AC on and nothing plugged in, about 8 W. The Solar Lab measured about 13.8 W on the C1000 Gen 2, which is 138 Wh over a 10-hour night. These are different labs, and the S2000 figure comes from the EU 230 V unit. $699.99: https://dealectric.com/go/anker-solix-s2000

If you need to carry the station from the car to the site, the Elite 100 V2 is the better buy per Wh in the 1 kWh class. It costs $499, $200.99 less than the C1000 Gen 2 at the two makers' stores, takes 400 W more solar input, and BLUETTI rates it for 4,000+ cycles to 80%. $499 on bluettipower.com, $474.05 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-100-v2

Not recommended: Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 for long-term solar or car recharging

Jackery's own help center rates the Explorer 1000 v2 for 4,000 cycles to at least 70% capacity, not 80%. Its allowed fade is 30% ÷ 4,000 = 0.0075% per cycle, against 20% ÷ 4,000 = 0.005% for the Anker, BLUETTI and EcoFlow units. Its car input is limited to 12–16 V at 8 A (12 V × 8 A = 96 W), and Jackery gives around 12 h for a full charge from the car socket. Its 400 W solar ceiling means at least 2.7 h to refill. In its favour, OutdoorGearLab measured 910 Wh usable and said its fans stayed nearly silent even near maximum output.
