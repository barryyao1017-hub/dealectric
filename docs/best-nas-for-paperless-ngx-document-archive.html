What a Paperless-ngx NAS costs to run, year after year

A document archive sits idle almost all day. Paperless-ngx runs OCR only when a scan lands in the consume folder, so the box's resting draw sets the electricity bill, not its peak. Over a 5 to 10 year archive, that resting draw can add up to a sizeable share of what you paid for the NAS.

The makers test differently, so treat cross-brand gaps as rough. UGREEN's lab used Seagate 2 TB drives. QNAP tests with every bay filled, which is the harder test. Synology and Asustor publish an access figure and a hibernation figure from their spec sheets. Each model's own figure is solid; the gaps between brands are approximate.

Measured at the wall by named reviewers:

  • igor'sLAB: DXP2800 at 5.6 W in standby and 14.5 W during data transfer, with one NVMe SSD and no hard drives fitted (5.6 W × 8.76 ≈ 49 kWh a year before HDDs).
  • Dong Knows Tech: DS225+ at about 275 Wh per 24 hours with two Synology 4 TB HDDs (275 ÷ 24 ≈ 11.5 W average, ≈ 100 kWh a year).
  • KitGuru: DXP4800 Plus at 24.25 W idle on 2.5GbE and 30.45 W on 10GbE, with WD Red 6 TB drives (24.25 × 8.76 ≈ 212 kWh a year).

Letting the drives sleep is where the savings are. The asleep figures above are about a third to two thirds of the active ones. Hard drives can only spin down when nothing writes to them. Putting PostgreSQL and the app data on an SSD leaves the HDD mirror holding just the originals and archived PDFs. On the DXP2800 the two M.2 slots take NVMe for cache or extra storage (The Gadgeteer). On the Synology DS725+ and DS925+, Synology's spec pages say creating an M.2 storage pool requires Synology-verified SSDs, so budget for one.

Not recommended

Synology DS225+ for the SSD-plus-mirror layout. Synology's spec page lists 2 GB of RAM installed, a 6 GB maximum, and no M.2 slot. An SSD for the database would take one of its two bays, which leaves your scanned originals with no RAID 1 mirror. It is cheap to run, at $27.01 a year with drives active. It works if you keep Postgres on the mirrored HDDs, but for the layout above, the DXP2800 does the job and draws less power with drives active.
