How much solar each station can take

A kit's panels are where you start, not the most the station can take. The station's solar charge controller caps three things: total watts, the voltage window (which limits how many panels you can wire in series) and the current (which limits panels wired in parallel). The ALLPOWERS R3500 takes up to 2,000 W on one XT60 input at 12–150 V and 40 A. The 400 W panel in the kit uses a fifth of that, and the wide voltage window leaves room for series strings. Check each added panel's open-circuit voltage against 150 V, with a margin for cold mornings, when panel voltage rises. The R2500 V2 lists a 1,000 W solar maximum.

BLUETTI's Elite 200 V2 and Elite 400 take 12–60 V at 20 A, up to 1,000 W. StorageReview lists the Elite 300 at 1,200 W, 12–60 V and 22 A. A 60 V window allows only short series strings. StormWatts works out that the Elite 200 V2's two 200 W panels fit in series (about 49 V) but go over the 20 A input limit in parallel. StormWatts also lists the inputs of the other common 2 kWh kits: Jackery Explorer 2000 v2, 400 W combined at 16–60 V; EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max, 500 W at 11–60 V and 13 A; Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2, 800 W at 11–60 V and 17 A. On the Jackery, the two 200 W panels in its kit already use the whole input.

Two kits are left out of the table because their stores didn't show a buyable kit price on 2026-11-17. EcoFlow's store showed the DELTA 3 Max Solar Generator from $1,229, but the page didn't say which panels that price includes. Anker's C2000 Gen 2 + 400 W kit page read Sold Out, with shipping to begin in early December.
