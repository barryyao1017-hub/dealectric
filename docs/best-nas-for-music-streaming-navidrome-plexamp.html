The apps

  • Navidrome: free, a single Docker container (navidrome/navidrome, port 4533, amd64 and arm64), per-user play counts and playlists, on-the-fly transcoding to Opus per user or player, Last.fm and ListenBrainz scrobbling, Jukebox mode to a DAC on the server, and 100-plus Subsonic clients: Symfonium and DSub on Android, play:Sub and Amperfy on iPhone and Mac, plus CarPlay and Android Auto clients.
  • Plexamp: the Plex music app against Plex Media Server; loudness levelling and CarPlay in the free tier per Plex's blog, offline downloads with a Plex Pass, Sonos casting since 3.2; Sonic Analysis (the mixes and "sonically similar" features) is CPU-heavy, x86-only per forum reports, and one guess puts 25,000 tracks at days on an N100-class chip.
  • Jellyfin: free; one folder per album, tags from the files, .lrc lyrics; enable "always remux FLAC" if embedded WebP art breaks browser playback.
  • Roon: Roon Server is x86 only (no ARM), 4 GB minimum and 8 GB recommended, SSD for the database, Docker on the NAS; light use runs on Atom or Celeron chips, DSP and multi-zone want a Core i3/i5; about $12–15 a month or $830 lifetime, or a Nucleus One with five years for $949.99.
  • Lyrion (LMS): free, with Docker guides for Synology, QNAP and OpenMediaVault.

UGOS's own Music app

UGREEN ships a Music app in UGOS that plays a collection from tags, with lyrics from a same-named file, and the UGREEN NAS iPhone app supports CarPlay. Transcoding and the DH-series feature set are not documented. For a FLAC library the Subsonic ecosystem (Navidrome plus a client of your choice) is the richer route, and UGREEN's own blog walks through Navidrome on UGOS Pro.

Sizing

CD-quality FLAC runs about 30–40 MB a track, so 10,000 tracks is roughly 350 GB; 24-bit hi-res is two to three times that. Two 4 TB drives in RAID 1 hold a large library several times over; put Navidrome's or Plex's database on an M.2 SSD and let the drives spin down.
