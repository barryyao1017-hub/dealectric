What the differences mean

Every figure in the table is one line of arithmetic. It uses Anker SOLIX's planning factors (0.9 system efficiency, 0.85 panel derate) and SolutionEnergy's weekend-cabin sizing: about 1 kWh a day for lights, phones and a small fridge, and 2.5–3 kWh of storage for two days.

  • Usable battery: rated Wh × 0.9. R3500: 3,168 × 0.9 = 2,851 Wh. That is within SolutionEnergy's 2.5–3 kWh two-day figure, so the weekend doesn't depend on the panel. Elite 200 V2: 2,073.6 × 0.9 = 1,866 Wh, which is below it.
  • Sunny day from the panel: panel W × 5 sun-hours × 0.85. 400 W × 5 × 0.85 = 1,700 Wh; a 200 W panel gives 850 Wh.
  • Poor-sun day: panel kW × 2 kWh per kW (SolutionEnergy's December example rate). 0.4 × 2 = 800 Wh; 200 W gives 400 Wh.
  • Weekend energy, poor sun: usable battery + 2 poor-sun days. R2500 V2: 1,728 + 1,600 = 3,328 Wh.
  • Cost per usable Wh: kit price ÷ usable Wh. R3500 kit: $1,498 ÷ 2,851 = $0.53.
  • Inverter left on for the fridge: The Solar Lab measured 9.5 W idle on the Elite 200 V2. Over 44 h (Friday evening to Sunday afternoon) that is 9.5 × 44 = 418 Wh, about a fifth of its battery. Budget a line like this whatever station you choose.

How many days each kit's panel needs to refill its battery

Refill days = battery Wh ÷ what the panel makes in a day. This decides how much of the weekend the battery has to carry by itself. One 200 W panel on the 2,073.6 Wh Elite 200 V2 needs 2,073.6 ÷ 400 = 5.2 poor-sun days, so over a cloudy weekend the battery does nearly all the work. Doubling the panel to 400 W halves that to 2.6 days. On the 3,168 Wh R3500 a single 400 W panel needs 4.0 poor-sun days. That matters less there, because the battery alone already covers SolutionEnergy's two-day figure. Measured figures behind the factors: APH Networks drew 2,028 Wh from the Elite 200 V2 at a steady 987 W, 97.8% of its rating. The 0.9 factor is therefore cautious at heavy loads, but a light fridge load with the inverter's idle draw on top lands lower. Battery Skills (Bernard Ryan) saw a 780 W peak from two 400 W panels in series on the R3500. Its 2,000 W solar input has room for a second panel.

Not recommended

BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 + 350 W, $1,449. It costs $50 less than the 2× 200 W kit for 50 W less panel, and needs 3.0 poor-sun days to refill against 2.6. Stepping up to two 350 W panels (the $2,148 bundle) doesn't scale cleanly either. Each panel is 33 V open-circuit, so two in series reach 66 V, over the Elite 200 V2's 60 V input limit. In parallel, the 20 A input limit holds them under 20 A × 33 V = 660 W, below their 700 W nameplate. BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 + 200 W, $1,199: 5.2 poor-sun days to refill leaves the battery covering a cloudy weekend almost alone. A fridge weekend needs 400 W of panels on a 2 kWh station.
