What to know: ENERGY STAR data puts a 3–4.4 cu ft compact fridge at 220–270 kWh a year, 600–740 Wh a day (a 17 cu ft full-size fridge is 1.2–1.3 kWh a day), so the fridge is most of the load; lights at 4–6 × 9 W for five hours are about 225 Wh, phones and a laptop about 135 Wh, and the fan decides the rest: a 75 W box fan for eight hours is 600 Wh, a 12 V fan 120 Wh. Call it 1.1–1.6 kWh a day, 2.8–4.0 kWh for a two-night weekend, or 1.2–2.4 kWh without the fridge. NREL's PVWatts gives peak-sun hours of 5.5–6.9 in June–July (Denver 6.9, Asheville 6.0, Minneapolis 6.0, Seattle 5.5) and 1.5–4.3 in December (Seattle 1.5, Minneapolis 2.6, Denver 4.3) for a tilted panel aimed south, and folding panels deliver about 65–80 % of their rating, so a 200 W panel returns about 770 Wh on a summer day and 210–360 Wh in a northern December, a 350–400 W panel about 1.3–1.5 kWh and 400–730 Wh. The load sheet

Fridge: ENERGY STAR-certified compact units of 3.0–4.4 cu ft are rated 220–270 kWh a year (Arctic King 3.3 cu ft 220, 4.4 cu ft 226; Avallon 3.0 cu ft 230; Arctic King 3.1 cu ft 270), 600–740 Wh a day in the lab, more in a hot cabin or after a week switched off while it pulls the box down; a 17 cu ft bottom-freezer is 450–460 kWh a year, 1.2–1.3 kWh a day. Lights: 4–6 LED bulbs at 9 W for five hours, 180–270 Wh. Phones 10–30 Wh each, a laptop 50–100 Wh a day. Fan: the swing item, 400–800 Wh for a 75–100 W box fan over eight hours against 80–160 Wh for a 12 V fan. Station idle with the inverter on adds 10–20 W around the clock (the Elite 30 V2 quotes 4.5 W standby); running lights and fans from the DC ports avoids it. Figures other than the ENERGY STAR and BLUETTI ones are planning assumptions.

Sun, by season and region

PVWatts (NREL, fixed panel tilted 30° facing south, 14 % losses) gives daily peak-sun hours of: Denver 4.3 January, 6.9 June, 4.3 December, 5.9 annual; Asheville 4.1, 6.0, 3.8, 5.1; Minneapolis 3.2, 6.0, 2.6, 4.7; Seattle 1.6, 5.5, 1.5, 3.9. A folding panel laid flat or shaded by trees does worse. At 70 % of rating, a 200 W panel yields about 770 Wh on a 5.5-hour day and 210–360 Wh on a northern December day; a 350–400 W panel about 1.3–1.5 kWh and 400–730 Wh. That is why the summer weekend balances with the Elite 200 V2 and 350 W of panel, and the winter weekend is battery-only.

Between visits

Leaving the fridge running means the station lives as a small solar system for the week, cycling every day; neither BLUETTI nor ALLPOWERS publishes guidance on continuous solar cycling for these units, so the safer habit is to empty the fridge, switch it off and let the station top up unloaded, or bring a cooler. A cabin used every weekend eventually wants a proper install (two or more fixed panels, a LiFePO4 bank, charge controller and inverter), which this page does not price. Check the kits' cables: ALLPOWERS kits ship a PV cable to XT60 with adapters; BLUETTI's in-the-box lists did not show an MC4 lead.
