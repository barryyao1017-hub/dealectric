Which solar skimmers can fall back on a plug, and which can't

Every skimmer here charges from the panel on its top while it floats, so none needs an outlet on a sunny day. The Aiper EcoSurfer S2 drives itself into sunlight for a 15-minute top-up when its battery runs low (Aiper). PCWorld ran it 24 hours nonstop and it lost about 25% of its battery. Through weeks of rain and overcast weather it never stopped on a 10-hour-a-day schedule. Sun runs short in the colder months: Betta's support says that in fall and winter 'even bright days may not provide enough solar energy because of the lower sun angle'. That is when a plug-in backup matters, and the four makers handle it differently:

  • Aiper EcoSurfer S2: Aiper lists '1 × DC Charger' in the box.
  • Betta SE Plus: Betta sells it as the SE with 'dual charging options, solar power and AC adapter', recharging in 3.5 h on the adapter.
  • Betta SE: solar only. Betta's replacement charging adapter is listed for the SE Plus and Neo, 'Not compatible with other Betta models', so the SE can't be given a plug-in backup later.
  • Beatbot iSkim: the 65 W adapter is $25 when added with the robot and $59.99 bought on its own (Beatbot).
  • WYBOT F1: the F1 charger is a separate $19.99 item (WYBOT).

Not recommended

Don't buy the WYBOT F1 if your goal is never plugging in. PCWorld's Christopher Null was 'never able to get the robot to run for a full day without its battery dying, even under the summer sun of Texas'. In standard mode it often didn't run during the day because it had never fully recharged. In the same review, its app showed 98% battery when the true level was 13%. It is a strong leaf catcher, though: PCWorld measured about 85% of test leaves over about 4 hours. If you plan to buy its $19.99 charger and top it up by hand, it is the lowest-cost option.
