What the differences mean

  • The Browning starts video 0.40 s sooner. Trailcampro: 0.70 s − 0.30 s = 0.40 s.
  • The Bushnell is 8.75 times slower to start video than to take a photo. 0.70 s ÷ 0.08 s = 8.75. Its 0.08 s photo trigger is the fastest here.
  • The Browning is ready for its next photo about 3 times sooner. Picture recovery 1.9 s ÷ 0.6 s ≈ 3.2 (Trailcampro).
  • The Browning watches a wider area, farther out. 46.8° − 33.6° = 13.2° wider detection angle, and 90 ft − 70 ft = 20 ft more range (Trailcampro).

Advertised trigger speeds are photo-mode numbers

Browning advertises a 0.22-second trigger and battery life of up to a year on 6 AA. Bushnell lists 0.2 s for the Core S-4K. Trailcampro times each camera separately in photo and video mode on its own test equipment, and in video every camera here starts later:

Trailcampro's battery estimates assume 35 day and 35 night photos every 24 hours, or 15 day and 15 night 10-second clips, on lithium AAs for the two AA cameras. Trailcampro's Tactacam photo estimate assumes 15 day and 15 night photos, so it isn't directly comparable. On those figures, Browning's year-long claim holds for photos but not for video. Trailcampro also scores the Browning 85/100 overall and the Bushnell 81/100. The labs disagree on the Bushnell's range: TechGearLab's day-and-night walk test gave consistent results out to 100 ft, while Trailcampro measured 70 ft. The methods differ, so we show both figures.

None of these cameras actually sees motion. A PIR sensor reacts to a warm body crossing its zones against a cooler background. GardePro puts a mammal at about 98°F, so on a 90°F day there is only about 8°F of contrast (98 − 90) and the camera can miss animals in range. GardePro also says the sensor reacts best to sideways movement and fires later when an animal walks straight at the lens. Mount the camera at about 45° to the trail and raise the sensitivity in hot weather.

Not recommended

GardePro A3S for video of moving animals. GardePro advertises a 0.1-second trigger. TechGearLab rated its trigger below average, found that video often started with the animal already in the second half of the frame, and says the advertised 0.5 s recovery left gaps, often only 1–2 images per animal. TechGearLab also says the A3S's megapixel rating comes from software upscaling. At $71.99 it is cheap, but for motion-triggered video the Browning's measured 0.3 s start is worth the extra cost.
