What the differences mean

  • Cards behind the phone: a standard bank card is 0.76 mm thick (ISO/IEC 7810). So 3 cards = 3 × 0.76 = 2.28 mm, and 4 cards = 4 × 0.76 = 3.04 mm. Apple's limit for a whole MagSafe case is 2.1 mm, as MacRumors reported from Apple's 2020 accessory guidelines. Three cards alone exceed it by 2.28 − 2.1 = 0.18 mm, before the case's own back is counted.
  • Nomad per card: $74 / 3 slots = $24.67 for each card that can stay in while the phone sits on a MagSafe charger. That $74 is a 25%-off sale; the list price is $99.
  • Folio vs case plus MagSafe wallet: Gripmunk with MagSafe $19.99 + ESR Geo Wallet Boost $36.99 = $56.98. The folio costs $74 − $56.98 = $17.02 more, and you don't have to pull a wallet off every time you charge.
  • Folio vs Wallet Slayer Vol. 1: $74 − $29.99 = $44.01 more to keep MagSafe charging with the cards in.
  • What cable-only charging costs in speed: ChargerLAB measured the iPhone 17 peaking at 29 W on Apple's MagSafe Charger and 27–28 W over USB-C. That is 29 − 28 = 1 W to 29 − 27 = 2 W of peak given up.

Why where the cards sit decides MagSafe charging

Apple's MagSafe Charger page says: "Don't place credit cards, security badges, passports, or key fobs between your iPhone and MagSafe Charger," because this might damage magnetic strips or RFID chips. For cases that hold cards, Apple says to remove them before charging or make sure they aren't between the back of your device and the charger. A case's "MagSafe" label doesn't settle it. What settles it is whether the cards sit on the charger's side of the phone.

Folio, cards on the screen side. Nomads Nation's review (Aaron Radcliffe) describes the folio's "wallet storage layout built directly into the leather flap". Nomad's own photo shows a MagSafe charger attached to the back of the closed folio, and Nomad lists it as "MagSafe & Qi2 charging compatible" with 8 ft drop protection. Nomad doesn't say in words that charging works with the cards loaded. The design meets Apple's condition because the cards fold over the screen, not onto the coil. Nomads Nation does note that "the magnetic connection is slightly weaker due to the leather layers". Nomad sells a separate folio for each iPhone model, so choose the iPhone 17 one: Nomad Modern Leather Folio for iPhone 17.

Case plus snap-on wallet, cards on the coil. Smartish says the magnets in its Gripmunk with MagSafe line "stay fully wireless-charging compatible" ($19.99: https://dealectric.com/go/smartish-gripmunk-magsafe-iphone-17). The ESR Geo Wallet Boost holds 4 cards and 3 bills with 1,800 g of magnetic hold, but it sits exactly where the charger goes, so it comes off before charging ($36.99: https://dealectric.com/go/esr-geo-wallet-boost-halolock).

Rear-slot cases, cards in the path. Smartish describes the Wallet Slayer Vol. 1 line's slot as holding 1 to 4 cards and states "Cable charging only, not MagSafe compatible" ($29.99: https://dealectric.com/go/smartish-wallet-slayer-vol-1-iphone-17). Thickness behind the phone costs watts even where charging still runs. Maplee-Tech, an accessory seller citing unnamed engineers, measured an iPhone 17 at 30.14 W wireless with no case and 21.98 W through a 3.05 mm case: 21.98 / 30.14 = 0.73, about 27% less. 3.05 mm is roughly the thickness of four cards.

Not recommended

Scooch Wingmate, if you want MagSafe charging. Scooch sells a MagSafe ring for it, but its own help page says "Wingmate cannot charge wirelessly or with MagSafe while the case is on" and "The ring does not enable wireless charging through Wingmate." Its back compartment holds 3 cards plus cash, or up to 4 slim cards, and it is drop-tested to 10 ft, which makes it fine if you charge by cable. Check Scooch's model selector for an iPhone 17 version before ordering.
