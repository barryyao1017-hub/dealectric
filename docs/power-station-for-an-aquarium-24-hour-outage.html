The budget, line by line

Heater: 300 W × 30 % × 24 h = 2.16 kWh in a cool room; about 1.1 kWh at 15 % after a lowered setpoint and insulation (owner data, not a lab figure: BLUETTI's own aquarium example assumes a 200 W heater at 40 %, 80 W average). Filter: Fluval 407 23 W (0.55 kWh), FX6 43 W (1.0 kWh). Air pump: a few watts (0.1 kWh). Station idle with the inverter on: 15–19 W (0.35–0.45 kWh), unless the air pump runs from the 12 V port with AC off. Lights: skip them in an outage. Usable energy is about 85 % of rated (the Elite 200 V2's 1,910 Wh is measured; the others are estimates). That gives 3.1–3.4 kWh for the cold-room case, 2.0–2.3 kWh for the managed case, and about 1 kWh with no heater, which is why the Elite 300 and R4000 are the only "with heat" picks and only with the setpoint managed.

Keeping the fish alive with less

Fishkeeping guidance agrees on the order: aeration and circulation first (oxygen falls within hours in a stocked tank), heat second (a slow drop of a few degrees over a day is survivable for most tropicals; wrap the tank in blankets kept off the water and keep the lid on), filtration third (bacteria survive a day; keep the media wet), lights last. Run the heater in bursts rather than continuously, drop the setpoint 2–4 °F, and keep a battery air pump that switches on by itself (Aquarium Co-Op's lithium pump, up to 40 hours in power-save mode; Penn-Plax Silent Air B-11, 8–12 hours on D cells; Bulk Reef Supply's Maxum $109.99). Unplug the heater if the water level drops.

Set it up as a UPS

Plug the tank's power strip into the station and the station into the wall so it takes over in 10–20 ms with nobody home. Turn ECO mode off: the heater cycles to 0 W and the station will otherwise shut its outlets after a few hours of low load (AC180 owners have reported outlets cutting out; BLUETTI support treated one as a defect). Anker's own guidance says not to put a heater on a computer UPS unless it is rated for it; an APC 1500 VA unit gives minutes, not a day.
