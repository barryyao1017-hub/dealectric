What the differences mean

Each coverage figure below treats the maker's lens angles as horizontal and ignores pillars and bodywork, so it is a best case. No maker here says whether its angles are horizontal or diagonal.

  • Botslab G980H 4-camera, side cameras facing out: 170 + 120 + 120 + 150 = 560°, 200° more than a full circle. Measured from the nose, the front lens reaches 85° each way (170 ÷ 2), a side lens aimed at 90° covers 30° to 150° (90 ± 60), and the rear lens starts at 105° (180 − 150 ÷ 2). That gives a 55° and a 45° overlap on each side and no gap.
  • The same camera with the side cameras turned to the cabin: 170 + 150 = 320° outside; 360 − 320 = 40° unseen, 20° on each side between 85° and 105° from the nose, beside the front doors.
  • Vantrue E360 and E360 ACE: 210 + 210 = 420° in one stitched frame, a 60° overlap. Detail: 5,184 px ÷ 360° = 14.4 px per degree across the panorama.
  • Botslab detail per direction: front 2,880 px ÷ 170° = 16.9 px per degree; each side 1,920 ÷ 120 = 16.0; rear 1,920 ÷ 150 = 12.8. So the four-camera kit has about 17% more detail ahead than the E360 panorama (16.9 ÷ 14.4 = 1.17) and slightly less behind.
  • REDTIGER F17 Plus: 170 + 140 = 310° outside; 25° unseen beside each front door. Front detail: 2,560 ÷ 170 = 15.1 px per degree.
  • REDTIGER F17 3-channel: 150 + 155 = 305° outside; 27.5° unseen on each side.
  • Hours per card: DashCamTalk's EricSan logged about 1.05 GB per 3 minutes from the E360 ACE's three cameras. That is 1.05 × 20 = 21 GB an hour, about 6 hours on a 128 GB card (128 ÷ 21 = 6.1). DashCamTalk lists the F17 Plus at 16 + 10 + 10 + 10 = 46 Mbps, which is 46 × 3,600 ÷ 8 ÷ 1,000 = 20.7 GB an hour, about 6.2 hours on its included 128 GB card.
  • Parking drain: EricSan measured the E360 ACE at 4.68 W in low-bitrate parking with all three cameras on. That is 4.68 × 24 = 112 Wh a day, about 9.4 Ah from a 12 V battery.

Side cameras out or in: where the 40° goes

Botslab's spec page gives the G980H 4-camera's lenses as 170° front, 120° left, 120° right and 150° rear. With all four on, it records 3K front and 1080p on the other three. The side cameras sit on magnetic mounts on the windshield unit, and Botslab's listing says each swivels 90° to face either the cabin or the side windows. Each side camera faces one way at a time. Facing out, they close the circle. Facing in, they film the seats, and the arc beside each front door goes unrecorded. For parking-lot damage, aim them out. Botslab's feature text also quotes 4K. That is the front resolution only after the side cameras come off (Botslab lists 4K + two 1080p and 4K + one 1080p modes).

The Vantrue E360 gets to 360° a different way. Its two 210° fisheye lenses are stitched into one 5184 × 1944 frame (Notebookcheck's spec table), so nothing around the car is left out, but the detail is spread across the whole circle. Notebookcheck's Stephen Pereyra called its night vision and stitching excellent, and listed frequent app crashes and tedious file management as drawbacks. Not recommended

REDTIGER sells its F17 3-channel as ‘360° full-protection’, but that figure is its front (150°), cabin (160°) and rear (155°) lenses added together: 150 + 160 + 155 = 465°. None of the three faces the sides, so 27.5° beside each front door goes unrecorded outside the car. REDTIGER's listing also says the front records 4K only with the cabin camera turned off.
