Two inputs, and why it matters

Portable 400–500 W panels run at 40–50 V open-circuit, which is near the 60 V ceiling of these XT60 inputs, so two panels can't go in series into one port. A unit with one input tops out at what a single panel gives (400–500 W) unless you wire two in parallel with a Y-branch and stay inside the port's current limit; the DELTA 3 Plus avoids that by taking each panel on its own MPPT input. The Elite 100 V2 and Dabbsson take 1,000 W or more on one port because they accept 20–25 A, so two panels in parallel work there.

What 1,000 W of panels really gives

Panels rarely deliver their rating: one owner measured 380–395 W at noon from a 400 W panel on a DELTA 3, 250–320 W under light tree cover, and just under 3.5 hours to fill from zero. The Solar Lab's two 415 W bifacial panels came close to 1,000 W combined on a DELTA 3 Plus without a logged refill time. Plan on 70–85 % of panel rating in good sun and much less in winter or cloud, so 1,024 Wh takes roughly 1.3–1.5 hours on a perfect day and most of a day otherwise.

Panel sizing per port

Each DELTA 3 Plus port takes up to 500 W and 15 A at 11–60 V: EcoFlow's own 400 W portable panel (about 48 V open-circuit) fits one per port, and a 500 W panel or two smaller panels in parallel reach the cap. Check a panel's open-circuit voltage on a cold morning, which rises above its label, against the 60 V limit before buying.
