What the differences mean

  • Interface gain over XLR. Every dB of lower sensitivity is a dB more your preamp has to add. PD400X: −51 − (−55) = 4 dB less than the MV7+. PD200XS: −52 − (−55) = 3 dB less. PodMic USB and Q9U: −57 − (−55) = 2 dB more. Maono lists its figures as "dBV" without "/Pa", so treat them as Maono states them.
  • Output voltage. 1000 × 10^(−55/20) = 1.78 mV/Pa for the MV7+, and 1000 × 10^(−57/20) = 1.41 mV/Pa for the Q9U. Both are low-output dynamics, so a budget interface will be working hard either way.
  • Price gap. MV7+ $255 − PD400X $154.99 = $100.01. PodMic USB $209 − PD400X $154.99 = $54.01. Q9U $149.99 is $5 below the PD400X, which rates 6 dB hotter on XLR (−51 vs −57).

What each mic keeps when you plug in XLR

Shure MV7+. Shure's user guide calls the XLR jack a "Passive XLR output" and says: "XLR audio is not affected by software settings." Over XLR you get the raw dynamic capsule with none of the Auto Level, denoiser or EQ that justify the price. Thomann's spec list says "Both outputs are active simultaneously", so you can send a processed USB feed to a stream and a clean XLR feed to a recorder. Sarah Buchynski (The Podcast Host) had to turn an SSL2+ to almost 80% gain over XLR and heard distortion from the interface. Over USB, Auto Level landed her recording at −24 LUFS with −4 dBFS true peak, without any manual setup.

RØDE PodMic USB. Bandrew Scott (Podcastage): "the XLR port is inactive when you have the mic connected over USB", so you can't run both outputs at once. Michael Schäfer (ComputerBase, 2023-07-25) found that over XLR the audio interface has to handle monitoring, and a Behringer UMC204HD needed its input fully up, which audibly raised the noise. He heard no tonal difference between USB and XLR. RØDE ties the APHEX processing and the Revolution preamp to the digital USB output.

Samson Q9U. Samson says its onboard mute switch "silences the microphone on both the XLR and USB outputs" and that the mic has enough output for any mixer or interface without external amplification. Its −57 dBV/Pa rating is 2 dB below the MV7+.

Maono PD400X and PD200XS. Maono rates their XLR outputs at −51 and −52 dBV, the highest in this group. Use $154.99 on maono.com, $131.75 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/maono-pd400x-usb-xlr-dynamic-microphone if you expect to move to an interface. Use $89.99: https://dealectric.com/go/maono-pd200xs-usb-xlr-dynamic-mic-boom-arm if you need a hybrid under $100 that comes with a boom arm.

Fifine AmpliGame AM8. Itechguides reports that its RGB and function key are inactive over XLR and that its headphone volume control works only over USB.

Not recommended

Samson Q9U as a budget pick today. Samson's own page shows it as "Coming Soon" and says "this item is unavailable". Its −57 dBV/Pa XLR output needs 6 dB more interface gain than the Maono PD400X, which costs $5 more at Maono and is in stock.
