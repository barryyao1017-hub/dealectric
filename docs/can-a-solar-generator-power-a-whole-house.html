What the differences mean

The average US home used 10,791 kWh in 2022 (EIA). That is 10,791 ÷ 365 = 29.6 kWh a day, or 29,564 Wh ÷ 24 h = 1,232 W around the clock. None of these kits carries that for long, so each one backs up chosen circuits through BLUETTI's single-unit home kit.

  • Hours at average home draw: 5,529.6 Wh ÷ 1,232 W = 4.5 h for the multi-day and lowest-cost kits; 8,294.4 Wh ÷ 1,232 W = 6.7 h for the overnight kit. StorageReview drew 7.38 kWh from an Apex 300 with two B300K: 7,380 ÷ 1,232 = 6.0 h.
  • Full-sun hours to refill from empty (rated panel output, before charging losses): 5,529.6 Wh ÷ 2,000 W = 2.8 h; 8,294.4 Wh ÷ 1,000 W = 8.3 h; 5,529.6 Wh ÷ 500 W = 11.1 h.
  • Full-sun hours to make one average day of household use: 29,564 Wh ÷ 2,000 W = 14.8 h; ÷ 1,000 W = 29.6 h; ÷ 500 W = 59.1 h. Even at the SolarX 4K's 4,000 W ceiling it takes 29,564 ÷ 4,000 = 7.4 h.
  • Standing loss: the Apex 300 manual gives about 20 W of self-consumption. 20 W × 24 h = 480 Wh a day, which is 8.7% of a 5,529.6 Wh kit.
  • Load limit: 3,840 W rated output ÷ 240 V = 16 A (or ÷ 120 V = 32 A), shared by every backed-up circuit.

Panel wiring decides the solar input: 60 V or 150 V, nothing in between

The Apex 300's two built-in solar inputs (XT60) take 12–60 V, 20 A and 1,200 W each, and its manual says not to use panels with an open-circuit voltage above 60 V. Its 4,000 W solar figure needs the SolarX 4K, which accepts only 150–500 V and steps that down below 60 V. A panel string between 60 V and 150 V fits neither input.

Impulse Gamer's spec table gives the SORA 500 as 49.1 V open-circuit, 40.92 V at maximum power and 13.31 A short-circuit. That gives:

Notebookcheck lists the SolarX 4K's MPPT range as 120–450 V and its panel current limit as 20 A. Four and eight panels both fall inside that range, and 13.31 A is under the limit. Panel voltage rises in cold weather. Eight panels leave 107 V of room under the 500 V limit at their rated 49.1 V each.

That is why the multi-day kit has a SolarX 4K and four panels, while the other two kits put one panel on each built-in port with BLUETTI's MC4-to-XT60 cable (the Apex 300 manual says the solar cable is sold separately). For a single Apex 300, buy the SolarX 4K version with the P090D cable 'for Single Use'. The XT60 version at the same price is for parallel units.

According to Notebookcheck's Christian Hintze, the SolarX 4K also needs a 600 V / 20 A DC disconnect between panels and controller, which is not in the box. It is not weatherproof, so it mounts on a dry wall under cover, and it works off-grid only. BLUETTI's Home Integration Kit lists a Reliance sub panel with a 50 A SS2-50 inlet box, a 50 A outlet box and a NEMA 14-50 to SS2-50R cable. StorageReview confirmed that a single Apex 300 puts out 120/240 V on its NEMA 14-50R in 240 V mode. Have a licensed electrician install the panel and the disconnect. BLUETTI's Apex 300 page listed it as expected to ship late January (read 2026-11-13), so an order today won't arrive before then.

Not recommended

Don't pair a SolarX 4K with two or three SORA 500 panels: 2 × 49.1 = 98.2 V and 3 × 49.1 = 147.3 V are both under its 150 V minimum. BLUETTI's 'Apex 300 + 2 × 500W' bundle ($3,299) can't feed a SolarX 4K until you add two more panels. The 'Apex 300 + SolarX 4K' bundle ($2,199) costs $1 more than buying the Apex 300 ($1,499) and SolarX 4K ($699) separately.
