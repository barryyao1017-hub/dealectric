What the differences mean

Apple's spec pages settle the first half of the question. The 14-inch MacBook Pro with the plain M4 chip has three Thunderbolt 4 ports rated up to 40 Gb/s; the M4 Pro and M4 Max models have Thunderbolt 5 ports. Every enclosure in the table drops to the 40 Gb/s rung on a base M4. Phototrend measured the same OWC Express 1M2 80G at 3,354 / 3,263 MB/s on a Thunderbolt 4 MacBook Pro against 6,856 / 5,550 MB/s on a Thunderbolt 5 one.

  • Time to read 1 TB, OWC 1M2 80G: 1,000,000 MB ÷ 6,319 MB/s = 158 s (2 min 38 s) on a Thunderbolt 5 Mac; 1,000,000 ÷ 3,354 = 298 s (4 min 58 s) on a Thunderbolt 4 Mac. The Thunderbolt 5 port saves about 2 min 20 s per terabyte read.
  • Write gap between enclosures: 1,000,000 ÷ 6,001 = 167 s per TB in the UGREEN fan enclosure (Macwelt) versus 1,000,000 ÷ 3,000 = 333 s in the ORICO X50 Pro (Notebookcheck), both on Thunderbolt 5 Macs.
  • The SSD's cache sets long copies, not the enclosure: Howard Oakley (Eclectic Light) measured a Thunderbolt 5 SSD writing at about 5.5 GB/s for roughly 50 GB, then about 1.4 GB/s once its SLC cache filled. A 500 GB copy is therefore about 9 s at full speed plus 450 GB ÷ 1.4 GB/s = 321 s, about 5.5 minutes, whatever enclosure holds the drive. Oakley puts the practical Thunderbolt 5 ceiling at about 6 GB/s.

Measured on a Mac: how the enclosures compare

Only three bare enclosures have named-tester figures on a Thunderbolt 5 Mac. Thomas Bergbold at Macwelt ran AJA System Test on the UGREEN fan enclosure with a Samsung 990 Pro 4 TB and got 6,511 MB/s read and 6,001 MB/s write; a Kingston KC3000 2 TB gave 6,211 / 5,908 MB/s, and DriveDx showed the SSD at 34 °C idle and 40 °C under stress. Macwelt's one-hour Blackmagic endurance run on the OWC Express 1M2 80G with an OWC Aura IV on a Mac Studio M4 Max returned 6,319 / 5,776 MB/s, with the Aura IV throttled only minimally. Louis Royer at Phototrend measured the same OWC enclosure at 6,856 / 5,550 MB/s on a MacBook Pro 16-inch M5 Pro, and 3,354 / 3,263 MB/s on a Thunderbolt 4 MacBook Pro 14-inch M2 Pro. Christian Hintze at Notebookcheck measured the ORICO X50 Pro at about 6,000 MB/s read but about 3,000 MB/s write on a MacBook Pro 16 M5 with a TeamGroup MP44 2 TB, a drive that writes 5,700 MB/s internally; on a Thunderbolt 4 Razer Blade the same enclosure wrote over 3,700 MB/s.

The remaining candidates rest on maker numbers or Windows tests. TerraMaster's 7,061 / 6,816 MB/s for the D1 SSD Pro is its own benchmark on a Mac mini M4 Pro, as reported by CNX Software, which also carries TerraMaster's base M4 Mac mini figure of about 3,186 MB/s read. Jon L. Jacobi at PCWorld tested the Satechi DotDisk on a Thunderbolt 5 Windows desktop with a Crucial T710 and ranked it behind the OWC 1M2 80G and TerraMaster D1 SSD Pro in the 450 GB write, noting it runs warm and its small fan is audible under load.

Two pairing notes from the maker pages. OWC allows PCIe 5.0 drives in the 1M2 80G only if they average 11 W or less, and names the Samsung 9100 Pro as one that passes. UGREEN's fan enclosure lists PCIe 5.0 drives and the Samsung 9100 as unsupported, accepts 2280 drives only, and has no USB 2/3 fallback, so it mounts only on Thunderbolt or USB4 ports. A PCIe 4.0 drive such as the Samsung 990 Pro is the right fit for all of them; none of the measured figures above exceeds what a PCIe 4.0 drive delivers. UGREEN's fanless finned version, $229.99 on us.ugreen.com, $159.99 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-thunderbolt-5-nvme-enclosure-80gbps, is $229.99 at UGREEN today (2026-11-08); it stays out of the table until a named tester publishes Mac figures for it.

Not recommended

The ORICO X50 Pro is the trap for Mac write work. Notebookcheck's about 3,000 MB/s write on a Thunderbolt 5 MacBook Pro is lower than the 3,700 MB/s the same enclosure wrote on a Thunderbolt 4 Windows laptop, so it gives a MacBook Pro M4 Pro or M4 Max no write-side gain over a 40 Gb/s enclosure, and its PCIe 5.0 drive was not detected. Notebookcheck also notes the bare case is sold in the US only bundled with a 1 TB drive. For a base M4 MacBook Pro, every Thunderbolt 5 enclosure here is a poor buy: the port holds it to the Thunderbolt 4 rung, and UGREEN's own 80 Gb/s fan enclosure at $259.99 costs $150 more than its $109.99 40 Gb/s fan enclosure for the same speed on that port (UGREEN, 2026-11-08).
