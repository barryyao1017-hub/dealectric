What the differences mean

Why 1,000 Wh and $400. 1,024 Wh is the standard battery at this size: DJI, Pecron and ALLPOWERS each state exactly 1,024 Wh (Pecron gives it as 51.2 V × 20 Ah = 1,024 Wh) and BLUETTI states 1,004.8 Wh. $400 is the line this week because these four were the name-brand units at or under it on 2026-11-27; the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Classic, Jackery Explorer 1000 v2 and Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2 were all above $400 in Popular Science's roundup dated 2026-11-26. Every row meets 1,000 Wh on its maker's own rating. Left out for being just short: Grecell's 999 Wh unit (OutletPicks). Left out for price: the three above and the OUPES Mega 1 Lite (OutletPicks).

Dealectric does not test units. Every measurement here is a named reviewer's and every rating is the maker's. These are Black Friday week prices read on 2026-11-27.

  • What you get out of 1,000 Wh. DJI: 880 ÷ 1,024 = 86% (GearLab). BLUETTI: 903 ÷ 1,004.8 = 90% (There's A Trick For That, via The Cool Down). Pecron: 1,556 W × 33 ÷ 60 h = 856 Wh, and 856 ÷ 1,024 = 84% (The Solar Lab's run, our arithmetic). The spread is 903 − 856 = 47 Wh, about 5%, measured by different reviewers at different loads, so tested energy does not separate the three.
  • AC watts do. 2,600 W (DJI) against 1,800 W (Pecron, ALLPOWERS) against 700 W (BLUETTI). At a 1,500 W load the DJI's 880 Wh lasts 880 ÷ 1,500 = 0.59 h, about 35 minutes; The Solar Lab ran the Pecron at 1,556 W for 33 minutes.
  • A kettle. The Gadgeteer measured a kettle peaking at 1,074 W on the Pecron, using about 16% of the battery per boil: 100 ÷ 16 = about 6 boils per charge. That kettle is 374 W over the Elite 100 mini's 700 W limit.
  • Idle draw over a day. 18 W × 24 h = 432 Wh, 42% of 1,024 Wh (Pecron and DJI, The Solar Lab). 13.5 W × 24 h = 324 Wh, 32% of 1,004.8 Wh (Elite 100 mini, The Solar Lab). The mini keeps 108 Wh more per day for a small always-on load.
  • Rated lifetime energy, all to 80%. BLUETTI 1,004.8 × 6,000 = 6,029 kWh; DJI 1,024 × 4,000 = 4,096 kWh; Pecron 1,024 × 3,500 = 3,584 kWh. ALLPOWERS rates the P1800 to 70%, a looser basis, so its 3,000+ is not like for like.
  • Weight. DJI states 14.2 kg, which is 14.2 × 2.2046 = 31.3 lb (GearLab weighed 31.5 lb); The Solar Lab gives the Pecron as 29 lb; BLUETTI states 23.6 lb, 7.7 lb less than the DJI.

Not recommended

  • The Elite 100 mini for anything that heats. BLUETTI's product page caps total AC output at 700 W. Its launch release lists a Power Lifting mode for "compatible resistive appliances up to 1,400W". A mode like this feeds a heating element a lower voltage, so it heats more slowly, and it does not run motors above the rated watts. The Cool Down reports that There's A Trick For That's review saw the unit shut down at 900 W.
  • The DJI if you plan to charge from solar panels. DJI's release says panels connect through its Solar Panel Adapter Module or its 1.8kW Solar/Car Super Fast Charger, both sold separately. The Solar Lab puts the solar and car adapter at about $329, which takes a solar-charged DJI well past $400. The other three take panels on a built-in XT60 port.
  • The wrong DJI listing. On 2026-11-27 Amazon had two listings with the same DJI Power 1000 V2 title; one was under $400 and the other above it. Check the price on the listing before you buy.
  • Lower-priced units ranked on title watts. Baldr, Staryline, Ctolity and Arkpax units of 1,024 to 1,042 Wh were listed on Amazon below these four (OutletPicks, 2026-11-27). Their wattage comes from listing titles (Baldr's 1,200 W is marked as peak) and we read no named reviewer's measurement for them, so they are left out of the ranking.
