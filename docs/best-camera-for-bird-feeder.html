Why a security camera in a feeder case struggles up close

A feeder case holds your camera in one fixed spot right at the seed tray, so the birds land wherever the feeder puts them, not where the lens works best. Two hands-on tests found the same problem:

  • Digital Camera World (Adam Juniper, 4 April 2023) tested the Wasserstein case with a Ring Stick Up. He found the birds were 'closer than the Ring's nearest focusing distance' and he could not get 'a completely crisp image of birds'. 'Security cameras can't focus closely enough' is one of his listed cons.
  • Bob Vila (Mark Wolfe, 19 May 2023) tested with a Ring Stick Up HD: 'Those feeding closest to the camera were either in a blind spot outside the field of view or were too close to identify.' He rated it 7.8/10, and notes the case must be taken off its mount to reach the camera.
  • Blink's own support page says its cameras 'detect motion best from about 7 to 19 feet'. That is 84 inches or more, far more than the gap between a camera in a feeder case and the tray. Blink lists the Outdoor 4 as a PIR (heat-sensing) model, and says objects at the same temperature as the background may not be detected.

The Birdfy Cam 2 4K also uses a passive infrared sensor, according to Birdfy's spec sheet, so the sensor type is not the difference. Placement is. Its bendable arm mounts on a pole or wall, so you can set the camera back from the perch instead of right at it. Birdfy publishes no focus distance or detection range for the Cam 2 4K, so try it at a couple of distances before you fix the arm in place. If you go the case route with a Wyze camera, note that Wasserstein's listing names the Cam OG, V3, V3 Pro and Outdoor, but not the Cam v4.
