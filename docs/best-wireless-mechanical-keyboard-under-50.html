What the differences mean

Every board in the table was at or under $50 at the seller named on 2026-11-21, and every one works without a cable. What separates them is the wireless mode you will use, the layout and what is left of the budget.

  • Bluetooth against the receiver. Bluetooth LE before Core 6.2 allows one connection event every 7.5 ms: 1 ÷ 0.0075 s = 133 reports a second. A 1,000 Hz receiver or cable reports every 1 ms, so the Bluetooth wait is 7.5 ÷ 1 = 7.5 times longer (Bluetooth SIG figures).
  • Price gap to the pick. ATK's store listing led at $29.98 (list $84.98) for the A87. Royal Kludge's RK61 and Redragon's white K530 Pro were $49.99 at their makers' stores: $49.99 − $29.98 = $20.01 more for a smaller board. ATK sells the A87 with several switch options and $29.98 is the listing's lead price, so check the version you choose.
  • Battery, maker figures. ATK states 12,000 mAh for the A87 and Royal Kludge states 1,450 mAh for the RK61: 12,000 ÷ 1,450 = 8.3 times the capacity. Capacity is not runtime, and neither listing we read gives hours.
  • Hall effect by cable and by air. Gamakay rates the NS68 at 8,000 Hz: 1 ÷ 8,000 s = 0.125 ms between reports, the latency figure Gamakay prints. Crafting Worlds' review says “Wireless is limited to 1000Hz”, which is 1 ms: 8 times the cable's interval and still 7.5 times shorter than Bluetooth's 7.5 ms.
  • Keys. Gamakay counts 101 keys on the TK101 and a 60% board has 61: 101 − 61 = 40 more keys, the number pad among them.

Why the 2.4 GHz receiver decides it

A wireless keyboard can only hand the computer a keypress when its radio link next opens. In a Q&A on the Bluetooth SIG's blog, Nordic Semiconductor's representatives say that before Core 6.2 “the minimum Bluetooth LE connection interval was 7.5 ms with a resolution of 1.25 ms”, and that “a 133 Hz update rate, achievable with a 7.5 ms interval, may not be sufficient” for latency-sensitive uses. They also say that for gaming “consumers expect HID devices such as gamepads and mice to support report rates of 1 kHz or higher”; applying that to keyboards is our reading. Core 6.2 adds Shorter Connection Intervals, down to 375 µs and up to 2,666 Hz, but no maker here states Bluetooth 6.2: Royal Kludge lists Bluetooth 5.1, Redragon 3.0/5.0 and Gamakay 5.1 on the TK101.

So Bluetooth is the mode for typing and for hopping between a laptop, tablet and phone, and the 2.4 GHz receiver or the cable is the mode for games. What to check on each board:

  • ATK A87. ATK's listing says “tri-mode connectivity”. In this category that means USB-C cable, 2.4 GHz receiver and Bluetooth, the three modes Royal Kludge and Redragon spell out. The listing text we read gives no polling rate per mode.
  • Gamakay NS68. Gamakay's 8,000 Hz and 0.125 ms are headline figures. Crafting Worlds (Tristan) reports up to 8,000 Hz wired and “Wireless is limited to 1000Hz”. Techxreviews (Moussa Obscur, May 8, 2025) reviewed the wired version: 68 keys, Outemu Peach magnetic switches, rapid trigger adjustable from 0.01 mm to 2 mm, sockets that take 3-pin magnetic switches.
  • Gamakay TK101. Gamakay's spec list says “Tri-mode connection”, while its description names Bluetooth 5.1 and USB-C and its box list names the keyboard, a cable and a keycap puller. For number work Bluetooth is enough. If you plan to game on it, look for the receiver before you rely on it.
  • Redragon K530 Pro. The tri-mode board has three positions on its side switch: 2.4Ghz, BT1 and BT2.

One lab figure exists for this price band. RTINGS tested a Royal Kludge RK61 that connects by Bluetooth and cable only and found “a low effective update rate of 250Hz bottlenecks its polling rate of 1000Hz”, with latency it calls “high and inconsistent” (test bench 1.4.3, review updated 2026-01-22). RTINGS lists the RK61 with a 2.4 GHz receiver as a separate variant.

Not recommended

Any Bluetooth-only version of a familiar model name, for games. Two names in this budget are sold both ways. Redragon's store has a bundle titled K530 PRO that describes “Bluetooth 5.0 connection and 3000mAh long-lasting battery capacity” and a “Dual Mode Switch” between wireless and wired, with no 2.4 GHz. The RK61 that RTINGS measured at 250 Hz is the Bluetooth and cable version. Before buying either name, look for the 2.4 GHz receiver in the listing.

The tri-mode RK61 as the default buy. At $49.99 on Royal Kludge's store it sits one cent under the line, costs $20.01 more than the A87 at ATK's store, has no arrow keys or function row, and Royal Kludge states 1,450 mAh against the 12,000 mAh ATK states (prices read 2026-11-21). It fits only if 61 keys is the layout you want.
