What the differences mean

Idle use per day is the idle draw × 24 h. It is the energy the inverter burns while it stays on to run a 120 V fridge or the trailer's outlets, before any appliance draws power.

  • Jackery HomePower 3000: 8 W × 24 h = 192 Wh; 192 ÷ 3,072 Wh = 6% a day. Idle alone would take 3,072 ÷ 8 = 384 h, about 16 days, to empty it.
  • BLUETTI Elite 300: 15 × 24 = 360 Wh; 360 ÷ 3,014 = 12% a day, about 8.4 days to empty.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Ultra: 22 × 24 = 528 Wh; 528 ÷ 3,072 = 17% a day, about 5.8 days.
  • Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2: 18 × 24 = 432 Wh; 432 ÷ 2,048 = 21% a day, about 4.7 days.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max: 27 × 24 = 648 Wh; 648 ÷ 2,048 = 32% a day, about 3.2 days.

Over a three-night dry camp the two 3,072 Wh, full-30 A stations end up far apart: (22 − 8) W × 72 h = 1,008 Wh. That is about a third of the battery left in the HomePower 3000 that the DELTA 3 Ultra has already spent on standby.

RV outlet amps equal the continuous watts ÷ 120 V. 3,600 ÷ 120 = 30 A, which matches a 30 amp trailer inlet. 2,400 ÷ 120 = 20 A, so the Elite 300 and C2000 Gen 2 fit the plug but deliver two-thirds of what the trailer panel expects. A roof air conditioner plus a microwave on those two can trip the inverter. Jackery's own spec sheet lists no 50 A port on the HomePower 3000, and none of the five has a 50 amp outlet. On a 50 amp trailer, each station supplies only what its own outlet is rated for.

Real usable energy also falls short of the label. Notebookcheck got about 1,600 Wh (79%) out of the C2000 Gen 2 at a fridge-sized 200 W load. Outdoor Gear Lab measured 3,790 Wh from the 4,096 Wh EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 (93%).

Idle draw: what drains a trailer battery overnight

A travel trailer's 120 V fridge, router or CPAP keeps the inverter running all night. The inverter's own draw adds to every hour of that. The Solar Lab measured each station with the inverter on and no load. The Jackery HomePower 3000 came in at 8 W, which The Solar Lab called the best it has seen from a large power station. The BLUETTI Elite 300 measured about 15 W, the Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2 about 18 W against Anker's advertised 9 W, the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Ultra about 22 W and the DELTA 3 Max about 27 W. These figures matter more in a trailer than for home backup, because off-grid the battery only refills from solar. The HomePower 3000 also takes 1,000 W of solar across its two ports (Jackery).

Check your trailer's EMS before you plug in. Jackery's spec sheet lists the HomePower 3000's neutral as floating. A hardwired energy management system that checks for ground can read that as an open ground and cut power to the trailer. The AIR GEAR help center's article on Progressive EMS units says this is normal with any power station. Its advice is to remove or bypass the EMS, and it says not to use a neutral-ground bonding plug in a power station. RV electrical writer Mike Sokol shows a bonding plug as the fix instead. If your EMS is built in, plan for the bypass switch Progressive offers. Also note that the HomePower 3000 cannot take expansion batteries, so 3,072 Wh is its ceiling.

Not recommended

EcoFlow DELTA 3 Max for a travel trailer. The Solar Lab lists only four 120 V outlets, and its only rear connection is a 12 V car socket. It has no RV outlet to plug the trailer's shore cord into. It also measured the highest idle draw here, 27 W, which uses 32% of its 2,048 Wh battery per day before anything is switched on.
