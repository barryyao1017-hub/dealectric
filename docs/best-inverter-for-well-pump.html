Best inverter for a well pump

Updated 2026-11-06

Short answer. The best inverter for a well pump is the Sun Gold Power 6000W 48V Split Phase Inverter Charger ($1,220; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/sungoldpower-6000w-48v-split-phase-inverter-charger): 6,000 W continuous, an 18,000 W surge for 20 seconds, 120/240 V split-phase output from one unit, and a 60 A charger that runs from a generator. The concern that matters is start-up surge: a well pump pulls 3 to 7 times its running watts for a second or two, so a 1 HP, 240 V pump that runs on about 1,000 W can demand 5,000 W or more at start, and one forum owner measured 38 A at 240 V, about 9,100 W. A transformer inverter that holds 18,000 W for 20 seconds covers that with margin. The usual all-in-one pick, the EG4 6000XP, holds 12,000 W for about 3.5 seconds, which starts most pumps up to 1 HP but leaves less room for an old motor or a weak battery bank.

Two honest exceptions. If you want solar charging built into the same box and the lowest idle draw, the EG4 6000XP wins: 8,000 W of solar input, about 50 W idle, a 5-year warranty, and $1,499.99 at Signature Solar on 2026-11-06. If reliability outranks price, the Victron MultiPlus-II 48/5000 120V (9,000 VA peak, 19 W idle, 5-year warranty) is the proven unit, but one makes only 120 V, so a 240 V pump needs two of them at $1,307.32 each. The Sun Gold Power unit wins for the shopper with a 240 V pump, a 48 V battery bank and a generator: it starts the hardest pumps and costs about half of the equivalent AIMS Power unit.

What owners complain about

The complaint that fills forum threads is an inverter that trips or browns out when the pump starts. Owners on the Solar Electric forum put pump inrush at 3 to 5 times running current, some at 7 times, and one measured 38 A at 240 V on a 1 HP pump. The fix is surge headroom, thicker battery cables and a bank that holds voltage under the hit; a soft-start pump such as a Grundfos SQ or a soft starter cuts the surge for a fraction of a second inverter's cost. Second is voltage: most submersible pumps are 240 V, and a single 120 V inverter cannot run them. Third is idle draw and weight: transformer inverters sit at roughly 80 W doing nothing (AIMS publishes 1.67 A DC idle, 25 W in power-save) and weigh 70 lb or more, so they drain a small bank overnight and hum under load. Sun Gold Power buyers on Trustpilot (4.3 from 195 reviews, 9% one-star) mention display and setup faults and slow technical support more than failed output; the company's inverter warranty reads 12 or 36 months by product, while its dealers list 2 years, so confirm before you buy.

Which one by need

240 V pump, 48 V bank, generator charging: the Sun Gold Power 6000W 48V unit. Same 18,000 W, 20-second surge and 60 A charger as the AIMS Power unit for about $1,000 less. Solar and pump on one box: the EG4 6000XP, with 8,000 W of solar input, about 50 W idle and a 5-year warranty, as long as the pump is 1 HP or smaller or has a soft starter. The Sun Gold Power SPH6548P is the budget version of that idea with more charge current, but it does not publish how long it holds its 13,000 VA peak. Mission-critical reliability: two Victron MultiPlus-II 48/5000 120V units configured for split phase, at roughly $2,600 for the pair.

Not recommended

A single 120 V inverter for a 240 V pump, whatever its wattage. A 4,000 W-class high-frequency inverter with no published surge hold time for anything above a 1/2 HP pump. A modified sine wave inverter, which overheats pump motors and solid-state control boxes. Any inverter fed by a 120 V-only generator if you pick the Sun Gold Power unit, since its charger needs a 240 V input.
