What the differences mean

Why 20,000 mAh with a 100 W port is the line: Apple's spec page says the MacBook Air is “fast-charge capable with 70W or higher USB PD power source”. Power bank USB-C ports step from 65 W to 100 W, so 100 W is the first step that clears it. 20,000 mAh is 72 Wh as these makers rate it, a little more than the 53.8 Wh battery in a 13-inch Air. Every row meets both numbers by its maker's or seller's own figure (UGREEN, Baseus and Anker store listings; the Cuktech's Amazon listing and Notebookcheck's spec table). Dealectric does not test units: every figure below is a maker's or a named reviewer's.

  • Faster than the charger in the box. Apple ships the M5 Air with a 40 W adapter that peaks at 60 W. 60 W is under 70 W, so a 100 W bank port can fast-charge where the bundled adapter cannot. Macworld's Thomas Bergbold measured a MacBook Air M3 gaining 56% in 30 minutes from the UGREEN's USB-C1 port; Apple's own fast-charge figure is up to 50% in around 30 minutes.
  • Watts the Air cannot use. ChargerLAB saw a 2023 15-inch Air take about 71 W from Apple's 96 W and 140 W adapters. 100 − 71 = 29 W of a 100 W port goes unused, and 140 − 71 = 69 W of a 140 W port. That test predates the M4 and M5 Air.
  • One charge, not two. Guillaume Hoarau at Best Batteries got 57.9 Wh out of the UGREEN 130W at a 60 W load and 54.8 Wh at 100 W. For a 13-inch Air that is 57.9 ÷ 53.8 = 1.08 and 54.8 ÷ 53.8 = 1.02 charges. For a 15-inch (66.5 Wh) it is 57.9 ÷ 66.5 = 0.87 and 54.8 ÷ 66.5 = 0.82. These are ceilings, because the Mac's own charging loss is not subtracted. Macworld's Simon Jary says a 74 Wh Baseus 20,000 mAh bank fills a 13-inch Air once with about 5% remaining.
  • Refill time. 72 Wh ÷ 65 W = 1.1 hours is the floor for the 65 W banks. Macworld's UGREEN 130W recharge took less than two hours, and Best Batteries logged 1 h 45 to 2 h 40. Notebookcheck's Marcus Schwarten refilled the Cuktech in 44 min 30 s using both USB-C inputs.
  • Weight. The Cuktech is 590 − 476 = 114 g heavier than the pick. The Baseus is 476 − 428 = 48 g lighter, and the UGREEN 100W is 476 − 419 = 57 g lighter.

What the Air takes, and what each bank keeps for it

Apple's fast-charge list covers MacBook Air models introduced in 2022 or later. It names 70 W, 96 W and 140 W adapters, each with either a USB-C to MagSafe 3 cable or a USB-C charge cable, and a 67 W adapter for 13.6-inch models only. The bank's watts in the name do not decide this. What decides it is whether the port feeding the laptop still gives 70 W once something else is plugged in.

  • UGREEN Nexode 130W: the 130 W is 100 W + 30 W. Macworld and Best Batteries both report that USB-C1 keeps 100 W with a phone on USB-C2, and that a third device leaves USB-C2 and USB-A sharing 15 W (115 W total). The laptop port stays above 70 W in every combination.
  • Baseus EnerFill FC41 20,000 mAh: each built-in cable gives up to 100 W alone. Notebookcheck reports that with several outputs in use one cable gives up to 65 W and the other three share 15 W. 65 W is under 70 W, and under the 67 W Apple allows for the 13.6-inch.
  • UGREEN Nexode 100W: UGREEN's spec table and Macworld both give 100 W as the total for all three ports, so a phone on the second port comes out of the laptop's share.
  • Cuktech 15 Ultra: 165 W total with USB-C2 capped at 65 W leaves 165 − 65 = 100 W for USB-C1 by subtraction; Notebookcheck gives no per-port split. It measured about 160 W with several ports in use.
  • Anker Prime 20K 220W: Macworld gives two USB-C ports at 110 W each.

One limit on the pick: Best Batteries saw the UGREEN's 100 W port fall to 65 W on a constant 100 W load with 34% of charge left (about 22 minutes in), and to 45 W on its test laptops after 18 minutes with 50% left. An Air asks for about 71 W, not 100 W, and no reviewer read for this page tested at that load. Macworld's 56% in the first 30 minutes is the measured part; the last third of the bank may charge more slowly.

Not recommended

  • 20,000 mAh banks with a 45 W or 65 W port. They sit under Apple's 70 W line, which is why $39.99 on baseus.com, $28.99 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/baseus-enerfill-fc11-20000mah-45w is left out of the table despite its size. It still suits a MacBook Air from before 2022: those models are not on Apple's fast-charge list, so a 100 W port buys them no extra speed.
  • Paying for 140 W ports for an Air alone. The Anker Prime 20K 220W was $119.99 at Anker US on Nov 26, 2026, against $54.99 for the pick. Its 140 W ports serve a 16-inch MacBook Pro; an Air used about 71 W in ChargerLAB's test.
  • The Baseus FC41 20,000 mAh if a phone will share it. Its laptop cable drops to 65 W. Alone, it is a sound choice.
