What the differences mean

  • Wayne ESP25n: 10,000 gal per charge ÷ 1,500 GPH at 10 ft ≈ 6.7 h of nonstop pumping. Consumer Reports says a fully charged deep-cycle battery typically gives 7–8 h of active pumping; it tests backup pumps on a 12 V 75 Ah AGM battery at 10 ft of lift.
  • BLUETTI Apex 300: BLUETTI's formula is Wh × 0.8 ÷ running watts. 2,764.8 × 0.8 ÷ 800 W ≈ 2.8 h of continuous running on a 1/3 HP pump, or 11.0–16.6 h if the pump runs 10–15 min per hour.
  • BLUETTI AC180: 1,152 × 0.8 ÷ 800 W ≈ 1.2 h of continuous running on a 1/3 HP pump. BLUETTI says a 1/3 HP pump draws 1,300–2,900 W to start, and its 1.5× rule gives 1.5 × 1,300 = 1,950 W, already above the AC180's 1,800 W rated output. Its 2,700 W Power Lifting mode is for resistive loads only, so test your pump's start on it first.
  • Any power station: it keeps your main pump powered and nothing more. If that pump jams or its switch fails, nothing empties the pit. The ESP25n has its own pump and float, so it covers both cases.

Why the check valves decide whether the backup works

The backup pump sits in the same pit as your main pump. If either discharge line has no check valve, water pumped up the pipe can run straight back down into the pit. Wayne's ESP25 manual: 'A check valve will be required in the discharge line of BOTH the Main AC pump and the back-up pump', and 'System will not function without two check valves.'

  • Plumbing: Wayne prefers a separate discharge line for the backup (Method 1), with a rubber check valve that is sold separately. You can also tie into the existing line (Method 2), with the valves above the sump. The pump has a 1-1/2 in. NPT outlet and installs on 1-1/4 or 1-1/2 in. rigid PVC. A 1-1/4 in. line needs an adapter, which is not included.
  • Float: mount it at least 10–12 in. above the bottom of the pit, so the backup only runs once the water is above the main pump's normal 'on' level.
  • Battery: the oversize case holds a 12 V sealed lead-acid battery up to group 27, and Wayne recommends its WSB1275 75 Ah. The manual describes sealed lead-acid batteries only, so a 12 V LiFePO4 battery is not a documented match for its charger.
  • Power: plug the charger into a separately fused, GFCI-protected 115 VAC outlet, with the battery within six feet of the sump.

Most main pumps already have a check valve on their line. If yours doesn't, add a second one.

If you power the main pump from a power station instead, the AC180 manual describes ECO mode: it switches the AC outlets off after 1–4 h at a load of 15–30 W or less. A pump draws next to nothing between cycles, so turn ECO off and run a test before you count on it.
