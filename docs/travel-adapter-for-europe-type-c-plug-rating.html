What the differences mean

  • Type C plug ceiling: 575 W. 2.5 A × 230 V = 575 W. A 65 W laptop charger uses 65 ÷ 575 = 11% of that, so phones, cameras and laptops are fine on any Type C adapter.
  • The '2,500 W' on TESSAN's Type C adapters assumes 10 A. 2,500 W ÷ 230 V = 10.9 A, which is 10.9 ÷ 2.5 = 4.3 times the Europlug standard's rating.
  • A Schuko (Type E/F) circuit carries up to 16 A. 16 A × 230 V = 3,680 W, which is 3,680 ÷ 575 = 6.4 times the Type C figure.
  • WTA09 vs WTA07: same 65 W port, 15 dollars apart. $42.99 − $27.99 = $15.00. That works out to $27.99 ÷ 65 W = $0.43 per USB-C watt against $42.99 ÷ 65 W = $0.66. The WTA07 weighs 7.18 − 6.04 = 1.14 oz less but has one AC outlet instead of two.
  • 15 W shared on TESSAN's Type C four-outlet models. Three phones charging at once average 15 ÷ 3 = 5 W each, a quarter of the 20 W one-port figure.
  • Converters: 250 W vs 2,000 W. 2,000 ÷ 250 = 8 times the converter capacity for $45.99 − $43.99 = $2 more.

Why '2,500 W' on a Type C adapter doesn't make it a hair-dryer adapter

Germany, France, Spain, Italy and Switzerland all supply 230 V at 50 Hz. Type C is the two-round-pin Europlug, defined in CEE 7/16 Alternative II and EN 50075. It is rated for up to 250 V and 2.5 A, and it is intended only for low-power, double-insulated devices. That small rating is also why it fits nearly everywhere on the continent: Schuko (Type F), French (Type E), Danish (Type K), Italian 10 A and dual Type L sockets, and every Swiss SN 441011 socket. It does not fit Italy's 16 A-only Type L sockets, and nothing round-pinned fits the UK's Type G.

TESSAN's Type C adapters advertise '2500 watt (max 250 Volt, 10 A)', and the WTA07 says '2500W MAX' next to a double 10 A fuse. That number comes from the fuse and outlet arithmetic. None of these TESSAN pages says the pins are built to the 16 A unearthed CEE 7/17 pattern rather than the 2.5 A Europlug, and Ceptics' store page gives no amp figure for the CTU-9C. TESSAN also tells buyers a dual-voltage hair dryer, straightener or curling iron will work. Our reading: anything over 575 W, which means heat tools, belongs in a Schuko-pattern plug on a 16 A circuit, not on Type C pins.

Chargers are a different story. They accept 100–240 V and draw a small fraction of 575 W, so the figure that matters is the output of each port. On the WTA09 and WTA07, only USB-C3 delivers 65 W, and only when nothing else is plugged in. USB-C1, C2, A1 and A2 stop at 15 W each, so a laptop on the wrong port gets 15 W. On TESSAN's Type C four-outlet adapters, the 20 W USB-C figure applies to one device, and all three USB ports share 15 W.

Buy universal sliders from a named brand. In a July 2026 lab test by the UK charity Electrical Safety First, all 14 travel adaptors bought from AliExpress, eBay, Amazon Marketplace and TikTok Shop failed. On 6 of the 8 universal ones, more than one set of pins could be exposed and live at the same time.
