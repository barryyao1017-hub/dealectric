How many extender screens your laptop can light

FOPO stated the rule plainly for its S1, and its other duals work the same way: "Two displays. Two video connections. Each S1 screen needs its own supported video connection." FOPO no longer sold the S1 on 2026-11-21. No driver is involved, so the laptop itself has to drive both screens. FOPO adds that charging-only USB-C ports cannot carry video. On a laptop with one video-capable USB-C port and an HDMI port, the second screen can run from HDMI with a separate USB-A cable for power.

Apple's spec pages give the limit for each Mac:

  • MacBook Air M1 and M2: one external display.
  • 13-inch MacBook Air M3 and base 14-inch MacBook Pro M3: one external display with the lid open. A second works only with the lid closed, and you can't close the lid with an extender clipped to it.
  • MacBook Air M4 and current M5: up to two external displays with the built-in screen on.

A single-cable hub won't get around the one-display limit. Plugable, a dock maker, states that "MST is not supported on macOS for extended displays": screens on one MST cable mirror instead of extending. On those Macs, three working screens need a driver-based extender, and the one we list, the FOPO S788, costs $599.99, over this budget. On Windows, count the USB-C ports that carry video (DisplayPort Alt Mode) plus any HDMI port.
