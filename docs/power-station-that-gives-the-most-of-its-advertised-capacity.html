What the differences mean

Usable share is the AC energy a tester drew out until shutoff divided by the maker's rated Wh. Dealectric does not test units; every figure here is a named tester's, and the sums below are our arithmetic.

  • Elite 200 V2, best and worst test: 2,028 ÷ 2,073.6 = 97.8% (APH Networks); 1,910 ÷ 2,073.6 = 92.1% (StorageReview). Even the lower result is 1,910 Wh usable.
  • Against a 1 kWh station: the Elite 200 V2's lowest result (1,910 Wh) ÷ the C1000 Gen 2's highest (907 Wh) = 2.1 times the delivered energy.
  • DELTA 3 Classic disagreement: (0.925 − 0.83) × 1,024 Wh = about 97 Wh between CNET's 947 Wh and The Solar Lab's 83%.
  • Idle draw over an 8-hour night with the inverter on: Elite 200 V2 9.5 W × 8 = 76 Wh; C1000 Gen 2 13.8 W × 8 = 110 Wh; DELTA 3 Classic 17 W × 8 = 136 Wh.
  • C1000 Gen 2, AC vs 12 V: 907 ÷ 1,024 = 88.6% through AC at 1 kW; 844 ÷ 1,024 = 82.4% through the 12 V socket at 8 A.
  • Charging loss on the Elite 200 V2: 1.91 kWh out ÷ 2.44 kWh in = 78% round trip (StorageReview).

Why one test is not enough: what each tester measured

A power station loses part of its rated battery on the way out: the inverter converts battery DC to 120 V AC with some loss, the inverter draws its own idle power for as long as it is on, and the battery management system keeps a reserve before shutoff. How big those losses are depends on the load, so each tester's number reflects its own setup:

  • APH Networks ran the Elite 200 V2 into a 987 W electric heater for 2 h 1 min: 2,028 Wh.
  • CNET runs every unit at a fixed 480 W until empty (as compiled by WattPowerStation): Elite 200 V2 96%, DELTA 3 Classic 93%, Dabbsson 2000L 92%.
  • The Solar Lab measured the Elite 200 V2 at 94% efficiency, which it calls the highest it has seen on a portable power station, against about 83% for the DELTA 3 Classic and about 86% for the C1000 Gen 2.
  • StorageReview drew 1.91 kWh from the Elite 200 V2 (load not stated) and needed 2.44 kWh to recharge it.
  • OutdoorGearLab uses a bank of light bulbs as a measured AC load: DELTA Pro 3 3,790 Wh, C1000 Gen 2 890 Wh, Elite 100 V2 890 Wh, Explorer 1000 v2 910 Wh.
  • The Technology Man drew 907 Wh from the C1000 Gen 2 at 1 kW AC and 844 Wh from its 12 V socket at 8 A, so DC output is not automatically the more efficient route.
  • Notebookcheck drew 850 Wh from an EU C1000 Gen 2 at about 250 W, and less under fluctuating loads.

Light loads cut the share sharply. At 34 W, Outdoor Life measured six other models at 49–71% of rated capacity (compiled by WattPowerStation); published low-load results so far cover those models, not the seven above. For small overnight loads, a low idle draw such as the Elite 200 V2's 9.5 W matters as much as the steady-load share.

Not recommended

The EcoFlow DELTA 3 Classic as a top-efficiency pick: its 93% rests on one CNET run, The Solar Lab measured about 83% on the same model, and its 17 W idle draw ties the C1000 Gen 2's 17 W reading (The Technology Man) as the highest measured here. It may still suit a buyer after a low price per rated Wh, but its usable share is unsettled.
