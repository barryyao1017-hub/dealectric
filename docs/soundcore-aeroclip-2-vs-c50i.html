LDAC on both, and what it costs you

LDAC sends more audio data over Bluetooth than AAC, and the radio uses more power to do it. Soundcore's own battery table for the AeroClip 2 shows the cost. On AAC at 50% volume with soundcore Signature mode, it plays for 8 h. Turn LDAC on and it plays for 5 h at 50% volume, or 3 h at 100% (4.5 h on AAC at 100%). The same table puts call mode at 5 h at 50% volume. Soundcore adds that custom EQ and dual-device connection also raise the power draw.

The C50i lists the same codecs: SBC, AAC and LDAC. Its trade-off is a different one. Soundcore's C50i FAQ says: "Dual connection is not supported when using LDAC. Please disable LDAC and enable the dual connection function." So on the C50i you choose between LDAC and staying connected to a phone and a laptop at once. Soundcore's AeroClip 2 support pages do say dual-device connection can't be switched on during a call or online meeting.

LDAC only matters on Android. Kopfbox (Markus) says the AeroClip 2's LDAC is for Android users only and isn't available while 3D Spatial Audio is on. Expert Reviews' Andy White heard LDAC from a Google Pixel 11 and AAC from an iPhone 13 Pro. White found the LDAC gain smaller on open-ear buds than on sealed ones. On an iPhone, both earbuds play AAC. So the case for the AeroClip 2 comes down to its call hardware and its faster charge, not its codec or its battery. If calls are what you buy for, $169.99: https://dealectric.com/go/soundcore-aeroclip-2. For music, runs and everyday listening, $69.99: https://dealectric.com/go/soundcore-c50i.

Not recommended

The soundcore C40i costs $49.99 at Soundcore's store today, $10.01 more than the C50i. Soundcore's FAQ lists it with Bluetooth 5.4, SBC and AAC only (no LDAC) and IPX4 water resistance. The C50i has Bluetooth 6.0, LDAC and IP55. HeadphoneCheck's Sven Opitz got at least 7 h per charge and 21 h with the case, against the C50i's rated 28 h with its case.
