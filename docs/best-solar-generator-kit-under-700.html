Battery chemistry and cycle life under $700

Below $700, the kits with the most Wh per dollar are ALLPOWERS's S2000 Pro and S2000 bundles, and both use NCM (ternary) lithium-ion cells, not LiFePO4. StorageReview says the S2000 Pro 'includes the older NCM or Ternary battery, as opposed to the newer LiFePO4 cells'. ALLPOWERS's own US listing gives the pack as 43.2 V × 33.6 Ah. 43.2 V is 12 cells × 3.6 V, the nominal voltage of NCM cells. LiFePO4 cells are 3.2 V, and 43.2 ÷ 3.2 = 13.5, which is not a whole number of cells. The Gadgeteer's review lists the S2000's battery as 'Ternary lithium battery', and ALLPOWERS rates the S2000 at '1000+ life cycles'.

The LiFePO4 kits carry longer ratings. BLUETTI rates the Elite 100 mini at 6,000+ cycles and the Elite 100 V2 at 4,000+ cycles to 80%. ALLPOWERS rates the P1800 at over 3,000 cycles. Makers state these on different bases, and only the Elite 100 V2 and S2000 Pro figures name an end-of-life point (80%).

The Elite 100 mini's manual gives the limits that come with its price. Total AC output is 700 W, with a surge of 735–840 W for 2 minutes. Power Lifting reaches 1,400 W, but only for resistive loads, and the manual says actual output is still 700 W. Solar input is 350 W max at 12–60 V and 10 A, so the kit's 200 W panel leaves 150 W of room for another panel and two 200 W panels (400 W) would go over the limit. The battery charges only between 32 °F and 104 °F, so in winter keep the station indoors and run the cable out to the panel. If the battery ever drains to 0%, BLUETTI says to recharge it from AC, or from solar only with an input of over 100 W. That rule is why the 200 W kit, not the 100 W kit, is the one for outages without wall power.

Not recommended

The ALLPOWERS S2000 Pro kits (SE100, ships now; SF200, dispatch Nov 19) and the S2000 + SF200 kit (dispatch Nov 20) look like the best value per Wh. Once chemistry and testing are counted, they aren't. They use NCM cells rated 2,500+ cycles to 80% (S2000 Pro) and 1000+ cycles (S2000). StorageReview drew 920 Wh of the S2000 Pro's rated 1,451 Wh (63%), and in another test the unit shut off with 22% still showing. In The Solar Lab's high-load test, the S2000 Pro shut off with an overload error at 55% battery under a 2,250 W load, below its 2,400 W rating. Buy one only if you need 2,400 W this week and accept the shorter rated life.
