What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure here is the maker's stated figure or a named reviewer's result, and every per-pound figure is arithmetic on those numbers:

  • DJI Power 1000 V2: 3,000 W ÷ 31.5 lb = 95 W/lb at the measured cut-out; 2,600 W ÷ 31.5 lb = 83 W/lb at its continuous rating.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus: 2,600 W ÷ 27.6 lb = 94 W/lb, held about a minute. Per pound it ties the DJI; the DJI reaches about 400 W higher before it cuts out.
  • Anker SOLIX C1000 Gen 2: 2,000 W ÷ 24.9 lb = 80 W/lb.
  • OUPES Mega 1: 2,000 W ÷ 27.8 lb = 72 W/lb.
  • BLUETTI Elite 100 V2: 1,800 W ÷ 25.3 lb = 71 W/lb. By bulk it edges the Anker: 32.0 × 21.5 × 25.0 cm = 17.2 L, and 1,800 W ÷ 17.2 L = 105 W/L, against the Anker's 2,000 W ÷ 19.5 L (15.12 × 8.19 × 9.61 in) = 103 W/L.
  • BLUETTI Elite 30 V2: 600 W ÷ 9.5 lb = 63 W/lb.
  • Advertised surge per pound, for comparison: OUPES 4,500 ÷ 27.8 = 162; Elite 100 V2 3,600 ÷ 25.3 = 142; DJI V2 4,400 ÷ 31.5 = 140; DELTA 3 Plus 3,600 ÷ 27.6 = 130; Anker 3,000 ÷ 24.9 = 120 W/lb. None of these peaks comes with a time, and the order flips once held watts are used.

Who measured what, and who puts a time on surge

A motor pulls several times its running watts for the moment it takes to spin up. A station starts it only if the inverter holds that draw long enough; if not, it trips. A peak or surge figure with no time on it can't tell you that, which is why this table ranks by watts a station was rated or seen to hold.

Only DJI states a duration. For the original DJI Power 1000, DJI's spec page says the 120 V DYM1000L holds 2,600 W for 30 seconds and the 220–240 V DYM1000H for 60 seconds, measured on a prototype at about 25 °C (77 °F) and 50% battery, and that the time varies with battery temperature and charge. Its 4,400 W peak carries no time. The V2 lifts 2,600 W to its continuous rating, per DJI as quoted by The Technology Man; DJI's US spec page for the V2 is offline, so the V2 figures here come from The Technology Man and OutdoorGearLab. EcoFlow (3,600 W), Anker (3,000 W), OUPES (4,500 W) and BLUETTI (3,600 W in its blog) give surge figures with no time.

Named results. The Technology Man ramped a load on the DJI Power 1000 V2 until it beeped at about 2,900 W and cut out just over 3,000 W, and it started his induction-motor bandsaw but not his table saw. The Solar Lab held the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus at 2,600 W for about a minute, 800 W over its rating, and saw it shut down almost at once above that. The Solar Lab also found the OUPES Mega 1 lowers its voltage during surges rather than going briefly past 2,000 W. CleanTechnica's Paul Fosse saw the BLUETTI Elite 100 V2 shut down under about 2,700 W of kitchen appliances (his estimate) until he switched on Power Lifting.

Boost modes are not surge. BLUETTI's Lifting Power lets appliances over the rating run at a lower level instead of tripping; in Fosse's words, it "does not lift power". BLUETTI's Elite 30 V2 page ties its 1,500 W Lifting Power to pure resistive devices, so it is not a motor-start figure.

Not recommended

The OUPES Mega 1 bought for its 4,500 W surge. It has the biggest advertised surge per pound (162 W/lb), but OUPES gives that surge no time, and The Solar Lab saw the voltage sag during surges instead. Its 2,000 W rating works out to 72 W/lb, behind the DJI, EcoFlow and Anker. The BLUETTI Elite 30 V2 for motors: its 1,500 W is Lifting Power, not surge, and its rating is 600 W.
