What the differences mean

Why 40,000 mAh is the line. A bank this size holds 144 to 154 Wh, which is over the 100 Wh a passenger can fly with unasked. The TSA allows up to two spare lithium batteries of 101–160 Wh per person only with airline approval, in carry-on bags and never in checked ones. So this is a desk, car, cabin or campsite size, bought for close to two charges of a 14-inch laptop. Every row is 40,000 mAh or more as its maker labels it and has a USB-C port of at least 100 W. Omnicharge's Omni 40+ is left out for being just short: its maker lists 38,400 mAh (142 Wh).

  • Delivered energy, AOHi. ChargerLAB drained the Starship 40K at 20 V 5 A and counted 121.6 Wh. 121.6 ÷ 146 = 83% of the label. At 28 V 5 A it gave 116.52 Wh: 116.52 ÷ 146 = 80%.
  • Delivered energy, UGREEN. Macworld recharged a 14-inch M2 MacBook Pro 1.8 times, and Apple rates that battery at 69.6 Wh. 1.8 × 69.6 = 125.3 Wh, and 125.3 ÷ 153.6 = 82%. Counting laptop recharges is a looser method than a bench load, so read this as approximate.
  • The mAh labels are on different scales. 153.6 Wh ÷ 48 Ah = 3.2 V, the LiFePO4 cell voltage; 146 Wh ÷ 40 Ah = 3.65 V. 153.6 ÷ 146 = 1.05, so 20% more mAh on the box is 5% more energy. Compare watt-hours, not mAh.
  • Weight per delivered watt-hour. 916.2 g ÷ 121.6 Wh = 7.5 g for the AOHi; 1,650 g ÷ 125 Wh = 13.2 g for the UGREEN. That is 1,650 − 916 = 734 g more to carry for about 4 Wh more.
  • Hours. ChargerLAB timed the AOHi at 1 h 29 min on a 100 W load, 2 h 42 min at 45 W and 1 h 3 min at 140 W.

  • UGREEN (Macworld, Simon Jary, July 17, 2024). It took the 14-inch MacBook Pro from 5% to 90% in an hour: 85% × 69.6 Wh = 59 Wh in 60 minutes.
  • UGREEN (Android Central, Harish Jonnalagadda, June 24, 2024). It refilled in just over 100 minutes and ships with one 240 W USB-C cable. He lists the weight as 4.1 lb (1.8 kg), heavier than Macworld's figure. His 55% efficiency compares the mAh the bank lost with the mAh of the phone battery it filled. A 3.2 V bank feeding a higher-voltage phone cell makes that ratio look worse than the energy loss is.

What your laptop asks for. Apple lists 140 W for fast-charging a 16-inch MacBook Pro, 96 W for the 14-inch and 70 W for a 2022 or later MacBook Air, so a 100 W port already covers the last two. For 140 W, Apple names a 240 W USB-C cable only for the 16-inch from Nov 2023 on; earlier 16-inch models need Apple's USB-C to MagSafe 3 cable. On either bank the 300 W or 140 W on the box does not change what one laptop gets: 140 W at most.
