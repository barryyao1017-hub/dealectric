What the differences mean

Miles here are rated Wh × 0.85 (ryzzun's estimate of the share that reaches the car) ÷ 270 Wh per mile (the EPA figure for a Tesla Model Y Long Range RWD, as ryzzun quotes it). Hand-over time is the energy that reaches the car ÷ 1,440 W, the draw of a 120 V, 12 A Level 1 cord.

The grounding catch: which adapter actually charges the car

Jackery explains the failure: power stations use a floating ground, and the EV charger 'detects the absence of a low-impedance path between the neutral and ground lines', treats that as a ground fault and opens its relay. What gets past this check is a route the maker built for it, and the makers built three different kinds:

  • EcoFlow Portable Power Station Grounding Adapter ($49). EcoFlow's US store says it lets a DELTA Pro, DELTA 2, DELTA 2 Max or DELTA Max 'charge your EV safely as an emergency backup power'. Its setup steps add the DELTA 3 Classic, DELTA 3 Max, DELTA 3 Max Plus, DELTA 3 Ultra and DELTA 3 Ultra Plus. The adapter links to the station by its own USB-C cable ('Only use the USB-C Cable included'), so a generic neutral-ground bonding plug is not the same thing. The DELTA Pro 3 is not on the list.
  • EcoFlow EV X-Stream Adapter ($99). This one works in the opposite direction. EcoFlow says it 'allows you to charge your EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3 / EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra / TITAN Series' at Level 2 J1772 stations, at up to 7,200 W into the station. It does nothing for a stranded car.
  • Anker F3800 Plus EV mode. This is built into the firmware with no accessory: double-press the AC Outlet Button and set the EV charger below 25 A. Anker lists the TT-30 and L14-30 outlets. The NEMA 14-50 appears only on the older F3800, so a 14-50 EVSE will not plug into the Plus.
  • Jackery Grounding Adapter ($49). Jackery describes it as 'simulated grounding' for the HomePower 3600 Pro Max and the Explorer 5000 Plus. The 5000 Plus needs a firmware update first. While the adapter is connected, charging from the wall is not allowed and the parallel port shuts off.

Cold weather cuts the miles. Tesla's manual says a plugged-in car 'uses the charging system, rather than the Battery itself, to keep the Battery warm'. InsideEVs reported that a Model 3 on a 9 A Level 1 cord at -15 °F stored only about 7% of the outlet's energy over 14 hours. In deep cold, the miles above can shrink to about a mile or less, and 240 V output from the F3800 Plus leaves more power after heating than a 12 A Level 1 cord does.

Not recommended

The EcoFlow DELTA Pro 3, bought to rescue a car. Its $99 EV X-Stream Adapter charges the station from an EV charger, and EcoFlow's grounding adapter does not list the DELTA Pro 3. For this job it is $2,799 of capacity with no route EcoFlow documents for getting that energy into a car. The BLUETTI Elite 300 has the same problem at a lower price ($1,199 on BLUETTI's store, 2026-11-17). It is a capable 3 kWh home-backup box, but BLUETTI sells no grounding accessory and publishes no EV-charging method for it.
