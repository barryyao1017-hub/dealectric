What the differences mean

  • Output gap: 20·log10(252 ÷ 140) = 5.1 dB. At the same volume setting, the two Audio-Technica decks play about 5 dB louder than any of the three Fluance decks.
  • Audio-Technica gain check: 20·log10(252 ÷ 4.0) = 36.0 dB, which matches the 36 dB in Audio-Technica's manual.
  • Fluance gain: Fluance doesn't publish a gain figure, so it is worked out from Fluance's own phono and line figures. RT80 and RT81: 20·log10(140 ÷ 3.5) = 32.0 dB. RT81+: 20·log10(140 ÷ 4.0) = 30.9 dB.
  • RT81+ over RT81: $349.99 − $299.99 = $50 for 0 dB more line output, because both put out 140 mV. The extra money buys the AT-VM95E cartridge, not a stronger signal.
  • Same-price pair: the AT-LP70X is $249.00 and the RT80 is $249.99. For the same money you choose between automatic play with 5.1 dB more output and a manual deck whose preamp can be fully bypassed.
  • Noise: Fluance's 67 dB is A-weighted, which reads 7 dB better than its own unweighted 60 dB. Audio-Technica doesn't state a weighting for its >55 dB, so 60 against 55 is a hint, not a ranking.

How a built-in preamp works, and why its line level matters

A moving-magnet cartridge puts out only a few millivolts. Audio-Technica rates the AT-LP70X's at 4.0 mV (1 kHz, 5 cm/s), and Fluance rates the RT81's at 3.5 mV and the RT81+'s at 4.0 mV. The built-in phono preamp does two jobs: it applies RIAA equalization and adds gain to reach line level. Set the rear switch to LINE for powered speakers or an amplifier's AUX input. Use PHONO only when the deck feeds a receiver's PHONO jack or an external phono stage.

Never use both preamps at once. A LINE signal plugged into a PHONO input is about 36 dB (63 times) stronger than the roughly 4 mV that input expects (4.0 mV × 10^(36/20) = 252 mV), and it gets equalized twice. The result is distortion and swollen bass.

Both preamps reach consumer line level, but not equally: 252 mV is −12.0 dBV and 140 mV is −17.1 dBV. With low-sensitivity powered speakers, or a Bluetooth speaker with a weak AUX input, the Fluance decks need the volume turned about 5 dB higher. In his RT81 review for Secrets of Home Theater and High Fidelity, John E. Johnson, Jr. wished for “higher output (0.5 volts)” from the built-in preamp. That is 20·log10(500 ÷ 140) = 11.1 dB more than it delivers. He also measured motor rumble 25 dB below a 1 kHz test tone recorded at −14 dB.

On speed, Thom Moon of SoundStage! Access (February 2024) used a phone speed app and read the RT81 at 33.5 rpm at the 33⅓ setting (33.5 ÷ 33.333 = 0.5% fast), with wow of ±0.17%. That is inside the ±1.00% speed tolerance Fluance publishes today, and Moon wrote that it “didn't seem to affect the music.”

Of Fluance's current decks, only the RT80, RT81 and RT81+ have a built-in preamp. The RT82 and up need an external phono stage.
