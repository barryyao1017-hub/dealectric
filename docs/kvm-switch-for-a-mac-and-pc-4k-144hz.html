What to know: DP 1.4 carries 4K120 at 8-bit without DSC and needs DSC on the host, the switch and the monitor for 4K144 at 10-bit; Apple lists its M3/M4 Pro MacBook Pros at 4K240 over HDMI and 6K60 over Thunderbolt but does not document DSC, and Level1Techs' own forum shows 4K144 through its KVM failing on LG monitors for reasons blamed on firmware and drivers, not the switch. KVMs without EDID emulation re-handshake on every switch, so the Mac rearranges windows (turn off "automatically rearrange Spaces") and may not wake (UGREEN KVM owners report closing and reopening the lid); TESmart and Cable Matters claim EDID emulation 2.0 and 4K144 but no independent Mac test exists. Why 4K144 breaks at the switch

4K at 144 Hz with 10-bit colour exceeds DP 1.4's uncompressed budget, so the host, the KVM and the monitor must all agree on Display Stream Compression; many "8K60" KVMs are DP 1.4 boxes that only pass DSC when every device negotiates it, which is why Level1Techs rates its switch at 4K120 and a forum member's LG 27GP950 pair held 4K144 only with the KVM removed. HDMI 2.1 (48 Gbps) carries 4K144 without DSC, and Apple's M3/M4 Pro HDMI ports do 4K240, but UGREEN's HDMI 2.1 KVM passes one HDMI link from an M-series Mac and most HDMI KVMs are 4K60. Macs add a second problem: a KVM without EDID emulation makes macOS see a new display on every switch, so windows and Spaces shuffle and sleep-wake sometimes fails; the vendor emulation claims (TESmart, Cable Matters) have no Mac test behind them.

Apple's own limits

Apple's external-display page lists the M4 and M4 Pro MacBook Pro at one display up to 4K240 (two displays at 4K144 each), the M2 Pro and M3 Pro at 4K240 over HDMI or 6K60 over Thunderbolt, and the base M3 at 4K144 over HDMI; Thunderbolt 4 carries DP 1.4 and Thunderbolt 5 DP 2.1. Owners still report USB-C-to-DP cables not recognised on an M4 Pro, a Mac Studio capped at 100 Hz on a 144 Hz BenQ, and a DisplayPort-version toggle needed after reconnects, all with direct connections, so the KVM is not the only variable.

The setup that works

Pick a 4K144-or-better monitor with a built-in KVM and a USB-C upstream (M27UP, PD3226G, Gigabyte M32U), run the Mac to the USB-C port with a USB-C-to-DP 1.4 or Thunderbolt cable, the PC to DisplayPort or HDMI 2.1, and let the monitor's input switch move the USB hub between them; where the hub lacks ports or you want a physical button for a USB mic, drives or a 10 Gb/s SSD, our UGREEN switch toggles four USB-C/A 10 Gb/s ports between two computers with its own button. Keep the Mac's charger: monitor USB-C power ranges from 18 W (M27UP) to 90 W, and no KVM here charges a laptop.
