What the differences mean

  • Any DisplayPort 1.4 KVM needs DSC for 4K 144 Hz. 3840 × 2160 × 144 Hz × 24 bit = 28.67 Gb/s of picture data before blanking, against DP 1.4's 25.92 Gb/s payload (VESA). That is 111% of the link, so it has to be compressed.
  • Why Level1Techs stops at 4K 120 Hz. 3840 × 2160 × 120 × 24 = 23.89 Gb/s, which is 92% of 25.92 Gb/s. It fits without compression once blanking is added, but only just.
  • HDMI 2.1 has room. On a 48G link with a 42.0 Gb/s payload, 4K 144 Hz takes 28.67 ÷ 42.0 = 68% at 8-bit and 35.83 ÷ 42.0 = 85% at 10-bit, before blanking.
  • AV Access B10's Mac side. Its USB-C laptop input is listed at 120 Hz, which is 120 ÷ 144 = 83% of the refresh you asked for.
  • Cost of a video switch. TESmart HKS201-M24 at 269.00 minus the UGREEN switch at 40.99 is 228.01 extra for a middle box whose maker's page makes no Mac claim.

Which MacBook Pro port Apple rates for 4K 144 Hz

Apple's display guide for the 2024 M4, M4 Pro and M4 Max MacBook Pro lists every Thunderbolt setup at 'up to 6K resolution at 60 Hz'. It lists 'one external display with up to 4K resolution at 144 Hz using the HDMI port', or a single display at 4K 240 Hz on HDMI. The M3 Pro/Max spec page has the same split: '6K resolution at 60Hz over Thunderbolt' and '4K resolution at 144Hz over HDMI'. Apple's HDMI support page puts the M2 Pro and later on its 4K 240 Hz HDMI list. The same page says 'Adapters aren't supported for these resolutions and refresh rates. Connect displays directly to your Mac for best results.' It doesn't mention KVMs, but a direct HDMI cable from the Mac to the monitor is the one path Apple documents.

This is why a USB-C-to-DisplayPort cable into a DP 1.4 KVM has no Apple rating for 4K 144 Hz on these Macs. It also rules out a monitor's built-in KVM when that KVM is tied to the monitor's USB-C input. If your monitor's KVM can pair its USB upstream port with the HDMI input, use it. If it can't, keep the Mac on HDMI and switch keyboard and mouse with $40.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-usb-c-switch-2-computers-4-port-10gbps. Only the current M5 Pro and M5 Max ('6K at 60Hz or 4K at 144Hz' over 'any combination of Thunderbolt and HDMI ports') and the M6 (two displays at 4K 165 Hz) are rated for 4K 144 Hz or more over USB-C.
