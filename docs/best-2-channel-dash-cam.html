A 2-channel dash cam is two cameras recording at the same time. On this page that means front (the road ahead) plus rear (the road behind). The count is a real line because each step changes what is recorded: a 1-channel camera records nothing behind the car, and a third (cabin) camera can take data from the front picture. On the Viofo A229 Pro, DashCamTalk forum tester Panzer Platform measured the front stream at about 57 Mbps alone and with the rear camera attached, and about 35 Mbps once a cabin camera was added. Every row above is sold by its maker as a front-plus-rear kit (Viofo's “2CH” listings, Vantrue's “4K + 4K”). Left out: front-plus-cabin kits such as Viofo's A329S 2CH IR; 3-channel kits such as the 70mai T800, which DashCamTalk calls “a much better buy” than the Vantrue because it adds a cabin camera; and the Vantrue S1 Pro Max 4K + 2.5K ($199.99, $199.99: https://dealectric.com/go/vantrue-s1-pro-max-4k-2-5k), because DashCamTalk's bitrate table covers only the 4K + 4K kit. Dealectric does not test units: the measurements are DashCamTalk's and its forum testers', and the prices are each maker's US store on 2026-11-26.

What the differences mean

  • Hours on a card. GB per hour = total Mbps × 0.45 (3,600 seconds ÷ 8 bits ÷ 1,000). A329S: (65 + 27) × 0.45 = 41.4 GB per hour, and 256 ÷ 41.4 = 6.2 hours before the loop overwrites (12.4 hours on 512 GB). A229 Pro: (57 + 25) × 0.45 = 36.9, and 256 ÷ 36.9 = 6.9 hours. A229 Ultra: (36 + 35) × 0.45 = 32.0, so 8.0 hours. Vantrue S1 Pro Max 4K + 4K: (32 + 24) × 0.45 = 25.2, so 10.2 hours, and 1,000 ÷ 25.2 = 39.7 hours on its 1 TB maximum. A229 Plus: (29 + 24) × 0.45 = 23.9, so 10.7 hours. Audio, locked clips and formatting take a little off each figure.
  • Data per frame on the front camera. Bitrate ÷ (width × height × frames per second). At 3840 × 2160 and 30 fps that is 248.8 million pixels a second. A229 Pro: 57 ÷ 248.8 = 0.23 bit per pixel. A329S at 60 fps: 65 ÷ 497.7 = 0.13, twice the frames with fewer bits in each. A229 Ultra: 36 ÷ 248.8 = 0.14. Vantrue: 32 ÷ 248.8 = 0.13, in H.265. The Viofos record H.264, so the Vantrue figure does not compare megabit for megabit.
  • What a 4K rear costs the front. A229 Ultra against A229 Pro, same front sensor, resolution, frame rate and codec: (57 − 36) ÷ 57 = 37% less data on the front picture. The rear gets 35 Mbps for 4K (35 ÷ 248.8 = 0.14 bit per pixel) against 25 Mbps for 2K on the Pro (25 ÷ 110.6 = 0.23).
  • Price with parking mode. A329S 2CH $459.99 + HK6 $25.99 = $485.98. A229 Pro 2CH $349.99 + HK4 $17.99 = $367.98. The gap is $118.00 with kits and $110.00 for the cameras alone (Viofo's US store, 2026-11-26).

What the rear camera does to the front picture

Makers print resolution. Bitrate, the megabits written for each second of video, shows how much data each camera keeps, and Viofo's and Vantrue's listings do not give it. These are the figures DashCamTalk publishes, with the codec:

  • Viofo A329S: front 3840 × 2160 at 60 fps, 65 Mbps; rear 2560 × 1440 at 30 fps, 27 Mbps; H.264 (review published 2026-01-04). DashCamTalk calls it “currently the benchmark for 3-channel video quality”.
  • Viofo A229 Pro: front 57 Mbps, rear 25 Mbps, H.264 (2023-12-22). Panzer Platform's file sizes from 2023-09-28: 430 MB per 1-minute front file alone and with the rear camera (430 ÷ 7.5 = about 57 Mbps), 174 MB for the rear (about 23 Mbps), and 260 MB for the front (about 35 Mbps) once the cabin camera is attached. He puts the difference down to an extra processor inside the rear camera, and notes H.264 is the only codec offered.
  • Viofo A229 Ultra: front 36 Mbps, rear 35 Mbps, both 3840 × 2160 at 30 fps, H.264. The table also lists the optional cabin camera at 35 Mbps and does not say which setup the figures come from, so the front figure may be the three-camera one.
  • Vantrue S1 Pro Max 4K + 4K: front 32 Mbps, rear 24 Mbps, both 3840 × 2160 at 30 fps, H.265 (2026-01-12). DashCamTalk: “an excellent dual camera dash cam”, with “larger size and high cost for a dual camera” as the main negative.
  • Viofo A229 Plus: front 29 Mbps, rear 24 Mbps, both 2560 × 1440 at 30 fps, H.264 (2024-01-01).

Two cautions. DashCamTalk's tables do not state how many cameras were connected for each figure; only the A229 Pro has a named two-camera measurement. And a firmware update can change a bitrate, so treat these as the published figures on the dates given.

Not recommended

The Viofo A229 Ultra 2CH at Viofo's $409.99. It costs $60 more than the A229 Pro 2CH (409.99 − 349.99) and, on DashCamTalk's table, writes 37% less data to the same 4K 30 fps front picture to pay for its 4K rear. DashCamTalk's summary: “Main drawbacks are high price, and not the best rear plate capture depending on lighting conditions.” If a 4K rear is the point, the Vantrue S1 Pro Max 4K + 4K puts the same IMX678 sensor at both ends for $239.99.

Adding a cabin camera to an A229 Pro for front evidence. Panzer Platform measured the front stream falling from about 57 to about 35 Mbps with it attached. Bought as a front-plus-rear kit, it keeps the full figure.
