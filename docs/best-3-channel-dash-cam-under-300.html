What the differences mean

All four cameras are 3-channel: a front camera, a cabin camera and a rear camera recording at the same time. Each was under $300 for the camera alone on 2026-11-21. None comes with a memory card, and none runs parking mode from the 12 V socket cable. Vantrue's pages for both of its cameras say parking mode “must be hardwired (hardwire kits are sold separately)”, and VIOFO's page says “VIOFO hardwire kit is required for Parking Mode”. So the useful figure is how much of the $300 is left for the camera.

  • Kit only. Vantrue's Voltage Display Hardwire Kit, whose listing names the N4S and N4 Pro S, was $29.99 at vantrue.com on 2026-11-21: $300 − $29.99 = $270.01 left for the camera. VIOFO's HK4 was $17.99 at viofo.com and on Amazon: $300 − $17.99 = $282.01.
  • Kit plus a 64 GB card. A SanDisk High Endurance 64 GB card was $25.99 on Amazon on 2026-11-21. Vantrue: $29.99 + $25.99 = $55.98, which leaves $244.02. VIOFO: $17.99 + $25.99 = $43.98, which leaves $256.02.
  • Where each camera lands. At the prices read on 2026-11-21, the N4 Pro S ($279.99) is under $300 alone and above the $270.01 line, and the N4S ($199.99) is below both Vantrue lines. Vantrue's store gives no end date for these sale prices, so compare the price you see with the line. The VIOFO A229 Pro 3CH on Amazon comes to $249.99 + $17.99 = $267.98 with the HK4 and $267.98 + $25.99 = $293.97 with the card. The A229 Plus 3CH comes to $209.95 + $17.99 = $227.94 and $227.94 + $25.99 = $253.93.
  • How long a card lasts. DashCamTalk lists a bitrate for each camera. N4 Pro S: 32.0 + 14.0 + 9.8 = 55.8 Mbps; 55.8 × 3,600 ÷ 8 ÷ 1,000 = 25.1 GB an hour, so 64 ÷ 25.1 = about 2.5 hours before loop recording overwrites, and about 39.8 hours on a 1 TB card. N4S: 3 × 14.8 = 44.4 Mbps = 20.0 GB an hour, so about 3.2 hours on 64 GB and about 50 hours on 1 TB. A229 Plus 3CH: 29 + 24 + 15 = 68 Mbps = 30.6 GB an hour, so about 2.1 hours on 64 GB and 512 ÷ 30.6 = about 16.7 hours on its 512 GB maximum. The A229 Pro 3CH is left out of this sum because we have a bitrate for only two of its three cameras.

What $300 covers once parking mode and a card are added

Why the kit is needed. All four cameras use a supercapacitor, which handles heat well and stores no recording time. Vantrue's N4 Pro S manual says of motion detection: “The device must remain continuously powered; it will automatically stop working if the power is cut off.” The N4S manual says: “If the power is disconnected, the dash cam will automatically shut down.” A hardwire kit feeds the camera from the fuse box while the car is parked. Vantrue's Voltage Display kit cuts power at 11.6, 12 or 12.4 V so the car battery is not drained. VIOFO's HK4 is a 13.1 ft (4 m) USB-C kit, and its listing names the A229 Plus and A229 Pro.

No resolution drop with three cameras on. Makers often quote the front camera alone. Vantrue's manuals list the three-camera settings: 4K+1080P+1440P at 30 fps (front, cabin, rear) on the N4 Pro S, and 1944P+1440P+1440P at 30 fps on the N4S. VIOFO's page gives 4K P30 + 2K P30 + 1080 P30 for the A229 Pro 3CH. Vantrue's “2.5K” and VIOFO's “2K” rear cameras are the same 2560 × 1440.

Card size. A 64 GB card is what fits the budget, and it holds two to three hours of three-camera footage before the oldest clips are overwritten. That covers a commute and the minutes around a crash. For longer history a larger card is needed, and the Vantrue pair take up to 1 TB against 512 GB for the VIOFO pair.
