How long a 2kWh power station lasts in an RV

A 12 V compressor fridge uses about 0.7 kWh a day, a furnace blower 0.3–0.5 kWh on a cold night, and lights, phones and the water pump about 0.2 kWh: roughly 1.2–1.5 kWh a day. That is about a day and a half with no sun, and two to three days with 200–400 W of solar. A 13,500 BTU rooftop AC needs 2,800–3,500 W to start (about 2,000 W with a soft starter) and runs 1,300–1,900 W, so a 2kWh station runs it for about an hour.

Recharging on the road

  • Campground or home outlet: about 1–1.5 hours on a 15/20 A outlet.
  • Driving: a DC-DC alternator charger adds 560–800 W; the 12 V socket gives about 100 W, a full day for a full charge.
  • Solar: a 400 W panel returns about 1–1.5 kWh on a good day. The AC200L and Renogy take 1,200 W, the Elite 200 V2 1,000 W, Anker 800 W, Jackery 400 W.

The Elite 200 V2 is lighter and rated for twice the cycles of the AC200L, but has no RV outlet: it needs a 5-20P to TT-30R adapter and is then limited to that outlet's 20 A. Jackery's Explorer 2000 v2 is a popular pick but was $999 on Jackery's store on 2026-10-31 and has no RV outlet.
