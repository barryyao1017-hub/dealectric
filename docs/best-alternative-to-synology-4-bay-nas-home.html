What the differences mean

  • Copying a 100 GB folder. 100,000 MB ÷ 606 MB/s ≈ 165 s, or about 2 min 45 s, to the DXP4800 Plus over 10GbE. 100,000 MB ÷ 290 MB/s ≈ 345 s, or about 5 min 45 s, to the TS-464. That makes the UGREEN 606 ÷ 290 ≈ 2.1× as fast.
  • A 2.5GbE network caps every box. 2,500 Mb/s ÷ 8 = 312.5 MB/s before protocol overhead. KitGuru's 275 MB/s small-file writes for the UGREEN on 2.5GbE and Club386's 290 MB/s for the QNAP are both at that ceiling. Club386's real copy (16.4 GB ÷ 62 s ≈ 265 MB/s) is too.
  • What the 10GbE port costs at idle. 30.45 W − 24.25 W = 6.2 W. Over a year that is 6.2 W × 8,760 h ≈ 54 kWh, even when nothing is being copied (KitGuru). If you only connect over 2.5GbE, you don't pay it.
  • The Synology's 522–565 MB/s needs both ports combined. CGMagazine reached it with link aggregation and SMB multichannel, so you need a switch and a computer that support that. A single 2.5GbE link stops near 300 MB/s.
  • Price. On the makers' US stores today, the UGREEN at $619.99 costs $19.01 less than the QNAP at $639.00 and measured about twice as fast. The TerraMaster F4-425 Plus at $539.99 is $80.00 under the UGREEN, with 5GbE in between.
  • Drive policy now. Synology's 2025 Plus models running DSM 7.3 can build storage pools on third-party SATA drives Synology hasn't validated. M.2 pools and M.2 cache still need drives on Synology's compatibility list (Synology, October 8, 2025). Drive rules are no longer a strong reason to switch. Plex transcoding and network speed still are.

Measured speed and power, side by side

Each figure comes from a named reviewer's own test, and the conditions differ, so read the test notes before comparing. KitGuru tested the DXP4800 Plus with WD Red WD60EFRX drives over both of its ports. Writes reached 606 MB/s on 10GbE in RAID 0, and small-file reads reached 865–900 MB/s from RAID 0 to RAID 10. Over the 2.5GbE port, small-file writes stopped at 275 MB/s. Club386 tested the TS-464 with four 8 TB Seagate IronWolf drives in RAID 10 from a 10GbE client. The NAS itself only has 2.5GbE, so CrystalDiskMark topped out just over 290 MB/s. NASCompares measured the F4-425 Plus at 500–520 MB/s in RAID 5 with four Toshiba MG08 drives, which fills one 5GbE port. CGMagazine measured the DS925+ at about 522 MB/s read and 565 MB/s write with its two 2.5GbE ports working together.

Power follows the network speed. KitGuru measured the UGREEN at 24.25 W idle on 2.5GbE and 30.45 W on 10GbE. CGMagazine measured the DS925+ at up to 28 W idle. NASCompares measured about 55 W idle for the F4-425 Plus with four 7,200 rpm drives, and those drives account for much of that figure. Club386's 19 W for the TS-464 is with its drives in standby, so it is the lowest figure in the table but was taken in a different state.

The CPU matters more than speed for streaming. Jellyfin's documentation says Intel AV1 decoding needs Tiger Lake or newer. The UGREEN's Pentium Gold 8505 is Alder Lake (Intel), which is newer. The QNAP's Celeron N5095 is Jasper Lake (Notebookcheck). Jellyfin says Jasper Lake has no AV1 acceleration and is "losing support for QSV on Linux". CGMagazine notes that the Synology's Ryzen V1500B can't hardware-transcode in Plex.

Not recommended

QNAP TS-464 at today's price. On QNAP's US store it costs $19.01 more than the UGREEN, and the store gives a 2–4 week lead time. Club386 measured it at about half the UGREEN's 10GbE write speed. Its M.2 slots run at PCIe Gen 3 x1 and RAM stops at 16 GB (QNAP). Its Jasper Lake chip can't decode AV1 (Jellyfin). Its one measured advantage is 19 W with drives in standby (Club386). Pick it only if you already run QNAP's QTS software and plan to add a 10GbE card in its PCIe Gen 3 x2 slot.
