The Synology policy, precisely

In April 2025 Synology required listed (in practice Synology-branded) hard drives for new pools on its 2025 Plus models (DS925+, DS1525+, DS1825+, DS225+, DS425+, DS725+), with deduplication, lifespan analysis and automatic drive firmware limited to its own drives, and third-party M.2 barred from pools and caches. On 2025-10-08 Synology announced that DSM 7.3 lets 2025 Plus, Value and J models install and create storage pools on non-validated third-party drives; its footnote keeps M.2 pools and caches on listed drives, and its compatibility policy page says unlisted drives get limited support and no cover for drive-caused faults. Reports of a warning at pool creation are secondary. The DS225+'s own list still shows only Synology drives.

Where each alternative stands for a home user

  • UGREEN DXP4800 Plus: WunderTech's default Plex pick for most people in 2026 (one or two 4K transcodes); Docker (engine 29 in UGOS 1.19), Btrfs snapshots, Time Machine (firmware 1.9+), UGREENlink remote access without port forwarding; no native Plex package (run it in Docker with the iGPU passed through); reviewers (ITPro, Digitec, KitGuru) call the hardware excellent and the software behind DSM; the fan gets loud under sustained load.
  • TerraMaster F4-425 Plus: the budget Quick Sync Plex box with Plex and Jellyfin in its app store, 16 GB soldered, 5GbE; TOS 6 is rated less polished than UGOS or DSM.
  • QNAP TS-464: mature QTS and Container Station, 2.5GbE only, priced like the DXP4800 Plus without the 10GbE.
  • Asustor: the Nimbustor 4 Gen2 is the N5105 value box with 3-year warranty; the Lockerstor 4 Gen3 is a $1,300 prosumer unit with no GPU.
  • Ubiquiti UNAS 4: a storage appliance for UniFi households; no Docker or apps, iOS-only photo backup, reported NIC drops.
  • DIY TrueNAS or Unraid: the most capable route if you build it; not an appliance.
