What the differences mean

Apple lists six fixed torque drivers for iPhone screws, from 0.35 to 0.85 kgf·cm. In N·m: 0.35 × 0.0980665 = 0.034 N·m, and 0.85 × 0.0980665 = 0.083 N·m.

  • Low step on Fanttik and HOTO: 0.05 ÷ 0.0980665 = 0.51 kgf·cm. That falls between Apple's green (0.45) and gray (0.55) drivers.
  • iFixit: 0.15 ÷ 0.083 = 1.8× Apple's heaviest iPhone spec. Ryobi: 0.2 ÷ 0.083 = 2.4×.
  • E1 MAX on a laptop: 0.2 ÷ 0.181 = 1.10, so the high step covers Lenovo's 0.181 N·m spec for the ThinkPad T420's M2 × 3 mm screw. For an M2.5 screw at 0.392 N·m, 0.392 ÷ 0.2 = 1.96, so you finish it in manual mode.
  • E2 Ultra and E2 MAX: 0.4 ÷ 0.392 = 1.02. The 0.4 N·m step lands on the M2.5 spec.

The torque specs device makers publish, and how these drivers stop

Apple's iPhone service pages tell technicians to "use only the torque driver indicated to install new screws during reassembly" and warn that "both overtightened screws and loose screws can damage parts." Any torque driver is fine for removing screws. The same Apple page also says removed screws aren't reinstalled: new screws go in because the adhesive can't be reused. So the low step matters when you put a device back together, not when you take it apart.

Even the 0.05 N·m step is 1.46× Apple's black spec (0.05 ÷ 0.0343) and 1.13× the green spec (0.05 ÷ 0.0441). For those screws, run the screw down on the low step and seat it by hand. Fanttik's E1 MAX guide gives the same advice for iPhone P2 pentalobe screws: use the low torque setting and "finish the last quarter-turn by hand." Fanttik also explains how the limit works: "Auto mode cuts torque when it senses resistance equal to its setting." When a screw needs more than the motor gives, the E1 MAX's manual mode goes up to 3 N·m. iFixit lists 1.0 N·m manual torque for its driver.

Laptop screws need more torque. Lenovo's ThinkPad T420 service manual specifies 0.181 N·m (1.85 kgf·cm) for an M2 × 3 mm screw. IBM's ThinkPad T30 manual gives 0.392 N·m (4 kgf·cm) for M2.5 screws and 0.245 N·m (2.5 kgf·cm) for M2 × 16 mm screws. That last figure is 1.23× the E1 MAX's 0.2 N·m cap (0.245 ÷ 0.2), so longer M2 screws need a hand finish on the E1 MAX as well.

How close the steps are to their ratings: Fanttik's own bench test of a 0.2 N·m setting read 0.192–0.208 N·m over the five runs it published. Fanttik ran that test itself and doesn't name the tester's make or model, and it published no reading for its 0.05 N·m step. Gidon at The Technology Man measured both gears of the HOTO Electric Precision Screwdriver Kit Pro, rated 0.05 and 0.2 N·m, at between 0.1 and 0.2 N·m. That puts the low gear at twice its rating or more. He tested the Kit Pro, not the ADV in the table.
