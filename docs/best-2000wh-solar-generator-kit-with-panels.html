What the differences mean

  • Panel cost inside the kit is the kit price minus the bare station's price on the same store on the same day, divided by the panel watts. ALLPOWERS: ($948 − $649) ÷ 400 W = $0.75/W. EcoFlow: ($1,579 − $1,029) ÷ 440 W = $1.25/W. BLUETTI 2 × 200 W: ($1,499 − $899) ÷ 400 W = $1.50/W. Jackery 2 × 200 W: ($1,649 − $999) ÷ 400 W = $1.63/W. Anker C2000 Gen 2: ($1,999.99 − $1,199.99) ÷ 400 W = $2.00/W.
  • Kit price per Wh: ALLPOWERS $948 ÷ 1,920 Wh = $0.49. BLUETTI 2 × 200 W $1,499 ÷ 2,073.6 Wh = $0.72. EcoFlow $1,579 ÷ 2,048 Wh = $0.77.
  • Capacity gap: the R2500 V2 holds 2,048 − 1,920 = 128 Wh (about 6%) less than the 2,048 Wh stations. That is why we call it a 2 kWh-class kit.
  • Sun-hours to refill, assuming the panels deliver 75% of their rating: ALLPOWERS 1,920 ÷ (400 × 0.75) = 6.4 h. BLUETTI 2 × 200 W 2,073.6 ÷ 300 = 6.9 h. BLUETTI single 200 W 2,073.6 ÷ 150 = 13.8 h. EcoFlow states 5.4 h for its two-panel kit.
  • More panel for $100 more: ALLPOWERS's R2500 V2 + 600 W kit costs $1,048, so ($1,048 − $649) ÷ 600 W = $0.67/W and $1,048 ÷ 1,920 Wh = $0.55/Wh.

What you pay for the panels in each kit

Each maker here also sells the station alone on the same store, so the kit price minus that bare price is what the panels cost you. Results, from cheapest to dearest per panel watt:

  • ALLPOWERS, $0.75/W. $649 + $299 = $948: the kit costs exactly the station plus the SP037 panel bought on its own, so the bundle saves nothing. The panel is cheap, though. One caveat: the SP037 is polycrystalline, and ALLPOWERS's own S2000 Pro + SP037 listing says a 100 W polycrystalline panel 'can output about 55W-65W'. At 55%, the 400 W panel gives 220 W. That works out to $299 ÷ 220 = $1.36 per delivered watt and 1,920 ÷ 220 = 8.7 h of sun to refill. Even then it is still the cheapest kit here by $251.
  • EcoFlow, $1.25/W. $550 buys two 220 W bifacial panels. Note the warranties: 12 months on the panels and 5 years on the DELTA 2 Max.
  • BLUETTI, $1.50/W. The SP200L costs $399 on its own, so two would be $798. The 2 × 200 W kit charges $600 for them, a $198 saving. The Elite 200 V2's 1,000 W rating applies to a single 12–60 V, 20 A input. With SP200L panels, three in series reach 3 × 24.6 V = 73.8 V, over the 60 V limit, so a series string tops out at two panels and 49.2 V. Even at the open-circuit voltage, 49.2 V × 20 A = 984 W, so these panels can't reach the full 1,000 W.
  • Jackery, $1.20–$1.63/W. The $1,239 kit with 2 × 100 W Air panels works out to ($1,239 − $999) ÷ 200 W = $1.20/W. The 2 × 200 W kit costs $1.63/W. Both are on pre-order, shipping Nov 16.
  • Anker, $1.75–$3.00/W. S2000 + PS400: ($1,699.99 − $999.99) ÷ 400 W = $1.75/W. The C2000 Gen 2 kits are below.

Not recommended

Avoid the Anker SOLIX C2000 Gen 2 + 400 W Solar Panel kit on Anker's store. It costs $800 more than the station for one 400 W PS400 panel ($2.00/W), which puts the kit at $0.98/Wh, twice the ALLPOWERS kit's figure. The 200 W bifacial version is worse: ($1,799.99 − $1,199.99) ÷ 200 W = $3.00/W. The station itself is sound (2,048 Wh, 2,400 W, 800 W solar input); the problem is the panel premium in these kits.
