What the differences mean

  • The two-device budget. The UGREEN 100W 4-Port gives 65 + 30 = 95 W of its 100 W. The Anker Prime 100W gives 65 + 35 = 100 W, which is all of it. The UGREEN 200W still gives 65 + 65 = 130 W after it throttles.
  • Anker's throttle compared with the laptop's 65 W. In Smartzone's test the Anker settled at 60 W, and 65 − 60 = 5 W short of the laptop's 65 W before the phone takes anything. Smartzone loaded a single port at 100 W, and a 65 W + 35 W split is the same 100 W total. How the Anker shares a throttled 60 W between two ports was never measured, so that part is our inference.
  • Phone speed. Apple gives 40 W or more as the adapter needed for an iPhone 17's fast charge. The 100 W models' phone ports fall short: 40 − 30 = 10 W short on the UGREENs and 40 − 35 = 5 W short on the Anker. The 200W's second port gives up to 100 W, or 65 W after throttling, which clears that line. On PPS, which Samsung phones use, Smartzone measured 5–21 V at 5 A on C1 and C2 of the 200W. techtest.org measured 3.3–21 V at 3 A on C1 and C2 of the 100W 4-Port and 5–11 V on the Anker.
  • Cable. 65 W at 20 V is 3.25 A (65 ÷ 20), which is more than a 3 A cable carries. Use a 5 A e-marked cable for the laptop. Anker's page says to use an e-marker cable and does not include one in the box.
  • Port position. On the Anker, the 65 W is only on USB-C1, the upper port. Anker's table drops USB-C2 + USB-A to 12 W + 12 W. On the UGREEN 100W 4-Port the laptop goes on USB-C1 and the phone on USB-C2.
  • Price. The UGREEN 200W is $67.99 and the 100W 4-Port is $42.99. The Retractable is $42.49. Anker's US store had the Prime 100W at $45.99 (list $69.99) on 2026-11-27. It was a pre-order shipping Nov 25 – Dec 5, 2026, and the page shows no sale end date.

How long each charger holds the laptop's 65 W

A port table tells you how many watts a charger gives at the moment you plug in. Whether it keeps giving them once it heats up is a separate question, and only a lab test answers it. These are the tests we found:

  • UGREEN Nexode 200W (X764), Smartzone, 14 May 2025. Two ports ran at 100 W each, and after an average of 60 minutes both dropped to 65 W. The housing reached 77 °C and the open ports up to 92 °C. A single port at 140 W, or 100 W + 60 W, held for more than two hours. This is the only two-port long-load test among these chargers, and the laptop port stayed at 65 W after it throttled.
  • UGREEN Nexode 100W 4-Port (CD226), techtest.org, Michael Barton, 18 Nov 2021. It passed his 6-hour test at a constant 100 W and, in his words, 'heated up massively' ('erwärmte sich massiv'). He gives no °C figure. With port 1 drawing about 70 W, plugging in a second device did not reset port 1. Port 1 was reset only once port 2 drew more than 30 W.
  • Anker Prime 100W, Smartzone, 12 Sep 2024. The test ran 90 minutes at 100 W in a 24 °C room. The housing reached 55 °C at 10 minutes, and the charger then cut to 60 W and stayed there, peaking at 72 °C on the housing and 85 °C at the ports. Smartzone gives no model number, but its 170 g weight and 65/35 W split match the A2688. techtest.org (Michael Barton, 7 Nov 2024) measured about one hour at 100 W before it overheated and throttled.
  • UGREEN Nexode 100W Retractable, Hi-Tech Century, 24 Dec 2025. The reviewer used it nonstop for several hours across port combinations and found it 'got slightly warm' without overheating. Hi-Tech Century ran no fixed full-power load and reports no throttled wattage.

The 100W tests loaded one port at 100 W rather than 65 W + 30–35 W. The total load is about the same (95–100 W), but no lab measured which port these chargers trim first when they heat up.

Not recommended for this: Baseus PicoGo Pro AH22

All three USB-C ports give 100 W when used alone. Two published tests disagree on the two-device split. techtest.org (Michael Barton, 23 Nov 2026) lists USB-C1 + USB-C2 at 45 W + 45 W, which is 65 − 45 = 20 W short for the laptop. Smartzone (30 Sep 2026) lists 67 W + 30 W in Smart mode when the load is at 20 V and 45 W + 45 W below 20 V. Turbo mode gives 80 W + 15 W and Gentle mode 25 W + 20 W. techtest.org also measured 100 W for about 40 minutes before it fell to 80 W. Whether your laptop gets 65 W from it depends on the mode and on the voltage the laptop asks for, so it is a poor buy for this question.
