What the differences mean

  • The 4K 60 Hz budget: 3840 × 2160 × 60 = 497.7 million pixels/s, and 1920 × 1080 × 240 is exactly the same, 497.7 million. That is why the HKS201-P23 can list both.
  • 1440p 144 Hz doesn't fit: 2560 × 1440 × 144 = 530.8 million pixels/s, and 530.8 ÷ 497.7 = 1.067, so 6.7% over the budget. At 165 Hz it's 608.3 million, 22% over. The P23's list stops at 1440p 120 Hz (442.4 million).
  • Ultrawide: 3440 × 1440 × 100 = 495.4 million, just under the budget, so the P23 lists 100 Hz. The M24's 3440 × 1440 × 144 = 713.3 million.
  • Price of the extra refresh: $269.00 − $129.99 = $139.01 moves you from the P23 to the M24, which lists 1440p at 240 Hz (884.7 million pixels/s, no DSC).

Why a 4K 60 Hz KVM caps a 1440p monitor at 120 Hz

A KVM's video rating is a pixel budget, not a list of separate features. Multiply each mode TESmart lists for the HKS201-P23 and the result never goes above 497.7 million pixels/s: 4K 60 (497.7), 1080p 240 (497.7), 3440×1440 100 (495.4), 1440p 120 (442.4). These counts are active pixels only. The real signal also carries blanking intervals, so the true load is higher still. A 1440p 144 Hz monitor needs 530.8 million and isn't on the list, so through this class of switch it runs at 120 Hz. UGREEN's one-monitor HDMI KVM (model B0CG1W5K7C) has the same ceiling. Its listing gives '4K@60Hz, also support 4K@30Hz, 2560*1440@120Hz or lower'.

To get past the budget over HDMI, you need an HDMI 2.1 switch such as the HKS201-M24. Each computer's HDMI port also has to output the mode itself. Over DisplayPort, StarTech's D86A2-2-PORT-8K-KVM shares '8K 60Hz DisplayPort between two DisplayPort enabled computers', and its listing adds 'DSC support required for maximum performance' and a 5120×1440 ultrawide limit of 60 Hz.

EDID emulation decides how a switch feels day to day. TESmart explains that without it 'the inactive computer may behave as if the monitor was unplugged', and windows 'may all move to one screen or shift to unexpected positions'. All four TESmart switches above list it.

Not recommended

UGREEN HDMI KVM Switch, 1 monitor, 2 computers (B0CG1W5K7C), if you keep windows open on both computers. Its listing says 'It does Not support EDID Emulation and Hotkey Switching'. You switch with a button or a desktop controller, and it is 'Not supported for use with docking station'. It tops out at 1440p 120 Hz like the others in its class. TESmart's $43.99 B Series has the same 4K 60 Hz class with both EDID emulation and hotkeys.
