What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test printers. Every figure here comes from the maker or from a named tester, and the arithmetic is shown.

  • X2D with the bed on: 20 min ÷ 2 ≈ 10 min to 65 °C. This is our arithmetic from CNC Kitchen's 'roughly half', not a separate measurement.
  • X2D vs H2D: both reach the chamber temperature for ABS-class work in about 10 min with the bed helping. The X2D was timed to 65 °C and the H2D to 60 °C, and the X2D costs less than half as much ($589 vs $1,399 at 3D Universe on 2026-11-15).
  • Chamber watts per litre of cabinet (QIDI's figures): Plus5 550 W ÷ (50.0 × 48.8 × 55.8 cm = 136.2 L) = 4.04 W/L. Q2 280 W ÷ (40.2 × 43.8 × 49.4 cm = 87.0 L) = 3.22 W/L. Plus4 400 W ÷ (50.5 × 48.7 × 55.0 cm = 135.3 L) = 2.96 W/L. Max4 500 W ÷ (55.8 × 57.8 × 61.2 cm = 197.4 L) = 2.53 W/L. Cabinet volume stands in for chamber air because QIDI does not state the air volume.
  • Plus4 on current firmware: 0.4 × 400 W = 160 W, and 160 W ÷ 135.3 L = 1.18 W/L. The Plus5 puts 550 ÷ 160 = 3.4× as much chamber-heater power into a cabinet of almost the same size.

Who timed what, and on which power

The only chamber times taken with instruments by a named tester are CNC Kitchen's (Stefan Hermann). On the X2D, thermocouples in the chamber read a real 65 °C after about 20 minutes with only the chamber heater on. On the H2D, chamber and bed together reached 60 °C in about 10 minutes. The X2D was a 230 V European unit. CNC Kitchen does not say which voltage its H2D ran on, and it expects US and 110 V units of both printers to be slower. Creality makes the same point about the K2 Plus: below a 15 °C room temperature on 110 V, its chamber may not reach target.

No QIDI chamber time has a named tester. The Plus4's '60 °C in about 5 minutes' comes from QIDI's own blog, which gives no room temperature, sensor position or bed state. QIDI's spec pages for the Plus5, Q2 and Max4 give heater wattage but no times. For the Plus5, QIDI only says the bed runs at full power for about 3–5 minutes during warm-up.

Nozzle and bed times: none of the makers or named reviewers we read publishes a time for these printers, such as 25 → 220 °C nozzle or 25 → 100 °C bed. Chamber warm-up is the step that holds up ABS, ASA and nylon prints, so that is what the table ranks. If you first want to know whether a printer's chamber is heated at all, see enclosed 3D printers with a heated chamber.

Not recommended

QIDI Plus4, if fast warm-up is the reason you're buying. QIDI's own blog claims about 60 °C in about 5 minutes, but QIDI's current Plus4 firmware limits its 400 W heater to 40% duty (about 160 W). Since V1.6.0 the chamber also starts heating only after the bed reaches temperature. An owner test at 40% power took 25 minutes to print-ready. For $50 more at QIDI US, the Plus5 has a 550 W heater in a cabinet of the same size.
