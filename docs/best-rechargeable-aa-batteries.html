Which rechargeable AA batteries last longest?

Two different things: run time per charge (capacity: eneloop pro and the 2,800 mAh cells win) and years of use (cycles and charge kept in storage: standard eneloop wins at 2,100 cycles and 70% after 10 years). High-capacity cells trade cycles for capacity: eneloop pro is rated 500 cycles against 2,100.

What owners complain about

Packs where half the cells stop holding a charge within a year (the most common theme in Amazon review summaries for EBL, Amazon Basics and no-name cells); devices that call 1.2 V cells "low"; fat cells that don't fit smoke detectors or toothbrushes; counterfeit eneloops in mixed Amazon stock (buy from a listing sold by Amazon or Panasonic); and cheap chargers that stop at about 70%.

Which charger

Use one that charges each slot on its own and stops on a voltage drop, not a timer. Tenergy's TN480U 8-bay ($23.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/tenergy-tn480u-8-bay-nimh-charger) does. 1.5 V lithium AAs need their own lithium charger. For solar lights, see rechargeable AA batteries for solar lights.

Not recommended

No-name "3,600 mWh" USB-C lithium AAs (40–100% lost per year in tests), Tenergy's non-LSD Premium 2,500 mAh, and Amazon Basics for devices that sit for months.
