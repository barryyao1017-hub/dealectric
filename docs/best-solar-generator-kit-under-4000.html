How panel watts turn into outage days

Days = battery Wh ÷ (daily load − panel watts × 3.825). While the panels harvest less than the load, the battery makes up the gap each day and runs down. Once they harvest more, an average day ends with the battery full. That is why 200 W more panel on the same R3500 moves the figure from 1.8 days to 3.2, and a second 600 W panel moves it past the load altogether.

The figure is very sensitive near the line. The S5000 kit's 22 days rests on a 224 Wh daily gap. Andrew Riha (pv magazine USA, 2026-10-28) measured about 10 W of standby draw on the S5000 with the 120 V output on, 5% of charge in 24 hours. That is 240 Wh a day, and the gap becomes 464 Wh: 5,024 ÷ 464 = about 11 days. The battery figures in the table are rated Wh with no inverter loss, so every row is a best case on the same footing.

One measurement supports the 0.85 factor. Riha recorded a 680 W peak from the S5000 kit's own two PS400 panels wired in series on the high-voltage input, on a sunny October day in Northern California: 85% of their 800 W rating. A peak is not a day's average. In a real utility shutoff, the same unit ran a large refrigerator plus phones and small devices for 61.5 hours, from 100% to 18%.

Do the pick's panels fit? ALLPOWERS lists the R3500's solar input as 2,000 W max, XT60, 12–150 V MPPT, 40 A, and the SP039 as a 44 V panel with MC4 leads. Two in series make 88 V, inside 150 V. In parallel, 600 ÷ 44 = 13.6 A each and 27.3 A together, inside 40 A. The pair uses 1,200 W of the 2,000 W limit. The listing gives no open-circuit voltage, which is higher than 44 V, so read the panel label before wiring in series. The two-panel box lists one solar PV connection cable. Each SP039 weighs 27.5 lb.

And the S5000's? Anker lists two inputs: 11–60 V, 17 A, 1,000 W and 16–165 V, 17 A, 2,000 W. The kit's panels are 48 V each, so the series pair (96 V) fits only the 16–165 V input. The kit uses 800 W of a 3,000 W limit, so adding panels later to get past 860 W is possible. Anker gives 32–104°F as the charging temperature, so a unit in an unheated garage takes no solar below freezing.

What the lower price gives up. ALLPOWERS states over 3,500 charging cycles for the R3500 with no capacity end point. BLUETTI states up to 6,000 cycles to 80% capacity for the Apex 300. Anker states up to 10,000 cycles for the S5000, with more than 60% of capacity left.

It belongs in an RV build, where its 5,000 W output and alternator wiring pack matter.
