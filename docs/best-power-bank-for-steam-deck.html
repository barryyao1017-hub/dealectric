Measured, not estimated: what the tests show

Delivered energy. Techtest.org (Michael Barton) drains each bank through an electronic load at fixed voltages and logs the energy that comes out. The UGREEN 25K gave 65.2 Wh at 5 V/1 A, 71.9 Wh at 9 V/1 A, 75.0 Wh at 9 V/3 A, 76.8 Wh at 20 V/1 A and 74.1 Wh at 20 V/3 A, averaging 72.6 Wh (78% of its 25,000 mAh rating). The Anker A1695 averaged 74.2 Wh. The UGREEN 20K 130W did worst under load: it dropped from 64.7 Wh at 9 V/3 A to 54.8 Wh at 20 V/5 A.

Heat throttling. Techtest found the UGREEN 25K holds 140 W for about half its capacity, then falls to 65 W until it cools. The 20K 165W drops to 65 W for its last 20%. Both floors are above the Deck's 45 W, so neither limits a Deck.

Why the 15V⎓3A line matters more than total watts. ChargerLAB measured the Steam Deck triggering the 15 V PD level and drawing about 39 W from Valve's own 45 W adapter. Level1Techs staff (forum thread) report that a source offering 20 V but no 15 V drops the Deck to 5 V / 15 W. A 165 W or 300 W total adds no speed; what matters is 15V⎓3A on the port you plug into. On the UGREEN 20K 130W that is USB-C1 only.

How hard the Deck pulls. The Phawx measured the Steam Deck OLED at 24 W total system power in Cyberpunk 2077, as reported by Notebookcheck. Valve rates the OLED at 50 Wh and 3–12 hours of play, which works out to 4.2–16.7 W average.

Airline limits. Under FAA rules, as AARP reports them, power banks up to 100 Wh need no approval, 101–160 Wh need the airline's approval (two spares at most), and any power bank must travel in carry-on bags only. Anker labels the Prime 26K at 99.75 Wh and the A1695 at 90 Wh. For the 25,000 mAh UGREEN, 25,000 mAh × 3.6–3.7 V = 90–92.5 Wh, under the 100 Wh line. Check the Wh printed on the label before you fly.

Not recommended

INIU Carry 20,000 mAh 45W (P51L-E2), $36.99 at INIU (2026-11-13). It lists 15V⎓3A, but Techtest measured it throttling from 45 W to about 27 W under sustained load from heat, and down to 17 W while charging a laptop. Against the Deck OLED's measured 24 W in heavy games, that leaves about 3 W to charge the Deck while you play. It is fine for topping up between sessions, not for playing while it charges.
