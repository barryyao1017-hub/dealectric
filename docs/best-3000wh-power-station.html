What the differences mean

Why 3,000 Wh is the line. The Solar Lab logged three household refrigerators at about 1,300 Wh a day, so two days of fridge is 2 × 1,300 = 2,600 Wh. A station rated 3,000 Wh is the smallest size whose AC output in the table reaches that: 2,646 to 2,759 Wh for the Jackery, BLUETTI and Anker, with the Pecron (about 2,550 Wh) and EcoFlow (about 2,519 Wh) just short; a 2,000 Wh unit holds less than the fridge alone needs. Every row is rated at 3,000 Wh or more in one unit by its maker: 3,072 Wh for the Jackery, Anker, EcoFlow and Pecron, 3,014.4 Wh for the BLUETTI (Jackery's figure is from its US manual as cited by Watt Power Station). Left out for being just short: BLUETTI's Apex 300 (2,764.8 Wh) and Apex 200 (2,560 Wh). Also left out: the ALLPOWERS R3500, Growatt HELIOS 3600, Nature's Generator L3600 and Mango Power E, which meet the number but have no named reviewer's usable-energy or idle measurement that we read, so they cannot be ranked on this page's terms.

Inside the line the ranking uses measured usable AC energy and idle draw first, because those two set how long the stored energy lasts, then price paid and weight. Dealectric does not test units; every measurement here is a named reviewer's, read on 2026-11-27. The Jackery is not a product Dealectric links or earns from; its row points to Jackery's own page.

  • Idle cost over two days = idle watts × 48 h. Jackery 8 × 48 = 384 Wh; Elite 300 15 × 48 = 720 Wh; Anker 20.5 × 48 = 984 Wh; EcoFlow 22 × 48 = 1,056 Wh; Pecron 37 × 48 = 1,776 Wh, which is 57.8% of its 3,072 Wh.
  • Fridge-hours ceiling = usable Wh ÷ (fridge watts + idle watts), with the fridge at 1,300 Wh ÷ 24 h = 54.2 W. Jackery 2,759 ÷ 62.2 = 44 h; Elite 300 2,670 ÷ 69.2 = 39 h; Anker 2,646 ÷ 74.7 = 35 h; EcoFlow 2,519 ÷ 76.2 = 33 h; Pecron 2,550 ÷ 91.2 = 28 h. None reaches 48 h on the fridge alone, and CNET's real fridge runs came in lower (next section), so read these as ceilings. Two full days needs a top-up from solar, a car or a generator, or the fridge unplugged overnight.
  • Price per usable Wh. Pecron $899 ÷ 2,550 = 35 cents. Elite 300: $1,044.05 ÷ 2,670 = about 39 cents through our link, about 41 cents at the $1,099 (sold out) store price (read 2026-11-26). Jackery $1,499 ÷ 2,759 = 54 cents. All prices were read in Black Friday week and may rise when the sales end.
  • Amps at the RV outlet. The Elite 300's TT-30 outlet sits behind a 2,400 W inverter: 2,400 ÷ 120 V = 20 A, two-thirds of a 30 A feed. A 3,600 W unit gives 3,600 ÷ 120 = 30 A; Anker states the full 3,600 W comes out only at the TT-30R, with each household outlet limited to 2,400 W.
  • Cycle life. Elite 300: 6,000+ cycles to 80% (StorageReview, reporting BLUETTI's rating), 6,000 ÷ 365 = 16.4 years of one full cycle a day. Pecron: 3,500+ cycles to 80% (Pecron), 3,500 ÷ 365 = 9.6 years.

Measured usable energy, idle draw and fridge runs

A power station spends some of its own battery whenever the AC inverter is switched on, whether or not anything is drawing power. The Solar Lab's sizing formula adds it to the load for every hour: fridge consumption (Wh) + idle rate (W) × run time (h) = battery needed. A fridge cycles on and off, so the inverter has to stay on the whole time and the idle draw runs around the clock.

  • Jackery HomePower 3000. The Solar Lab: “an idle consumption of just 8 watts”, the lowest it had seen on a large station; its review does not state the test conditions. CNET (reported by Watt Power Station, test dated 2026-08-13): 2,759 Wh at a 480 W load, and 32.05 hours running a full-size refrigerator. Jackery's figures, as cited by Watt Power Station: 2 h 12 min wall recharge (Jackery's site), 1,000 W solar input and 20 ms switchover (US manual).
  • BLUETTI Elite 300. StorageReview (Dylan Bryan, 2026-08-27): 2.67 kWh out and 3.49 kWh drawn from the wall for a full charge, so 2.67 ÷ 3.49 = 76.5% wall to outlet; switchover 8 ms to battery and 8.25 ms back, against BLUETTI's 10 ms rating. The Solar Lab: idle “around 15W”, about 86% efficiency, 2 h 44 min from empty to full on a wall outlet.
  • Anker SOLIX F3000. The Solar Lab: 20.5 W idle with only the AC inverter on, about 84% efficiency. CNET (reported by Watt Power Station): 2,646 Wh at 480 W and a 28.97-hour fridge run.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 3 Ultra. The Solar Lab: about 22 W with the inverter left on, about 82% efficiency, empty to full in two hours on a household outlet.
  • Pecron F3000LFP. The Solar Lab: 37 W idle, found by charging to 100% and letting the unit drain with only the inverter on; 83% at a sustained 3,600 W.

How far the idle figure carries. Watt Power Station reports that in CNET's fridge run the Jackery “used 36.4% of its energy on itself” and the Anker 37.81%. If those shares are of the rated 3,072 Wh, the Jackery's overhead was 0.364 × 3,072 = 1,118 Wh over 32.05 h, about 35 W, and the Anker's 0.3781 × 3,072 = 1,162 Wh over 28.97 h, about 40 W. That overhead includes conversion loss as well as idle draw, and it is far closer between the two than 8 W against 20.5 W suggests. The Jackery still ran the fridge about three hours longer, an 11% lead. So the idle column ranks the units correctly on the evidence we read, but the gap in real fridge hours is smaller than the idle watts alone imply.

The Elite 300's 4,800 W is not surge. BLUETTI's page gives “2,400 W output (4,800 W lifting)” and no separate surge figure. StorageReview describes Power Lifting as a mode for purely resistive loads such as heaters and kettles in which actual output stays at 2,400 W; it “does not turn the Elite 300 into a full 4,800W power source”. Size any motor or compressor against 2,400 W. The 7,200 W surge of the Jackery, Anker and EcoFlow is a different thing: brief headroom above 3,600 W for a motor starting.

Not recommended

The Pecron F3000LFP for a multi-day outage. It is the lowest-priced row at $899 (Pecron, pre-order, 2026-11-27), but its 37 W idle draw takes 1,776 Wh over 48 hours, more than half the battery, before the fridge gets any. Its surge is 4,500 W for 5 seconds against 7,200 W on the other 3,600 W units, and The Solar Lab reported a 4,500 W load tripping it after about 10 seconds. It suits short, heavy work where the inverter is switched off afterwards, not a fridge left running for two days.
