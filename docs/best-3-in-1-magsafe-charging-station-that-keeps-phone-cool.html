What the differences mean

  • Price gap. At the makers' US stores on 2026-11-11 the ESR was $109.99, the Belkin $129.99, the UGREEN $159.99 and the Anker $229.99. Anker over ESR: $229.99 − $109.99 = $120.00. Belkin over ESR: $129.99 − $109.99 = $20.00. Belkin's page showed the dock out of stock, due back by Nov 22, 2026.
  • What a fan is worth, measured. Android Authority (Brady Snyder, Feb 14, 2026) charged a Galaxy S25 Edge on the Belkin for 30 minutes with the fan off and again with it on. The glass peaked at 90°F and 82°F: 90 − 82 = 8°F. The battery sensor read 91.3°F after 10 minutes with the fan on, then 98.1°F, 98.8°F and 100.8°F over three fan-off intervals, then fell to 94.8°F after one more interval with the fan on (100.8 − 94.8 = 6.0°F). Belkin claims up to 12°F, so the glass result is 12 − 8 = 4°F short. Snyder wrote that he never saw the full 12°F. He did not report the room temperature.
  • ESR's claim next to that. ESR's average of 6.5°F is 6.5 ÷ 1.8 = 3.6°C; its white listing of the same station says 7.2°F (4.0°C). Both sit inside the 6 to 8°F Android Authority saw from Belkin's fan. That test used a different charger and phone, so it shows ESR's figure is a plausible size for a fan. It is not a test of the ESR.
  • Anker's one reading. Makurin (note.com, Dec 2, 2025, unit supplied by Anker Japan) reported about 33°C after 30 minutes of charging: 33 × 1.8 + 32 = 91.4°F. That is 91.4 − 89 = 2.4°F above Anker's below-89°F line and 93.9 − 91.4 = 2.5°F under the ceiling ESR quotes. The review names no cooling mode, room temperature or thermometer.
  • The speed that cooling protects. Macworld (Simon Jary) timed an iPhone 16 Pro from 0 to 50% in just over half an hour on the ESR and puts a 15 W charger at about 45 minutes: 45 − 30 = about 15 minutes saved per half charge while the phone holds 25 W.
  • Adapter headroom. ESR: 50 W − (25 W phone + 5 W watch + 5 W AirPods) = 15 W left for the fan and losses. Anker: 65 W − 35 W = 30 W. Anker advises a 65 W or higher adapter "for 25W charging and proper cooling". These are upper bounds, not measured draws.

How a charging station keeps the phone cool

The phone decides, not the dock. Apple's support page (May 14, 2026) says that if the interior temperature of the iPhone exceeds the normal operating range, "Charging, including wireless charging, slows or stops." Apple designs the iPhone for rooms between 0 and 35°C (32 to 95°F). Its MagSafe page (Sept 18, 2026) adds: "if the battery gets too warm, software might limit charging above 80 percent." A cooling station works by keeping the phone under those limits so it goes on accepting full power.

Two mechanisms. The ESR, Belkin and UGREEN cool with a fan. The Anker adds a thermoelectric cooler: in Anker's words it "uses a thermoelectric cooler and a fan to actively pull heat away from the phone and charging coil" and releases it through the fan.

Cooling stops when the fan stops. Each station has a quiet setting, and each quiet setting switches the cooling off. Macworld notes a front button that switches the ESR's fan off and on, and a side button that does the same on the UGREEN. Belkin's FAQ lists a "fan on/off switch for silent mode". Anker's page for the same A25X7 says "Sleep Mode turns off cooling and dims the display for quiet overnight charging."

The makers' figures are three kinds of number. ESR gives an average drop (6.5°F, or 7.2°F on its white listing) plus a ceiling of 93.9°F relayed by Macworld. Belkin gives a best case (up to 12°F, internal testing). Anker gives a ceiling (below 89°F). An average, a best case and a ceiling cannot be ranked against each other, and none of the three makers states the room temperature or the phone used.

How much power your iPhone takes. On Apple's own MagSafe Charger the iPhone 18 Pro, 18 Pro Max, 17 Pro, 17 Pro Max, 17, 16 Plus and 16 Pro Max reach 25 W; the iPhone 16 and 16 Pro peak at 22.5 W, the iPhone Air at 20 W, and the iPhone 17e and iPhone 15 and earlier at 15 W. Anker lists similar caps for its dock (16 and 16 Pro up to 22 W, Air up to 20 W) and says it is not compatible with the iPhone 16e.

Not recommended for a bedside table: Anker Prime

The Anker costs $120.00 more than the ESR, and that money buys the thermoelectric cooler. Anker's own Sleep Mode, the setting meant for overnight charging, turns that cooler off. At 21.87 oz (620 g) it is also about twice the ESR's 11 oz (312 g, Macworld). Buy it for daytime charging in Ice Mode, not for a nightstand. The UGREEN MagFlow Pro has a similar limit: Macworld found its fan "definitely audible" and judged the stand better suited to a desk than a quiet bedside table.
