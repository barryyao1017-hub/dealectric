What "600 W" means on a 300 W power station

BLUETTI's page for the Elite 30 mini carries a 600 W figure. The manual (p. 16) says of Power Lifting mode: "While the Elite 30 mini can handle such loads, its actual output power is still 300 W." It limits the mode to pure resistive loads rated 300–600 W and names kettles, electric blankets and hair dryers. The mode is off by default and is switched on in the BLUETTI app. A 600 W heating element fed 300 W gives half its rated heat, so the same job takes about twice as long (600 ÷ 300 = 2). For motors and compressors the manual says to keep total power below 300 W, because they can draw 2–4 times their rating at startup.

EcoFlow labels the same class the same way. Its store lists the RIVER 2 (240) as 300 W with 600 W X-Boost, and StorageReview's table for the RIVER 3 reads "300W total (Surge 600W, X-Boost 600W)". Compare these units on 300 W.

The figure that matters more for small loads is what the station uses itself. BLUETTI's manual (p. 24) gives the formula: operation time = capacity × DoD × η ÷ (load + self-consumption), with DoD 95%, η over 90% and self-consumption about 5 W. At η = 90%:

These are estimates from the maker's formula, not measurements. The 85.5% the formula implies (95% × 90%) matches the one measurement we have for this class: StorageReview ran an EcoFlow RIVER 3 into a 300 W fan and got 210 Wh of its rated 245 Wh, which is 85.7%. It took 270 Wh from the wall to refill.

Two settings change the result.

  • ECO mode. It is off by default. Switched on, it cuts the AC output after 1–4 hours below a threshold you set between 10 and 40 W (5–20 W for DC). The manual says to disable it "when connecting small devices or critical appliances such as lights and refrigerators". Leave it off for a router or a phone charger.
  • Cold. The manual gives a charging range of 32–104 °F and a discharging range of −4 to 104 °F. Below freezing the unit runs loads but does not take a charge.
