How a '5D holographic' projector makes its picture, and what 'smart' means on it

The generic 5D units are slide projectors. Device Diary's Sam bought a Topacme 5D projector and used it in three rooms (review dated 2026-06-28). It projects through 'HD optical lenses and rotating film discs', and you focus it by hand ('twist the top ring until the stars look crisp'). It 'does not need WiFi or an app' and 'works as a standalone device with a remote and touch buttons'. The remote sets brightness, colour, rotation speed and a 30, 60 or 120-minute sleep timer. It has no speaker. Sam found it covered the whole ceiling of a 12 × 12 ft bedroom and stayed clear up to a 12 ft ceiling in a dark room. Device Diary says '5D' describes 'the layered effect created by light, motion, color blending, and depth perception' and is not a five-dimensional display. Nothing in it is a hologram.

The same unit is sold under many names. Juliet at ibisik (2026-08-30) found near-identical listing copy for the Comauo, Topacme and Billhol versions. She describes a 'small USB-powered projection light' that 'appears to rely on optical projection and physical image films', and reports one Walmart listing at 2.6/5 from 18 ratings. The film count isn't consistent even within one source: Device Diary's text says 24 films, while the Amazon listing embedded in the same review says 12.

Named-maker disc projectors use the same principle, done carefully. POCOCO states a 'super-low-power 1-watt LED light source' shining through a '5K Ultra HD disc' 'etched using cutting-edge photolithography', with manual optical focus and a 130 ft² projection. You get crisp still images, but no ripple and no app.

A smart ocean projector works differently. Govee's H6094 makes the water with 4 RGBW LEDs under a dual-layer textured lens and the stars with a green laser, so there are no films to swap. It is controlled from the Govee Home app, Alexa, Google Assistant or Matter, with 40+ scenes, 18 white-noise tracks and a sleep timer. Its FCC filing (2A7VD-H6094) shows radios only in the 2.4 GHz band, so it joins a 2.4 GHz home network. MacRumors' Juli Clover (2025-12-12, review unit supplied by Govee) got on/off, brightness, scenes, automations and Siri working through Apple Home. Colour changes from the Home app did not work, and the laser is fixed green. She wrote 'I didn't think it necessarily looked quite like I was underwater' but recommended the Ocean Wave as a children's night light, ideally bought on sale. Govee's Matter FAQ adds two conditions: you need a Matter hub (HomePod, Apple TV, Nest Hub, Echo or SmartThings), and switching networks in the Govee Home app turns Matter off. You can buy it here: buy on Amazon: https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FL2431W5?tag=dealectric-20.

Not recommended

A generic 'Smart Holographic Star & Ocean 5D Projector' bought for its 'smart' label. On the unit Device Diary tested, 'smart' meant a remote, touch buttons and a sleep timer, with no Wi-Fi, no app and no voice assistant. If that is all you need, a cheap film-disc unit does the job, and the 'HD' or '4K' wording and the film count are the only real differences between listings. Don't pay smart-projector money for one. If you want app or voice control with a ripple effect, the Govee H6094 is the one that has it.
