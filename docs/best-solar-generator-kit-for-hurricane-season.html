What the differences mean

  • Measured output separates these kits more than the ratings do. The Elite 200 V2 delivered 1,990 of its 2,073.6 Wh (1,990 ÷ 2,073.6 = 96%). The AC180 delivered 950 of its 1,152 Wh (950 ÷ 1,152 = 82%). By rating, the Elite holds 1.8× an AC180 (2,073.6 ÷ 1,152). By measured output it delivers 2.1× (1,990 ÷ 950).
  • Fridge days with no sun. A fridge whose EnergyGuide label reads 600 kWh a year uses 600 ÷ 365 = 1.64 kWh a day. The Elite’s 1,990 Wh covers 1,990 ÷ 1,640 = 1.2 days. A 1 kWh station measured at 890–950 Wh covers 13–14 hours (950 ÷ 1,640 × 24 = 13.9 h).
  • Panel wattage decides how long the outage can go on. NREL PVWatts data for Jacksonville give a 400 W panel 49 kWh in September, so 49,000 ÷ 30 = 1,633 Wh a day. Two 200 W panels about match that 1,640 Wh fridge on a clear day (1,633 − 1,640 ≈ break-even, before charge losses). One 200 W panel returns about 817 Wh a day. That leaves the battery 823 Wh short each day, so it lasts 1,990 ÷ 823 = 2.4 days. Overcast days drain it faster.

Measured AC energy: what each kit actually delivers

The rated figure is the capacity of the battery cells. Less than that reaches your outlets. The inverter loses some energy as heat when it turns battery DC into 120 V AC, and the station’s own electronics draw power the whole time it is on. That standby draw takes a bigger share at light loads, and hurricane loads are light: a cycling fridge, a CPAP, a router.

  • BLUETTI Elite 200 V2: APH Networks ran a 987 W heater for 2 h 1 min: 987 × 2.017 ≈ 1,990 Wh. APH itself quotes 2,028 Wh, based on the unit’s own 1,006 W display reading. StorageReview measured 1.91 kWh out and 2.44 kWh drawn from the wall to refill, so 1.91 ÷ 2.44 = 78% round trip. The Solar Lab measured 9.5 W idle and 94% efficiency.
  • ALLPOWERS R2500 V2: Your Life Updated measured 1.92 kWh at about 900 W, but 1.55 kWh at 100 W. That is 370 Wh (19%) less at the lighter load.
  • EcoFlow DELTA 2 Max: StorageReview measured 1,800 Wh at 350 W. The Solar Lab measured 13.6 W idle with the AC inverter on.
  • 1 kWh class: OutdoorGearLab measured 950 Wh from the AC180 (at 80% load), 910 Wh from the Jackery 1000 v2 and 890 Wh from the Anker C1000 Gen 2. StorageReview measured 910 Wh from the EcoFlow DELTA 3 Plus.

Standby over a 72-hour outage adds up: 9.5 W × 72 h = 684 Wh on the Elite 200 V2, and 13.6 W × 72 h = 979 Wh on the DELTA 2 Max. Turn the AC inverter off whenever nothing needs it.

These figures come from different labs at different loads, so treat gaps of a few percent as approximate. No lab has published an Elite 200 V2 run at a fridge-like 100 W, so expect less than 1,990 Wh at light loads, as the R2500 V2’s 100 W test shows.

Two rules for the Elite 200 V2. First, BLUETTI rates its solar input at 12–60 V and 20 A, 1,000 W max. Since 1,000 W ÷ 20 A = 50 V, adding panels to approach 1,000 W needs a string that runs at 50 V or more under load, with its open-circuit voltage at or below 60 V. Second, StorageReview lists the operating range as -4 °F to 104 °F, so run and charge it in shade, not in a closed garage or car after the storm. $1,499 on bluettipower.com, $1,424.05 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-200-v2-2x200w-solar-kit

Not recommended

A 1 kWh station as your only hurricane kit. The AC180, Jackery 1000 v2, Anker C1000 Gen 2 and DELTA 3 Plus measured 890–950 Wh. That runs a fridge using 1.64 kWh a day for 13–14 hours with no sun. For a multi-day outage with a fridge, get a 2 kWh station with 400 W of panels.
