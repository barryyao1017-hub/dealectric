What the differences mean

  • Charger output needed to start heating (at a 14.4 V charge voltage): Epoch 0.5 A × 14.4 V ≈ 7 W; Renogy Pro 4 A × 14.4 V ≈ 58 W; LiTime in Regular mode 10 A × 14.4 V = 144 W. A small solar panel on a short winter morning can reach the first figure long before it reaches the last.
  • Renogy in a bank: the 4 A threshold is per battery, so a two-battery parallel bank needs more than 4 A × 2 = 8 A going in before both heaters run.
  • Battle Born's heater uses the battery's own energy: 28 W ÷ 12.8 V ≈ 2.2 A from the pack while it heats. An hour of heating uses 28 Wh, about 2.2% of a 12.8 V × 100 Ah = 1,280 Wh battery.
  • Price per rated Wh: Redodo $251.99 ÷ 1,280 Wh ≈ $0.20; Vatrer $299.99 ÷ 1,280 ≈ $0.23; LiTime $368.99 ÷ 1,280 ≈ $0.29; Epoch $549 ÷ 1,344 ≈ $0.41; Renogy Pro $579.99 ÷ 1,280 ≈ $0.45; Battle Born kit $999 ÷ 1,280 ≈ $0.78. Epoch costs $549 − $251.99 = $297.01 more than Redodo.
  • Epoch's 11-year warranty gives a free replacement in years 1–3, then 40% off MSRP on a replacement in years 4–11.
  • Wiring: Epoch allows up to 4 in series and 4 in parallel (48 V, or 4 × 105 Ah = 420 Ah). Renogy documents its self-heating Pro as parallel only, up to 8, so it cannot form a 24 V or 48 V bank.

What actually powers the heater

A lithium iron phosphate battery must not be charged below freezing. A self-heating battery warms its cells first, then lets charging begin. These batteries differ in where the heater's power comes from and how much charge current it takes to switch on.

  • Epoch 12105A-H: Epoch's spec table says heating needs a "charger connected and supplying at least 0.5A" and is "powered by incoming charge current only, never stored battery energy". Charging is disabled at 32°F and resumes automatically at 41°F. Epoch rates charging with heat down to -4°F.
  • Battle Born BB10012H: Battle Born's own explainer (April 28, 2026) says "the heater draws power from the battery itself", typically around 28 W, and switches on when the internal temperature reaches about 35°F and "a charge source is present". The kit includes a Heat Enable cable kit with a rocker switch that the owner wires in.
  • LiTime LiHeat Group 24: LiTime's listing says it can "automatically warm the battery while plugged in, or preheat with battery power before charging". CompareFoundry gives the conditions: at least a 10 A charger in Regular mode, and above 20% charge in Energy-efficient mode.
  • Renogy Pro 12V 100Ah: BatteryTrail and CompareFoundry both report that the heater runs only on a stable charge current above 4 A per battery, below 41°F.
  • Redodo 12V 100Ah Self-Heating: two 100 W heating pads that switch on below 32°F and off at 41°F. Discharge stops below -4°F.
  • Vatrer 12V 100Ah Heated: heats from charger input at 32°F, and charging starts above 41°F.
  • BLUETTI B1210: "with a steady power input", a heating film switches on below 32°F and holds the battery near 77°F.

How long it takes. Shane Monks O'Byrne of The Van Conversion logged a Renogy 12V 200Ah self-heating battery on alternator charge after nights of -5 to -10°C. The cells took about 30 minutes to climb from about -2°C to about 5°C, with 0 A going into the battery while it warmed. Once warm, it took 20–30 A. Expect a heater that runs on charger power to put no charge into the battery for up to half an hour of a cold start.

Not recommended

Redodo 12V 100Ah Group 24 Bluetooth (no heater), $259.99. It has only a low-temperature charge cutoff, so below freezing it stops charging instead of warming up. It also costs $8 more than Redodo's self-heating 100 Ah at $251.99.

Battle Born BB10012H heated kit, $999 (regular $1,149). At about $0.78 per rated Wh, it costs nearly four times as much as Redodo and $450 more than Epoch. Its roughly 28 W heater also runs on the battery's own charge.
