What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every timing on this page is HLPlanet's or RTINGS's, named where it appears. Every other figure is the maker's own and is labelled as a claim.

  • What wireless costs on the pick. 0.75 ms − 0.60 ms = 0.15 ms, and 0.15 ÷ 0.60 = 25% over the cable (HLPlanet's two timings of the TK75 TMR). HLPlanet's rule of thumb is that 2.4 GHz "will add usually 20-100% more latency".
  • What 8,000 Hz is in time. 1,000 ms ÷ 8,000 = 0.125 ms between reports to the PC. A maker latency of exactly 0.125 ms (Gamakay NS68, Epomaker HE68 Lite) is the polling rate restated. Claims of 0.06, 0.08 and 0.1 ms are shorter than the gap between two reports.
  • Timed against claimed. MCHOSE claims "up to 0.08 ms" for the Ace 68 and HLPlanet timed 0.3 ms: 0.3 ÷ 0.08 = 3.75 times the claim. Gamakay claims 0.06 ms for the NS75 and TK75HE V2. HLPlanet's 0.60 ms for the related TK75 TMR is 0.60 ÷ 0.06 = 10 times that figure. It is a different board from the same maker, so read it as scale, not as a timing of those two.
  • Why the two timed boards are not ranked on speed. They sit 0.60 − 0.3 = 0.3 ms apart on a cable. One frame on a 240 Hz monitor lasts 1,000 ÷ 240 = 4.17 ms, so the gap is 0.3 ÷ 4.17 = 7% of one frame.
  • Bluetooth is for typing. HLPlanet lists the TK75 TMR at 1,000 Hz on Bluetooth: 1,000 ÷ 1,000 = 1 ms between reports, eight times the 0.125 ms interval on cable or dongle. HLPlanet timed the cable and the dongle only.

The latency figures a named reviewer measured

Gamakay TK75 TMR: 0.60 ms wired, 0.75 ms on 2.4 GHz. Marcus Richardson's HLPlanet review (2026-05-18) used OSLTT, which he describes as measuring the time between physical keycap contact and signal receipt by the computer. His spec table lists 8,000 Hz on cable and 2.4 GHz, 1,000 Hz on Bluetooth, a 256,000 Hz scan rate, 8,000 mAh and about 1,100 g. His limits: a plastic case, software less polished than Wooting's, and plate bounce at extreme rapid trigger sensitivity. He suggests a rapid trigger release of about 0.4 mm, well above the 0.01 mm the spec table lists.

MCHOSE Ace 68: 0.3 ms wired. HLPlanet's Hall effect list (2026-10-26) carries the figure and states: "If we mentioned latency in the table that means we tested it using OSLTT." The board is wired only, so it has no dongle figure to compare.

MCHOSE Ace 60 Pro: verdict without a public number. RTINGS bought a black unit with KTEK magnetic switches, confirmed it "fully utilizes its 8,000Hz polling rate" and calls its single-key and multi-key latency "remarkably low and consistent". Its millisecond figures are shown to members only.

For scale. HLPlanet's latency table (2026-06-08) puts the Wooting 80HE at 0.42 ms and the Wooting 60HE v2 at 0.65 ms, at $199.99 and $189 in that table. The two timed boards here, at 0.3 ms and 0.60 ms, sit in the same range. The same table lists the Luminkey Magger 68 HE at 0.3 ms and $89; it has no row because its store price was not read on 2026-11-21.

For the NS75, TK75HE V2, NS68 and HE68 Lite, the table carries the maker's claim and says so. On those four, choose on layout, connections and price, not on the latency figure.

Not recommended: a wireless FUN60 Ultra with the lights switched off

The MonsGeek FUN60 Ultra appears in HLPlanet's latency table at 0.5 ms; that table's data note credits "our tests using OSLTT, rtings.com, eyejokerdb.com" without saying which supplied each row. MonsGeek states 8,000 Hz in wired or 2.4 GHz modes, with one exception in its own FAQ: "In 2.4G mode, pressing Fn + L to turn off the lights activates the power-saving mode, reducing the polling rate to 100Hz." At 100 Hz a report leaves every 1,000 ÷ 100 = 10 ms, which is 10 ÷ 0.125 = 80 times the 8,000 Hz interval. MonsGeek's fix is to dim the backlight to its lowest level with Fn + [ and leave it on. The board has no row above because monsgeek.com showed a $59.99 to $89.99 range with the buy area reading "Unavailable" and a waitlist form on 2026-11-21.
