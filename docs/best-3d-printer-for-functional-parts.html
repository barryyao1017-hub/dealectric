What the differences mean

  • Chamber ceiling: the Q2 is rated 10 °C higher than the CORE One+ (65 − 55 = 10 °C). The P2S has no rated chamber temperature because it has no heater.
  • Nozzle headroom: the Q2 runs 70 °C hotter than the P2S (370 − 300) and 80 °C hotter than the CORE One+ (370 − 290). That headroom is what PC, PA and PPS-based filaments need.
  • Build volume: the Q2 holds 18.66 L (270 × 270 × 256 mm) against the P2S's 16.78 L (256³ mm), 11% more. The CORE One+ holds 14.85 L.
  • Price per litre of build: Q2 $449 / 18.66 L = $24.06/L. P2S $449 / 16.78 L = $26.76/L. CORE One+ $1,202.78 / 14.85 L = $81.00/L. Plus5 $699 / 30.72 L = $22.75/L. Max4 $1,049 / 51.71 L = $20.29/L.

How each printer heats its chamber

ABS and ASA shrink as they cool. Prusa's ABS guide puts it at about 1–2% and says the printer "should be inside an enclosure with a higher ambient temperature". A warmer chamber means each layer cools less before the next one goes down, so large parts warp and crack less. Nylon and PC behave the same way, only more so. All three designs use an enclosure. They differ in where the heat comes from.

  • QIDI Q2, Plus5, Max4: a dedicated heater. QIDI lists a 280 W PTC chamber heater on the Q2 and a 500 W one on the Max4, each rated to 65 °C. The heater warms the air on its own, so it doesn't depend on how hot the bed runs or how big the part is. The Q2 ships with a bimetal hardened-steel nozzle, which is also what carbon-fibre-filled filaments need.
  • Bambu P2S: no heater, heat held in. Bambu's launch post says a "flap-controlled Adaptive Airflow System efficiently retains heat within the chamber" and gives no chamber temperature. Cameron Coward of Hackster says this "isn't the same as active chamber heating". He found it worked "well enough for materials like ABS to prevent warping", but says the printer "may still struggle with materials that need really high heat". The Crafty Catsman also states it has no actively heated chamber. One filament maker's printer wiki (Polymaker) lists the P2S as actively heated to 60 °C. Bambu's own launch post describes only a flap that keeps heat in, and the reviewers above describe no heater.
  • Prusa CORE One+: bed heat, fan-regulated. Prusa rates the chamber to 55 °C. Thomas Sanladerer (Toms3D) explains that the printer uses the heated bed and chamber fans to regulate the chamber, with no separate heater. In his ABS test, the original CORE One reached about 50 °C, while a Bambu X1 Carbon with a passive enclosure peaked at about 40 °C. The CORE One also printed the more accurate part. In his cool basement it paused for about half an hour to bring the chamber to 40 °C before printing. When the chamber falls short, Prusa's fix for error #31259 is to slow the chamber fan and close the top vent. The stock nozzle is brass, so filled filaments need a hardened nozzle bought separately.

Not recommended

The Bambu P2S as a nylon or PC printer. At $449 it is a strong PETG and small-ASA machine. But with no heater, its chamber only gets as warm as its bed and hotend make it, and Hackster's reviewer warns it may struggle with high-heat materials. If tough engineering parts are the reason you're buying, the Q2 costs the same and has a heater.
