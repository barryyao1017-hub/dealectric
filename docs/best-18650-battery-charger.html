What the differences mean

How long four 3,000 mAh 18650s take depends on the current each cell gets once every bay is full. The headline "3 A" doesn't tell you that. The times below cover only the constant-current stage. The slower top-up near 4.2 V adds roughly 0.2 h: 1lumen measured 1.2 h for one 3,000 mAh cell at 3,060 mA, against 0.98 h for the constant-current stage alone.

  • Vapcell S4 Plus V3: 3.0 Ah ÷ 3 A = 1.0 h. ZeroAir measured 2.6 A per bay with all four bays loaded on an earlier S4 Plus. At that rate: 3.0 ÷ 2.6 = 1.15 h.
  • XTAR VC4SL and VX4 V2: 3.0 Ah ÷ 1 A = 3.0 h. On a plain 5 V phone adapter the VC4SL drops to 0.5 A each: 3.0 ÷ 0.5 = 6 h.
  • Nitecore UMS4: also 1 A each with four loaded. besttacticalflashlighttoday says four 3,000 mAh cells take about 3.5 h on a QC adapter.
  • XTAR VC2SL: two bays at 1 A, so four cells take two loads: 3.0 ÷ 1 = 3 h × 2 = 6 h.
  • Tenergy TN270: two bays at 0.5 A: 3.0 ÷ 0.5 = 6 h × 2 loads = 12 h.

Why the USB-C chargers drop to 1 A with four 18650s

The limit is the power supply. A Li-ion cell near full sits at 4.2 V, so 3 A into each of four cells takes 4 × 3 A × 4.2 V = 50.4 W of output, before any conversion losses. The XTAR VC4SL runs on USB-C Quick Charge 3.0 at about 18 W (9 V × 2 A). The VX4 V2 runs on USB-C PD at 20 W (12 V × 1.67 A). One cell at 3 A needs about 12.6 W, which fits. Four cells at 1 A need 4 × 1 A × 4.2 V = 16.8 W, which also fits. Four cells at 3 A does not fit. So the 3 A maximum listed for each of these chargers applies to a single cell on the XTARs, and to every cell on the Vapcell.

The Vapcell S4 Plus V3 avoids that limit by using its own wall adapter; Vapcell lists it with a US, EU, UK or AU plug. Vapcell's page rates it at 3 A per slot and 12 A in total. The S4 Plus measurements published so far come from earlier units. HKJ at lygte-info.dk measured about 4.5 A drawn from 12 V (about 54 W) with four cells charging at 3 A. The cells reached 45–47 °C and the heatsink 69 °C. ZeroAir measured about 2.8 A per bay with all bays loaded, settling to about 2.6 A after five minutes.

The adapter matters on the USB-C chargers too. Gough Lui got the VX4's 3 A / 2 A / 1 A rates with XTAR's 20 W PD adapter. A third-party 65 W charger (Anker PowerPort III) gave it only 9 V at 1.25 A (11.25 W). On a basic 5 V adapter, besttacticalflashlighttoday lists the VC4SL at 0.5 A each with four cells, which doubles the full-load time to 6 h.

Charge at 3 A only if the cell's datasheet allows it. The S4 Plus V3 lets you set the charge current manually. The Tenergy TN270 ($18.99: https://dealectric.com/go/tenergy-tn270-li-ion-18650-charger) is the slow, gentle option: 500 mA per cell, 18650, 18500 and 14500 cells only (21700 is not on Tenergy's list), and a red/green LED instead of a screen.

Not recommended

XTAR Dragon VP4L Plus, if you want it for charging 18650s. XTAR's own comparison lists its maximum current as 2 A, where the VC4SL and VX4 reach 3 A. It also needs a 12 V 3 A DC input, where those two run on USB-C. Buy it for its battery-analyzer functions, not for charging speed.
