What the differences mean

All prices are from the makers' US stores on 2026-11-14.

  • Kit saving = station + panel(s) − kit. AC180: $499 + $399 − $769 = $129. Elite 200 V2 + 2× 200 W: $899 + 2 × $399 − $1,499 = $198. Elite 30 V2: $289.99 + $249 − $489.99 = $49. P1800 + 400 W: $399 + $299 − $698 = $0.
  • What the kit charges for its panel = kit − station alone. P1800: $698 − $399 = $299, and $299 ÷ 400 W = $0.75/W. AC180: $769 − $499 = $270, and $270 ÷ 200 W = $1.35/W.
  • Refill at rated output = battery Wh ÷ panel W. Elite 200 V2 + 200 W: 2,073.6 ÷ 200 = 10.4 h, which is more than one day of full sun. Treat every refill figure as a best case: OutdoorGearLab measured 150 W from BLUETTI's 200 W portable panel, so the AC180 kit needs 1,152 ÷ 150 = 7.7 h. BLUETTI's own Elite 30 V2 rating (200 W of solar, full in 2.2 h) implies 288 ÷ (200 × 2.2) = 65% of the panel's rated output.
  • Share of solar input used = panel W ÷ the station's maximum solar input. Elite 200 V2: 200 ÷ 1,000 = 20%. P1800: 400 ÷ 650 = 62%.

What a kit really charges for its panel

On these stores the station costs the same with or without a panel, so a kit's discount sits entirely on the panel. Subtract the station price from the kit price and compare what's left with what a panel costs on its own. Bought alone, BLUETTI's 200 W panel is $399 ($2.00/W) and its SP100L 100 W panel is $249 ($2.49/W). Inside BLUETTI's kits the same watts cost $1.35–2.00/W. A BLUETTI kit is therefore the cheapest way to get BLUETTI's own panels.

ALLPOWERS prices its SP037 400 W panel at $299 both inside and outside the kit. The $698 on iallpowers.com, $628.20 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/allpowers-p1800-sp037-solar-kit costs exactly the same as the parts, and at $0.75/W its panel undercuts every standalone panel priced here. Growatt's 200 W panel lists at $199 ($1.00/W) but was out of stock on 2026-11-14.

A third-party panel only beats the $769 on bluettipower.com, $730.55 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-ac180-200w-solar-kit if it costs less than $270 and fits the AC180's input. BLUETTI's manual sets that input at 12–60 V, 10 A and 500 W, and says the panel's open-circuit voltage must not exceed 60 V. At 10 A the station needs about 50 V to take its full 500 W (500 ÷ 10), so getting close to 500 W takes a string near 50 V that stays under 60 V open-circuit.

Adding a second panel works out differently on the two big BLUETTI stations. On the Elite 200 V2, the 2-panel kit is $1,499 against $1,199 + $399 = $1,598 for the 1-panel kit plus a loose panel, so the $1,499 on bluettipower.com, $1,424.05 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-200-v2-2x200w-solar-kit wins by $99. On the AC180 the 2-panel kit loses: see below. The $489.99 on bluettipower.com, $465.50 through this link: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-elite-30-v2-100w-solar-kit saves $49 and suits small loads.

Not recommended

BLUETTI AC180 + 2× 200 W kit ($1,232). Buying the AC180 + 200 W kit ($769) and one more BLUETTI 200 W panel ($399) costs $1,168, which is $64 less. Inside the 2-panel kit the second panel costs $1,232 − $769 = $463, more than its $399 standalone price.

BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 + 200 W kit, if you plan to recharge from solar. Its panel uses 20% of the station's 1,000 W solar input and needs 10.4 h at rated output to refill 2,073.6 Wh. For $300 more, the 2× 200 W kit halves that time and saves $198 against the parts.
