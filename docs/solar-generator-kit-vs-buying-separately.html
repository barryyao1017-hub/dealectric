When a third-party panel beats the kit

A brand's own panel is usually its priciest part: BLUETTI's 200 W panel is $399 alone. A third-party 200 W folding panel lists for about $210–280 (BougeRV about $210 with XT60, DC7909 and DC5521 leads; Renogy's E.FLEX about $246–281 with MC4 only). With the AC180 at $449 in BLUETTI's cart after its automatic $50 discount, an AC180 plus a $210 panel comes to about $659, under the $729 kit. Check the panel's open-circuit voltage against the station's input window and the connector before buying; a mismatch needs an adapter or doesn't charge at all.

What the kit still gives you

One order, one return window and a panel the maker says works with the station. With ALLPOWERS and Anker, where the kit saves nothing, that is the only reason to buy the bundle.

Why sale prices move the answer

Stations go on sale more often than panels, so the gap changes week to week: BLUETTI's Elite 200 V2 alone is already $200 under its earlier price. Re-add the two parts to the cart before you buy and compare with the kit's cart total.
