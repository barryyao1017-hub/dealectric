What the differences mean

A 17-inch laptop is nearly always a 17.3-inch 16:9 panel, and its picture is 8.48 in tall. A side screen of the same height lines up with it top and bottom. A shorter one leaves a step at the edges that your eye crosses every time you move a window across.

  • 17.3-inch 16:9 laptop: 17.3 × 9/√337 = 17.3 × 0.4903 = 8.48 in tall.
  • 16-inch 16:10 side screen (FOPO S11 and S788): 16 × 10/√356 = 16 × 0.5300 = 8.48 in, the same height.
  • 15.6-inch 16:9 side screen (FOPO S10, Cevaton Z2): 15.6 × 0.4903 = 7.65 in, so 8.48 − 7.65 = 0.83 in shorter.
  • 14-inch 16:10 side screen (FOPO S7): 14 × 0.5300 = 7.42 in, so 8.48 − 7.42 = 1.06 in shorter.
  • 17.3-inch 16:9 freestanding monitor (Arzopa A1M): 17.3 × 0.4903 = 8.48 in, the same height.
  • If your 17-inch laptop is 16:10: 17 × 0.5300 = 9.01 in, so a 16-inch 16:10 side screen is 9.01 − 8.48 = 0.53 in shorter, and none of these matches exactly.
  • Text size: 1920×1200 is √(1920² + 1200²) = 2264 px corner to corner, ÷ 16 in = 142 ppi. 1920×1200 on the S7's 14-inch panels is 2264 ÷ 14 = 162 ppi. 1920×1080 is 2203 px, ÷ 15.6 in = 141 ppi and ÷ 17.3 in = 127 ppi. 127 ÷ 141 = 0.90, so at the same scaling, text on the 16-inch and 15.6-inch side screens looks about 10% smaller than on the laptop.
  • Weight on the lid (S10): FOPO gives 4.19 lb ÷ 2 screens = about 2.1 lb per added screen.

Fit, height and video path on a 17-inch laptop

Where we drew the line. A screen that clamps to the lid is listed only if its maker states a fit range that includes 17 inches. The FOPO S11 and S788 (12–18.5 in) and the S10 and S7 (12–17.3 in) do. FOPO's S10 page says that the screen diagonal alone does not tell you whether the extender will physically fit your laptop. 17.3 in is also the top of the S10's and S7's range, so measure your lid first. The S11 and S788 leave 1.2 in of headroom (18.5 − 17.3). The Arzopa A1M and Cevaton Z2 stand on their own and never touch the lid, so lid size does not limit them. We left out the Mobile Pixels Trio 3 Pro and Duex Float 2 Pro, which Mobile Pixels rates for 15–16-inch laptops. We also left out the FOPO S1, which FOPO rates for 13.5–16.5-inch laptops, and the FOPO S9G, for which FOPO states no laptop size range.

Video path decides whether a screen works at all. A driver-free extender needs one USB-C port with DisplayPort Alt Mode for each added screen. Microsoft's USB-C help page says Windows shows "Display connection might be limited" when a port lacks the right Alternate Mode. Without such ports, each screen falls back to HDMI plus a separate power cable. Notebookcheck's Allen Ngo saw exactly that on FOPO's older 12-inch triple. On a large laptop this is the usual snag. Matt Gregersen of The Gadgeteer tested FOPO's 15-inch triple on an 18-inch Asus G18 that had only one video-capable USB-C port, so one screen had to run on HDMI plus a power-only USB-C cable. The S11, S10 and S7 are driver-free and need those ports. The S788 runs through FOPO's UsbDisplay driver over one cable, so it skips the Alt Mode check, but you install FOPO's software. FOPO's list of supported systems for the S788 includes macOS 2019+ with M1, M2 and M3. FOPO says the S11 and S10 do not support base M1, M2 or M3 MacBooks.

What the panels measure. No reviewer has measured the S788 or S10. Notebookcheck's lab figures for FOPO's older 12-inch triple show what FOPO's attached panels have measured: 193.1 cd/m² peak brightness, 247:1 contrast and 79% sRGB. Notebookcheck also advises using the extender's rear kickstand "to avoid straining the hinges of your laptop", which matters more on a heavy 17-inch lid.
