What the differences mean

  • Keys. The Keychron J5 has 108 keys. The Attack Shark X98 has 102 (108 − 102 = 6 fewer), the Gamakay TK101 has 101 (108 − 101 = 7 fewer) and the Rosewill NEON S96 has 98 (108 − 98 = 10 fewer). All four keep the number pad.
  • Workdays per charge at 8 hours a day. Keychron J5: 300 h ÷ 8 = 37.5 workdays with the backlight off, 172 h ÷ 8 = 21.5 workdays with RGB at its lowest brightness. Attack Shark X98: 200 h ÷ 8 = 25 workdays at Attack Shark's "up to" figure, which comes with no backlight condition. Gamakay and Rosewill give capacity without hours, so there is no workdays figure for the TK101 or the S96.
  • Capacity is not runtime. Keychron rates 300 h from 3,750 mAh (3,750 ÷ 300 = 12.5 mA average draw, backlight off). Attack Shark rates 200 h from 4,000 mAh (4,000 ÷ 200 = 20 mA). With RGB on, the J5's own figure is 3,750 ÷ 172 = 21.8 mA.
  • Bluetooth is the slow mode on the J5. Keychron states 1,000 Hz on the 2.4 GHz receiver and by cable (1 ÷ 1,000 = 1 ms between reports) and 125 Hz on Bluetooth (1 ÷ 125 = 8 ms). For games, use the receiver or the cable.

What each size label means in keys, and the battery behind it

Percent labels do not rank these boards. Gamakay calls 101 keys "98%". Attack Shark calls 102 keys "full-size". Rosewill's S96 has 98 keys. Keychron's "100%" is 108 keys on a board 435.93 mm wide and 128.93 mm deep that weighs 1,100 g (Keychron's figures). Count the keys, then look at the product photo for the navigation keys you use (Home, End, Page Up, Page Down, Insert), because the compact layouts reach their count by closing the gaps between key blocks and trimming that cluster.

Battery claims need the same care. Only Keychron gives hours with the backlight condition attached: about 300 h off and about 172 h with RGB at lowest brightness, the 300 h labelled a lab test result. Attack Shark gives "up to 200 hours" with no condition. Gamakay (4,000 mAh) and Rosewill (3,750 mAh) give capacity alone. Womier quotes 10,000 mAh for its X98 and gives no hours in the store text we read.
