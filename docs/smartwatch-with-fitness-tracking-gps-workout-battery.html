What the differences mean

Days per charge uses each maker's own two ratings: days = 1 ÷ (23 ÷ everyday hours + 1 ÷ GPS hours), so every day has one hour of GPS and 23 hours of normal wear. These are ceilings built from rated maxima, not measurements.

  • Amazfit Active Max: 1 ÷ (23/600 + 1/64) = 18.5 days; on The5KRunner's 40 h estimate, 1 ÷ (23/600 + 1/40) = 15.8 days.
  • Amazfit Balance 2: 1 ÷ (23/504 + 1/33) = 13.2 days in accurate GPS mode.
  • Garmin Venu 4: 45 mm 1 ÷ (23/288 + 1/20) = 7.7 days; 41 mm 1 ÷ (23/240 + 1/15) = 6.2 days.
  • Apple Watch Series 12: Apple's 24 h day already contains a 60-minute workout, so 24 ÷ 24 = 1.0 day. The SE 3's 18 h works out to 18 ÷ 24 = 0.75 day.

GPS hours per 100 dollars at today's maker prices: Active Max 64 ÷ 1.6999 = 37.6 h (23.5 h on the 40 h estimate); Balance 2 33 ÷ 2.9999 = 11.0 h; Venu 4 45 mm 20 ÷ 5.4999 = 3.6 h; Series 12 10 ÷ 3.99 = 2.5 h.

GPS hours on the same footing

A battery headline like 12 days is a smartwatch-mode figure. What matters during a run is the GPS figure, and each maker states it differently:

  • Garmin lists every mode in the Venu 4 manual. In GPS-only mode the 45 mm gets 20 h and the 41 mm gets 15 h. With all satellite systems it's 19 / 13 h, and with all systems plus multi-band 17 / 12 h. All satellites with music drops to 9 / 6 h. In that last mode the 45 mm gets 1 h less than an Apple Watch Series 12, and the 41 mm gets 4 h less.
  • Apple rates the Series 12 at 10 h and the SE 3 at 7 h. Both figures are for an outdoor run with the heart-rate sensor on and the watch used without an iPhone, so the watch's own GPS does the work.
  • Amazfit rates the Active Max at 64 h of continuous GPS. Notebookcheck reported this figure when the watch was announced, and The5KRunner estimated about 40 h at minimum brightness with the always-on display off. The Balance 2 is rated at 33 h in accurate GPS mode and 67 h in power-saving mode. Compare the Active Max's 64 h with the Balance 2's 67 h power-saving figure, not with its 33 h accurate-mode figure. $299.99: https://dealectric.com/go/amazfit-balance-2
  • Fitbit (Google's help page) rates the Versa 4 at up to 12 h of continuous built-in GPS.
  • Samsung and Google give no workout-hours rating for the Galaxy Watch8 or Pixel Watch 4. Samsung quotes up to 30 h with always-on display for both the 40 mm and 44 mm Watch8. Notebookcheck measured about 24 h with always-on display and about 1.5 days without it on a 44 mm unit.

Not recommended

Apple Watch SE 3 for distance training. Apple rates it at 7 h of outdoor-run tracking, the lowest in this table. That's enough for one long run, but not for a long run plus the rest of the day on the same charge. Apple's 18 h everyday figure already includes one 60-minute workout, so it needs charging every night. If you want an Apple Watch for running, the Series 12's 10 h and 24 h ratings give you more headroom.
