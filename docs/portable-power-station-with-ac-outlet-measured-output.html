What the differences mean

Every unit here loses part of its rated capacity on the way from the battery to the AC outlet. Plan on the measured figure, not the label:

  • BLUETTI Elite 30 V2: 231 ÷ 288 Wh = 80%. A 60 W laptop charger runs about 231 ÷ 60 ≈ 3.9 hours.
  • EcoFlow RIVER 3 Plus: 231 ÷ 286 Wh = 81%, also about 3.9 hours on 60 W. SmartZone used 330 Wh from the wall to refill it, so 231 ÷ 330 ≈ 70% round trip.
  • Anker SOLIX C300X: 300 W × 50 ÷ 60 h = 250 Wh, and 250 ÷ 288 = 87%. That is the best share here, but the inverter is rated for only 300 W.
  • EcoFlow RIVER 3: 210 ÷ 245 Wh = 86%. 210 ÷ 60 = 3.5 hours on a 60 W laptop.
  • Cost per Wh at the outlet, Elite 30 V2: $289.99 ÷ 231 Wh ≈ $1.26.

How much of the rating reaches the AC outlet

The rated Wh describes the battery. The inverter that turns it into 120 V AC uses some of that energy, so the same unit gives more through DC ports. The Technology Man measured 244 Wh on DC against 231 Wh on AC from the Elite 30 V2. SmartZone got 250 Wh from the RIVER 3 Plus's 12 V socket at 120 W, against 231 Wh on AC (228 Wh at a 100 W load, 233 Wh at 600 W). nextpit measured 237 Wh from the 300 W Jackery Explorer 300 Plus (82% of 288 Wh) and needed 327 Wh to refill it, which nextpit gives as 72% round trip. SmartZone, nextpit and The Technology Man tested 230 V European and UK units.

The two 600 W units delivered 80–81% of their rating, against 82–87% for the 300 W units. A heavier load did not cost capacity in SmartZone's test: the 600 W run on the RIVER 3 Plus returned slightly more than its 100 W run.

Idle drain is the catch with the Elite 30 V2. The Technology Man left the AC on with Eco mode off and nothing plugged in, and the battery fell from 100% to 19% in 12 hours. That is 0.81 × 288 Wh ÷ 12 h ≈ 19 W. Turn on Eco mode, or switch the AC off when nothing is plugged in. BLUETTI quotes 4.5 W standby. The 1,500 W Lifting figure is not continuous power: BLUETTI says it can reach that only momentarily, and only with purely resistive devices. Not recommended

Anker SOLIX C300 DC: Anker's own spec table lists no AC output and no AC ports for the DC model. The C300 and C300X have AC outlets, so check the suffix before you buy. Jackery Explorer 300 v2: Jackery's US store marks it sold out at $299 and sends buyers to the Explorer 300. That model costs $289 with 2 AC outlets rated at 300 W, which is about the same price as the 600 W Elite 30 V2 for half the AC rating.
