What the differences mean

Price per 10GbE port. DXP4800 GT: $559.99 ÷ 2 ports = $280.00. The DXP4800 Plus and Pro have one 10GbE port each, so that port costs the whole $619.99 or $679.99. UniFi UNAS Pro 4 on a copper network: $549 (the surcharge-included price Ubiquiti shows) + $65 adapter = $614 for one link. For both ports: ($549 + 2 × $65) ÷ 2 = $339.50 each. QNAP TS-432X with QNAP's own direct-attach cable: $659 + $69 = $728, which is $28 over $700.

What four hard drives deliver. PCWorld measured the GT at 430 MB/s read and 340 MB/s write copying large video files over 10GbE, with four 2 TB WD Red Plus drives in RAID 5. Its iperf3 run carried 9.4 Gbit/s, about 1,175 MB/s, so that read uses 430 ÷ 1,175 ≈ 37% of the link. A 2.5GbE port carries at most 2,500 ÷ 8 = 312 MB/s, so the same read is 430 ÷ 312 ≈ 1.4× what 2.5GbE could move. Two PCs copying at once reached about 780 MB/s combined, and that is where the GT's second port pays off. NASCompares measured the DXP4800 Pro at 450–500 MB/s read and 300–350 MB/s write from hard drives in RAID 5.

Power. PCWorld measured the GT at 25 W idle with the drives asleep and 40–42 W in normal use. NASCompares measured the Pro at about 28 W idle with four hard drives and both ports connected.

What a working 10GbE link costs on each box

A 10GbE port does nothing until something is plugged into it, and the two port types need different parts. The three UGREEN boxes have RJ45 10GbE: all they need is an Ethernet cable to a 10GbE switch or to a PC's 10GbE card (PCWorld tested the GT through a switch on Cat 7 cable). Their sticker price is the price of a working link. The DXP4800 Plus pairs its 10GbE port with a 2.5GbE port and costs $619.99. $619.99: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nasync-dxp4800-plus

The QNAP TS-432X has one SFP+ cage instead of an RJ45 port. QNAP's box holds an Ethernet cable, an AC adapter, a power cord, drive screws and a quick installation guide, but no SFP+ module or cable. The transceivers QNAP's US store lists for it are the TRX-10GITSFPP-SR and TRX-10GSFPP-LR optical modules, with no price shown and no RJ45 (10GBASE-T) module among them. QNAP's CAB-DAC15M-SFPP direct-attach cable costs $69 with a 2–4 week lead time, and a direct-attach cable only connects to another SFP+ port, on a switch or a network card. The other copper route is a 10GbE RJ45 card such as the QXG-10G2T, which QNAP lists for this model, in its single PCIe Gen 3 x4 slot. That slot is also the only way to add M.2 SSDs.

QNAP's headline TS-432X figure, 1,181 MB/s read and 887 MB/s write over 10GbE, was measured in QNAP's lab with four Samsung 860 PRO SSDs in RAID 5, not with hard drives.

The UniFi UNAS Pro 4's two 10G ports are SFP+ ("10G only" in Ubiquiti's spec sheet), next to one 1 GbE RJ45 port. Ubiquiti's SFP+ to RJ45 adapter costs $65 and runs 10 Gbps over Cat 6A up to 100 m. One copper link therefore costs $499 + $65 = $564, or $614 at the $549 surcharge-included price that Ubiquiti's store shows beside the sticker.

Not recommended

QNAP TS-432X on a home network built on RJ45 copper. At $659 it looks under budget, but nothing in the box connects its only 10GbE port, QNAP lists no copper module for it, and QNAP's own cable takes it to $728. It also ships with 4 GB of DDR4 (16 GB maximum) and has no M.2 slots. It makes sense only if you already run SFP+ switches and cables.
