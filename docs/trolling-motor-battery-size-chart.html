What the differences mean

Three numbers decide a trolling motor battery: its voltage, its amp hours, and the continuous amps its BMS will deliver. Here is the arithmetic behind the table:

  • Headroom = battery continuous amps ÷ motor max draw. For the 12V 120Ah kit, 120 A ÷ 50 A = 2.4×. For the 24 V kit, 100 A ÷ 60 A = 1.67×. For the 36 V kit, two packs give 2 × 50 A = 100 A, so 100 A ÷ 60 A = 1.67×, and each pack carries 60 A ÷ 2 = 30 A.
  • Worst-case runtime at full draw = amp hours ÷ max draw. That is 120 Ah ÷ 50 A = 2.4 h; 100 Ah ÷ 60 A ≈ 1.7 h for both QUEST kits; and 101 Ah ÷ 50 A ≈ 2 h for the budget kit. Johnson Outdoors says the maximum draw happens only now and then, so a normal day lasts far longer. Redodo rates its 12V 120Ah at up to 19.2 h of typical 55 lb use.
  • Refill from empty = amp hours ÷ charger amps. That is 120 Ah ÷ 20 A = 6 h; 100 Ah ÷ 20 A = 5 h; 100 Ah ÷ 25 A = 4 h. A Minn Kota onboard charger bank at 10 A needs 100 Ah ÷ 10 A ≈ 10 h.
  • Energy = volts × amp hours, for example 12.8 V × 100 Ah = 1,280 Wh. Redodo lists 1,536 Wh for the 12V 120Ah, 2,560 Wh for the 24V 100Ah and 1,920 Wh for each 36V 50Ah pack (3,840 Wh for two).

Trolling motor battery size chart by thrust, with the amps the battery must carry

Minn Kota sizes by voltage first. A motor up to 55 lb runs on one 12 V battery, over 55 to 80 lb on two 12 V batteries in series (24 V), and over 80 lb on three (36 V), with 110 Ah per battery for 6–8 hours. Current is the column most charts skip. Johnson Outdoors' Humminbird help page lists each motor's maximum draw and breaker size:

ThrustVoltageMax amp drawBreaker

40 / 45 lb12 V42 A50 A
50 / 55 lb12 V50 A60 A

QUEST24 V or 36 V60 A60 A

Minn Kota's rule for lithium is that the battery's continuous discharge rating must cover the motor's maximum draw. When it doesn't, Minn Kota lists the symptoms: the motor runs briefly and then shuts off, powers on without turning the prop, steers erratically, or loses Spot-Lock. Minn Kota also says QUEST motors are built not to pull more than 60 A on high speed. It wants a breaker or fuse of no more than 60 A on the positive terminal, as close to the battery as possible. Redodo's store does not sell one, so buy it separately.

Minn Kota gives two more limits. Brushed motors on lithium should stay at 85% (speed 8.5) or below. Its PCL onboard chargers deliver 10 A per bank and at most 14.5 V, and Minn Kota tells owners to check that figure with the battery maker. At 14.5 V per bank, an onboard charger cannot reach the 29.2 V a single 24 V lithium battery needs or the 43.8 V a 36 V one needs, so every kit here includes its own matched charger. Redodo sells its 12 V batteries for 30–70 lb motors, but the chart puts 70 lb motors on 24 V, so a single 12 V battery only fits the 12 V rows.

Not recommended

Redodo 36V 50Ah on its own for a 36 V QUEST motor. Redodo sells it for 100–120 lb motors and rates it at 50 A steady, with 60 A for only up to 30 minutes. That is below the 60 A a QUEST can draw on high speed (50 ÷ 60 = 0.83×), so the BMS can cut power at full throttle or while Spot-Lock holds against wind. Two in parallel fix that, which is the 36 V kit above.

Redodo 24V 50Ah Bluetooth on a 24 V QUEST motor. Redodo rates it at 60 A continuous for 30 minutes. That equals the QUEST ceiling with no margin (60 ÷ 60 = 1.0×), and only for half an hour. The 24V 100Ah's 100 A BMS costs $215 more as a kit and removes the question.
