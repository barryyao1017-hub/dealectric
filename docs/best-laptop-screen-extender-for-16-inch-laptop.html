What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure below comes from the maker or the named reviewer; the arithmetic is ours.

  • Pixels added. FOPO S11 and INVZI MagHub Dual Lite 16: 2 × 1920 × 1200 = 4.61 MP. Duex Float 2 Pro: 2560 × 1600 = 4.10 MP. Trio 3 Pro: 2 × 2240 × 1400 = 6.27 MP. FOPO S788: 3 × 1920 × 1200 = 6.91 MP.
  • Height next to a 16-inch screen. The 16-inch MacBook Pro's display is 2234 px ÷ 254 ppi = 8.80 in tall (Apple). A 16-inch 16:10 panel is 16 × 10 ÷ √356 = 8.48 in tall, 0.32 in shorter. The Trio's 14.1-inch panels are 14.1 × 10 ÷ √356 = 7.47 in tall, 1.32 in shorter.

One video cable per screen: what decides whether it works

macOS does not extend displays over DisplayPort MST. Plugable (2026-03-06) says an MST hub on a Mac shows the same image on both monitors, even on the M5 Max, and that two extended screens from one cable need a Thunderbolt or DisplayLink dock. Macworld's Simon Jary (2026-03-18) says the same: the M5 Pro and M5 Max reach several displays through Thunderbolt tunnelling, not MST. So on a Mac, a dual extender gives you two separate extended screens in one of two ways: one video cable per screen, or a display driver.

  • FOPO S11: FOPO's own FAQ answers "Can I connect both screens with one USB-C cable?" with "No." Both laptop ports must carry video and supply enough power. On compatible ports no driver is installed.
  • Trio 3 Pro: Macworld quotes Mobile Pixels' manual: with "two DP enabled USB-C ports … driver installation is not necessary." Running both screens from one cable needs Mobile Pixels' software.
  • FOPO S788: always needs the UsbDisplay driver. It does not depend on Alt Mode ports, but you install the driver, restart, and allow it under Screen Recording on macOS.
  • Duex Float 2 Pro: one screen, so it needs only one video-capable port. Mighty Gadget's James Smythe ran it from one USB-C cable with no software on Windows.

16-inch MacBook Pro. Apple rates the first Apple-silicon 16-inch model (M1 Pro, 2021) for two external displays. It rates today's M5 Pro for three and the M5 Max for four, and the current model has three Thunderbolt 5 ports plus HDMI. Macworld names base M1 and M2 MacBooks as the ones limited to one external screen. Apple sells today's 16-inch MacBook Pro only with M5 Pro or M5 Max chips. FOPO's S11 chip list stops at M3 Pro and Max, so owners of an M4 or M5 16-inch should send FOPO their model before ordering, as FOPO asks.

16-inch Windows laptops. Check the laptop's spec sheet for two USB-C ports with DisplayPort Alt Mode (often marked with a DP or Thunderbolt logo). Notebookcheck tested FOPO's 12-inch triple extender on an HP Pavilion Plus 14, and each added panel appeared as an ordinary external monitor over its own USB-C DisplayPort cable.

Brightness figures in the table are the makers' own. In the only lab test of a FOPO extender we found, Notebookcheck measured FOPO's 12-inch triple at 193.1 cd/m² peak with 247:1 contrast.

Not recommended

A two-screen extender sold on "one USB-C cable", for a Mac. Flexpire's Tri-screen Pro 16 ($459.99 for the screen only on flexpire.com, 2026-11-18) says "Connect quickly with a single USB-C cable. Plug & play compatibility with macOS and Windows." macOS does not extend over MST, so one cable can drive two separate screens on a Mac only through a driver or Thunderbolt. Flexpire's page does not say which one it uses.
