What the differences mean

Added workspace, counted in pixels from each maker's resolution:

  • Trio 3 Pro: 2 × 2240 × 1400 = 6.27 MP, the most of any two-screen unit here.
  • FOPO S7: 2 × 1920 × 1200 = 4.61 MP, in two 14-inch 16:10 panels.
  • Duex Float 2 Pro: 2560 × 1600 = 4.10 MP, in a single 16-inch panel, a little less than the S7's two screens together.
  • Duex Max DS: 1920 × 1080 = 2.07 MP, one screen.
  • FOPO S788: 3 × 1920 × 1200 = 6.91 MP, the most here, but only through FOPO's driver.

Battery cost on the current 13-inch MacBook Air (53.8 Wh, Apple):

  • Duex Max DS at 4.5 W (Mobile Pixels): 53.8 ÷ 4.5 = 12.0 hours to drain the battery from the screen alone.
  • Duex Float 2 Pro at 7.5 W in Limited Power Mode (Macworld): 53.8 ÷ 7.5 = 7.2 hours.

Both figures leave out the laptop's own draw, so real runtime is shorter.

How many screens your laptop runs with the lid open

A screen extender hangs on the open lid, so the number that matters is how many external displays the laptop drives with its own screen on. Apple's spec pages say:

  • The M1 and M2 MacBook Air drive one external display.
  • The M3 MacBook Air drives one with the lid open. Apple adds: “Close the MacBook Air lid to use a second external display.”
  • The current MacBook Air drives up to two.
  • The MacBook Neo drives one at up to 4K 60 Hz. Only one of its two USB-C ports carries DisplayPort; the other is USB 2 for charging and data.

Mobile Pixels gives the same split in its own guide: M4 runs two with the lid open, M3 runs two only with the lid closed.

So each extender's video path decides what you get:

  • Native (USB-C DisplayPort Alt Mode). No software, but one video-capable port and one display slot per added screen. On a base M1/M2/M3 Air that means one added screen. A single-screen unit like the $215.99: https://dealectric.com/go/mobile-pixels-duex-max-ds fits that limit exactly. The FOPO S7 needs its own video connection for each screen, so a base M1, M2 or M3 Air lights one of its two screens.
  • Driver (DisplayLink, Silicon Motion or the maker's own). Video goes over USB and gets around the Mac's display limit, but each needs its manager app installed on macOS (Plugable). Plugable also notes that on macOS, protected video may stay blank on every screen while a DisplayLink display is active. The FOPO S788 and the Aura Dual Boost always use a driver.
  • The Trio 3 Pro uses either path. It ships two 20-inch USB-C cables, one per screen. Macworld quotes the manual: “if your laptop has two DP enabled USB-C ports … driver installation is not necessary.” Running both screens from one cable needs Mobile Pixels' software plus a power adapter on the Trio, which also passes up to 65 W to the laptop. Without that adapter, the screens can't reach full brightness on laptop power. Macworld lists plain M1 and M2 Macs and the MacBook Neo as needing the software, and M1/M2/M3 Pro/Max or any M4/M5 MacBook as fine without it. Apple's lid-open rule puts the base M3 Air in the software group too.

Fit ranges also differ by source. Mobile Pixels lists the Trio 3 Pro and the Duex Float 2 Pro for 15–16-inch laptops. Macworld ran the Trio on a 14-inch MacBook Pro and lists 13–16-inch MacBooks.

Not recommended

ViewSonic VG1457 for a MacBook. ViewSonic's own page says “Dual-screen Extend mode is unavailable on macOS and mobile devices due to MST limitations”. Only the lower screen activates, at up to 1920×1080. A Mac owner pays for two screens and gets one. On Windows it is a reasonable freestanding pair that puts no weight on the hinge.

Xebec Tri-Screen 3 on a Mac. Xebec says its Tri-Screen 3 Mac Adapter “is required for all Mac users”, because Apple does not support MST, and the free DisplayLink driver must be installed. That takes the Mac cost to $699 + $59 = $758 at Xebec's store on 2026-11-18, and it still runs on a driver.
