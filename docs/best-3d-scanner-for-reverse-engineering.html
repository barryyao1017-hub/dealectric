What the differences mean

A handheld scanner's volumetric accuracy is a base error plus a rate for each metre of part length. The error to plan for is base + rate × the part's length in metres.

  • FreeScan UE Pro2 (with photogrammetry): 0.02 + 0.015 × 0.3 = 0.0245 mm on a 300 mm part; 0.02 + 0.015 × 1 = 0.035 mm on a 1 m part.
  • MetroX Pro: 0.02 + 0.04 × 0.3 = 0.032 mm; 0.02 + 0.04 × 1 = 0.060 mm.
  • HandySCAN 3D PRO: 0.020 + 0.060 × 0.3 = 0.038 mm; 0.020 + 0.060 × 1 = 0.080 mm. With MaxSHOT: 0.030 + 0.015 × 1 = 0.045 mm. MaxSHOT only beats the default past about 0.22 m (0.020 + 0.060L = 0.030 + 0.015L at L ≈ 0.22).
  • Raptor Pro: 0.02 + 0.08 × 0.3 = 0.044 mm; 0.02 + 0.08 × 1 = 0.10 mm.
  • EinScan Rigil, Laser HD: 0.04 + 0.06 × 0.3 = 0.058 mm; 0.04 + 0.06 × 1 = 0.10 mm. IR Rapid: 0.1 + 0.4 × 1 = 0.50 mm, five times the laser figure.
  • MetroX: 0.03 + 0.1 × 0.3 = 0.060 mm; 0.03 + 0.1 × 1 = 0.13 mm.

Headline accuracy vs volumetric accuracy, and who backs each figure

The big number on a spec sheet is usually a single-frame or sphere-diameter figure: 0.02 mm for the UE Pro2, up to 0.030 mm for the HandySCAN 3D PRO, up to 0.02 mm for the MetroX Pro. That number does not grow with part size. Creaform's footnotes show the difference. Its 0.030 mm is a typical value for diameter measurement on a calibrated sphere artefact, while its volumetric figure is sphere spacing measured on a calibrated length artefact. On a long part, the volumetric figure is the one that tells you whether two holes end up where the drawing says.

The figures are not all backed the same way. SHINING 3D states the UE Pro2's accuracy is tested to VDI/VDE 2634 Part 3 and ISO 10360 in an ISO/IEC 17025 accredited lab, subject to the certificates it actually issues. The Rigil's spec page names no standard. Revopoint's MetroX and MetroX Pro formulas, as 3DTechValley lists them, come with no test method. Creality's US store pages carry no mm/m number at all; the Raptor Pro formula above is the one CNC Kitchen quotes from Creality.

Here is what named testers measured. CNC Kitchen scanned a 100.25 mm ball bar three times with the original CR-Scan Raptor ($879 on Creality's store, 2026-11-08) and got 100.24, 100.25 and 100.22 mm. The worst error was 0.03 mm over 100 mm. CNC Kitchen's 2026 Raptor Pro ball-bar test stayed within about ±0.02 mm, with one reading slightly outside spec. igor'sLAB found the MetroX reaches 0.03 mm on an ideal rectangular block (caliper vs CAD) but published no table. For the UE Pro2, HandySCAN and Rigil, the numbers on this page are the makers' own.

A scanner gives you a mesh, not a STEP file, so budget for scan-to-CAD software too. Creality sells QUICKSURFACE Lite as a 540-dollar one-year add-on to the Raptor. Revopoint's Revo Metro measurement module is currently available only for MetroY Ultra models, not the MetroX line. Not recommended

The EinScan Rigil for precision reverse engineering. At $4,999 (MatterHackers, 2026-11-08), its best mode, Laser HD, works out to 0.058 mm on a 300 mm part. That is looser than the MetroX Pro's 0.032 mm. Its IR Rapid mode reaches 0.50 mm at 1 m, which is only good enough to capture a shape. The extra money buys standalone, wireless scanning, not accuracy.
