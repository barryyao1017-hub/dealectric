Can a NAS replace iCloud+ 2 TB for a family of iPhones and a Mac? (2026)

Updated 2026-11-08

Short answer. Partly. A NAS can replace iCloud+ 2 TB for a family of iPhones for photo backup, shared files and the Mac's Time Machine, but not for iPhone device backup and restore, iCloud Photos sync with Optimized Storage, Messages in iCloud or Apple's recovery, and with an off-site copy it costs more than $599 over five years. If you want one as the family's own photo library, buy the UGREEN NASync DXP2800 ($389.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nasync-dxp2800), $389.99 diskless on 2026-11-03, and run Immich in Docker.

What to know: iCloud+ 2 TB is $9.99 a month, shared by up to six people with separate spaces, and it is the only place an iPhone backs itself up to (a Finder backup on the Mac is the other). Every NAS photo app on iOS (UGREEN's, Synology Photos, Immich) depends on iOS granting background time; UGREEN's December 2025 app update fixed background backup stopping when the screen turned off and Live Photos sticking on iOS 26, so expect to open the app. The $239.99 DH2300 ($239.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/ugreen-nasync-dh2300) is the cheapest entry with UGREEN's app and Time Machine but no Docker, so no Immich. For a non-technical Apple family Synology's BeeStation Plus (about $370–400, 8 TB, BeePhotos, no setup, no RAID) or a DS225+ with Synology Photos and Drive's on-demand Mac sync is the more polished route. Five years: iCloud $599; a NAS with two 4 TB drives ($130–230 each in 2026's rising market) plus Backblaze B2 for 2 TB ($6.95 per TB a month, about $834) runs $1,300–1,700; skip the off-site copy and the NAS is cheaper, and a fire or theft takes everything.

What iCloud+ does that no NAS does

  • iPhone backup and restore: iCloud backs each phone up daily while charging, locked and on Wi-Fi, and a new phone restores from it. A NAS app backs up photos, videos and Files-app items only; the nearest NAS equivalent is a Finder backup on the Mac copied to the NAS.
  • Photos sync with Optimized Storage: iCloud keeps full-resolution originals in the cloud and small versions on the phone; with a NAS the originals are on the NAS and the phone's own storage still fills, unless you use Immich's "free up space" (which deletes locally and, with iCloud sync on, from iCloud too).
  • Everything else in the plan: Messages in iCloud, Shared Photo Library, Hide My Email, Private Relay, custom email domain, HomeKit Secure Video, and Apple-handled recovery.

What a NAS takes over well

A per-person photo and video archive from four phones (each app uploads new items over Wi-Fi in the background when iOS allows), shared folders for the family, the Mac's Time Machine over SMB, and a library larger than 2 TB without a bigger subscription. Immich on the DXP2800 is the closest thing to a self-hosted Photos app (faces, map, albums, sharing); Synology Photos and BeePhotos are the polished appliance routes; UGREEN's own app covers backup and browsing with fewer frills.

The arithmetic

iCloud+ 2 TB: $9.99 × 60 = $599 over five years, tax extra. DH2300 with two 4 TB drives: $240 + $260–460; DXP2800: $390 + $260–460; add Backblaze B2 for 2 TB at $6.95 per TB a month (about $834 over five years) for the off-site copy iCloud gives you by default, and the NAS lands at $1,300–1,700. A BeeStation Plus with BeeProtect is about $970–1,000 and is unmirrored. Drive prices rose sharply from late 2025 (IronWolf 4 TB from about $94 to $132 by January 2026; Synology's own 4 TB at about $229), so price them the week you buy. Power is small change: the DH2300 draws 3–14 W, the DXP2800 5–16 W.
