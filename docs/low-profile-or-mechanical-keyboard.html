What the differences mean

  • Travel: Cherry's full-size MX2A Red travels 4.0 mm. Ergonaut lists Gateron's second-generation low-profile switch (KS-33) at 3.2 ±0.2 mm on most variants and 3.0 ±0.2 mm on Moss and Banana. 4.0 − 3.2 = 0.8 mm and 4.0 − 3.0 = 1.0 mm, so each press is 0.8 to 1.0 mm shorter, or 20 to 25%. That is the laptop-like feel people mean by low-profile.
  • Height at the wrist: KitGuru lists the full-height Q1 Max at 22.6 mm at the front edge and 35.8 mm at the back, before keycaps, with a fixed 5.2° angle. That is why a full-height board usually wants a wrist rest.
  • Weight and price: 1,724 g ÷ 525 g = 3.3×, so the Q1 Max weighs over three times as much as the K3 Max. $209.99 − $89.99 = $120 more for the same 75% layout and the same 2.4 GHz, Bluetooth and wired modes.
  • Battery with lights on: The Q1 Max's 180 h rating is with the backlight off. KitGuru lists 30 h at the lowest RGB brightness, and 180 ÷ 30 = 6× less. Among low-profile boards, the K3 Ultra 8K's 550 h rating is 550 ÷ 73 = 7.5× the K3 Max's.
  • Replacement switches: Keychron sells Gateron low-profile 2.0 switch sets for $13.99. Lofree's Kailh-made low-profile POM switch packs are $59 each.

A low-profile board ties you to one switch family

A low-profile keyboard uses the same mechanism as a full-height one: a sprung mechanical switch under each key. It is just shorter. The catch is that each low-profile board only takes its own family of short switches, while a full-height board like the Q1 Max takes ordinary full-size switches. Keychron sells Gateron Jupiter sets for it at $13.99 and offers a barebone version without switches or keycaps.

  • Keychron K3 Max: Keychron says you can hot-swap Gateron low-profile MX-style switches "for RGB Backlight Hot-Swappable Version only". On Gateron's 2.0 low-profile switch, Ergonaut lists SA, MT3 and Cherry-profile keycaps as fitting but advises against GMK. On the older first-generation switch, none of those fit.
  • Keychron low-profile keycaps are generation-specific: Keychron's LSA keycap set Version 2 "is not compatible with the Keychron K1 versions 1 to 5, K1 SE, K3, K5, K5 SE, and K7". Check your board's version before buying keycaps.
  • Lofree Flow 2: Gadgetoid found the Flow 2's Kailh low-profile switches "are not pin compatible with MX low profile switches". It also found that full-height Cherry or SA keycaps "will most likely stick over the switch when pressed down". In practice you upgrade through Lofree's own switch packs. That is fine if you like the feel out of the box: $159: https://dealectric.com/go/lofree-flow-2-84

Not recommended

A Keychron K3 Max that isn't the RGB hot-swappable version. Keychron limits switch swapping to that version, so any other K3 Max locks you to its factory switches. That gives up the main advantage the K3 Max has over other low-profile boards.
