What the differences mean

  • Refill time per 100 Ah: at 10 A per bank, 100 Ah ÷ 10 A = 10 h, with all three banks charging together (NOCO GENPRO10X3, Minn Kota MK 330 PCL). On a 36V 100Ah pack the Redodo 43.8V 25A takes 100 Ah ÷ 25 A = 4 h, and the Dakota 36V 8A takes 100 Ah ÷ 8 A = 12.5 h (Dakota quotes under 12 h). These figures cover only the constant-current stage. The final constant-voltage stage adds time.
  • Same energy, different speed: three 12V 100Ah batteries and one 36V 100Ah pack hold the same energy (3 × 12.8 V × 100 Ah = 38.4 V × 100 Ah = 3,840 Wh). The GENPRO puts back 30 A × 12.8 V ≈ 384 W. The Redodo 43.8V 25A puts back 25 A × 38.4 V = 960 W, 2.5 times as fast.
  • Onboard cost per amp: GENPRO10X3: $324.95 ÷ 30 A = $10.83 per amp. MK 330 PCL: $399.99 ÷ 30 A = $13.33 per amp. Both are 3 × 10 A. The NOCO costs $75.04 less and BatteryStuff lists it as IP68. Vance Outdoors' MK 330 PCL listing says only “waterproof”, with no IP number.
  • Battery size per bank: BatteryStuff's GENPRO10X3 listing recommends about 130 Ah or smaller per bank for everyday charging. It treats 230 Ah per bank as maintenance only.

Why one 24V or 36V lithium battery needs its own charger

A multi-bank onboard charger gives one 12V output per bank. Minn Kota says its chargers “are designed for 12 Volt battery systems only” and that the PCL line is “not compatible with single 24V and 36V Lithium Batteries”. NOCO's GENPRO guide lists only 12V modes for each bank: 14.6 V lithium, 14.8 V AGM, 14.5 V wet or gel. On a boat wired from separate 12V batteries, each bank charges one battery. A single 36V pack has just two terminals and needs the full 36V-class charge voltage across them, so no 12V bank can charge it.

A 36V (38.4V) LiFePO4 pack charges at three times a 12V pack's voltage: Redodo uses 3 × 14.6 V = 43.8 V and Dakota Lithium uses 3 × 14.4 V = 43.2 V. For 24V packs the figures are 29.2 V (Redodo) and 28.8 V (Dakota). Because the setpoints differ, buy the charger your battery maker specifies, or one whose voltage matches the battery's stated charge voltage. Redodo's 36V 50Ah trolling battery, built for 100–120 lb thrust motors, pairs with the $149.99: https://dealectric.com/go/redodo-43-8v-25a-lifepo4-charger. Check the pack's maximum charge current before you use a 25 A charger on it. For a 24V pack the match is the $109.99: https://dealectric.com/go/redodo-29-2v-20a-mini-lifepo4-charger, and for one 12V lithium battery charged at home it is the $82.99: https://dealectric.com/go/redodo-14-6v-20a-lifepo4-charger.

Water and cold: the Redodo 43.8V 25A is rated IP65 and has a sealed, capped output connector, so it can stay on the boat. Redodo lists no water rating for the 29.2V 20A or the 14.6V 20A, so use those as shore chargers. Minn Kota's PCL applies no temperature compensation in lithium mode. Redodo's trolling batteries stop taking charge below 32°F and resume above 41°F, so in cold weather the battery decides when charging starts, not the charger.

Not recommended

For a 100 Ah lithium battery that you run down hard, skip chargers with 5 or 6 A per bank. Minn Kota's MK 318 PCL gives 6 A per bank: 100 Ah ÷ 6 A = 16.7 h. NOCO's GEN5X3 gives 5 A per bank: 100 Ah ÷ 5 A = 20 h, and NOCO's guide limits each bank to batteries up to 120 Ah. Neither refills a drained trolling battery overnight. Also, never connect a single 36V lithium pack to one bank of a multi-bank onboard charger: each bank supplies only a 12V charge.
