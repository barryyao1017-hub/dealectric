What the differences mean

  • Apple's 18-hour rating mostly assumes an iPhone nearby. 4 h on cellular ÷ 18 h rated = 22% of the day away from the phone. Apple's all-day cellular test (Apple Watch For Your Kids, Find My sharing on, lighter use) gives up to 16 h: 18 − 16 = 2 h less.
  • The 40 mm Galaxy Watch8 has less battery than the one Notebookcheck measured. 325 mAh ÷ 435 mAh = 0.75, about a quarter less than the 44 mm watch that Notebookcheck says 'won't last for a whole day' when used long away from a phone.
  • TickTalk's line is a running cost. Plans start at $9.99 a month: 9.99 × 24 = $239.76 over two years, on top of the watch.
  • The Active Max trades calls for battery. 25 days × 24 h = 600 h; 600 ÷ 16 h (Apple's all-day cellular figure) = 37.5 times longer between charges, with no line to pay for.

Who owns the phone decides which watch works

None of these watches can be set up with no phone at all. Samsung says its Wear OS models, Galaxy Watch4 and later, 'require a compatible phone during the initial setup'. Only the older Galaxy Watch, Watch Active, Active2 and Watch3 could skip that step. So what matters is whose phone does the one-time setup, and what the watch can do afterwards.

  • Apple Watch SE 3 GPS + Cellular. In normal pairing the iPhone and watch must use the same carrier, and prepaid accounts aren't supported. Apple Watch For Your Kids removes the carrier rule. A family member's iPhone (iPhone 11 or later on iOS 27 for the SE 3) sets up the watch in the same Family Sharing group. The watch then gets its own line on AT&T, BetterRoaming, Boost Mobile, Cellcom, C Spire, Cricket Wireless, T-Mobile, Verizon or Xfinity Mobile. In that mode there is no international roaming, ECG, Sleep, Blood Oxygen or card Apple Pay, and Fall Detection needs age 18 or over.
  • Samsung Galaxy Watch8 LTE. Galaxy Watch for Kids works on the Watch8, Watch7 and Watch6 LTE. It needs a one-time pairing from a parent's Samsung phone, a Samsung Account, Google Family Link and an LTE plan; Verizon, AT&T and T-Mobile were named at launch. Verizon adds that the parent phone must run One UI 5.1 or later and be on the same Verizon account as the watch. For adults the watch needs Android 12 or later with more than 1.5 GB of memory, and it doesn't work with iPhone.
  • Google Pixel Watch 4 LTE. Google says the watch and the Android phone 'must use the same carrier network' for full talk, text and data. It needs Android 11 or later and doesn't support iPhone.
  • TickTalk 6. The parent app runs on iPhone and Android. The watch needs a TickTalk Wireless plan (AT&T or T-Mobile networks) and 'cannot be added directly to an existing AT&T, T-Mobile, or Verizon family phone plan'.

Not recommended

Pixel Watch 4 LTE for someone who has no phone. Google's compatibility page ties the watch to the wearer's own Android 11+ phone on the same carrier. Apple and Samsung both document a kids setup; Google's page describes none. Buy it to leave your own Android phone at home, not to stand in for a phone you don't have.
