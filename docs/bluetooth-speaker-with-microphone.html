What happens when a Bluetooth speaker's microphone opens

Bluetooth carries music and microphone audio over two different profiles. Microsoft's Windows driver documentation states that A2DP, the stereo music profile, "only supports audio output". When an app uses the speaker's microphone, the link has to switch to the Hands-Free Profile (HFP), which carries mono playback and mono capture at 8 kHz (narrowband) or 16 kHz (wideband).

  • Windows 10: once an app opens the hands-free microphone, "any audio streaming to the stereo output is discarded".
  • Windows 11: the switch happens automatically whenever an app opens the mic, and any other sound playing to the speaker is resampled down to the 8 or 16 kHz call rate. Music goes back to stereo only after the mic closes.
  • Narrowband fallback: Microsoft notes that some wideband-capable devices are not compatible with the Bluetooth radio in some PCs and drop to 8 kHz narrowband.
  • LE Audio removes the trade-off: Microsoft's LE Audio documentation describes stereo playback while the microphone is in use on Windows 11 (for example 48 kHz stereo out with 32 kHz mono capture). The speaker and the PC both need LE Audio. Marshall describes the Emberton III as LE Audio-ready, for when source devices support it.

On a phone call the hands-free profile is the normal mode, so any speaker with a built-in mic and HFP works. The trade-off matters on a laptop. A USB speakerphone such as the Jabra Speak2 55 avoids it, because the computer treats it as a wired audio device. Its speak mode runs 150–7,000 Hz, which is the voice band of 16 kHz wideband sampling. Jabra lists the 55's Bluetooth profiles as A2DP, AVRCP, BLE and HFP. Over Bluetooth it pairs with up to 8 devices, two at a time. Jabra says the computer connection is "via cable only", so a laptop on cable and a phone on Bluetooth can be connected at once.

Not recommended

  • JBL Clip 5 as a call speaker. JBL Support, answering on Best Buy, says it "is designed for music playback only and does not support making or receiving phone calls". SoundGuys' review confirms there is no mic. It is a fine clip-on music speaker, but it is not a speaker with a microphone.
  • Bose SoundLink Flex (2nd Gen) special editions. Bose support says the special edition "does not contain a microphone" and "does not have speakerphone functionality". Their serial numbers begin with 086252 or 086253, so check the serial before you buy one for calls.
