How many watts a window air conditioner uses

From the makers' spec sheets: a 5,000 BTU Frigidaire (FFRA051WAE) 450 W; an 8,000 BTU GE (AHEK08AC) 695 W; an 8,000 BTU Midea U inverter (MAW08V1QWT) 710 W rated, less once the room is cool. The compressor cycles, so over an hour a fixed-speed unit uses about 50–70% of its running watts on a warm day.

Startup surge

A fixed-speed compressor draws its locked-rotor current (LRA on the nameplate) for a fraction of a second; LRA × 115 V is the surge to compare with the station's surge rating. BLUETTI's "Power Lifting" figures are for resistive loads such as heaters, not motor starts, so go by the 2,400 W rating; 5,000–8,000 BTU units are within what these stations start. An inverter window AC starts slowly and avoids the spike.

Runtime math

Hours ≈ battery Wh × 0.85 ÷ the AC's watts. For a night, 8 hours of an 8,000 BTU AC cycling takes about 3–4 kWh of battery; with less, cool the room before sleeping and let the station run the AC in the hottest hours.
