What the differences mean

On a 3 A cable, all five chargers stop at 60 W. 20 V × 3 A = 60 W. A 65 W rating needs 65 ÷ 20 = 3.25 A, and 67 W needs 67 ÷ 20 = 3.35 A. Both are above 3 A. On a basic cable, Native Union's extra 2 W makes no difference.

With a phone also plugged in, the laptop gets 45 W on every split we could source. Best Batteries gives the UGREEN as 45 W + 20 W (45 ÷ 65 = 69% of the rating to the laptop). Verbatim lists its 32201 at 45 W + 18 W, and D-Link lists its DCP-651 at 45 W + 20 W. At 45 W the laptop draws 45 ÷ 20 = 2.25 A, which any 3 A cable carries. So the cable only matters when the laptop is charging on its own.

The cable decides whether you get 65 W or 60 W

USB-C chargers reach 65 W at 20 V and 3.25 A. TI's engineers Brian Gosselin and Nate Enos explain that USB-C cables "are required to be electronically marked if they are either full-featured and/or rated at more than 3A current", and that "electronically marked cables use USB PD to provide the cable's characteristics". A cable without that chip is treated as a 3 A cable, so the charger offers at most 20 V × 3 A = 60 W.

UGREEN's own specs show the same limit. On its Nexode 65W charger with a retractable cable, UGREEN rates the built-in cable at "20V/3A 60W Max" and the separate USB-C port beside it at "20V/3.25A 65W Max". The travel charger does not come with a USB-C cable (SmartGadgetKit and Best Batteries), so the cable you pack sets the ceiling. Best Batteries says about 62 W reaches a laptop from the UGREEN on one port, which is more than the 60 W a 3 A cable can carry at 20 V.
