What owners complain about

We read owner forums, 1-3 star review themes and five independent lab-style reviews. Seven sources flagged wrong wrist heart rate during lifting or intervals: the sensor lags when the wrist flexes, and a Cleveland Clinic study found wrist monitors less accurate than a chest strap off the treadmill. The fix is the same on every brand: wear the sensor on the upper arm or add a chest strap. Five sources reported battery falling short of the claim, mostly Fitbit Charge 6 owners whose tracker died early, and reviewers who got 5 to 9 days from watches rated 10 or 11. Six threads were about bands too small for large wrists, nearly all Fitbit. Four sources complained about subscriptions: Whoop's cheapest plan is $199 a year and Fitbit holds full insights behind Premium. Four reviews called Amazfit's Zepp app cluttered, and four reported sync problems or phantom auto-detected workouts.

Which one by need

Lifting plus daily wear on a big wrist: the Amazfit Active Max. The 48.5 mm case and 1.5-inch screen suit a larger wrist, the battery runs weeks and it stores offline maps. Its single-band GPS scored 81% on the5krunner's 10-mile test, behind dual-band watches, so it is not the pick for trail navigation.

Heart-rate-zone running: the Garmin Forerunner 165. Two reviewers measured it against a Polar H10 and found almost no error on steady runs. Its weak spots are breadcrumb-only navigation and a 2024 launch price near the Amazfit Balance 2.

Recovery tracking without a fee: the Amazfit Helio Strap. It costs less than half of one year of Whoop, has no screen to break under a barbell, and can sit on the upper arm where optical readings are steadier. Start workouts manually because auto-detection invents sessions.

Under $100 with a screen: the Amazfit Active 2 Round. It tracked within 1 to 2 bpm of an armband reference and locked GPS in about 10 seconds in central London, but it comes in one 44 mm size and real-world battery lands nearer 5 to 6 days with heavy use.

Not recommended

Fitbit Charge 6 for men with large wrists or who lift. Its battery drops to 3 days with the always-on display, it has one button, and owner reviews repeat three themes: sync failures, early battery drain and band fit. Whoop 5.0 only if you want its coaching: the hardware is free but the One plan is $199 a year, and the Helio Strap matched it on wrist heart rate in reviewers' tests. Amazfit Active 2 Square at $149.99 adds sapphire glass but not a bigger case, so the Active Max is the better spend for $20 more.
