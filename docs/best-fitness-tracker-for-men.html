Why a WHOOP costs more than its card suggests

WHOOP doesn't sell the band on its own. In Engadget's words (Matt Tate, May 2025), 'Whoop bands aren't available as standalone devices.' The band comes with a yearly membership. In August 2026 Engadget (Anna Washenko) still listed the tiers at $199 (One), $239 (Peak) and $359 (Life), and the 2025 report says Life is only for the WHOOP MG. If you stop paying, you stop getting the service. Over two years that adds up to more than a vívoactive 6, which you pay for once. WIRED says Garmin's $70-a-year Connect+ isn't needed for most people.

The makers also measure battery life under different conditions, so the day counts don't compare directly. Garmin's 11 days for the vívoactive 6 drops to 5 with the always-on display (WIRED). Google's 7 days for the Charge 6 comes with a note that always-on display and SpO2 'will require more frequent charging'. Apple quotes 24 hours for the Series 12. WHOOP and the Helio Strap have no screen to power. Apple's US store now sells the Series 12 and SE 3 (from $249), not the Series 11.

If you lift weights, the vívoactive 6 has an advantage: Garmin's manual says it is 'compatible with chest heart rate monitors' and uses the strap's data over the wrist sensor when both are available at the start of an activity. The Helio Strap can be worn on the upper arm and broadcasts live heart rate to compatible watches, apps and gym equipment over Bluetooth (Amazfit).

Not recommended

WHOOP 5.0 as a first tracker on a budget. On the cheapest plan, two years cost $398, more than a vívoactive 6 and four times a Helio Strap, and the band comes only with the membership. WIRED gives its 'high ongoing cost' as a reason it is 'harder to recommend'.
