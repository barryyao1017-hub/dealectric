What the differences mean

Every figure starts from one fridge: the 630 kWh/yr example on the Building America Solution Center's outage page. 630,000 Wh ÷ 365 = 1,726 Wh a day, about 72 W on average.

  • The station's own draw adds 240 Wh a day. The Gadgeteer lists the Elite 200 V2's idle draw as 10 W with AC and DC on: 10 W × 24 h = 240 Wh, for 1,966 Wh a day at the outlet.
  • Conversion loss raises that to about 2,140 Wh taken from the battery. StorageReview measured 1.91 kWh of AC from the 2,073.6 Wh battery (92%): 1,966 ÷ 0.92 ≈ 2,140 Wh. These figures count both idle draw and conversion loss, so they lean cautious.
  • On battery alone the fridge runs about 23 hours. 1,910 Wh ÷ (72 + 10) W ≈ 23 h.
  • Full-sun hours to replace one day: 2,140 ÷ 400 W ≈ 5.3 h; 2,140 ÷ 200 W ≈ 10.7 h; 2,140 ÷ 700 W ≈ 3.1 h. A fridge labeled 400 kWh/yr needs (1,096 + 240) ÷ 0.92 ≈ 1,450 Wh, or 3.6 h on 400 W.
  • The sun you can count on: most of the continental US averages about 4 to 4.5 peak sun hours a day (Unbound Solar, from NREL PVWatts data). At 4.25 h, 400 W puts back about 1,700 Wh, leaving a daily gap of about 440 Wh. 2,073.6 ÷ 440 ≈ 4.7 days before the battery is empty. With one 200 W panel the gap is 2,140 − 850 = 1,290 Wh, which comes to about 1.6 days.
  • Panels deliver less than their label. The Technology Man got about 300 W from two 200 W panels on a sunny, partly cloudy day, so treat these hours as best case.

Why two 200 W panels, and how to wire them

The kit: The kit: Fridge outage solar backup (BLUETTI Elite 200 V2 + 2× 200 W Solar Panel Kit + BLUETTI Solar Charging Cable): $1,528 on bluettipower.com, $1,451.60 through this link, which opens BLUETTI's checkout with both in the cart and the discount already applied (tested 2026-11-10): https://dealectric.com/go/kit-fridge-solar-backup. It is BLUETTI's own Elite 200 V2 + 2×200W Solar bundle at $1,499. Bought separately, the station ($899) and a two-pack of 200 W panels ($798) come to $1,697, so the bundle saves $198. Add BLUETTI's 20 A MC4-to-XT60 cable: BLUETTI's Elite 200 V2 page says the station ships without a solar charging cable and should be paired with a 20 A MC4-XT60 cable bought separately.

Wire the two panels in series. The Elite 200 V2 takes 12–60 V, 20 A and 1,000 W max on its solar input. BLUETTI rates each 200 W panel at 24.6 V open-circuit and 10.8 A short-circuit. In series: 2 × 24.6 V = 49.2 V, inside the 60 V limit. In parallel: 2 × 10.8 A = 21.6 A, over the 20 A limit. A third panel doesn't fit either way, since 3 × 24.6 V = 73.8 V. That makes 400 W the largest array of these panels this station takes.

The 350 W bundle needs care. BLUETTI rates its 350 W panel at 33 V open-circuit, so two in series reach 66 V, above the 60 V limit, and the pair must go in parallel. Its parallel current against the 20 A limit isn't on BLUETTI's US page, so ask BLUETTI before choosing it.

Compressor startup watts. BASC's example fridge draws 3.5 A (about 420 W) running and about 800 W to start. Some older or side-by-side units need up to 2,000 W to start. The Elite 200 V2 is rated 2,600 W continuous. Its 3,900 W Power Lifting figure applies to resistive loads only, and BLUETTI publishes no separate surge rating.

One setting before you trust it overnight: turn AC ECO off. BLUETTI's AC70P manual says ECO mode is on by default and switches the output off when the load stays under 40 W. A fridge resting between compressor cycles can sit below that. StorageReview notes the Elite 200 V2's ECO mode can be toggled separately on the AC and DC outputs. To leave the fridge plugged in for automatic switchover, note that BLUETTI claims 15 ms UPS switching for the Elite 200 V2, as quoted by StorageReview.

Not recommended

The Elite 200 V2 + 200W Solar bundle for a full-size fridge. One panel needs 10.7 full-sun hours to cover a 630 kWh/yr fridge's day, more than twice the 4 to 4.5 hours most of the country gets. It stretches about 23 hours of battery to about 1.6 days. For $300 more, the two-panel bundle reaches about 4–5 days.
