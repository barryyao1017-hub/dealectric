What the differences mean

  • Cost per Wh: price ÷ rated Wh. Vatrer $899.99 ÷ 5,120 Wh = $0.176; Redodo 200Ah $889.99 ÷ 5,120 = $0.174; LiTime LiHeat $1,063.99 ÷ 5,888 = $0.181; Redodo 100Ah $469.99 ÷ 2,560 = $0.184; LiTime 100Ah $499.99 ÷ 2,560 = $0.195; Epoch $1,199 ÷ 2,560 = $0.468.
  • Continuous watts: BMS amps × 25.6 V nominal. 200 A × 25.6 = 5,120 W; 120 A × 25.6 = 3,072 W; 100 A × 25.6 = 2,560 W.
  • What a 3,000 W inverter draws: 3,000 W ÷ 0.9 (assumed inverter efficiency) = 3,333 W DC, and 3,333 ÷ 25.6 V = about 130 A. That is more than a 100 A or 120 A BMS allows.

  • Heat for the money: the Vatrer costs $10 more than the Redodo 200Ah ($899.99 vs $889.99) and adds self-heating.

Why one 200A pack beats a 24V 100Ah for inverter use

A LiFePO4 battery can only deliver the current its BMS allows. At 24V, a 100 A BMS caps output at 2,560 W DC, which is about 2,304 W AC after inverter losses. That covers a 2,000 W inverter but not a 3,000 W one running at full load. The 200A packs here (Vatrer, Redodo and LiTime LiHeat) list 5,120 W continuous. They cost less per Wh than every 24V 100Ah battery in the table because the BMS, case and terminals are shared across twice the cells. If you need still more energy, the Vatrer 24V 300Ah Bluetooth is $1,259.99 for 7,680 Wh, or $0.164 per Wh, with the same 200 A BMS and 5,120 W.

A big 24V inverter can also trip a battery on hook-up, not just under load. Epoch's own installation guide explains that an inverter's DC capacitors can look like a short circuit when first connected. For a Victron MultiPlus-II 24/3000 it puts the spike at 'several hundred to over 1,000 A'. Its fix is a 47 Ω pre-charge resistor (50 W minimum, 100 W recommended) held across the main disconnect for 5 to 10 seconds. That limits the starting current to 25.6 V ÷ 47 Ω ≈ 0.54 A. Plan this step for any of these batteries paired with a 3,000 W-class inverter.

Not recommended

Epoch 24V 100Ah Heated (B24100A-C). At $1,199 it costs $0.468 per Wh, 2.7 times the Vatrer's per-Wh price, for half the energy. On 2026-11-13 Epoch's US store listed it as sold out. Its 120 A BMS (3,072 W) still falls short of a fully loaded 3,000 W inverter. Epoch's page lists Bluetooth to its own app but no CAN or Victron link. Its '11-year' warranty gives free replacement only in years 1–3, then 40% off MSRP in years 4–11. If you want a heated 24V battery, the LiTime LiHeat 24V 230Ah costs $135 less and stores 2.3 times the energy.
