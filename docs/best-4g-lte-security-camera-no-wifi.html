What the differences mean

The eufy S330 is our pick, but we don't earn from it: its buy link goes to eufy's own store. Our partner camera here is the Arenti GO3G, the low-cost option.

  • Price gap: the eufy S330 costs $210 more than the Arenti GO3G on the two makers' stores ($249.99 − $39.99). The GO3G costs about 16% of the eufy's price (39.99 ÷ 249.99 = 0.16).
  • Battery claims don't compare directly. eufy says about a month per charge at 10 ten-second motion events and 5 live views a day. Reolink says about 2 months for the Go PT Ultra and gives no workload. Reolink's claim implies about 0.36 Wh a day (21.6 Wh ÷ 60 days). At its rated output, the 6 W panel would put that back in about 4 minutes (0.36 ÷ 6 = 0.06 h).
  • Solar on the eufy: eufy says about 2 hours of direct sun a day keeps it charged. At rated output that is at most 9 Wh a day from the included panel (4.5 W × 2 h).
  • Live view eats data. Reolink rates an hour of Go PT Ultra live view in Clear mode at about 1.3 GB, so a 1 GB plan covers about 46 minutes (1 ÷ 1.3 = 0.77 h). Fluent mode gives 3.5 hours per GB, about 4.5× longer (3.5 ÷ 0.77). With Reolink's 1 GB plan at $5.99 a month, Clear-mode viewing works out to about $7.79 an hour (5.99 ÷ 0.77).
  • Alerts only use far less. eufy puts the S330 at about 700 MB a month for 10 ten-second live views and 25 ten-second clips a day. Outdoor Tech Lab measured a Go PT Ultra at 2.7 GB over 90 days on Verizon 4G LTE with 847 motion events. That is about 0.9 GB per 30 days (2.7 ÷ 90 = 30 MB a day) at about 9.4 events a day (847 ÷ 90).

Which SIM works where: the carrier rules each maker publishes

With no Wi-Fi, the SIM is the camera's only link. Reolink states plainly that the Go PT Ultra 'does not support WiFi connection'. It also says the camera takes no 5G or 2G SIM cards, and its US SIM guide says the card must be a nano SIM. If the plan or SIM fails, you lose remote access entirely.

The SIM in the box and your own SIM follow different rules. Both makers ship a SIM that picks the strongest of AT&T, T-Mobile and Verizon. The difference shows up when that trial runs out and you want your own plan. eufy says 'your own nano SIM also works with a data-only plan'. Reolink's US spec table lists only T-Mobile and AT&T, both prepaid data-only, plus Mint and FreedomPop. Reolink's US SIM guide recommends a T-Mobile prepaid data-only SIM first. For Verizon, it only says to test a Verizon SIM you already have before relying on it. Both cameras list the same US LTE bands (B2/B4/B5/B12/B13/B14/B66/B71), so the difference is which carriers each maker supports, not the radio. In the Reolink Community forum, one owner (Maz_8193) reported that a Go PT Ultra connected on an EIOTCLUB SIM but not on a Verizon phone-number SIM. Reolink's troubleshooting article warns that 'some phone-only SIM plans may not work properly' and recommends a data or IoT plan.

Same camera, two names. Reolink's US SIM guide lists 'Reolink Go PT Ultra, Go Series G440' and 'Reolink Go PT Plus, Go Series G430' together. Reolink's own G440 page gives the same 4K sensor, 355° / 140° pan and tilt, 21.6 Wh battery and US bands as the Go PT Ultra, and the G440 listing adds a 32 GB card. Compare prices under both names before you buy either.

None of these records 24/7. eufy lists the S330's recording method as 'Event'. Reolink lists the Go PT Ultra as motion-triggered, and it isn't on Reolink's list of battery cameras that can record continuously.
