Which systems really have the inverter and battery in one box

A true all-in-one has a hybrid inverter inside: solar panels wire straight into the battery unit through its own MPPT inputs, and the same unit makes household AC power. Tesla's spec sheet gives Powerwall 3 six MPPTs and 20 kW of solar input in the same enclosure as its 13.5 kWh battery. FranklinWH says the aPower S "integrates formerly external inverter with battery storage into a single unit". It has four MPPTs, 20 kW of solar input and 11.5 kW of AC output.

The FranklinWH aPower 2 looks similar on paper (15 kWh, 10 kW), but FranklinWH's own spec table lists it as "AC-coupled" and gives it no MPPT or solar input. Panels have to feed a separate solar inverter first, and that inverter then charges the battery over AC. FranklinWH also says the aPower S can run in parallel with aPower 2 units, so the two can be mixed in one system.

The Anker SOLIX E10 and EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra X sit in between. The inverter and solar inputs are in one power unit, and battery modules from the same maker stack beside or under it. The EG4 route is a hybrid inverter plus a separate 48 V wall battery: two boxes, but the most solar input here (21 kW). If you are comparing efficiency, Tesla rates Powerwall 3 at 89% for solar to battery to home. Its 97.5% figure applies only when solar power goes straight to the home.

Not recommended

FranklinWH aPower 2 for a new solar install. It is a good battery, but it has no solar input, so buying it as an all-in-one means paying for a second inverter. If you want FranklinWH, choose the aPower S, which has the same 15 kWh and the same 15-year/60 MWh warranty with the solar inverter built in. Also avoid the original EcoFlow DELTA Pro Ultra: EcoFlow's US store listed it as unavailable on 2026-11-13, and its in-stock successor is the DELTA Pro Ultra X in the table.
