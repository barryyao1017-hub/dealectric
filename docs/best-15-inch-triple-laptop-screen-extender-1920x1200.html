What the differences mean

Sharpness. A 1920×1200 panel has a diagonal of √(1920² + 1200²) = 2,264.2 pixels. That gives 2,264.2 ÷ 15.3 = 148.0 ppi on the S980's screens and 2,264.2 ÷ 16 = 141.5 ppi on the 16-inch FOPO screens, about 4.6% apart. A 15.6-inch 1920×1080 panel works out to 2,202.9 ÷ 15.6 = 141.2 ppi and shows 1,200 − 1,080 = 120 fewer rows of pixels per screen.

Width. A 16:10 screen is 0.848 × its diagonal wide. Each S980 screen is 15.3 × 0.848 = 13.0 in wide, and each 16-inch screen 16 × 0.848 = 13.6 in.

Working space. Three added 1920×1200 screens give 3 × 2,304,000 = 6.9 million pixels. Two give 2 × 2,304,000 = 4.6 million.

Weight. FOPO states 2.64 kg for the S788 (2.64 ÷ 3 = 0.88 kg per added screen) and 2 kg for the S11 (2 ÷ 2 = 1.0 kg per screen). FOPO's S980 page gives no weight.

Fit. FOPO rates the S980, S788 and S11 for laptops of about 12–18.5 inches, subject to physical fit. Macsecor rates its S6 for 13–16-inch laptops.

One cable, three screens: display driver or DisplayPort MST

To run several screens from one USB-C port without a driver, an extender has to split the port's DisplayPort signal using Multi-Stream Transport (MST). Plugable, a dock maker, says Windows and ChromeOS “fully support MST for extended desktop configurations”, while on macOS “MST-connected displays are mirrored, not extended”. So the same one-cable, driver-free extender can give three separate screens on a Windows laptop and three copies of one picture on a Mac. Plugable points Mac owners to driver-based (DisplayLink) setups instead.

The Mac also limits how many screens it drives without a driver. Apple lists one external display for the 15-inch MacBook Air M2 (2023) and up to two for the 15-inch MacBook Air M4 (2025). UPERFECT notes that base M1 and M2 MacBooks drive one. On these laptops, three added screens need a display driver.

Both FOPO triples use a driver. The S788 runs on FOPO's UsbDisplay driver, which FOPO lists for Macs from 2019 including M1, M2 and M3. On macOS the driver has to be allowed under Screen Recording. The S980 also needs FOPO's supplied driver and a separate PD power supply, over USB-C or USB-A. Its page lists no operating systems and asks buyers to confirm OS support and permission to install the driver, so Mac owners should check with FOPO first or choose the S788. The S11 and S9G need no driver: each screen takes its own video-capable USB-C port, or USB-C plus HDMI. FOPO excludes base-chip MacBooks from the S11 setup.
