Connecting to the house panel: what BLUETTI sells for each

Apex 300. BLUETTI's Apex 300 Home Integration Kit costs $699 for one Apex 300 and $799 for parallel setups. Its listing says 'Compatible Models: Apex300'. The kit contains a Reliance sub panel with a 50 A SS2-50 inlet box, a 50 A outlet box, a NEMA 14-50 to SS2-50R cable and a 50 A fast-charging cable. That cable route uses the Apex 300's 120/240 V NEMA 14-50R, so one unit can feed both legs of a split-phase panel. $699 on bluettipower.com, $664.05 through this link with code DEALECTRIC: https://dealectric.com/go/bluetti-apex-300-home-integration-kit The sub panel wires into your home's electrical system, so plan for an electrician to install it.

Apex 200. BLUETTI's Apex 200 page lists B300K and B500K batteries (up to six per unit), the Hub A1, Charger 2 and a TT-30 charging cable. It lists no transfer switch or panel kit. The only Apex 200 home backup listing on BLUETTI's store is a $5,699 bundle of two Apex 200s, two B300Ks, a Hub A1 and a Home Backup Kit. The Hub A1 manual (V1.2) covers only Apex 300 units. The Solar Lab says 'the Apex 200 doesn't offer the same 240V split-phase capabilities that the Apex 300 offers'. BLUETTI's own article on split-phase power says that with only 120 volts, you would be powering 'half your home'.

Switchover during an outage. BLUETTI advertises 0 ms for the Apex 300. StorageReview reports that 0 ms applies only to two outlets in 240 V mode. In 120 V mode the unit is rated at 20 ms, and StorageReview measured 16 ms. The Apex 200 is rated at ≤15 ms, but its UPS mode is off until you turn it on in the BLUETTI app.

Shipping. BLUETTI's US pages say both units are expected to ship in late January. For the Apex 200, that applies to orders placed on or after Nov 14.
