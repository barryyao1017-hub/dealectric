Best quiet gaming controller

Short answer. The best quiet gaming controller is the EasySMX X05 Pro ($35.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/easysmx-x05-pro): silicone-damped face buttons, D-pad, bumpers and triggers that three reviewers describe as a whisper or a soft click, Hall Effect sticks, 1,000 Hz polling over the 2.4 GHz dongle and wired, and a 1,000 mAh battery that reviewers ran for 6 to 9 hours. The one loud input left is pressing the sticks in (L3/R3). The catch: it does not work on Xbox or PlayStation consoles. It covers Windows PC, Steam Deck, Nintendo Switch, Android and iOS.

If you play on a PS5, the quiet pick is Sony's own DualSense: its membrane face buttons were called the quietest first-party pad by every respondent who compared it on three forums we read. If you play mostly on Switch 2 and want a Switch-style layout with a 15-hour battery, Mobapad sells a Silent Edition of its Chitu 2 HD for $49.99 (mobapad.com, 2026-11-06), though no one has published a noise test of it yet. Xbox console owners have no quiet option: the standard Xbox Wireless Controller is the pad most often named as the loud one.

Why this matters: in six threads we read, the buyer was not asking for silence for themselves. A partner with misophonia or Asperger's, a sleeping spouse, or a roommate was being woken or set off by the clack. The loudest parts are consistent: bumpers and shoulder buttons (loudest input on 9 of 26 controllers in Game Informer's decibel test), the Xbox D-pad, and the stick click.

What owners complain about

Across the Steam, Tom's Hardware, OzBargain, Tinnitus Talk and Reddit threads we read, the request was the same: a partner or roommate is bothered by the clack, often at night. Three things come up most. First, the Xbox controller is the one named loud, by five posters across four threads, with the D-pad and bumpers singled out. Second, the stick click (L3/R3) and squeaky sticks are a separate noise source that quiet face buttons do not fix. Third, controllers marketed as pro, with mechanical microswitch buttons, are louder, not quieter: one 8BitDo Ultimate 2C owner said the face buttons were too loud to use while a partner slept.

For the X05 Pro itself, reviewers' complaints are battery life (6 to 9 hours per charge), mushy bumpers, a short-trigger mode that still travels further than a true hair trigger, and one report from GamingOnLinux of triggers misreading fast light presses as full pulls until pulled fully. No reviewer complained about noise.

Which one by need

  • PC, Steam Deck or Switch in a shared room: EasySMX X05 Pro ($35.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/easysmx-x05-pro). It is the only sub-$40 controller built for quiet, and the reviews back the claim.
  • PS5: Sony DualSense. Membrane face buttons, and the pad forum users who compared it call quieter than Xbox and 8BitDo pads.
  • Switch 2 first, with a Switch layout and long battery: Mobapad Chitu 2 HD Silent Edition. It has TMR sticks and the standard Chitu 2 measured 15 hours of battery, but it has no 2.4 GHz dongle for PC and nobody has measured how quiet it is.
  • Budget, noise does not matter: EasySMX X05 ($29.99; buy: https://dealectric.com/go/easysmx-x05) saves $6 and keeps the Hall sticks.
  • Competitive shooters: Use the X05 Pro wired. Gamepadla measured about 10 ms button and 36 ms stick latency over the dongle versus 5 ms and 14 ms wired.

Not recommended for quiet play

The standard Xbox Wireless Controller, for the clicky D-pad and bumpers. Any controller advertised with mechanical or microswitch ABXY buttons, including the EasySMX X20 and S10, GameSir and 8BitDo pro models: those switches are the clicky kind. And do not count on vibration-off alone to fix noise; the clack comes from the buttons hitting the shell, which only damping fixes.
