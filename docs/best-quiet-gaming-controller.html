What “quiet” actually covers on each controller

Most of the noise comes from the switch under each button. EasySMX's own explainer says microswitch buttons “often produce a clear click when activated”. Rubber membranes “usually sound softer” but can still make noise when the keycap bottoms out or returns. It also warns that “strong rumble can create greater room noise than the buttons”. None of these four makers publishes a decibel figure, so the comparison rests on what each maker says it silenced and on named reviewers' ear tests.

Mobapad Chitu 2 HD Silent Edition. In HLPLANET's spec table (Snimtas, 2026-05-21), every button that uses a microswitch on the standard model uses liquid silicone rubber on the Silent Edition. That covers ABXY, D-pad, triggers, bumpers and +/−. The sticks also glide on silicone tracks instead of hitting a hard plastic ring, with “almost no sound at all when you move the sticks”. No other pad here deals with stick-ring knock. HLPLANET calls it “one of the quietest controllers you can get (and maybe even the quietest overall)”. The trade-off: the D-pad cap is fixed.

EasySMX X05 Pro. EasySMX says its buttons use “restructured mechanics and noise-dampening silicone” but does not list which ones. Its launch post describes an “8-Directional D-Pad with metal dome switches”. Two named ear tests split. Eric Hauter (Gaming Nexus, 2025-11-14) found the face buttons “not loud, but nothing special” with a small “pop”. He found the triggers, bumpers and D-pad “extraordinarily quiet”, and called it “definitely the quietest” of his many controllers. Liam Squires-Hand (GamingOnLinux, 2025-11-10) found “every other button is pretty much a whisper” apart from the thumbstick clicks, and felt “a clear click” on the D-pad. He also hit a trigger fault where light presses read as full pulls, which he called “a complete deal-breaker”. It is still the cheapest controller here with a maker quiet claim. $35.99: https://dealectric.com/go/easysmx-x05-pro

GameSir Super Nova. GameSir limits its claim to “silent ABXY buttons”. Its spec sheet lists a membrane D-pad, but it makes no quiet claim for the bumpers or triggers.

EasySMX S05 Pro. EasySMX promises “silicone-damped buttons” and a “smooth disc-pivot D-pad”, with Switch 2 wake-up and a dock. We have no named ear test for it yet. $54.99: https://dealectric.com/go/easysmx-s05-pro

Turn the rumble down too. Quiet buttons don't help if the motors buzz against a desk. On PS5, Sony's controller settings have Vibration Intensity and Trigger Effect Intensity under Settings > Accessories > Controller (General). Lower both to quiet the DualSense's motors and trigger effects.

Not recommended

EasySMX X05 (non-Pro) for quiet play. It looks like the X05 Pro and costs $29.99, $6.00 less, but EasySMX's listing makes no quiet-button claim for it. If silence is the reason you're buying, get the Pro.
