What the differences mean

Dealectric does not test units. Every figure here comes from the maker, from Apple or from a named reviewer.

Without a driver, you get the smallest of three numbers: the screens in the box, the video links your laptop can feed them, and how many external displays the laptop drives with its lid open.

  • FOPO S11 on a Windows laptop with one DP Alt Mode USB-C port plus HDMI: min(2 screens, 2 links, 2 displays) = 2. FOPO allows USB-C to one screen and HDMI to the other.
  • Trio 3 Pro on the same laptop: min(2, 1 DP-enabled USB-C port, 2) = 1 without Mobile Pixels' software, because its driver-free path needs two DP-enabled USB-C ports (Macworld).
  • Any two-screen extender on a MacBook Air M2: min(2, 2, 1) = 1, since Apple specifies one external display.
  • The same extender on a MacBook Air M4 or M5: min(2, 2, 2) = 2.
  • Pixels per added screen: Trio 3 Pro 2240 × 1400 = 3.14 MP; FOPO S11 1920 × 1200 = 2.30 MP; FOPO S1 1920 × 1080 = 2.07 MP; Duex Float 2 Pro 2560 × 1600 = 4.10 MP.
  • Weight per added screen: FOPO S1 1.42 kg ÷ 2 = 0.71 kg (FOPO); Trio 3 Pro 4.13 lb ÷ 2 = 2.07 lb (Macworld); Duex Float 2 Pro 2.9 lb ÷ 1 = 2.9 lb (Gadget Pilipinas).
  • Brightness on laptop power: Notebookcheck measured a one-cable UPERFECT UStation Delta at 160 cd/m² on laptop power against 226 cd/m² with its adapter. That is 160 ÷ 226 = 71%. Macworld found the Trio 3 Pro could not reach full brightness on laptop power alone. Plug in the extender's own power to get the rated brightness.

How many screens a MacBook runs with the lid open

You use a clip-on extender with the lid open, so the count that matters is Apple's lid-open figure.

  • MacBook Air M1 and M2: one external display alongside the built-in screen (Apple).
  • MacBook Air M3: one external display with the lid open. A second works only with the lid closed, which an extender mounted on the lid can't use (Apple).
  • MacBook Air M4 and M5: up to two external displays with the built-in display on (Apple).
  • Pro chips: M1 and M2 Pro drive 2 and M5 Pro drives 3 (Plugable's chip list).

Each added screen needs its own video stream. A dual extender that runs both panels from one cable does it through a display driver; the Trio 3 Pro's one-cable mode, for example, needs Mobile Pixels' software (Macworld). A USB-C MST splitter doesn't get around this on a Mac. Plugable says macOS mirrors the same image on MST displays instead of giving each its own desktop. Mobile Pixels' own FAQ says its Trio runs driver-free only from a USB-C port with DisplayPort; otherwise it needs drivers.

Not recommended

FOPO S788 triple extender. FOPO lists its UsbDisplay display driver as required on both USB-C and dual-head USB-A connections, so it fails "without drivers" on every laptop. The Trio 3 Pro's single-cable mode is the same trap: it needs Mobile Pixels' software, so use two cables.
